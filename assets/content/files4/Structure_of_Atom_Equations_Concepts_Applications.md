## SECTION 0: FUNDAMENTAL ATOMIC STRUCTURE CONCEPTS

|---------|------------|-------------------|------------------|-------------|----------|-----------|
| **Atom Composition** | Nucleus (protons + neutrons) + electron cloud around nucleus; nucleus diameter ~10⁻¹⁵ m, atom ~10⁻¹⁰ m (nuclear radius ~1/100,000 atomic radius) | Nucleus: Z protons + (A−Z) neutrons; mass number A = Z + N; charge = +Ze | Mostly empty space (Rutherford's findings); positive nucleus surrounded by negative electron cloud; electrons carry 99.95% of atom's mass despite being ~1836× lighter than proton | Atom size determined by electron cloud, not nucleus; nuclear density enormous (~10¹⁴ g/cm³) | 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐ |

| **De Broglie Wavelength (Matter Waves)** | Particles (electrons, atoms) exhibit wave properties; wavelength λ = h/p where p = mv (momentum) | λ = h/p = h/(mv); for electron: λ = h/(m_e × v) ≈ 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ / (9.109 × 10⁻³¹ × v) meters | Electron wavelength ~ atomic size (10⁻¹⁰ m), explaining wave-like behavior in atoms; macroscopic objects have negligible wavelength | Electron diffraction experiments confirm matter waves; basis for quantum mechanical description of atoms | 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐ |
| **Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle** | Cannot simultaneously determine position and momentum with arbitrary precision: Δx·Δp ≥ ℏ/2 | Uncertainty product: Δx·Δp ≥ h/(4π); also Δt·ΔE ≥ ℏ/2; smaller Δx → larger Δp and vice versa | Explains why orbits undefined (can't know both position and velocity precisely); supports orbital concept (probability region) | Fundamental limit on classical description of particles; sets minimum energy (~ℏω/2, zero-point energy) | 2021, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐⭐ |

### PART B: Electron Configuration & Aufbau Principle

| Concept | Rule/Definition | Method | Example (Atoms) | Special Cases | PYQ Tags | JEE Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Aufbau Principle (Building-Up)** | Fill orbitals in order of increasing energy (not strictly n order due to shielding); (n+l) order, then n order for tie | Fill 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p, 5s, 4d, 5p, 6s, 4f, 5d, 6p, 7s, 5f, 6d, 7p | H (1s¹), He (1s²), Li (1s² 2s¹), Be (1s² 2s²), B (1s² 2s² 2p¹), C (1s² 2s² 2p²) | 4s fills before 3d (but 3d electrons removed first when ionizing transition metals); f-block elements (lanthanides, actinides) fill 4f and 5f after 6s and 7s | 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐ |
| **Pauli Exclusion Principle** | No two electrons in atom can have identical set of all four quantum numbers (n, l, m_l, m_s) | Check each orbital: maximum 2 electrons with opposite spins; once (n, l, m_l) filled with one e⁻, second must have opposite m_s | Prevents all electrons collapsing to 1s orbital; explains periodicity (when shell fills, element properties repeat) | Critical for determining electron capacity: shells 2n², subshells 2(2l+1), orbitals 2 | 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐ |
| **Hund's Rule (Maximum Multiplicity)** | Fill orbitals in subshell with parallel spins (same m_s) before pairing; minimizes electron-electron repulsion | One electron in each orbital first (all spin-up ↑), then pair electrons (add spin-down ↓); maximizes total spin S | Explains why O is 1s² 2s² 2p⁴ as ↑↓ ↑ ↑ (not ↑↓ ↑↓ ↑) in 2p orbitals; C is ↑ ↑ (not ↑↓) in 2p | Valence spin affects magnetism (paramagnetic if unpaired electrons); explains why C and O are paramagnetic in certain configurations | 2021, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐ |
| **Full vs. Condensed Configuration** | Full: list all orbitals (1s² 2s² 2p⁶ ...); Condensed: [Noble gas core] + valence electrons | Use nearest noble gas with fewer electrons + remaining config; e.g., [Ne] = 1s² 2s² 2p⁶; Na = [Ne] 3s¹ | Cu: full = 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d¹⁰ 4s¹ (not 4s² 3d⁹); condensed = [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹; allows half-filled 3d for stability | Transition metals (d-block): often 4s² before 3d filling, but 3d¹⁰ 4s¹ or 3d⁵ 4s¹ more stable than 3d⁹ 4s² or 3d⁴ 4s² (half-filled/full subshell stability) | 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐ |

### PART B: De Broglie Wavelength & Wave-Particle Duality
