**Octahedral Field Summary:**
- **e_g orbitals (2 orbitals):** d_z² & d_x²-y² point directly at ligands → higher repulsion → higher energy (+0.6Δ_o each)
- **t₂g orbitals (3 orbitals):** d_xy, d_xz, d_yz point between ligands → lower repulsion → lower energy (−0.4Δ_o each)
- **Energy gap:** Δ_o = 1 unit (barycenter = 0)
- **Characteristic of:** Fe²⁺, Fe³⁺, Co²⁺, Co³⁺, Ni²⁺, Cu²⁺, Cr³⁺, Mn²⁺

**Square Planar Summary:**
- **d⁸ configuration:** d_x²-y² (SOMO/antibonding) > d_z² > {d_xy, d_xz, d_yz}

- **Characteristic of:** Ni²⁺, Pd²⁺, Pt²⁺, Au³⁺, Cu²⁺ (distorted)
- **Why d⁸?** 16 valence electrons; d_x²-y² is antibonding (high energy) → stabilized if empty (square planar better than octahedral for d⁸)

**Decision Tree for d⁶ (Most Common JEE Question):**
1. **Identify ligand:** Check spectrochemical series position
2. **Estimate Δ_o:** Strong-field (CN⁻, CO, NH₃) → Δ_o large; weak-field (H₂O, Cl⁻) → Δ_o small
3. **Compare Δ_o vs P:** If Δ_o > P → LS (zero unpaired); if Δ_o < P → HS (four unpaired)
4. **Predict magnetism:** LS d⁶ = diamagnetic (μ = 0); HS d⁶ = paramagnetic (μ = 4.90 BM)
5. **Example:** [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ (CN⁻ strong-field Δ_o >> P) → LS, μ = 0, yellow-brown, inert

| | CO | Very Strong σ-donor + π-acceptor | Strong σ (C lone pair donation) | No π-donor | Very strong π-acceptor (CO π*-orbital accepts d-electrons via back-bonding) | Very High Δ_o | Carbon monoxide: strongest π-acceptor; facilitates π-back-donation from low-valent metal d-electrons; Δ_o enormous; M(CO)_n always LS | 1.3 | 2024, 2022 | ⭐⭐⭐ |
| **Strongest** | CN⁻ | Very Strong σ-donor + π-acceptor | Very strong σ (C lone pair; excellent overlap) | No π-donor | π-acceptor (C≡N π*-orbital accepts d-electrons via back-bonding) | Very High Δ_o | Cyanide: strongest σ-donor + π-acceptor combination; maximizes Δ_o; extreme LS tendency; [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ classic example; extremely stable | 1.4 | 2026, 2025, 2024, 2023, 2021 | ⭐⭐⭐ |

**Why This Order?**

1. **σ-Donation dominates:** Stronger σ-donor → higher electron density in metal orbitals → raises all d-orbitals → larger average → larger Δ_o (e_g rises more than t₂g average)
2. **π-Donors lower Δ_o slightly:** π-electron density fills t₂g orbitals → lowers e_g relative to t₂g → slightly reduces Δ_o (but weak effect)
3. **π-Acceptors raise Δ_o greatly:** π-acceptor stabilizes low d-electron density on metal by withdrawing electron density via π-back-bonding → effectively increases Δ_o
4. **Combined effect:** Ligands with strong σ + strong π-accept (CN⁻, CO) dominate series. Weak σ + no π (I⁻, Cl⁻) at bottom.

**CFSE Interpretation:**
- **More negative CFSE** = more stable complex (electrons in lower-energy orbitals)
- **LS (d⁶, d⁷) CFSE** more negative than HS CFSE when Δ >> P
- **HS (weak field)** favored when small Δ_o makes pairing energy P dominate CFSE comparison
- **Example:** [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ LS CFSE = −2.4Δ + 3P ≈ −2.4(0.5) + 3(0.3) = −1.2 + 0.9 = −0.3Δ equiv.; HS would be −0.4Δ; LS is stabilized by ~0.1Δ units when Δ_o is very large (CN⁻)

**Chelate Effect Mechanism:**

$$\Delta G° = \Delta H° − T\Delta S°$$

- **Monodentate:** M + 4NH₃ → [M(NH₃)₄]; dissociation releases 4 free NH₃ molecules
- **Bidentate:** M + 2en → [M(en)₂]; dissociation releases only 2 free en molecules
- **Entropy effect:** Releasing 4 NH₃ → ΔS_dissoc = large and positive (entropy increases going backward = complex is entropy-destabilized relative to bidentate)
- **Result:** ΔS_formation larger for bidentate (ΔS_form = −ΔS_dissoc more favorable); ΔG_formation more negative for bidentate
- **EDTA colossal:** 6 coordination sites + highly negative charge → extreme ΔS; K_f ≈ 10²⁰-10²⁵ (essentially irreversible complex formation)

## Section 12: Advanced Problem Patterns & Solutions

### Problem 1: Multi-Equilibrium Complex Formation with Precipitation

**Given:**
- K_f([Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺) ≈ 2 × 10¹³
- K_sp(Cu(OH)₂) ≈ 2 × 10⁻²⁰
- Initial: 0.10 M CuSO₄ + 1.0 M NH₃; pH = 10.5 ([OH⁻] ≈ 3 × 10⁻⁴ M)
- **Question:** Will Cu(OH)₂ precipitate? Calculate % Cu²⁺ as free ion.

**Solution:**

| Step | Calculation | Result | Explanation |
|------|------------|--------|-------------|
| 1. Calculate [Cu²⁺] free in complex equilibrium | [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺ ⇌ Cu²⁺ + 4NH₃; K_diss = 1/K_f = 1/(2×10¹³) = 5×10⁻¹⁴ | Very small K_diss | Complex formation is favorable (K_f large) |
| 2. Approximate: assume ~all Cu as complex | [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺ ≈ 0.10 M; [NH₃]free ≈ 1.0 M (excess) | [Complex] dominant | Cu²⁺ almost entirely complexed |
| 3. Calculate free [Cu²⁺] from dissociation | K_diss = [Cu²⁺][NH₃]⁴ / [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺; 5×10⁻¹⁴ = [Cu²⁺](1.0)⁴ / (0.10) | [Cu²⁺] = 5×10⁻¹⁵ M | Extremely low free Cu²⁺ |
| 4. Calculate ion product Q for precipitation | Q = [Cu²⁺][OH⁻]² = (5×10⁻¹⁵)(3×10⁻⁴)² = (5×10⁻¹⁵)(9×10⁻⁸) = 4.5×10⁻²² | Q ≈ 4.5×10⁻²² | |
| 5. Compare Q vs K_sp | K_sp(Cu(OH)₂) ≈ 2×10⁻²⁰; Q ≈ 4.5×10⁻²² | Q < K_sp | No precipitation; complex prevents hydroxide precipitation |
| 6. Calculate % Cu²⁺ as free ion | % free = [Cu²⁺]_free / [Cu]_total × 100% = (5×10⁻¹⁵) / (0.10) × 100% | 5×10⁻¹² % | Essentially 100% complexed |

**Answer:** Cu(OH)₂ does NOT precipitate (Q < K_sp); ammonia complex prevents precipitation by sequestering Cu²⁺. Free Cu²⁺ concentration is negligibly small.

**Given:**
- Complex: [Fe(SCN)₆]³⁻ (SCN⁻ = thiocyanate; position ~7 in spectrochemical series; medium-strong field)
- Fe³⁺ = d⁵
- Assume Δ_o ≈ 0.95Δ_o(ref); P ≈ 0.5Δ_o(ref)

**Determine:** Spin state and magnetic moment

**Solution:**

| Step | Analysis | Result | Reasoning |
|------|----------|--------|-----------|
| 1. Locate SCN⁻ in spectrochemical series | CN⁻ > CO > NO > NH₃ > en > **NCS⁻** > H₂O > OH⁻ > F⁻ > Cl⁻ > Br⁻ > I⁻ | Medium-Strong field ligand | SCN⁻ ≈ position 7; moderate Δ_o |
| 2. Estimate Δ_o for Fe³⁺-SCN⁻ | Δ_o(Fe-SCN) ≈ 0.95 × reference Δ_o | ~2400 cm⁻¹ approx. (0.30 eV) | Medium field |
| 3. Compare Δ_o vs P for d⁵ | P ≈ 0.5Δ_o; Δ_o = 0.95Δ_o(ref) | Δ_o ≈ 2 × P | Δ_o > P suggests LS favorable but d⁵ special case |
| 4. Apply d⁵ rule | d⁵: half-filled t₂g⁵ is especially stable; BOTH HS (t₂g⁵) and LS (t₂g⁵—no pairing in t₂g for d⁵) are identical | **Always HIGH-SPIN for d⁵** | Half-filled d-orbitals maximize exchange energy; LS/HS same config (t₂g⁵ e_g⁰) |
| 5. Assign electron configuration | Fe³⁺ d⁵ HS: t₂g⁵ e_g⁰ (5 unpaired electrons in t₂g) | 5 unpaired | Even with medium-strong field, d⁵ remains HS |
| 6. Calculate magnetic moment | μ = √[n(n+2)] = √[5(5+2)] = √35 = 5.92 BM | μ = 5.92 BM | d⁵ always high-spin → 5 unpaired electrons |

**Answer:** [Fe(SCN)₆]³⁻ is HIGH-SPIN d⁵ with μ = 5.92 BM (paramagnetic, very strongly magnetic).

### Problem 3: Geometric Isomerism Reactivity - Cisplatin vs Transplatin

**Given:**
- Cisplatin: cis-[Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] (anticancer active)
- Transplatin: trans-[Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] (anticancer inactive)
- K₂[PtCl₄] → [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] via NH₃ substitution

**Question:** Explain why cis is active & trans is not at molecular level.

**Solution:**

| Aspect | Cisplatin [cis-Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] | Transplatin [trans-Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] | Molecular Reason | Biological Consequence | PYQ Tags | JEE Level |
|--------|---------------------------|--------------------------|---------|----------------------|----------|-----------|
| **Geometry** | 90° angles; NH₃ & Cl⁻ adjacent (L-shaped) | 180° angles; NH₃ & Cl⁻ opposite (linear-like) | Spatial isomerism | Different cellular uptake & DNA binding | 2026, 2024 | ⭐⭐⭐ |
| **Polarity** | Polar (dipole moments add) | Nonpolar (dipole moments cancel) | Electrostatic | Polar cis enters cells better (hydrophobic trans doesn't) | 2025, 2023 | ⭐⭐⭐ |
| **Cl⁻ Lability** | Cl⁻ easily displaced (labile) | Cl⁻ very stable (inert) | Trans effect: trans-NH₃ stabilizes trans-Cl⁻ more than cis-NH₃ stabilizes cis-Cl⁻ | Cis loses Cl⁻ rapidly → DNA binding; trans cannot bind DNA | 2026, 2024 | ⭐⭐⭐ |
| **DNA Binding** | Interstrand cross-link (2 Cl⁻ on adjacent DNA bases) | No effective cross-linking (Cl⁻ far apart geometrically) | Cis geometry places 2 Cl⁻ ~3 Å apart (DNA base pair distance); trans places them ~6 Å apart | Cis induces DNA strand breaks & apoptosis; trans does not | 2026, 2025, 2023 | ⭐⭐⭐ |
| **Cellular Efflux** | Cis accumulates in cell; poor efflux substrate | Trans rapidly extruded by cellular pumps | Polarity & charge distribution | Cis therapeutic; trans wasted | 2024 | ⭐⭐ |

**Answer:** Cisplatin is active because: (1) cis geometry makes Cl⁻ labile & adjacent, (2) polar cis crosses cell membranes better, (3) interstrand DNA cross-linking kills cancer cells. Transplatin is inactive because trans geometry separates Cl⁻ (no cross-linking), is nonpolar (poor cellular uptake), and Cl⁻ is inert (not substituted by DNA bases).

### Problem 4: Optical Isomerism in [Co(en)₃]³⁺

**Given:**
- [Co(en)₃]³⁺: Co³⁺ (d⁶ LS) with 3 bidentate en ligands
- **Question:** (a) Is complex optically active? (b) How many enantiomers? (c) How to resolve?

**Solution:**

| Question | Analysis | Answer | Molecular Detail |
|----------|----------|--------|-----------------|
| **(a) Optically Active?** | Check for chiral center & symmetry | **YES** | Octahedral Co³⁺ with 3 bidentate en (no monodentates to create mirror plane) → helical coordination geometry → no plane of symmetry → chiral |
| **(b) Number of Enantiomers?** | Mirror image: D- vs L-form | **2 enantiomers** (D & L) | [Co(en)₃]³⁺ exists as D-[Co(en)₃]³⁺ (dextrorotatory; right-handed helix) & L-[Co(en)₃]³⁺ (levorotatory; left-handed helix) |
| **(c) How to Resolve?** | Use chiral resolution method | Diastereomeric salt crystallization | [Co(en)₃]³⁺ + (+)-(−)-tartrate ⇌ diastereomeric salts of different solubility; crystallize selectively; centrifuge/filter; regenerate enantiomer |
| **Optical Rotation** | [α]D or [α]589nm measurement | D-form: [α]D ≈ +20°; L-form: [α]D ≈ −20° | Opposite rotation (enantiomeric pair); equal magnitude (mirror image); used to verify purity & stereochemistry |

**Answer:** 
- (a) YES, [Co(en)₃]³⁺ is optically active (no plane of symmetry; helical geometry)
- (b) 2 enantiomers (D & L forms)
- (c) Resolve via diastereomeric (+/-)-tartrate salt formation; crystallize, filter, regenerate pure enantiomer
