# 1. CLASSIFICATION AND NOMENCLATURE

## 1.1 Classification of Alcohols
**Importance: ⭐⭐ JEE MAINS | ⭐⭐⭐ JEE MAINS + ADVANCED**

### Primary Alcohols (1°)
- General formula: R-CH₂-OH
- Examples: Methanol, Ethanol, n-Propanol
- Oxidation gives aldehydes

### Secondary Alcohols (2°)
- General formula: R₂CH-OH
- Examples: Isopropanol, 2-Butanol, Cyclohexanol
- Oxidation gives ketones

### Tertiary Alcohols (3°)
- General formula: R₃C-OH
- Examples: tert-Butanol, tert-Pentyl alcohol
- Not easily oxidized by mild oxidizing agents

## 1.2 Classification of Phenols
**Importance: ⭐⭐ JEE MAINS | ⭐⭐⭐ JEE MAINS + ADVANCED**

### Monohydric Phenols
- Single -OH group attached to benzene ring
- Examples: Phenol (C₆H₅OH), Cresol, Xylenol

### Dihydric Phenols
- Two -OH groups on benzene ring
- **Ortho-dihydric (Catechol):** 1,2-positions
- **Meta-dihydric (Resorcinol):** 1,3-positions
- **Para-dihydric (Hydroquinone/Quinol):** 1,4-positions

### Trihydric Phenols
- Three -OH groups
- Example: Pyrogallol (1,2,3-trihydroxybenzene)

## 1.3 Classification of Ethers
**Importance: ⭐ JEE MAINS | ⭐⭐ JEE MAINS + ADVANCED**

### Simple (Symmetrical) Ethers
- Both alkyl groups identical
- Examples: Diethyl ether (C₂H₅-O-C₂H₅), Dimethyl ether

### Mixed (Asymmetrical) Ethers
- Different alkyl groups
- Examples: Ethyl methyl ether (C₂H₅-O-CH₃)

### Cyclic Ethers
- Oxygen incorporated in ring
- Examples: Dioxane, Tetrahydrofuran (THF), Epoxide

# 2. PHYSICAL PROPERTIES

## 2.1 Physical Properties of Alcohols
**Importance: ⭐⭐ JEE MAINS | ⭐⭐ JEE MAINS + ADVANCED**
**Question Year Tags: 2024, 2023, 2022 (Boiling Point Trends)**

### Boiling Point Order
| Compound | M.W. | B.P. (°C) | Reason |
|----------|------|----------|---------|
| Methanol | 32 | 64.7 | H-bonding |
| Ethanol | 46 | 78.4 | H-bonding |
| n-Propanol | 60 | 97.2 | H-bonding |
| Isopropanol | 60 | 82.4 | Less H-bonding (2°) |
| Diethyl ether | 74 | 34.6 | No H-bonding |

### Key Points
- **Boiling point:** Higher than corresponding alkanes/ethers due to H-bonding
- **1° > 2° > 3° alcohols** (among isomers): More branching → fewer H-bonds
- **Solubility in water:** All alcohols soluble (miscible with water)
  - Reason: H-bonding between O-H and water
  - Solubility decreases with increasing alkyl chain length
- **Density:** Generally less than water
- **Miscibility:** Alcohols dissolve in both polar and non-polar solvents

## 2.2 Physical Properties of Phenols
**Importance: ⭐⭐ JEE MAINS | ⭐⭐⭐ JEE MAINS + ADVANCED**
**Question Year Tags: 2023, 2022, 2021 (Water Solubility, Isomer Properties)**

### Boiling Point and Density
| Phenol Type | B.P. (°C) | Solubility in Water |
|-------------|----------|-------------------|
| Phenol | 181.8 | Soluble (~8.3 g/100 mL at 20°C) |
| o-Cresol | 190.8 | Soluble |
| m-Cresol | 202.2 | Slightly soluble |
| p-Cresol | 201.9 | Soluble |
| Resorcinol | 281 | Highly soluble |
| Hydroquinone | 287 | Slightly soluble |

### Key Points
- **Boiling point:** Higher than alcohols (aromatic stabilization)
- **Water solubility:** Phenol and its derivatives form H-bonds with water
- **Acidic character:** Phenols show weak acidic properties
  - pKa of phenol ≈ 9.95
  - Dihydric phenols: Variable pKa values
- **Color:** Phenol (colorless at room temp but can darken on exposure to air)
- **Crystalline nature:** Many phenols are crystalline solids

## 2.3 Physical Properties of Ethers
**Importance: ⭐ JEE MAINS | ⭐⭐ JEE MAINS + ADVANCED**
**Question Year Tags: 2024, 2023, 2022 (Polarity, Inertness)**

### Key Physical Properties

| Property | Characteristics |
|----------|-----------------|
| **Boiling Point** | Lower than alcohols of similar MW (no H-bonding) |
| **Solubility in Water** | Low for simple ethers; slightly soluble |
| **Polarity** | Polar molecule (C-O-C bond is polar) |
| **Density** | Generally less than 1 |
| **Refractive Index** | Higher than hydrocarbons |

### Specific Examples
- **Diethyl ether:** B.P. 34.6°C, highly volatile, flammable
- **Dioxane:** B.P. 101°C, miscible with water
- **Anisole (Methoxybenzene):** B.P. 154°C, aromatic ether

# 3. ALCOHOLS: CHEMICAL PROPERTIES AND REACTIONS

## 3.1 Oxidation Reactions
**Importance: ⭐⭐⭐ JEE MAINS | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ JEE MAINS + ADVANCED**
**Question Year Tags: 2024, 2023, 2022, 2021, 2020**

### Oxidation of Primary Alcohols
**General Mechanism:**
```
R-CH₂-OH → R-CHO → R-COOH
(Aldehyde) (Carboxylic Acid)
```

**Using K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄ (Acidic Medium):**
- **Mild conditions:** First oxidation stops at aldehyde
- **Complete oxidation:** Continues to carboxylic acid
- Yellow dichromate → Green Cr³⁺ (color change is KEY)

**Reaction Conditions:**
- Primary alcohol → Aldehyde: Use PCC (Pyridinium Chlorochromate) or DMP
- Primary alcohol → Carboxylic acid: Use excess K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄ or KMnO₄

**Using Potassium Permanganate (KMnO₄):**
- Acidic/neutral medium: Oxidation to RCOOH
- Alkaline medium: RCOO⁻ salt formation
- Purple KMnO₄ → Colorless/Brown MnO₂ (observation)

**Using Swern Oxidation:**
- **Reagent:** (COCl)₂, DMSO, -78°C, then base
- **Product:** Aldehyde (no over-oxidation to carboxylic acid)
- **Advantage:** Selective, mild conditions
- **Mechanism:** Forms activated intermediate

**Jones Oxidation:**
- **Reagent:** CrO₃/H₂SO₄ in acetone
- **Product:** Aldehyde or ketone (primary or secondary alcohols)
- **Speed:** Fast reaction at room temperature

### Oxidation of Secondary Alcohols
**General Mechanism:**
```
R₂CH-OH → R₂C=O
(Ketone - final product, no further oxidation)
```

**Key Features:**
- All oxidizing agents (K₂Cr₂O₇, KMnO₄, PCC, Jones) → Ketone
- Ketone is NOT oxidized further (stable under normal conditions)
- Used to distinguish primary from secondary alcohols

**Reaction Conditions:**
- K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄: Yellow → Green, complete oxidation to ketone
- KMnO₄: Decolorizes, product is ketone

### Oxidation of Tertiary Alcohols
**Key Point:** NO OXIDATION under normal mild conditions
- No hydrogen on carbon bearing -OH group
- C-OH bond is broken under extreme conditions (C-C cleavage with rearrangement)
- Used to distinguish tertiary alcohols from primary and secondary

### Catalytic Oxidation
- **Copper catalyst with O₂ at high temperature:** Primary alcohols → Aldehydes
- **Silver catalyst:** Primary alcohols → Aldehydes
- **Industrial application:** Methanol → Formaldehyde (Cu catalyst)

## 3.2 Dehydration Reactions
**Importance: ⭐⭐ JEE MAINS | ⭐⭐⭐ JEE MAINS + ADVANCED**
**Question Year Tags: 2024, 2023, 2022, 2021**

### Mechanism: E1 Elimination (Unimolecular)
**General Reaction:**
```
R₂CH-OH → R₂C=CH₂ + H₂O
```

**Reaction Conditions:**
- **Heat:** 140-180°C
- **Catalyst:** H₂SO₄ (conc.) or H₃PO₄ (conc.)

**Detailed Mechanism (3-step):**
1. **Protonation of -OH group:** Alcohol + H⁺ → Alkoxonium ion (R₂CH-OH₂⁺)
2. **Carbocation formation:** Loss of water → Carbocation (R₂C⁺)
3. **Elimination:** Loss of H⁺ from β-carbon → Alkene + H₂O

**Key Observations:**
- **Reactivity Order:** 3° > 2° > 1° alcohols
  - Tertiary: Most stable carbocation → Fastest dehydration
  - Primary: Least stable carbocation → Slowest dehydration
  - 1° alcohols require more stringent conditions

### Zaitsev's Rule (Hofmann's Opposite)
- **Major product:** More substituted alkene (more stable)
- **Minor product:** Less substituted alkene (less stable)
- **Exception in E2:** Under basic conditions (Hofmann rule): Less substituted alkene is major

**Example:**
```
        CH₃
        |
C₆H₅-CH-CH₂-OH  →  C₆H₅-CH=CH₂ + C₆H₅-CH₂-CH=CH₂
(1-Phenylethanol)    (Major, Zaitsev)    (Minor)
```

### Intramolecular Dehydration (Dehydration with rearrangement)

**Conditions for carbocation rearrangement:**
- Primary alcohols can form secondary/tertiary carbocations via **hydride shift** or **methyl shift**
- **Neopentyl alcohol (Me₃C-CH₂OH):** Rearranges to isoprene (2-methylbut-2-ene)

**Example (Pinacol Rearrangement-like mechanism):**
```
(CH₃)₃C-CH₂-OH  →  (CH₃)₃C-CH=CH₂
Rearranges to      via 2° carbocation
more stable alkene
```

## 3.3 Esterification Reaction
**Importance: ⭐⭐ JEE MAINS | ⭐⭐⭐ JEE MAINS + ADVANCED**
**Question Year Tags: 2024, 2023, 2022, 2021, 2020**

### General Reaction
```
R-OH + R'-COOH ⇌ R'-COO-R + H₂O
(Alcohol) (Carboxylic Acid) (Ester)
```

**Reaction Conditions:**
- **Catalyst:** H₂SO₄ (conc.) or H₃PO₄
- **Heat:** Usually reflux or slight heating
- **Equilibrium:** Reversible reaction

**Mechanism (Fischer Esterification):**
1. Protonation of carboxylic acid
2. Nucleophilic attack by alcohol on carbonyl carbon
3. Tetrahedral intermediate formation
4. Loss of water
5. Deprotonation to form ester

**Key Points:**
- **Reversibility:** Can be driven to products by removing water (Dean-Stark apparatus)
- **Selectivity:** Primary alcohols > Secondary > Tertiary (hindered)
- **Acyl halide esterification:** R-Cl + R-OH → R-COO-R' (faster, irreversible)

### Ester Hydrolysis
```
R'-COO-R + H₂O → R'-COOH + R-OH
```

**Acid-catalyzed hydrolysis:** Reverse of esterification, equilibrium
**Base-catalyzed hydrolysis (Saponification):** Irreversible, using NaOH

## 3.4 Nucleophilic Substitution Reactions
**Importance: ⭐⭐ JEE MAINS | ⭐⭐⭐ JEE MAINS + ADVANCED**
**Question Year Tags: 2024, 2023, 2022**

### Reaction with Hydrogen Halides
```
R-OH + HX → R-X + H₂O
(X = Cl, Br, I)
```

**Mechanism:** E1/SN1 (depending on conditions)
- **Reaction order:** 3° > 2° > 1° (carbocation stability)
- **Product:** Alkyl halide

**Specific Reactions:**
- **With HCl (conc.):** Lucas test (tertiary alcohols turn cloudy fastest)
- **With HBr:** Bromination, can involve carbocation rearrangement
- **With HI:** Iodination (less common, HI is reducing)

### Reaction with Phosphorus Halides
**With PBr₃:**
```
3 R-OH + PBr₃ → 3 R-Br + H₃PO₃
```
- **Mechanism:** SN2 (inversion of stereochemistry if stereogenic center)
- **Advantage:** No carbocation rearrangement
- **Conditions:** Room temperature

**With SOCl₂:**
```
R-OH + SOCl₂ → R-Cl + SO₂ + HCl
```
- **Mechanism:** SN2 with inversion
- **Advantage:** Clean reaction, gaseous byproducts escape
- **Conditions:** Room temperature or mild heat

```

# 4. PHENOLS: CHEMICAL PROPERTIES AND REACTIONS

## 4.1 Acidic Nature of Phenols
**Importance: ⭐⭐⭐ JEE MAINS | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ JEE MAINS + ADVANCED**
**Question Year Tags: 2024, 2023, 2022, 2021, 2020**

### pKa Values and Relative Acidity

| Compound | pKa | Relative Acidity |
|----------|-----|------------------|
| Water | 15.7 | — |
| Phenol | 9.95 | 10¹¹× stronger than water |
| Cresol (o-, m-, p-) | ~10.3 | ~10¹⁰× stronger than water |
| Resorcinol | ~9.3 | Stronger than phenol |
| Hydroquinone | ~9.85 | Similar to phenol |
| Carboxylic acid | ~4.8 | Much stronger than phenol |
| Alcohols | ~15-17 | Much weaker than phenol |

### Reason for Acidity
**Stabilization of Phenoxide Anion (C₆H₅-O⁻):**
- Resonance stabilization through benzene ring
- Charge distributed over ortho-, meta-, and para-positions
- Delocalization through π-electrons of benzene

**Resonance Structures:**
```
      O⁻              O⁻              O⁻
     /              /              /
    ◯    ←→        ◯    ←→        ◯
     \              \              \
(original)    (ortho-para)    (meta-para)
```

### Reactions due to Acidity

**1. Reaction with Sodium Hydroxide:**
```
C₆H₅-OH + NaOH → C₆H₅-O⁻Na⁺ + H₂O
(Sodium phenoxide/phenolate salt)
```
- Phenol dissolves in NaOH solution (unlike alcohols)
- Forms salt that is water-soluble
- **Test:** Phenol + NaOH → Colorless solution

**2. Reaction with Sodium Carbonate:**
```
2 C₆H₅-OH + Na₂CO₃ → 2 C₆H₅-O⁻Na⁺ + H₂CO₃↑
```
- CO₂ gas evolution (bubbling)
- Weaker base than NaOH, but phenol is acidic enough
- Cresols and other weakly acidic phenols may not react easily

**3. Reaction with Weak Bases:**
- **Ammonia (NH₃):** No reaction (too weak)
- **Sodium bicarbonate:** No reaction (too weak)

## 4.2 Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution Reactions
**Importance: ⭐⭐⭐ JEE MAINS | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ JEE MAINS + ADVANCED**
**Question Year Tags: 2024, 2023, 2022, 2021, 2020**

### Key Feature: Activated Ring
- **-OH group is activating and ortho-/para-directing**
- Resonance donation through lone pair makes benzene ring more electron-rich
- **Electron-donating group → Faster substitution than benzene**

### 4.2.1 Halogenation (Cl₂, Br₂)

**With Bromine Water (Br₂/H₂O):**
```
      OH              OH              OH              OH
      |               |               |               |
   ◯  + 3 Br₂  →   ◯  + 3 HBr
   |              Br  Br
                  |  |

(Phenol)           (2,4,6-tribromophenol - White ppt)
```

**Key Observations:**
- **No catalyst needed** (unlike benzene halogenation)
- **Polyhalogenation occurs:** All available ortho- and para-positions are substituted
- **Product:** 2,4,6-tribromophenol (white precipitate)
- **Rapidity:** Extremely fast (even at room temperature)
- **Compare:** Benzene + Br₂ needs FeBr₃ catalyst and gives monosubstituted product

**With Chlorine (Cl₂):**
- **Vapor state:** Substitution occurs
- **Aqueous Cl₂:** 2,4,6-trichlorophenol (white ppt)
- **In presence of NaOH:** Forms chlorophenoxides (useful for selective substitution)

### 4.2.2 Nitration

**With Dilute HNO₃:**
```
      OH              OH
      |               |
   ◯  + HNO₃  →   ◯  + H₂O
   |              NO₂
      
(Phenol)    (2,4,dinitrophenol + 4-nitrophenol)
```

**Key Points:**
- Mixture of ortho- and para-products (NO₂ group is electron-withdrawing, activates benzene ring)
- **Extremely exothermic:** Requires dilute HNO₃ and cooling
- **Danger:** Runaway reaction possible
- **Products:** 2,4-Dinitrophenol (major, due to steric effects) and 4-nitrophenol

**With Concentrated HNO₃:**
```
      OH              NO₂
      |               |
   ◯  + HNO₃(conc) →    ◯  + H₂O
   |                  |
                     NO₂
                     |
                    NO₂
(Phenol)      (2,4,6-trinitrophenol/Picric acid - Yellow crystals)
```

### 4.2.3 Sulfonation

**With Concentrated H₂SO₄:**
```
      OH              OH
      |               |
   ◯  + H₂SO₄(conc) →    ◯  + H₂O
   |                  |
                     SO₃H
                     
(Phenol)        (p-Hydroxybenzenesulfonic acid/p-phenolsulfonic acid)
```

**At higher temperature (160-170°C):** Ortho-product predominates
**At lower temperature:** Para-product is favored (kinetic control)

### 4.2.4 Friedel-Crafts Alkylation

**General Reaction:**
```
      OH              OH
      |               |
   ◯  + R-X  FeBr₃  ◯  + HX
   |            →     |
                     R
       
(Phenol)     (Alkylphenol)
```

**Example:**
```
Phenol + CH₃Cl → 2-methylphenol (o-cresol) + 4-methylphenol (p-cresol)
```

**Important Note:** Direct Friedel-Crafts acylation with R-CO-Cl is NOT successful because:
- Phenolic -OH is highly activated and acts as nucleophile instead of ring
- OH group accepts acyl chloride's proton → Phenoxide anion
- Acylium ion attacks phenoxide → O-acylation (not C-acylation)

**Alternative:** Use Lewis acid-protected phenol or other methods

### 4.2.5 Friedel-Crafts Acylation (Modified)

**Unsuccessful direct route:**
```
Phenol + R-CO-Cl / AlCl₃ → O-acylated product (not C-acylated)
```

**Successful routes:**
1. **Fries Rearrangement:** 
   - First O-acylate: Phenol + R-CO-Cl → Phenyl ester
   - Heat with AlCl₃: Rearrange to C-acylated product

2. **Use protected phenol:**
   - Protect -OH as methyl ether (anisole)
   - Perform Friedel-Crafts acylation
   - Deprotect -OH group

## 4.3 Condensation Reactions
**Importance: ⭐⭐ JEE MAINS | ⭐⭐⭐ JEE MAINS + ADVANCED**
**Question Year Tags: 2023, 2022, 2021**

### 4.3.1 Phenol-Formaldehyde Condensation (Bakelite Formation)

**Reaction:**
```
C₆H₅-OH + CH₂=O → (Polymerization) → Bakelite (Phenol-Formaldehyde Resin)
```

**Mechanism (Acid-catalyzed):**
1. Formaldehyde attacked by phenolic ring (electrophilic aromatic substitution)
2. Hydroxymethyl phenol formed: C₆H₅-OH + CH₂=O → C₆H₄(OH)-CH₂OH
3. Condensation between phenols via -CH₂- bridges
4. Cross-linked 3D polymer network forms

**Reaction Conditions:**
- **Catalyst:** HCl or H₂SO₄ (acidic medium)
- **Temperature:** 100-120°C
- **Molar ratio:** Usually phenol:formaldehyde = 1:1 to 1:1.5

**Products:**
- **Linear stage (Novolac):** Fusible, soluble (deficient formaldehyde)
- **Cross-linked stage (Resole):** Infusible, insoluble (excess formaldehyde)

**Uses of Bakelite:**
- Electrical insulators
- Molded products
- Adhesives
- Coatings

### 4.3.2 Phenol-Aldehyde Condensation (General)

**With acetaldehyde:**
```
C₆H₅-OH + CH₃CHO → Various condensation products

# 5. ETHERS: CHEMICAL PROPERTIES AND REACTIONS

## 5.1 General Characteristics of Ethers
**Importance: ⭐ JEE MAINS | ⭐⭐ JEE MAINS + ADVANCED**

### Chemical Inertness
- **C-O-C bond is strong** (similar to C-C bond)
- **No reaction with:**
  - Dilute H₂SO₄ (at normal temperature)
  - NaOH solution
  - Na metal (unlike alcohols)
  - Oxidizing agents (mild)
  - Reducing agents
  
- **Reason:** Ether lacks active functional group
  - No -OH group (like alcohols) → No H-bonding to make acidic
  - No carbonyl group (like aldehydes/ketones) → No electrophilic center
  - Oxygen lone pair poorly accessible (due to C-O-C overlap)

### Limited Reactivity with Nucleophiles
- Very stable under basic conditions
- Can be protonated under strongly acidic conditions

---

## 5.2 Cleavage Reactions (Main reactions of ethers)
**Importance: ⭐⭐ JEE MAINS | ⭐⭐⭐ JEE MAINS + ADVANCED**
**Question Year Tags: 2024, 2023, 2022, 2021**

### 5.2.1 Cleavage with HI (Hydriodic Acid)

**General Reaction:**
```
R-O-R' + HI → R-I + R'-OH
(or R-OH + R'-I, depending on structure)
```

**Detailed Mechanism (SN2 with protonated ether):**
1. **Protonation:** R-O-R' + H⁺ → [R-O⁺-R'] (oxonium ion)
2. **Nucleophilic attack:** I⁻ attacks carbon bearing most H atoms
3. **Formation:** R-I + R'-OH (or vice versa)

**Reactivity with different ether types:**
- **Methyl ethers:** Break preferentially at C-O bond adjacent to CH₃
  - CH₃-O-R + HI → CH₃I + R-OH
  
- **Primary alkyl ethers:** Break at primary carbon
  - R-CH₂-O-R' + HI → R-CH₂I + R'-OH

- **Asymmetrical ethers:** Break at less substituted (more accessible) carbon
  - C₂H₅-O-C(CH₃)₃ + HI → C₂H₅-OH + (CH₃)₃C-I

**Excess HI:**
- If alcohol formed can react further
- R-OH + HI → R-I + H₂O
- **Example:** Diethyl ether + excess HI → 2 C₂H₅I + H₂O

### 5.2.2 Cleavage with HBr

**General Reaction:**
```
R-O-R' + HBr → R-Br + R'-OH
```

**Similarities and Differences with HI:**
- Similar mechanism to HI
- Less vigorous than HI reaction
- Reactivity: HI > HBr > HCl (due to nucleophilicity of halide ions)
- HCl is generally ineffective (Cl⁻ is poor nucleophile)

### 5.2.3 Cleavage with Concentrated H₂SO₄ (High Temperature)

**General Reaction:**
```
R-O-R' + H₂SO₄(conc) → (heat) → R-OSO₃H + R'-H or R-H + R'-OSO₃H
(Further decomposition)
```

**Mechanism:**
1. Protonation of ether oxygen
2. SN1-type cleavage to form carbocation
3. Hydration/elimination to form alkene or alcohol

**Products:**
- Alkenes (if carbocation can be formed and eliminated)
- Alcohols (if hydration occurs)
- Sulfuric acid esters (intermediate)

**Example:**
```
C₂H₅-O-C₂H₅ + H₂SO₄(conc, heat) → C₂H₅-OH + C₂H₄
(Diethyl ether)
```

### 5.2.4 Cleavage with PBr₃

**General Reaction:**
```
R-O-R' + PBr₃ → R-Br + R'-Br + H₃PO₃
```

## 5.3 Oxidation of Ethers
**Importance: ⭐ JEE MAINS | ⭐⭐ JEE MAINS + ADVANCED**

### Oxidation in Air/Atmospheric O₂
**Very important for storage and safety:**
- Ethers form peroxides when exposed to air and light
- **Reaction:** 2 R-O-R' + O₂ (light) → Peroxides (R-O-O-R')
- **Danger:** Peroxides are explosive when concentrated or heated
- **Prevention:** Store ethers in dark bottles with anti-oxidant inhibitors

### Oxidation with KMnO₄
- Ethers are generally resistant to mild oxidation
- Strong oxidation breaks C-O bonds
- **Products:** Carboxylic acids, ketones, aldehydes (depending on structure)

---

## 5.4 Reactions of Cyclic Ethers
**Importance: ⭐⭐ JEE MAINS | ⭐⭐⭐ JEE MAINS + ADVANCED**
**Question Year Tags: 2023, 2022, 2021**

### Ring Opening with HX

**Epoxide (Oxirane) - 3-membered ring:**
```
      O
     / \
    /   \
   R     R' + HBr → R-CHBr-CHR'-OH
                    (trans-addition, inverted stereochemistry)
```

**Mechanism:**
1. Ring protonation (O gets protonated first due to basicity)
2. SN2-type ring opening
3. Br⁻ attacks less hindered carbon
4. Inversion of configuration
5. OH adds to other carbon

**Dioxane (6-membered ring):**
```
    O
   / \
  /   \
 O     + HI → Ring opens with difficulty
  \   /
   \ /
    
(1,4-Dioxane)
```
- More stable than epoxides
- Requires more severe conditions to open
- Less prone to cleavage

### Polymerization of Oxirane
- Anionic polymerization: Produces polyethers
- Cationic polymerization possible under acidic conditions

## 5.5 Crown Ethers and Cryptands
**Importance: ⭐ JEE MAINS | ⭐ JEE MAINS + ADVANCED**

### Properties and Uses
- Cyclic polyethers that can complex metal cations
- **Crown ether example:** 18-Crown-6 [1,4,7,10,13,16-hexaoxacyclooctadecane]
- Used in phase-transfer catalysis
- Selective for specific cations based on ring size
- Not typically tested in detail in JEE, but good to know

# 7. IMPORTANT MECHANISMS

## 7.1 Oxidation Mechanism: Chromic Acid (K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄)

### Mechanism for Primary Alcohol → Aldehyde

**Step 1: Chromate ester formation**
```
R-CH₂-OH + H₂CrO₄ → R-CH₂-O-CrO₃H + H₂O
(Chromate ester intermediate)
```

**Step 2: Hydride abstraction (key step)**
```
R-CH₂-O-CrO₃H → R-CHO + HCrO₂ + H⁺
(Aldehyde formed)
```

**Mechanism Type:** Concerted process (C-H bond breaks as O-Cr bond breaks)

### Mechanism for Primary Alcohol → Carboxylic Acid
```
Aldehyde + Chromic acid → (further oxidation) → Carboxylic acid
```

### Mechanism for Secondary Alcohol → Ketone
```
Similar to primary alcohol mechanism
R₂CH-OH + H₂CrO₄ → R₂C=O (Ketone)
```

---

## 7.2 Dehydration Mechanism: E1 Elimination

### Three-Step Mechanism for R₂CH-OH → R₂C=CH₂

**Step 1: Protonation (Fast equilibrium)**
```
R₂CH-OH + H⁺ ⇌ [R₂CH-OH₂]⁺
(Oxonium ion - more electrophilic)
```

**Step 2: Carbocation formation (Slow, rate-determining)**
```
[R₂CH-OH₂]⁺ → R₂C⁺ + H₂O
(Carbocation intermediate)
```

**Step 3: Deprotonation (Fast)**
```
R₂C⁺ + B⁻ → R₂C=CH₂ + HB
(Base removes β-hydrogen)
```

### Why E1 (not SN1)?
- **High temperature favors elimination** over substitution
- **Alcohol is a poor leaving group** (even when protonated)
- **Carbocation intermediate** readily loses β-H

---

## 7.3 Fischer Esterification Mechanism

**Four-Step Mechanism: R-OH + R'-COOH → R'-COO-R + H₂O**

**Step 1: Carboxylic acid activation**
```
R'-COOH + H⁺ ⇌ R'-C(OH)₂⁺
(Protonated carboxylic acid)
```

**Step 2: Nucleophilic attack**
```
R'-C(OH)₂⁺ + R-OH → R'-C(OH)(OR) + H⁺
(Tetrahedral intermediate)
```

**Step 3: Protonation of hydroxyl**
```
R'-C(OH)(OR) + H⁺ ⇌ R'-C(OH₂)⁺(OR)
```

**Step 4: Water elimination**
```
R'-C(OH₂)⁺(OR) → R'-CO-OR + H₂O + H⁺
(Ester formed, catalyst regenerated)
```

---

## 7.4 Ether Cleavage with HI: SN2 Mechanism

**Three-Step Mechanism: R-O-R' + HI → R-I + R'-OH**

**Step 1: Ether protonation**
```
R-O-R' + H⁺ → [R-O⁺(H)-R']
(Oxonium ion - good leaving group)
```

**Step 2: Nucleophilic attack (SN2)**
```
I⁻ + [R-O⁺(H)-R'] → R-I + R'-OH + H⁺
(Inversion at carbon being attacked, if stereogenic)
```

**Important:** Which carbon is attacked?
- **Primary ether:** Cleavage at primary C-O bond
- **Methyl ether:** Cleavage at C-O(CH₃) bond (CH₃I major product)
- **Asymmetrical:** Cleavage at less hindered carbon

---

## 7.5 Halogenation of Phenol: Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution

**Mechanism for Br₂/H₂O addition to Phenol (No catalyst needed!)**

**Step 1: Phenol reacts directly with Br₂**
```
Phenol (electron-rich aromatic) + Br₂ → [Brominium intermediate]
```

**Step 2: π electrons of benzene ring attack Br⁺**
```
      OH              OH
      |               |
    ◯ + Br⁺ →    ◯  (σ-complex/Wheland intermediate)
      |              |
                    Br
```

**Step 3: Deprotonation to restore aromaticity**
```
      OH              OH
      |               |
    ◯  → ◯  + H⁺
      |              |
      Br            Br
(Bromophenol)
```

**Step 4: Further brominations at ortho- and para-positions**
- Repeat mechanism at ortho and para carbons
- **Final product:** 2,4,6-tribromophenol (after 3 equiv. Br₂)

**Why no catalyst?**
- Phenolic -OH is strongly electron-donating
- Makes benzene ring extremely electron-rich
- Br₂ is electrophilic enough without FeBr₃ catalyst

---

## 7.6 Fries Rearrangement (Phenyl ester → Acylphenol)

**Two-Step Process:**

**Step 1: O-Acylation of Phenol**
```
C₆H₅-OH + R-CO-Cl / AlCl₃ → C₆H₅-O-CO-R
(Phenyl ester - O-acylated, not C-acylated)
```

**Step 2: Fries Rearrangement (Heat with AlCl₃)**
```
C₆H₅-O-CO-R / AlCl₃, heat  → HO-C₆H₄-CO-R + Heat
                               (o- and p-hydroxyaryl ketones)
```

**Mechanism of Fries Rearrangement:**
- AlCl₃ acts as Lewis acid
- Cleavage of O-CO bond with carbocation formation (RCO⁺)
- Intramolecular aromatic acylation via Friedel-Crafts
- Carbocation attacks benzene ring at ortho- or para-position

# 9. IMPORTANT POINTS FOR JEE EXAM

## Quick Revision Checklist

### Alcohols
- ✓ Order of reactivity: 1° < 2° < 3° (for SN1-based reactions)
- ✓ Order of reactivity: 1° > 2° > 3° (for oxidation with ease)
- ✓ Primary alcohols → Aldehydes (PCC) → Carboxylic acids (excess K₂Cr₂O₇)
- ✓ Secondary alcohols → Ketones (all oxidizing agents)
- ✓ Tertiary alcohols → No oxidation (mild conditions)
- ✓ Dehydration: E1 mechanism, Zaitsev's rule for major product
- ✓ Esterification: Fischer method, equilibrium reaction
- ✓ With Na: H₂ gas evolution (vigorous)

### Phenols
- ✓ Acidic nature: pKa ~10 (stronger than alcohols, weaker than carboxylic acids)
- ✓ Dissolve in NaOH (unlike alcohols)
- ✓ Spontaneous halogenation with Br₂/H₂O (no catalyst, polyhalogenation)
- ✓ Electrophilic aromatic substitution (ortho/para directing)
- ✓ Oxidation → Quinones (1,4-benzoquinone for phenol)
- ✓ Phenol + formaldehyde → Bakelite (Novolac or Resole)
- ✓ Fries rearrangement for C-acylation

### Ethers
- ✓ Chemically inert (no reaction with NaOH, Na, mild oxidizing agents)
- ✓ Main reaction: Cleavage with HI/HBr (SN2 mechanism)
- ✓ Forms peroxides with O₂ (storage hazard)
- ✓ Epoxides undergo ring opening with HX
- ✓ Crown ethers form complexes with metal cations
- ✓ Very good solvents for polar compounds
