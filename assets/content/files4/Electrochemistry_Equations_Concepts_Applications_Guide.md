## SECTION 1: CELL POTENTIAL & EMF (ELECTROMOTIVE FORCE)

### Table 1.1: Cell Potential Calculations (Standard Conditions: 25°C, 1 M)

| Equation/Concept | Mathematical Form | Variables & Units | Physical Meaning | Conditions | Rearrangements | PYQ Tags | JEE Level |
|------------------|-------------------|-------------------|------------------|-----------|-----------------|----------|-----------|
| **Standard Cell EMF** | E°_cell = E°_cathode − E°_anode | E° in volts (V); reduction potentials from tables | Overall driving force for spontaneous reaction; >0 means spontaneous | Standard: 25°C, 1 M, 1 atm | E°_cell = E°_reduction(cathode) − E°_oxidation(anode) | 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐ |
| **Standard Reduction Potential** | E°_reduction = reference vs. SHE (Standard Hydrogen Electrode) | E° in V; SHE defined as 0.00 V at 25°C; half-reactions | Ability of species to be reduced; more +ve = stronger oxidizing agent | 25°C, 1 M, 1 atm for all species | E°_oxidation = −E°_reduction (for reverse half-reaction) | 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐ |
| **Gibbs Free Energy Relation** | ΔG° = −nFE°_cell | ΔG° (J/mol), n = moles e⁻, F = 96,500 C/mol, E° in V | Thermodynamic feasibility; negative ΔG° ↔ positive E° ↔ spontaneous reaction | Standard state; 25°C | E°_cell = −ΔG°/nF; ΔG° = −nFE° | 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐⭐ |
| **Equilibrium Constant** | log K = (nE°_cell) / 0.0592 (at 25°C) | K = equilibrium constant; n = electrons transferred; E° in V | Connection between thermodynamic favorability and equilibrium; K > 1 if E° > 0 | 25°C; natural log to base 10 conversion | K = 10^(nE°_cell/0.0592); ln K = nFE°/RT | 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐⭐ |
| **Numerical Example: Zn-Cu Cell** | E°_cell = 0.34 V (Cu²⁺/Cu) − (−0.76 V) (Zn²⁺/Zn) = 1.10 V | ΔG° = −2 × 96,500 × 1.10 = −212,300 J/mol | Spontaneous; ΔG° < 0; reaction proceeds forward | Standard conditions: [Zn²⁺] = [Cu²⁺] = 1 M | K = 10^(2 × 1.10/0.0592) = 10^37.16 ≈ 1.45 × 10³⁷ | 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024 | ⭐⭐ |

## SECTION 2: FARADAY'S LAWS OF ELECTROLYSIS

### Table 2.1: First & Second Law of Faraday

| Law | Mathematical Form | Variables & Units | Physical Meaning | Proof/Derivation | Numerical Example | PYQ Tags | JEE Level |
|-----|-------------------|-------------------|------------------|------------------|-------------------|----------|-----------|
| **Faraday's 1st Law** | m = (M × Q) / (nF) or m = (M × I × t) / (nF) | m = mass (g); M = molar mass (g/mol); Q = charge (Coulombs); I = current (A); t = time (s); n = electrons per formula unit; F = 96,500 C/mol | Mass of substance deposited ∝ charge passed through electrolyte | Q = I × t; at electrode: I = nF × dn/dt → m = M × Q / (nF) | Electroplate Cu: m = (64 × 10,000) / (2 × 96,500) = 3.32 g for 10,000 C, 2e⁻ | 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐ |
| **Faraday's 2nd Law** | m₁/m₂ = (M₁/M₂) × (n₂/n₁) | m₁, m₂ = masses of two substances; M₁, M₂ = molar masses; n₁, n₂ = electrons transferred per formula unit | Same charge through different electrolytes deposits masses in ratio of (M/n) | Same Q deposited different species → m ∝ (M/n) for each | If 3.2 g Cu (M=64, 2e⁻) deposited, then Ag (M=108, 1e⁻) deposits (108/64)×(2/1)×3.2 = 10.8 g | 2021, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐ |
| **Faraday Constant** | F = e × N_A = 1.602×10⁻¹⁹ C × 6.022×10²³ mol⁻¹ = 96,500 C/mol | F exactly 96,485 C/mol (modern value); represents charge of 1 mole electrons | Converts mole-scale chemistry to macroscopic current/charge | Coulomb charge × Avogadro's number; one fundamental constant | 1 mole electrons = 96,500 Coulombs = 1 Faraday (F) | 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐ |
| **Electrochemical Equivalent** | Z = M / (nF) | Z in g/Coulomb or g/Ampere-second; unique for each substance | Mass deposited per Coulomb passed; intrinsic property | m = Z × Q; Z = M/(nF) from Faraday's 1st Law | Cu: Z_Cu = 64/(2×96,500) = 0.000331 g/C; Ag: Z_Ag = 108/(1×96,500) = 0.00112 g/C | 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025 | ⭐⭐ |
