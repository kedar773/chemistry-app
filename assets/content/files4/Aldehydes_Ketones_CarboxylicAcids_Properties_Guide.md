## SECTION 1: CLASSIFICATION & STRUCTURAL CHARACTERISTICS

### 1.1 Aldehydes - Classification by Type

| Aldehyde Class | General Formula | Structure | Key Characteristic | Common Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Aliphatic (Saturated)** | R-CHO | C=O at terminal carbon | No C=C or C≡C | Ethanal, Propanal, Butanal |
| **Aliphatic (Unsaturated)** | R-CH=CH-CHO | Contains C=C + C=O | α,β-Unsaturated | Acrolein, Cinnamaldehyde |
| **Aromatic** | Ar-CHO | -CHO directly on benzene | Benzaldehyde type | Benzaldehyde, o-Tolualdehyde |
| **Dialdehyde** | OHC-R-CHO | Two -CHO groups | Dicarbonyl compound | Malondialdehyde, Glyoxal |
| **Formaldehyde** | H-CHO | HCHO, no alkyl groups | Special case (no α-H) | HCHO (Methanal) |

**Year Tags:** 2024, 2023, 2022  
**JEE Level:** ⭐ Mains | ⭐⭐ Mains+Advanced

### 1.2 Ketones - Classification by Type

| Ketone Class | General Formula | Structure | Key Characteristic | Common Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Simple Aliphatic** | R₁-CO-R₂ | Alkyl groups on both sides | Most common | Acetone, Butanone, Pentanone |
| **Cyclic** | Cyclic ring with C=O | Ketone in ring | Ketone group in cycle | Cyclopentanone, Cyclohexanone |
| **Aromatic** | Ar-CO-R | Aryl group + alkyl | Ketone with benzene | Acetophenone, Benzophenone |
| **α,β-Unsaturated** | R-CO-CH=CH-R' | Contains C=O + C=C | Conjugated system | Mesityl oxide, Chalcone |
| **Diketone** | R-CO-R'-CO-R'' | Two -CO- groups | Dicarbonyl compound | Diacetyl (Butane-2,3-dione) |
| **Methyl Ketone** | R-CO-CH₃ | CH₃ on one side | Special (haloform reaction) | Acetone, Acetophenone |

**Year Tags:** 2024, 2023, 2022  
**JEE Level:** ⭐ Mains | ⭐⭐ Mains+Advanced

### 1.3 Carboxylic Acids - Classification by Type

| Acid Class | General Formula | Structure | Key Characteristic | Common Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Aliphatic (Saturated)** | R-COOH | Straight/branched chain | Simple carboxylic acids | Acetic acid, Propionic acid |
| **Aliphatic (Unsaturated)** | R-CH=CH-COOH | Contains C=C + COOH | Unsaturated carbon skeleton | Acrylic acid, Oleic acid |

| **Polycarboxylic** | Multiple -COOH | Multiple acidic groups | Citric acid type | Citric acid, Tartar acid |
| **β-Hydroxy Acid (Ester)** | R-O-CH(OH)-COOH | Hydroxyl on β-carbon | Special structure | Malic acid |

## SECTION 3: CHEMICAL PROPERTIES - ACIDITY

### 3.1 Acidity Order & pKa Values

#### 3.1.1 Complete Acidity Scale

| Compound Class | Specific Compound | pKa | Relative Acidity | Acidic Nature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Water** | H₂O | 15.7 | Reference (1) | Neutral |
| **Aldehydes** | R-CHO | ~16 (α-H) | ~0.1 × water | Extremely weak |
| **Ketones** | R₁-CO-R₂ | ~19-20 (α-H) | ~0.01 × water | Extremely weak |
| **Carboxylic Acids (Saturated)** | CH₃-COOH (Acetic) | 4.74 | 10¹¹ × water | Weakly acidic |
| **Carboxylic Acids (Aliphatic)** | R-COOH | 4-5 | 10¹⁰¹¹ × water | Weakly acidic |
| **Aromatic Carboxylic Acids** | C₆H₅-COOH (Benzoic) | 4.20 | Slightly stronger than acetic | Weakly acidic |
| **Formic acid** | H-COOH | 3.77 | **Strongest monocarboxylic** | Weakly acidic |

**COMPLETE ACIDITY ORDER:**
**Carboxylic acids >> Aldehydes/Ketones >> Water >> Alcohols**

**Key Difference:** Carboxylic acids ~10⁷ times more acidic than aldehydes/ketones  
**Reason:** COOH has polar O-H; aldehyde C=O not as acidic  
**Year Tags:** 2024, 2023, 2022, 2021, 2020  
**JEE Level:** ⭐⭐⭐ Mains | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Mains+Advanced

#### 3.1.2 Carboxylic Acid Acidity Trends

| Factor | Effect on pKa | Example | pKa Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Electron-donating alkyl** | Increases pKa (weaker acid) | Propanoic vs Acetic | 4.87 vs 4.74 |
| **Electron-withdrawing (Cl, F)** | Decreases pKa (stronger acid) | Trichloroacetic vs Acetic | 0.65 vs 4.74 |
| **Halogen at α-position** | Significantly decreases pKa | Chloroacetic acid | pKa 2.87 |
| **Multiple EWG** | Additive effect | Trichloroacetic acid | pKa 0.65 (**very acidic**) |
| **Aromatic ring (neutral)** | Slight decrease (electron sink) | Benzoic vs Acetic | 4.20 vs 4.74 |
| **Para-Electron-donating group** | Increases pKa | 4-Methoxybenzoic acid | pKa 4.49 |
| **Para-Electron-withdrawing group** | Decreases pKa | 4-Nitrobenzoic acid | pKa 3.41 |

**Trend:** **Electron-withdrawing groups → Stronger acid (lower pKa)**  
**Trend:** **Electron-donating groups → Weaker acid (higher pKa)**  
**Quantitative:** Each Cl at α-position ≈ 1 pKa unit decrease  
**Year Tags:** 2024, 2023, 2022  
**JEE Level:** ⭐⭐⭐ Mains | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Mains+Advanced

## SECTION 4: CARBONYL REACTIVITY

### 4.1 Nucleophilic Addition Reactivity Order

#### 4.1.1 Aldehyde vs Ketone vs Carboxylic Acid Derivatives

| Compound | C=O Reactivity | Nucleophilic Addition Rate | Reason | Order |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Formaldehyde (HCHO)** | HIGHEST | Fastest | No steric hindrance, high dipole | 1st |
| **Acetaldehyde (CH₃CHO)** | Very high | Very fast | Low steric hindrance | 2nd |
| **Aliphatic aldehydes** | High | Fast | One alkyl group | 3rd |
| **Aromatic aldehydes (Ar-CHO)** | Moderate | Moderate | Aromaticity reduces reactivity | 4th |
| **Ketones (R₁-CO-R₂)** | Low-Moderate | Slow | Two alkyl groups → steric hindrance | 5th |
| **Conjugated carbonyl** | Lower | Slower | Resonance deactivates C=O | 6th |
| **Acid chlorides (R-COCl)** | VERY HIGH | Fastest in acyl substitution | Highly electrophilic | 1st (acyl) |
| **Anhydrides** | Very high | Very fast (acyl sub.) | Resonance deactivates C=O slightly | 2nd (acyl) |
| **Esters (R-CO-OR')** | Moderate | Slow | Resonance from OR' group | 3rd (acyl) |
| **Carboxylic acids** | Low | Very slow | Resonance, COOH deactivates | 4th (acyl) |

**REACTIVITY ORDER (Nucleophilic Addition):**
**Formaldehyde > Aldehydes > Ketones**

**REACTIVITY ORDER (Acyl Substitution):**
**Acid chlorides > Anhydrides > Esters > Carboxylic acids**

**Year Tags:** 2024, 2023, 2022, 2021  
**JEE Level:** ⭐⭐⭐ Mains | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Mains+Advanced

#### 4.1.2 Factors Affecting Carbonyl Reactivity

| Factor | Effect on Reactivity | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Steric hindrance** | More bulky alkyl groups → Less reactive | tert-Bu-CO-tert-Bu << CH₃CHO |
| **Electron-donating groups** | Decrease reactivity (destabilize carbocation) | Alkyl groups reduce reactivity |
| **Electron-withdrawing groups** | Increase reactivity (stabilize carbocation) | Aromatic -CHO (less reactive due to resonance) |
| **Aromatic conjugation** | Generally decreases reactivity for aldehydes | Benzaldehyde < Acetaldehyde |
| **α,β-Unsaturation** | Conjugation can increase or decrease (case-dependent) | α,β-Unsaturated aldehydes |
| **Hydrogen bonding** | Increases reactivity (stabilizes transition state) | Aqueous conditions favor addition |

**Year Tags:** 2023, 2022  
**JEE Level:** ⭐⭐ Mains | ⭐⭐⭐ Mains+Advanced

## SECTION 5: HYDROGEN BONDING & DIMER FORMATION

### 5.1 Carboxylic Acid Hydrogen Bonding & Dimers

| Property | Carboxylic Acids | Aldehydes | Ketones |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H-bonding (Donor)** | YES (O-H) | NO (C=O only) | NO |
| **H-bonding (Acceptor)** | YES (C=O + O-H) | YES (C=O) | YES (C=O) |
| **Dimer formation** | Strong (cyclic dimers) | Weak (via C=O) | Weak (via C=O) |
| **Boiling Point** | Very high (B.P. elevated by dimers) | Lower | Lower |
| **Solubility in water** | Good (H-bonding) | Good (initially) | Good (initially) |
| **Vapor density** | Abnormal (dimers in gas phase) | Normal | Normal |

**Carboxylic Acid Dimers:**
```
   O···H
   ║       ║
R-C        C-R
   O-H   O
```

**Explanation:** Two carboxylic acid molecules form cyclic dimers through hydrogen bonding  
**Effect:** Increases B.P. significantly; affects spectroscopy (IR shows broad O-H stretch)  
**Year Tags:** 2023, 2022, 2021  
**JEE Level:** ⭐⭐ Mains | ⭐⭐⭐ Mains+Advanced
