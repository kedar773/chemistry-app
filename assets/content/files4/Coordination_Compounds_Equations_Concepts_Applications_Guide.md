### Problem 1: Complex Formation Constant & Chelate Effect

**Given:** 
- K_f([Cu(en)]²⁺) ≈ 2 × 10¹⁶ for bidentate ethylenediamine
- K_f([Cu(NH₃)]²⁺) ≈ 2 × 10⁴ for first ammonia
- Compare stability; calculate Δ log K_f

**Solution:**
| Step | Calculation | Result | Explanation |
|------|------------|--------|-------------|
| 1. Calculate log K_f for bidentate | log K_f(en) = log(2 × 10¹⁶) = 16.3 | log K_f(en) = 16.3 | Bidentate chelate forms one step |
| 2. Calculate log K_f for monodentate | log K_f(NH₃) = log(2 × 10⁴) = 4.3 | log K_f(NH₃) = 4.3 | Monodentate forms in first step; overall formation constant for [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺ ≈ 2 × 10¹³ |
| 3. Compare overall stability for 4 donors | Overall K_f([Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺) ≈ 2 × 10¹³ vs K_f([Cu(en)₂]²⁺) ≈ 2 × 10³⁰ | K_f([Cu(en)₂]²⁺) / K_f([Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺) ≈ 10¹⁷ | Bidentate drastically more stable; two en ligands >> four NH₃ |
| 4. Calculate Δ log K_f | Δ log K_f = 16.3 − 4.3 = 12 (per chelate cycle) | Δ log K_f ≈ 12 per bidentate coordination | Entropy-driven: releasing one en ≪ releasing four NH₃ molecules |

**Answer:** Bidentate ligands are ~10¹² times more stable than their monodentate equivalents (chelate effect dominates).

### Problem 2: Crystal Field Stabilization Energy (CFSE) Calculation

**Given:**
- [Fe(H₂O)₆]²⁺ (high-spin d⁶): t₂g⁴ e_g²
- [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ (low-spin d⁶): t₂g⁶ e_g⁰
- Set Δ_o = 1 unit; pairing energy P ≈ 0.5Δ_o

**Calculate CFSE for both:**

| Configuration | Orbital Occupancy | CFSE Calculation | Result | Stability |
|---------------|------------------|-----------------|--------|-----------|
| High-spin d⁶ (HS) | t₂g⁴ e_g²: 4 e⁻ at −0.4Δ_o; 2 e⁻ at +0.6Δ_o | CFSE_HS = 4(−0.4) + 2(+0.6) = −1.6 + 1.2 = −0.4Δ_o | −0.4Δ_o (ref: 1 unit) | Less stable if Δ_o large; stable if Δ_o small (weak field) |
| Low-spin d⁶ (LS) | t₂g⁶ e_g⁰: 6 e⁻ at −0.4Δ_o; + pairing cost 3P | CFSE_LS = 6(−0.4) + 3P = −2.4Δ_o + 3(0.5Δ_o) = −2.4 + 1.5 = −0.9Δ_o | −0.9Δ_o (ref: 1 unit) | More stable when Δ_o > P (strong field) |
| Energy difference LS vs HS | ΔE = CFSE_LS − CFSE_HS = −0.9 − (−0.4) = −0.5Δ_o | LS is 0.5Δ_o lower (more stable) | LS favored for CN⁻ (large Δ_o >> P) |

**Answer:** 
- [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ low-spin is 0.5Δ_o more stable than high-spin (CN⁻ large Δ_o).
- [Fe(H₂O)₆]²⁺ prefers high-spin because H₂O gives small Δ_o << P (pairing cost > crystal field benefit).
