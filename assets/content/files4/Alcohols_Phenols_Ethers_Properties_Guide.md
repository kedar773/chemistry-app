## SECTION 1: CLASSIFICATION & STRUCTURAL CHARACTERISTICS

### 1.1 Alcohols - Classification by Type

| Alcohol Class | General Formula | Structure | Key Characteristic | Common Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary (1°)** | R-CH₂-OH | C-OH on primary carbon | One alkyl group on C-OH carbon | Ethanol, n-Propanol, n-Butanol |
| **Secondary (2°)** | R₂CH-OH | C-OH on secondary carbon | Two alkyl groups on C-OH carbon | Isopropanol, 2-Butanol, Cyclohexanol |
| **Tertiary (3°)** | R₃C-OH | C-OH on tertiary carbon | Three alkyl groups on C-OH carbon | tert-Butanol, tert-Pentanol |
| **Polyhydric** | R(OH)ₙ | Multiple -OH groups | Two or more -OH on different carbons | Ethylene glycol, Glycerol |
| **Unsaturated** | R=C-R-OH | Contains C=C or C≡C | Alkenol or Alkynol | Allyl alcohol, Propargyl alcohol |
| **Aromatic** | Ar-OH | -OH directly on benzene (Phenol) | See Phenols section | Phenol, Cresol |

**Year Tags:** 2024, 2023, 2022  
**JEE Level:** ⭐ Mains | ⭐ Mains+Advanced

### 1.2 Phenols - Classification by Substituents

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| **Dihydric (Diphenol)** | (HO)₂-C₆H₄ | Two -OH on benzene ring | 2 | — | — |
| — **Ortho (1,2-)** | 1,2-(HO)₂C₆H₄ | Adjacent -OH groups | 2 | Catechol/Pyrocatechol | 9.3, 13.0 |

**Note:** Dihydric and trihydric phenols have multiple pKa values (shown in order)  
**Year Tags:** 2024, 2023, 2022, 2021, 2020  
**JEE Level:** ⭐⭐ Mains | ⭐⭐⭐ Mains+Advanced

### 1.3 Ethers - Classification by Structure

| Ether Type | General Formula | Structure | Key Characteristic | Common Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Simple/Symmetrical** | R-O-R | Same alkyl on both sides | Identical R groups | Diethyl ether, Dimethyl ether, Dipropyl ether |
| **Mixed/Asymmetrical** | R-O-R' | Different alkyl groups | Different R and R' | Ethyl methyl ether, Methyl propyl ether |
| **Alicyclic (Cyclic)** | Oxygen in ring | 3+ membered ring | Ring contains O | Tetrahydrofuran (THF), Dioxane, Dioxolane |
| **Aromatic** | Ar-O-R | Aryl ether | O bonded to benzene | Anisole (Methoxybenzene), Phenetole |
| **Aryl-Aryl** | Ar-O-Ar' | Both aryl groups | Diphenyl ether | Diphenyl ether, Naphthyl methyl ether |
| **Epoxide** | C-C(O)-C | 3-membered ring with O | Strained ring | Ethylene oxide, Propylene oxide |
| **Macrocyclic** | Large ring with O atoms | Crown ethers | Large cavity for cations | 18-Crown-6, Dibenzo-18-Crown-6 |

**Year Tags:** 2024, 2023, 2022  
**JEE Level:** ⭐ Mains | ⭐⭐ Mains+Advanced

### 2.1 Boiling Points & Melting Points

#### 2.1.1 Alcohols - Boiling Point Trends

| Compound | M.W. | B.P. (°C) | M.P. (°C) | State (RT) | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Methanol** | 32 | 64.7 | -97.6 | Liquid | H-bonding |
| **Ethanol** | 46 | 78.4 | -114.1 | Liquid | H-bonding |
| **n-Propanol** | 60 | 97.2 | -127 | Liquid | H-bonding |
| **Isopropanol** | 60 | 82.4 | -89.5 | Liquid | Less H-bonding (2°) |
| **n-Butanol** | 74 | 117.7 | -89 | Liquid | H-bonding |
| **tert-Butanol** | 74 | 82.2 | 25.5 | Solid/Liquid | Least H-bonding (3°) |
| **n-Pentanol** | 88 | 138.0 | -79 | Liquid | H-bonding |
| **Cyclohexanol** | 100 | 160.8 | 25.9 | Solid | H-bonding in ring |

**Trend:** B.P. order for isomers: **1° > 2° > 3°** (branching reduces H-bonding)

**Key Observation:** tert-Butanol (B.P. 82.2°C) < n-Butanol (117.7°C) by 35°C!

## SECTION 5: PHENOL-SPECIFIC PROPERTIES

### 5.1 Phenol vs. Phenoxide Ion Characteristics

| Property | Phenol (C₆H₅-OH) | Phenoxide Ion (C₆H₅-O⁻) | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Structure** | -OH on benzene | -O⁻ on benzene | Sp² hybrid O |
| **Resonance** | Weak (O lone pairs) | Strong (charge delocalization) | 3 resonance structures |
| **Charge** | Neutral | Negative (-1) | — |
| **Color** | Colorless | Pale yellow (in solution) | — |
| **Solubility in H₂O** | ~8.3 g/100 mL | Highly soluble (salt) | Greatly increased |
| **pKa** | 9.95 | — | — |

**Key Resonance Stabilization:** Charge on O⁻ is distributed to ortho-, meta-, and para-carbons

**Year Tags:** 2024, 2023, 2022  
**JEE Level:** ⭐⭐⭐ Mains | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Mains+Advanced

### 5.2 Effect of Substituents on Phenol Acidity

| Substituent | Position | Effect on pKa | Acidic Strength | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Electron-donating (alkyl)** | Any | Increases pKa | Weaker acid | o/m/p-Cresol (pKa 10.3) |
| **Electron-withdrawing (NO₂)** | Ortho | Decreases pKa | Stronger acid | 2-Nitrophenol (pKa 7.23) |
| **Electron-withdrawing (NO₂)** | Meta | Decreases pKa | Stronger acid | 3-Nitrophenol (pKa 8.39) |
| **Electron-withdrawing (NO₂)** | Para | Decreases pKa | Stronger acid | 4-Nitrophenol (pKa 7.15) |

| **Methoxy (-OCH₃)** | Any | Increases pKa | Weaker acid | 4-Methoxyphenol (pKa 10.26) |

**Trend:** 
- **EWG (ortho, meta, para) << Phenol << EDG (ortho, meta, para)**
- **Polysubstituted:** Most strongly EWG phenols are extremely acidic
- **Picric acid (2,4,6-Trinitrophenol):** pKa 0.25 (comparable to strong acids!)

**Year Tags:** 2024, 2023, 2022  
**JEE Level:** ⭐⭐⭐ Mains | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Mains+Advanced

## SECTION 6: ETHER-SPECIFIC PROPERTIES

### 6.1 Ether Inertness - Chemical Stability

| Reagent | Reaction with Ethers | Notes | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dilute H₂SO₄** | No reaction (RT) | Stable solvent | C-O-C bond strong |
| **Dilute NaOH** | No reaction | Stable solvent | Nucleophile cannot attack |
| **Dilute HCl** | No reaction (RT) | Stable | No reactive site |
| **Na metal** | No reaction | Inert to reducing conditions | Ether not cleaved |
| **Reducing agents** | Stable | Good solvent for reductions | Very inert |
| **Mild oxidizing agents** | Stable (mostly) | Good solvent for oxidations | Resistant to oxidation |
| **KMnO₄(dilute)** | No reaction | Stable under mild conditions | — |
| **Halogens (Br₂, Cl₂)** | No reaction (RT) | Inert | No reactive site |

**Conclusion:** Ethers are among the **most chemically inert functional groups**

**Use:** Ideal solvents for many organic reactions

**Year Tags:** 2024, 2023, 2022, 2021

### 6.2 Ether Cleavage Reactivity - Selectivity

#### 6.2.1 Ether Cleavage with HI - Selectivity

| Ether Type | Structure | HI Cleavage Product | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Symmetric simple** | R-O-R (same R) | R-I + R-OH | SN2 at either C |
| **Asymmetric simple** | R-O-R' (different) | Cleaves at less hindered C | SN2 at less hindered |
| **Methyl ether** | CH₃-O-R | CH₃-I (major) + R-OH | Methyl group always cleaved first |
| **Primary ether** | 1°-O-R' | 1°-I + R'-OH | Cleaves at primary C |
| **Secondary ether** | 2°-O-R' | Mixture (2°-I or R'-I) | SN1 partially (carbocation) |
| **Tertiary ether** | t-Bu-O-R' | t-Bu-I (major) | SN1 (carbocation path) |
| **Aryl ether** | C₆H₅-O-R | R-I (only) + C₆H₅-OH | Aryl C-O NOT cleaved |

**Trend:** Cleaves at **less hindered alkyl carbon** (NOT at aromatic carbon)

**Year Tags:** 2024, 2023, 2022, 2021  
**JEE Level:** ⭐⭐ Mains | ⭐⭐⭐ Mains+Advanced

#### 6.2.2 Ether Cleavage Reactivity with HX

| Ether Class | HI Reactivity | HBr Reactivity | HCl Reactivity | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Simple aliphatic** | Very fast | Fast | Slow | HI >> HBr > HCl |
| **Aromatic (anisole)** | Slow | Slower | Very slow | Aryl C-O bond intact |
| **Cyclic (dioxane)** | Requires heat | Requires heat | No reaction | Ring stable |
| **Epoxide** | Fast (ring opening) | Fast | Moderate | 3-membered ring strained |

**Trend:** **HI > HBr >> HCl** (halide nucleophilicity: I⁻ > Br⁻ >> Cl⁻)

**Year Tags:** 2024, 2023, 2022

## SECTION 7: HYDROGEN BONDING CHARACTERISTICS

### 7.1 Hydrogen Bonding Capacity

| Functional Group | H-Bond Donor | H-Bond Acceptor | Intermolecular H-bonds | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Alcohol (1°, 2°)** | YES (O-H) | YES (O lone pairs) | 2 (donor) + 2 (acceptor) | Strong H-bonding network |
| **Alcohol (3°)** | YES (O-H) | YES (O lone pairs) | 1 (donor) + 2 (acceptor) | Weaker network (less donor) |
| **Phenol** | YES (O-H) | YES (O lone pairs) | Aromatic ring affects | Moderate-strong H-bonding |
| **Ether** | NO (no O-H) | YES (O lone pairs) | 0 (donor) + 2 (acceptor) | Acts only as H-bond acceptor |
| **Water** | YES | YES | 2 + 2 | Strong H-bonding reference |

**H-Bonding Strength (in compounds):**
- **Alcohols > Phenols > Ethers** (in terms of intermolecular H-bonding)
- **Reason:** Alcohols form continuous H-bonding networks

**Year Tags:** 2024, 2023, 2022
