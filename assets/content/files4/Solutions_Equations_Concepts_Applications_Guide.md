### Table 0.1: Solution Definitions & Classifications

| Concept | Definition | Components | Characteristics | Typical Examples | Macroscopic Properties | PYQ Tags | JEE Level |

| **Concentrated Solution** | Solution with large amount of solute; mole fraction χ_solute significant (often χ > 0.1) | Solute: substantial proportion; Solvent: present but lower proportion relative to solute | Deviations from ideal behavior; activity coefficients needed; Raoult's law may not hold | Concentrated HCl (12 M), concentrated H₂SO₄ (18 M), saturated salt solution | Significant vapor pressure lowering; large boiling point elevation; non-linear colligative property changes | 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐ |

| **Supersaturated Solution** | Solution containing more solute than saturation point; metastable state | Solute: concentration > saturation; no solid phase initially present (unstable equilibrium) | Unstable; can crystallize rapidly if disturbed; requires careful preparation (cooling, slow evaporation) | Crystallization of sodium acetate from supersaturated solution; sugar syrup (boiled and cooled) | Metastable; crystallization releases latent heat; once seed crystal added, rapid precipitation occurs | 2023, 2024, 2025 | ⭐ |

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| **Mole Fraction** | χ | Moles of solute / total moles in solution | χ_solute = n_solute / (n_solute + n_solvent); Σχ = 1 | Dimensionless (fraction 0–1) | Vapor pressure calculations (Raoult's law); thermodynamic activities; ideal solutions | χ_solute + χ_solvent = 1; for binary solution, mole fraction defined clearly | 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐⭐ |
| **Mass Percent (w/w)** | wt% or % | Mass of solute / total mass × 100% | wt% = (m_solute / (m_solute + m_solvent)) × 100 | % (dimensionless) | Commercial products (vinegar 5%, bleach 6%); practical/industrial | 10% solution = 10 g solute per 100 g solution | 2021, 2022, 2024, 2025 | ⭐ |

## SECTION 2: THERMODYNAMICS OF SOLUTION & DISSOLUTION

### Table 2.1: Dissolution Process & Thermodynamic Functions

| Thermodynamic Quantity | Symbol | Definition | Sign Convention | Dissolution Type | Physical Meaning | Calculation | PYQ Tags | JEE Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Enthalpy of Solution** | ΔH_sol (or ΔH_dissolution) | Heat absorbed/released when solute dissolves in solvent; H_solution − H_components | ΔH_sol < 0: exothermic (heat released); ΔH_sol > 0: endothermic (heat absorbed) | Exothermic: salts (many), HCl, H₂SO₄; Endothermic: NH₄Cl, KNO₃, urea, most organics | If ΔH_sol < 0, dissolution releases heat (solution heats up); if ΔH_sol > 0, dissolution cools (solution freezes in extreme cases) | ΔH_sol = ΔH_hydration + ΔH_vaporization − ΔH_lattice (or analogous for solvent interactions) | 2021, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐ |
| **Entropy of Solution** | ΔS_sol | Change in entropy when solute dissolves; disorder increases | ΔS_sol > 0 for dissolution (almost always): solute particles dispersed throughout solvent; increase in disorder | Positive in vast majority of cases (dissolution increases entropy); rarely negative (e.g., some structurally complex molecules in water) | Dissolution increases disorder; solute goes from organized solid → dispersed in solvent; solution more random than pure components | ΔS_sol = ΔS_hydration + ΔS_mixing (hydration entropy increases; mixing entropy positive) | 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐ |
| **Gibbs Free Energy of Solution** | ΔG_sol | Thermodynamic driving force for dissolution; ΔG_sol = ΔH_sol − TΔS_sol | ΔG_sol < 0: dissolution spontaneous; ΔG_sol > 0: dissolution non-spontaneous (solute insoluble); ΔG_sol = 0: equilibrium (saturated) | Exothermic endothermically soluble: low T favors (ΔH dominates); Endothermic: high T favors (TΔS dominates) | Determines solubility: ΔG_sol < 0 → solute dissolves; ΔG_sol > 0 → solute precipitates; ΔG_sol = 0 → saturation point | ΔG_sol = −RT ln(K_sp) or ΔG_sol = ΔH_sol − TΔS_sol; connects to equilibrium & solubility | 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐⭐ |
| **Heat of Solution** | q_sol | Enthalpy of solution measured calorimetrically | Same as ΔH_sol (enthalpy change); q_sol = n_solute × ΔH_sol (heat released per mole) | Exothermic: q_sol < 0 (heat released); Endothermic: q_sol > 0 (heat absorbed) | Temperature change of solution: ΔT = q / (C_p × m_total); if exothermic, solution heats; if endothermic, cools | q_sol = n × ΔH_sol; can measure in calorimeter; related to ΔH_sol by n moles | 2022, 2023, 2024 | ⭐⭐ |

### Table 2.2: Factors Affecting Solubility

| Factor | Effect on Solubility | Mechanism | Temperature Dependence | Example | Quantitative Relation | PYQ Tags | JEE Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Temperature (T)** | Usually increases (but exceptions for some gases, salts) | Higher T → more kinetic energy → more effective collisions; entropy-driven dissolution more favorable at high T | For most solids: S(T₂) > S(T₁) if T₂ > T₁ (van 't Hoff equation applies); for gases: S decreases with T (exothermic dissolution) | NaCl: solubility increases ~0.1 M per 10°C rise (endothermic); CO₂: solubility decreases with T (exothermic) | van 't Hoff: ln(S₂/S₁) = −(ΔH_sol/R)(1/T₂ − 1/T₁) for solids; opposite for gases | 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐ |
| **Pressure (P)** | Minimal effect for solids/liquids (incompressible); significant for gases (Henry's law) | High P compresses gas → increases concentration → more dissolved; Le Chatelier applied to gas ↔ aq equilibrium | For gases: S_gas ∝ P_gas (Henry's law); for solids: pressure negligible (incompressible) | O₂, CO₂ in water: doubling pressure → doubles solubility; salt solubility nearly P-independent | Henry's law: [Gas] = K_H × P_gas; K_H decreases with T (gas solubility ↓ at high T) | 2021, 2023, 2024, 2025 | ⭐⭐ |
| **Nature of Solute & Solvent** | "Like dissolves like": polar solutes dissolve in polar solvents; nonpolar in nonpolar | Electrostatic/hydrogen bonding compatibility; ion-dipole forces for ionic; H-bonding for polar; dispersion for nonpolar | Depends on specific interactions; some salts: ΔH_sol > 0 (endothermic) → increases with T; some gases: ΔH_sol < 0 (exothermic) → decreases with T | NaCl in water (polar, ionic): highly soluble; NaCl in nonpolar CCl₄: insoluble; Br₂ in CCl₄: very soluble | Solubility product K_sp for salts; Henry's constant K_H for gases; distribution coefficient for partitioning | 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐ |
| **Common Ion Effect** | Decreases solubility of slightly soluble salt (e.g., CaCO₃) in presence of same ion | Addition of common ion shifts equilibrium left (Le Chatelier); reduces dissociation | Temperature-independent effect (kinetic vs. thermodynamic); occurs at any T where solute present | CaCO₃(s) ⇌ Ca²⁺ + CO₃²⁻; adding Na₂CO₃ (common CO₃²⁻) shifts left → CaCO₃ precipitates | K_sp unchanged; [ion_common] increases → [other_ion] decreases; solubility × [common] ≈ constant | 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐ |
| **pH (for salts of weak acids/bases)** | Affects solubility of salts like CaF₂, Ca(OH)₂, salts of weak acids | pH changes [H⁺], [OH⁻] → shifts acid-base equilibrium → affects anion/cation concentration → affects K_sp expression | Depends on pK_a/pK_b of conjugate acid/base | CaF₂: low pH → HF formation → [F⁻] decreases → more Ca²⁺ dissolves (solubility increases); Ca(OH)₂: low pH → H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O, decreases [OH⁻] → solubility increases | [Ca²⁺] × [F⁻]² = K_sp; [F⁻] ∝ K_a([HF]/[H⁺]); complex pH dependence | 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐⭐ |

## SECTION 3: RAOULT'S LAW & VAPOR PRESSURE

### Table 3.1: Raoult's Law & Ideal Solutions

| Concept | Equation | Conditions for Validity | Solute/Solvent Role | Prediction | Verification | PYQ Tags | JEE Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Raoult's Law (Solvent)** | P_solvent = χ_solvent × P°_solvent or P_solvent = (1 − χ_solute) × P°_solvent | Ideal solution; very dilute or chemically similar components (benzene + toluene); T constant | Solvent: vapor pressure reduced by presence of any solute (involatile or volatile); non-ideal if H-bonding breaks | VP of pure water 23.76 mmHg at 25°C; in 10% NaCl solution, P_water ≈ 23.0 mmHg (reduced by ~3%, proportional to χ_NaCl) | Measured VP vs. calculated from Raoult's; ideal: linear; real: deviations (positive if repulsive, negative if attractive) | 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐ |
| **Vapor Pressure Lowering** | ΔP = P°_solvent − P_solvent = χ_solute × P°_solvent (for dilute, χ_solute << 1) | Ideal dilute solution; involatile solute (does not vaporize); only solvent VP changes | Solute: non-volatile (e.g., salt, sugar); reduces solvent escaping into gas phase | 1 M NaCl: χ_NaCl ≈ 0.018 (dilute); ΔP = 0.018 × 23.76 = 0.43 mmHg (small but measurable); VP lowering ∝ concentration | ΔP_measured / χ_solute ≈ P°_solvent for ideal solutions | 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐ |
| **Relative VP Lowering** | (P°_solvent − P_solvent) / P°_solvent = χ_solute | Dilute solution; ideal behavior | Fractional decrease in VP; dimensionless | For 10% NaCl: ΔP/P° ≈ 0.018 (1.8% reduction); for more dilute, proportionally smaller | Experimental: measure VP of pure solvent vs. solution; ratio = χ_solute (if ideal) | 2021, 2023, 2024, 2025 | ⭐⭐ |
| **Non-Ideal Solutions (Positive/Negative Deviations)** | P_total = P_A + P_B (Dalton) if ideal; real solutions: P_measured ≠ P_ideal | Real solutions; strong intermolecular forces between unlike molecules (negative deviation) or repulsive (positive deviation) | Negative deviation: attractive solute-solvent (lower P than expected, e.g., H₂O + HCl); Positive: repulsive (higher P, e.g., CS₂ + acetone) | HCl + water: strong H-bonding → P_total < (χ_HCl × P°_HCl + χ_water × P°_water) minimum; azeotrope possible | Measured P vs. Raoult prediction; if below: negative; if above: positive; extreme deviations → azeotropes (constant boiling composition) | 2023, 2024, 2025 | ⭐⭐⭐ |

## SECTION 4: COLLIGATIVE PROPERTIES (PART 1)

### Table 4.1: Freezing Point Depression (FPD)

| Property | Definition | Equation | Constant (K_f) | Physical Basis | Measurement | Example | PYQ Tags | JEE Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Freezing Point Depression** | Lowering of freezing point of solvent when solute added (T_f decreases); solvent freezes at lower T | ΔT_f = T_f(pure) − T_f(solution) = K_f × m (or K_f × χ_solute for mole fraction form) | K_f (freezing point depression constant): water K_f = 1.86 K·kg/mol; benzene K_f = 5.12; camphor K_f = 37.8 | Solute particles disrupt solvent crystalline structure; lattice formation requires energy; solutes lower T at which solid forms | Measure freezing point of pure solvent vs. solution using thermometer; phase diagram shows T_f shift | 1 m NaCl solution (assuming i=2): ΔT_f = 1.86 × 2 × 1 = 3.72 K → T_f ≈ −3.72°C (vs. 0°C for pure water) | 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐⭐ |
| **Cryoscopic Constant** | K_f: empirical constant for each solvent; related to solvent's enthalpy of fusion | K_f = (R × T_f² × M_solvent) / (1000 × ΔH_fus) (where M_solvent in g/mol, ΔH_fus in J/mol) | Larger K_f → larger FPD effect per mole of solute; high K_f solvents useful for determining molar mass (camphor: K_f = 37.8) | Proportional to T_f² and solvent molar mass; inversely to enthalpy of fusion | Determine from literature (constants table) or measure experimentally | Camphor (K_f = 37.8) more sensitive than water (K_f = 1.86) for small molar mass determination | 2023, 2024, 2025 | ⭐⭐ |
| **Van 't Hoff Factor (i)** | Accounts for dissociation/association of solute in solution; relates colligative effect to concentration | ΔT_f = i × K_f × m; for non-dissociating: i = 1; for dissociating: i > 1; for associating: i < 1 | i_NaCl ≈ 1.8−1.9 (not exactly 2 due to ionic interactions); i_CaCl₂ ≈ 2.7; i_glucose = 1 (non-electrolyte) | Fraction of dissociation α: i = 1 + (n−1)α (n = number of ions from one formula unit) | Determine i experimentally from colligative property measurements (FPD, BPE, osmotic pressure) | i = 1.8 for NaCl → ΔT_f = 1.8 × 1.86 × 1 m = 3.35 K (less than theoretical 3.72 K if i=2) | 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 | ⭐⭐⭐ |

2. K_f (water) = 1.86 K·kg/mol; K_b (water) = 0.512 K·kg/mol; K_f (camphor) = 37.8
3. Raoult's law: P_solvent = χ_solvent × P°_solvent; ΔP = χ_solute × P°_solvent
