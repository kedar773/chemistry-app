|-------|---|---|---|---|---|---|----------|-----------|
| **First Ionization Energy (IE₁)** | Energy required to remove 1 e⁻ from neutral atom (M → M⁺ + e⁻); measure of "pull" on valence electron | Increases (H: 1312 kJ/mol → He: 2372 kJ/mol; exceptions at Be→B & Mg→Al due to orbital filling) | Decreases (alkali metals: Li: 520 → Na: 496 → K: 419 → Rb: 403 → Cs: 375 kJ/mol) | Across: increasing protons, same shell → stronger nuclear pull. Down: more shells, more shielding. Down: valence electrons farther from nucleus; weaker pull | F: 3.98 (most electronegative), H: 2.20, C: 2.55, N: 3.04, O: 3.44, Cl: 3.16, Br: 2.96, I: 2.66, Li: 0.98 (least electronegative among metals) | F > O > N > Cl > Br > I (trends across & down); halogens >> alkali metals; noble gases not assigned (rarely form bonds) | 2026, 2025, 2024, 2023, 2022, 2021 | ⭐⭐⭐ |
| **Metallic Character** | Tendency to lose electrons; form cations; conduct electricity; luster; malleability | Decreases across period (more electronegative, harder to lose e⁻) | Increases down group (larger atoms, more shielding, easier to lose valence e⁻) | Across: increasing proton pull on valence e⁻. ### Problem 1: Rank Elements by Periodic Trend

**Given:** Compare ionization energies: N, O, P

**Solution:**

| Element | Configuration | Valence Electrons | Reason for IE Value | IE₁ (kJ/mol) | Ranking |
|---------|---|---|---|---|---|
| N | [He]2s²2p³ | 5 | Half-filled 2p³ very stable (maximizes exchange energy); hard to remove any electron | 1402 | **Highest** |
| O | [He]2s²2p⁴ | 6 | One electron in 2p⁴ paired; less stable than half-filled N; easier to remove one electron | 1314 | **Middle** |
| P | [Ne]3s²3p³ | 5 | Half-filled 3p³ (like N half-filled 2p³); but P in Period 3 (larger, more shielding) → easier to ionize | 1012 | **Lowest** |

**Answer:** N > O > P (ranking by IE₁). The anomaly N > O occurs because half-filled 2p³ is more stable than 2p⁴ configuration.

### Problem 2: Isoelectronic Series Radius Ranking

**Given:** Rank by size: O²⁻, F⁻, Ne, Na⁺

**Solution:**

| Ion/Atom | Atomic # (Z) | # Electrons (e⁻) | Proton-Electron Ratio (Z/e⁻) | Reasoning | Ionic Radius (pm) |
|---------|---|---|---|---|---|
| O²⁻ | 8 | 10 | 0.8 | Lowest Z (fewest protons); same e⁻ as others → largest (electrons least pulled in) | 140 |
| F⁻ | 9 | 10 | 0.9 | Z = 9 (more protons than O²⁻) → pulls electrons more → smaller than O²⁻ | 133 |
| Ne | 10 | 10 | 1.0 | Z = 10; neutral atom; electrons most strongly attracted | 62 |
| Na⁺ | 11 | 10 | 1.1 | Z = 11 (most protons); +1 charge removes shielding → smallest (electrons most tightly pulled) | 102 |

**Answer:** O²⁻ (largest, ~140 pm) > F⁻ (~133 pm) > Na⁺ (~102 pm) > Ne (~62 pm, very small as neutral atom). The isoelectronic trend: same # electrons; size ∝ 1/Z (approximately).
