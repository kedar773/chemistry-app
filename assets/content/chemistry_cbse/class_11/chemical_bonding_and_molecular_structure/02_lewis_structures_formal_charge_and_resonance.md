## 1. Pedagogical Theory & Fundamental Concepts

### 1.1 Kössel–Lewis Approach to Chemical Bonding ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
* **Historical Context & Foundation (1916):** Albrecht Kössel and Gilbert N. Lewis independently provided the first logical electronic explanation of chemical bond formation based on the inertness of noble gases.
* **Lewis Postulate:** Lewis envisioned the atom in terms of a positively charged nucleus plus inner-shell electrons (together designated as the **"Kernel"**) surrounded by an outer shell that could accommodate a maximum of eight electrons located at the corners of a cube surrounding the kernel.
* **The Octet Rule:** Atoms transfer (lose/gain) or share valence electrons to achieve a stable outer-shell configuration of eight electrons ($ns^2 np^6$), isoelectronic with the nearest noble gas (with the exception of helium, which achieves a stable **duplet** $1s^2$).
* **Significance of Lewis Dot Symbols:** In the representation of an element, the chemical symbol denotes the atomic kernel, while surrounding dots represent valence shell electrons.
  - Group valence is equal to the number of dots, or $(8 - \text{number of dots})$.

```
Group 1 (Li)   :  Li ·           (Valence = 1)
Group 2 (Be)   :  · Be ·         (Valence = 2)
Group 13 (B)   :  · B · (with 3 dots around) (Valence = 3)
Group 14 (C)   :  : C :          (Valence = 4)
Group 15 (N)   :  : N · · ·      (Valence = 8 - 5 = 3)
Group 16 (O)   :  : O : · ·      (Valence = 8 - 6 = 2)
Group 17 (F)   :  : F : : ·      (Valence = 8 - 7 = 1)
Group 18 (Ne)  :  : Ne : : :     (Valence = 8 - 8 = 0)
```

***

### 1.2 Systematic Construction of Lewis Structures ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
To construct unambiguous Lewis representations for polyatomic molecules and ions:

1. **Calculate Total Valence Electrons ($Q$ or $n_{\text{total}}$):**
   

$$
Q = \sum (\text{Valence electrons of neutral constituent atoms}) - q_{\text{cation}} + q_{\text{anion}}
$$

   * For cations, subtract one electron per unit positive charge.
   * For anions, add one electron per unit negative charge.

2. **Select the Central Atom:**
   * The central atom is usually the element with the **lowest electronegativity** (excluding Hydrogen and Fluorine, which occupy terminal positions exclusively).
   * It is generally the atom with the highest covalency capacity or the least numerous atom in the formula.

3. **Form Single Bonds (Skeletal Framework):**
   * Connect all peripheral atoms to the central atom via a single two-electron covalent bond ($\sigma$-pair).

4. **Distribute Remaining Electrons:**
   * Calculate remaining non-bonded valence electrons:
     

$$
n_{\text{remaining}} = Q - 2 \times (\text{number of single bonds})
$$

   * Distribute these as lone pairs to **terminal (outer) electronegative atoms first** to complete their octet (duplet for $\text{H}$).
   * Any surplus electrons after satisfying terminal octets are assigned as lone pairs to the **central atom**.

5. **Form Multiple Bonds (if Central Atom Lacks Octet):**
   * If the central atom remains electron-deficient (octet not reached), convert one or more non-bonding electron pairs from adjacent terminal atoms into shared bonding pairs (forming double or triple bonds) until an octet is attained.

***

### 1.3 Formal Charge ($FC$) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
* **Definition:** Formal charge is the hypothetical charge assigned to an individual atom within a molecule or polyatomic ion, assuming all bonding electron pairs are shared equally between the bonded atoms, irrespective of electronegativity differences.
* **Thermodynamic Significance:** Formal charges do not represent real physical ionic charges (which are dictated by dipole moments and partial charges $\delta^{\pm}$). They serve as a bookkeeping tool to evaluate the lowest-energy electrostatic distribution among competing skeletal structures.
* **Criterion of Preferred Stability:**
  1. Smaller absolute formal charges ($0, \pm 1$) are favored over larger charges ($\pm 2, \pm 3$).
  2. Negative formal charges must reside on the **more electronegative** atoms.
  3. Positive formal charges must reside on the **less electronegative** (more electropositive) atoms.
  4. Like charges on adjacent atoms represent electrostatic destabilization.

***

### 1.4 Resonance Theory ⭐⭐⭐⭐
* **Definition:** When a single Lewis structure cannot accurately explain all experimentally verified properties (such as bond lengths, dipole moments, and vibrational frequencies) of a molecule, the actual molecule is represented as a **resonance hybrid** of two or more contributing classical structures called **canonical forms** or **contributing structures**.
* **Key Rules of Resonance:**
  1. The relative positions of atomic nuclei **must remain strictly unchanged**; only the distribution of electrons ($\pi$-electrons and lone pairs) changes.
  2. All canonical structures must possess the **same number of paired and unpaired electrons**.
  3. Canonical structures must be nearly coplanar to permit effective lateral $p$-orbital overlap ($\pi$-conjugation).
  4. The actual molecule has lower potential energy and higher thermodynamic stability than any individual canonical structure.

* **Resonance Energy:**
  

$$
\Delta E_{\text{resonance}} = E_{\text{most stable canonical form}} - E_{\text{experimental hybrid}}
$$

### 1.5 Limitations and Failures of the Octet Rule ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Although the octet rule accounts for the vast majority of organic and second-period inorganic compounds, it displays critical shortcomings:

1. **Incomplete Octet of the Central Atom (Electron-Deficient Compounds):**
   * Central atoms possessing fewer than 8 electrons in their valence shell.
   * Common in groups 1, 2, and 13 elements ($\text{LiCl}$, $\text{BeH}_2$, $\text{BeCl}_2$, $\text{BF}_3$, $\text{AlCl}_3$).
2. **Odd-Electron Molecules:**
   * Molecules containing an odd number of valence electrons where it is mathematically impossible for every atom to complete an octet.
   * Examples: Nitric oxide ($\text{NO}$, 11 valence $e^-$), Nitrogen dioxide ($\text{NO}_2$, 17 valence $e^-$), Chlorine dioxide ($\text{ClO}_2$, 19 valence $e^-$). These species are paramagnetic and dimerize to relieve electron deficit ($\text{e.g., } 2\text{NO}_2 \rightleftharpoons \text{N}_2\text{O}_4$).
3. **Expanded Octet (Hypervalent Molecules):**
   * Elements of Period 3 and beyond have accessible $3d$ subshells in addition to $3s$ and $3p$ orbitals.
   * Valence shells can expand to hold 10, 12, or 14 electrons.
   * Examples: $\text{PCl}_5$ (10 valence $e^-$), $\text{SF}_6$ (12 valence $e^-$), $\text{IF}_7$ (14 valence $e^-$), $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ (12 valence $e^-$ around $\text{S}$).
4. **Failure with Noble Gas Compounds:**
   * The octet rule assumes noble gases are entirely inert; however, xenon and krypton form compounds such as $\text{XeF}_2$, $\text{XeF}_4$, $\text{XeO}_3$, and $\text{KrF}_2$.
5. **No Explanation of Molecular Geometry:**
   * The Lewis-Octet model cannot predict bond angles, steric repulsion, or spatial arrangement.
6. **No Explanation of Relative Bond Energies:**
   * It provides no quantitative measure of covalent bond strengths or dissociation enthalpies.

## 2. Derivations, Mathematical Formulations & Reaction Mechanisms

### 2.1 Formal Charge Mathematical Derivation
Consider an atom $A$ bound in a polyatomic network.
* Let $V$ = number of valence electrons of atom $A$ in its isolated, neutral ground state.
* In the bonded state, all non-bonding (lone pair) electrons belong exclusively to atom $A$.
  - Let $L$ = total number of non-bonding (unshared) valence electrons.
* Bonding electrons are assumed to be shared equally between the two bonded atoms.
  - Let $B$ = total number of bonding (shared) electrons involved in single/multiple bonds to atom $A$.
  - Atom $A$'s assigned share of bonding electrons $= \frac{1}{2} B$.

The assigned electronic population ($N_{\text{assigned}}$) to atom $A$ is:

$$
N_{\text{assigned}} = L + \frac{1}{2}B
$$

The net formal charge ($FC$) is the difference between the neutral state valence count and this assigned count:

$$
\boxed{FC = V - L - \frac{1}{2}B}
$$

#### Law of Conservation of Charge in Formal Charge Summation:
For any polyatomic molecule or complex ion:

$$
\boxed{\sum_{i=1}^{k} FC_i = q_{\text{net}}}
$$

Where:
* $k$ = total number of constituent atoms in the chemical entity.
* $q_{\text{net}}$ = net algebraic charge of the molecule or ion ($0$ for neutral molecules, $\pm z$ for ions).

***

### 2.2 Resonance Hybrid Bond Order Formulation
When a molecule undergoes symmetric resonance across $m$ equivalent peripheral bonds, the fractional bond order ($BO$) is calculated as:

$$
\boxed{BO = \frac{\text{Total number of covalent bonds between two specific atoms across all canonical forms}}{\text{Total number of equivalent canonical structures}}}
$$

Alternatively, for delocalized $\pi$-systems across $n$ equivalent skeletal single bonds:

$$
\boxed{BO = 1 + \frac{\text{Number of delocalized }\pi\text{-pairs}}{\text{Number of resonating positions}}}
$$

#### Thermodynamic Verification of Resonance Energy:
For a molecule $M$ (e.g., Benzene or Ozone):

$$
\Delta H_{\text{combustion, theoretical (localized)}} - \Delta H_{\text{combustion, experimental}} = E_{\text{resonance}}
$$

Where $E_{\text{resonance}} > 0\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$, representing an exothermically stabilized state.

***

### 2.3 Mechanisms Involving Octet Limitation Phenomena

#### Reaction Mechanism A: Dimerization of Electron-Deficient Molecules
Gaseous Aluminium Chloride ($\text{AlCl}_3$) is an electron-deficient monomer ($6\ e^-$ around $\text{Al}$). It stabilizes through Lewis acid-base coordinate dative bonding:

$$
\text{Conditions: Gas phase, } T < 400\text{ K; Non-polar solvents}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
2\text{AlCl}_3 &\xrightarrow{} \text{Al}_2\text{Cl}_6 \\
\text{Enthalpy of Dimerization: } \Delta H^\circ &\approx -126\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

```
        Cl             Cl             Cl
          \           /  \           /
           Al  +   :Cl:   Al   --->  Al       Al
          /           \  /           / \     / \
        Cl             Cl          Cl   Cl:Cl   Cl
                                          (Bridge)
```
* Each bridging chlorine donates a lone pair into the empty $3p$ orbital of the adjacent $\text{Al}$ atom, completing the octet for both $\text{Al}$ centers.

#### Reaction Mechanism B: Dimerization of Odd-Electron Species ($\text{NO}_2$)
Nitrogen dioxide ($\text{NO}_2$) contains 17 valence electrons. The unpaired electron is localized in an $sp^2$ hybrid orbital on nitrogen:

$$
\text{Equilibrium: } 2\text{NO}_2(g) \rightleftharpoons \text{N}_2\text{O}_4(g) \quad (\Delta H^\circ = -57.2\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1})
$$

* At low temperatures ($T < 262\text{ K}$), the brown paramagnetic monomer dimerizes into colorless, diamagnetic dinitrogen tetroxide ($\text{N}_2\text{O}_4$), pairing the two odd electrons into a $\text{N}-\text{N}$ single $\sigma$-bond.

## 3. High-Yield Examples & Exemplar Applications

### Example 1: Exhaustive Formal Charge Evaluation of Ozone ($\text{O}_3$)
**Problem:** Draw the Lewis structure of Ozone ($\text{O}_3$), number each oxygen atom, and determine the formal charge on each atom.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. **Total Valence Electrons ($Q$):**
   

$$
Q = 3 \times 6 = 18\text{ electrons}
$$

2. **Skeletal Arrangement:**
   $\text{O}_1$ (Central), $\text{O}_2$ (Terminal, Left), $\text{O}_3$ (Terminal, Right).
3. **Connectivity:**
   

$$
\text{O}_2 - \text{O}_1 - \text{O}_3 \quad (\text{uses } 2 \text{ bonds} = 4\ e^-)
$$

   Remaining: $18 - 4 = 14\ e^-$.
4. **Satisfy Terminal Octets:**
   Distribute 6 electrons (3 lone pairs) to $\text{O}_2$ and 6 electrons (3 lone pairs) to $\text{O}_3$.
   Remaining: $14 - 12 = 2\ e^-$ assigned as 1 lone pair on central $\text{O}_1$.
5. **Octet Completion for Central Atom:**
   $\text{O}_1$ currently has only 4 electrons. Shift one lone pair from $\text{O}_2$ to form an $\text{O}_2=\text{O}_1$ double bond:

$$
\text{:\ddot{O}}_2 = \overset{\normalsize\text{..}}{\text{O}}_1 - \text{:\ddot{O}}_3\text{:}
$$

```
      ..
    : O (1)
     /   \
    /     \
  ::O (2)  :O: (3)
            ..
```

6. **Formal Charge Computation:**

* **Central Oxygen ($\text{O}_1$):**
  - $V = 6$
  - Lone pair electrons: $L = 2$ (one pair)
  - Bonding electrons: $B = 6$ (one double bond + one single bond $= 3 \text{ pairs} = 6\ e^-$)
  

$$
FC(\text{O}_1) = 6 - 2 - \frac{1}{2}(6) = 6 - 2 - 3 = \mathbf{+1}
$$

* **Double-bonded End Oxygen ($\text{O}_2$):**
  - $V = 6$
  - Lone pair electrons: $L = 4$ (two pairs)
  - Bonding electrons: $B = 4$ (two pairs in double bond)
  

$$
FC(\text{O}_2) = 6 - 4 - \frac{1}{2}(4) = 6 - 4 - 2 = \mathbf{0}
$$

* **Single-bonded End Oxygen ($\text{O}_3$):**
  - $V = 6$
  - Lone pair electrons: $L = 6$ (three pairs)
  - Bonding electrons: $B = 2$ (one pair in single bond)
  

$$
FC(\text{O}_3) = 6 - 6 - \frac{1}{2}(2) = 6 - 6 - 1 = \mathbf{-1}
$$

* **Verification of Net Molecular Charge:**
  

$$
\sum FC = FC(\text{O}_1) + FC(\text{O}_2) + FC(\text{O}_3) = (+1) + (0) + (-1) = 0
$$

### Example 2: Carbonate Ion ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$) Resonance & Bond Order
**Problem:** Draw the canonical structures for the carbonate ion ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$), determine the formal charge on every atom in each structure, and calculate the average $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond order and experimental bond characteristics.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. **Total Valence Electrons:**
   

$$
Q = 4 (\text{from C}) + 3 \times 6 (\text{from O}) + 2 (\text{anionic charge}) = 24\text{ electrons}
$$

2. **Canonical Structures:**
   The central Carbon atom forms one double bond with one oxygen and two single bonds with the other two oxygens. The double bond delocalizes across all three oxygen atoms:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Structure I:} &\quad \text{O}=\text{C}(\text{--O}^-)_2 \\
\text{Structure II:} &\quad ^-\text{O}--\text{C}(=\text{O})--\text{O}^- \\
\text{Structure III:} &\quad ^-\text{O}--\text{C}(--\text{O}^-)=\text{O}
\end{aligned}
$$

```
      :O:                     :O:-                   :O:-
      ||                      |                      |
      C                       C                      C
     / \                     / \                    / \
   :O:- :O:-               :O:  :O:-              :O:- :O:
   ..   ..                 ..   ..                ..   ..
   (I)                     (II)                   (III)
```

3. **Formal Charge Breakdown for Structure I:**
   * $FC(\text{C}) = 4 - 0 - \frac{1}{2}(8) = 0$
   * $FC(\text{O}_{\text{double}}) = 6 - 4 - \frac{1}{2}(4) = 0$
   * $FC(\text{O}_{\text{single, left}}) = 6 - 6 - \frac{1}{2}(2) = -1$
   * $FC(\text{O}_{\text{single, right}}) = 6 - 6 - \frac{1}{2}(2) = -1$
   * Net Charge $= 0 + 0 + (-1) + (-1) = -2$.

4. **Bond Order ($BO$) Calculation:**
   Between Carbon and any selected Oxygen atom across the 3 canonical structures, there are $(2 + 1 + 1) = 4$ shared pairs:
   

$$
\boxed{BO = \frac{4\text{ bonds}}{3\text{ canonical forms}} = 1.33}
$$

   * **Experimental Confirmation:** All three $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bonds are identical in length ($128\text{ pm}$), intermediate between a standard $\text{C}-\text{O}$ single bond ($143\text{ pm}$) and a $\text{C}=\text{O}$ double bond ($120\text{ pm}$).
   * **Effective Charge per Oxygen Atom:**
     

$$
\text{Average Charge} = \frac{-2}{3} = -0.67
$$

### Example 3: Thiocyanate Ion ($\text{SCN}^-$) — Selecting the Major Resonating Contributor
**Problem:** Write three possible Lewis structures for the thiocyanate ion ($\text{SCN}^-$) and identify the most stable (major) contributor using formal charge analysis. (Electronegativities: $N = 3.04, C = 2.55, S = 2.58$).

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
Total valence electrons: $Q = 6 (\text{S}) + 4 (\text{C}) + 5 (\text{N}) + 1 = 16\text{ electrons}$.

* **Structure A:**
  

$$
:\ddot{\text{S}}=\text{C}=\ddot{\text{N}}:^-
$$

  * $FC(\text{S}) = 6 - 4 - \frac{1}{2}(4) = 0$
  * $FC(\text{C}) = 4 - 0 - \frac{1}{2}(8) = 0$
  * $FC(\text{N}) = 5 - 4 - \frac{1}{2}(4) = -1$

* **Structure B:**
  

$$
:\text{S}\equiv\text{C}-\ddot{\text{N}}:^{2-}
$$

  * $FC(\text{S}) = 6 - 2 - \frac{1}{2}(6) = +1$
  * $FC(\text{C}) = 4 - 0 - \frac{1}{2}(8) = 0$
  * $FC(\text{N}) = 5 - 6 - \frac{1}{2}(2) = -2$

* **Structure C:**
  

$$
:\ddot{\text{S}}^--\text{C}\equiv\text{N}:
$$

  * $FC(\text{S}) = 6 - 6 - \frac{1}{2}(2) = -1$
  * $FC(\text{C}) = 4 - 0 - \frac{1}{2}(8) = 0$
  * $FC(\text{N}) = 5 - 2 - \frac{1}{2}(6) = 0$

**Comparative Evaluation:**
1. **Structure B** has a high formal charge separation ($+1$ on $\text{S}$, $-2$ on $\text{N}$), making it the least stable contributor.
2. Between **Structure A** and **Structure C**:
   * Both have low magnitudes of formal charges ($0, 0, -1$).
   * In Structure A, the $-1$ formal charge resides on **Nitrogen** (Electronegativity $= 3.04$).
   * In Structure C, the $-1$ formal charge resides on **Sulfur** (Electronegativity $= 2.58$).
   * Since Nitrogen is more electronegative than Sulfur, the negative formal charge is more stably held on Nitrogen.

**Conclusion:** **Structure A ($:\ddot{\text{S}}=\text{C}=\ddot{\text{N}}:^-$) is the major contributor.**

### Example 4: Hypervalence in Sulfuric Acid ($\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$)
**Problem:** Compare the classical Lewis structure of $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ complying strictly with the octet rule versus the expanded octet structure minimized by formal charge.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
Total valence electrons: $Q = 2(1) + 6(\text{S}) + 4(6) = 32\text{ electrons}$.

* **Case 1: Strict Octet Compliance (8 electrons around S):**
  - Central $\text{S}$ forms four single $\sigma$-bonds: two to $-\text{OH}$ groups and two to coordinate/dative oxygen atoms ($-\text{O}^-$).
  - $FC(\text{S}) = 6 - 0 - \frac{1}{2}(8) = \mathbf{+2}$
  - $FC(\text{O}_{\text{terminal}}) = 6 - 6 - \frac{1}{2}(2) = \mathbf{-1}$ (for each of the two terminal oxygens)
  - $FC(\text{O}_{\text{hydroxyl}}) = 6 - 4 - \frac{1}{2}(4) = 0$
  - Large charge separation: $\text{S}$ has $+2$ while two oxygens have $-1$.

* **Case 2: Expanded Octet (12 electrons around S):**
  - Convert one lone pair from each terminal oxygen into a $\text{S}=\text{O}$ double bond.
  - Central $\text{S}$ now has six bonds (two double bonds, two single bonds $= 12$ shared electrons).
  - $FC(\text{S}) = 6 - 0 - \frac{1}{2}(12) = \mathbf{0}$
  - $FC(\text{O}_{\text{terminal}}) = 6 - 4 - \frac{1}{2}(4) = \mathbf{0}$
  - $FC(\text{O}_{\text{hydroxyl}}) = 6 - 4 - \frac{1}{2}(4) = \mathbf{0}$
  - $FC(\text{H}) = 1 - 0 - \frac{1}{2}(2) = \mathbf{0}$

```
Strict Octet (High FC):                  Expanded Octet (Zero FC):
         :O:-                                      :O:
          |                                        ||
    H-O - S(+2) - O-H                        H-O - S - O-H
          |                                        ||
         :O:-                                      :O:
```

**NCERT Perspective:** Period 3 elements utilize vacant $3d$ orbitals to expand their valence shell beyond 8 electrons to minimize formal charge, making the expanded-octet representation the preferred structure.

## 4. Examiner Traps & Common Student Mistakes

| Trap / Pitfall | Typical Error | Correct NCERT Principle |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Bonded Electron Pairing in FC Formula** | Students plug in the number of **bonds** instead of the number of **electrons** for $B$. | $B$ represents the total number of **bonding electrons**. A single bond has $B=2$, a double bond has $B=4$, and a triple bond has $B=6$. If using bonds ($n_b$), use: $FC = V - L - n_b$. |
| **2. Physical Meaning of Formal Charge** | Assuming formal charge is an observable physical quantity measurable in the laboratory. | Formal charge is an **accounting tool**. The actual charge distribution is determined by electronegativity differences, dipole moments, and partial charges ($\delta^\pm$). |
| **3. Movement of Atoms in Resonance** | Moving hydrogen or halogen nuclei while drawing resonance forms (e.g., confusing tautomerism with resonance). | Resonance involves **only the displacement of electrons** ($\pi$ and non-bonding pairs). If an atomic position changes, it is an isomer/tautomer, **not** a resonance contributor. |
| **4. Equilibrium Sign vs Resonance Double Arrow** | Using the bidirectional chemical equilibrium arrow ($\rightleftharpoons$) between canonical forms. | The equilibrium arrow denotes interconverting distinct chemical species. Contributing structures are hypothetical; they must be separated **only** by a single double-headed arrow ($\longleftrightarrow$). |
| **5. Expanded Octet for Second-Row Elements** | Drawing pentacoordinate nitrogen (e.g., $\text{NCl}_5$ or $\text{NF}_5$, or five-bonded $\text{HNO}_3$ with no formal charge on $\text{N}$). | Nitrogen cannot expand its octet because it lacks vacant $d$-orbitals in the $n=2$ principal energy level. Maximum covalency of nitrogen is **strictly 4**. |
| **6. The Odd-Electron Octet Claim** | Writing complete 8-electron octets around both atoms in $\text{NO}$ or $\text{NO}_2$. | Molecules with an odd total number of valence electrons (e.g., $\text{NO}$ with 11, $\text{NO}_2$ with 17) **cannot** satisfy the octet rule for every atom. One atom must retain an unpaired electron. |

## 5. Speed Hacks, Mnemonics & Golden Points

### ⚡ Speed Hack: Rapid Formal Charge Calculation
Instead of writing the full formula every time:

$$
\boxed{FC = \text{Group Number} - (\text{Dots} + \text{Dashes})}
$$

* **Group Number:** IUPAC group valence or standard valence count ($1$ to $8$).
* **Dots:** Count each non-bonding electron individually.
* **Dashes:** Count each bond attached to the atom as $1$ (since each dash represents $\frac{1}{2} \times 2 = 1$ electron).

*Quick Test on $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$:*
* Oxygen is Group 6.
* Dots = 2 (one lone pair).
* Dashes = 3 (three single bonds to $\text{H}$).
* $FC = 6 - (2 + 3) = \mathbf{+1}$. (Takes 2 seconds).

### 🧠 The "OCTET" Mnemonic for Canonical Structure Stability
When deciding which resonance structure contributes most to the hybrid, apply criteria in this sequence:

1. **O — Octet:** Structures where **all** atoms have complete octets dominate overwhelmingly over structures with incomplete octets (even if an electronegative atom carries a positive formal charge, provided it has an octet).
2. **C — Charge Count:** Structures with the minimum number of formal charges (ideally zero) are favored.
3. **T — Topography of Charge:** Negative formal charges must be located on the most electronegative elements; positive charges on the most electropositive elements.
4. **E — Electrostatic Proximity:** Opposite formal charges should be close together; like formal charges should not reside on adjacent atoms.
5. **T — Total Bonds:** The structure with the maximum number of covalent bonds is the most stable.
