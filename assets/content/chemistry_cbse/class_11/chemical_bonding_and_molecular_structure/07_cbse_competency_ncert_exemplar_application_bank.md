### Question A.1: Case-Based / Data-Driven Passage (Atmospheric & Industrial Gases)
**Tag:** [CBSE Competency Pattern, 4 Marks] | **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

#### Case Passage:
In industrial cryogenics and atmospheric research, separation and condensation of atmospheric gases depend fundamentally on intermolecular interactions, molecular polarity, and orbital hybridization. An analytical laboratory measures the dipole moments ($\mu$), boiling points, and bond parameters of three common triatomic species: Carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$), Sulfur dioxide ($\text{SO}_2$), and Water vapor ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$).

The experimental data recorded at $298\text{ K}$ is summarized in the table below:

| Species | Molecular Geometry / Shape | Experimental Dipole Moment ($\mu$ in Debye) | Measured Bond Angle ($\theta$) | Normal Boiling Point ($\text{K}$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\text{CO}_2$ | Linear | $0.00\text{ D}$ | $180^\circ$ | $194.7\text{ K}$ (Sublimes) |
| $\text{SO}_2$ | Bent / Angular | $1.63\text{ D}$ | $119.5^\circ$ | $263.1\text{ K}$ |
| $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ | Bent / Angular | $1.85\text{ D}$ | $104.5^\circ$ | $373.15\text{ K}$ |

#### Questions:
1. Explain on the basis of vector addition of bond dipole moments why $\text{CO}_2$ possesses a zero net dipole moment despite having polar $\text{C}=\text{O}$ bonds ($\Delta \chi \approx 1.0$).
2. Account for the difference in observed bond angles between $\text{SO}_2$ ($119.5^\circ$) and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ ($104.5^\circ$) utilizing Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion (VSEPR) theory.
3. Both $\text{SO}_2$ and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ are polar molecules with bent geometries. Why is the boiling point of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ significantly higher than that of $\text{SO}_2$? Name the specific intermolecular force responsible.
4. Predict the steric number and hybridization state of the central atom in $\text{SO}_2$. Show the counting of $\sigma$-bonds and lone pairs.

***

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Rubric:

**Sub-question 1:**
- **Answer:** Carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) has a linear symmetrical geometry with central $\text{C}$ atom exhibiting $sp$ hybridization ($O=C=O$). The two individual $\text{C}=\text{O}$ bond dipole moments ($\vec{\mu}_1$ and $\vec{\mu}_2$) are equal in magnitude due to identical electronegativity differences between $\text{C}$ and $\text{O}$, but act in exactly opposite directions ($180^\circ$ apart).

$$
\mu_{\text{net}} = \vec{\mu}_1 + \vec{\mu}_2 = \mu_1 + \mu_2 \cos(180^\circ) = \mu_1 - \mu_2 = 0\text{ D}
$$

Hence, the individual bond dipoles cancel each other out completely.
- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Identification of linear geometry and collinear opposite vectors: **[½ Mark]**
  - Formulation of vector cancellation ($\mu_{\text{net}} = 0$): **[½ Mark]**

**Sub-question 2:**
- **Answer:** In $\text{SO}_2$, the central sulfur atom has Steric Number = $2\text{ }\sigma\text{-bonds} + 1\text{ lone pair} = 3$ (based on trigonal planar arrangement). It possesses only **one** lone pair on the sulfur atom. The lone pair-bond pair ($\text{lp}-\text{bp}$) repulsion slightly compresses the ideal $120^\circ$ trigonal planar angle to $119.5^\circ$.
In $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, the central oxygen atom has Steric Number = $2\text{ }\sigma\text{-bonds} + 2\text{ lone pairs} = 4$ (based on tetrahedral arrangement). Oxygen contains **two** lone pairs. According to VSEPR theory, the magnitude of repulsion follows:

$$
\text{lp}-\text{lp} > \text{lp}-\text{bp} > \text{bp}-\text{bp}
$$

The presence of two lone pairs exerting strong $\text{lp}-\text{lp}$ repulsions pushes the bonding pairs much closer, compressing the ideal tetrahedral angle ($109.5^\circ$) down to $104.5^\circ$.
- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Mentioning 1 lone pair in $\text{SO}_2$ vs 2 lone pairs in $\text{H}_2\text{O}$: **[½ Mark]**
  - Stating correct VSEPR repulsive order ($\text{lp}-\text{lp} > \text{lp}-\text{bp} > \text{bp}-\text{bp}$) and relative angle reduction: **[½ Mark]**

**Sub-question 3:**
- **Answer:** Although both molecules possess permanent dipole moments, water molecules ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) are associated through strong **intermolecular hydrogen bonding** ($\text{O}-\text{H}\cdots\text{O}$), driven by the high electronegativity and small covalent radius of oxygen. In contrast, $\text{SO}_2$ molecules are held together only by comparatively weaker dipole-dipole interactions and London dispersion forces. Substantially greater thermal energy is required to break the extensive hydrogen-bonded network in liquid water, resulting in its markedly higher boiling point ($373.15\text{ K}$).
- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Naming Intermolecular Hydrogen Bonding in $\text{H}_2\text{O}$: **[½ Mark]**
  - Comparison with dipole-dipole interactions in $\text{SO}_2$ and energy explanation: **[½ Mark]**

**Sub-question 4:**
- **Answer:** In $\text{SO}_2$, the Lewis structure is represented as $\text{O}=\ddot{\text{S}}=\text{O}$ (with resonance contributors involving single/double bonds).
  - Number of $\sigma$-bonds formed by $\text{S} = 2$
  - Number of localized lone pairs on $\text{S} = 1$
  - $\text{Steric Number (SN)} = 2 + 1 = 3$
  - Since $\text{SN} = 3$, the hybridization of the central Sulfur atom is $sp^2$.
- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Computation of Steric Number ($2\text{ }\sigma + 1\text{ lp} = 3$): **[½ Mark]**
  - Stating $sp^2$ hybridization: **[½ Mark]**

### Question A.2: Assertion-Reason Complex
**Tag:** [CBSE Competency Pattern, 1 Mark] | **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

**Directions:**
In the following question, a statement of Assertion (A) is followed by a statement of Reason (R). Choose the correct option:
(a) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
(b) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).
(c) (A) is true, but (R) is false.
(d) (A) is false, but (R) is true.

- **Assertion (A):** The bond dissociation enthalpy of diatomic Fluorine ($\text{F}_2$, $158.8\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$) is significantly lower than that of diatomic Chlorine ($\text{Cl}_2$, $242.6\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$).
- **Reason (R):** Due to the extremely small size of fluorine atoms, there are strong inter-electronic repulsions between the non-bonding lone pairs present in the compact $2p$ subshells of both fluorine atoms in the $\text{F}_2$ molecule.

***

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Rubric:
- **Correct Option:** **(a)** Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
- **CBSE Explanation & Marking Scheme:**
  - Fluorine has an anomalous low bond dissociation enthalpy compared to chlorine. In $\text{F}_2$, each fluorine atom has three localized non-bonding lone pairs housed in very compact $2p$ orbitals. The internuclear distance is small ($142\text{ pm}$), causing severe inter-electronic repulsions among non-bonding electrons ($\text{lp}-\text{lp}$ repulsions), which weakens the single $\text{F}-\text{F}$ covalent bond.
  - In $\text{Cl}_2$, the valence electrons are in larger $3p$ orbitals; the larger inter-nuclear distance and dispersed electron density drastically reduce lone-pair repulsions, making the $\text{Cl}-\text{Cl}$ bond stronger.
  - Correct option selection: **[½ Mark]**
  - Accurate theoretical justification referencing non-bonding lone-pair repulsions: **[½ Mark]**

### Question A.3: Assertion-Reason Complex
**Tag:** [CBSE Competency Pattern, 1 Mark] | **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ [High-Yield]

- **Assertion (A):** The experimentally determined dipole moment of Nitrogen trifluoride ($\text{NF}_3$, $\mu = 0.23\text{ D}$) is dramatically lower than that of Ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$, $\mu = 1.47\text{ D}$), despite Fluorine being far more electronegative than Nitrogen.
- **Reason (R):** In $\text{NF}_3$, the orbital dipole resultant of the central nitrogen lone pair opposes the resultant dipole moment of the three $\text{N}-\text{F}$ bonds, whereas in $\text{NH}_3$, the lone pair dipole reinforces the resultant of the three $\text{N}-\text{H}$ bonds.

***

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Rubric:
- **Correct Option:** **(a)** Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
- **CBSE Explanation & Marking Scheme:**
  - Both $\text{NH}_3$ and $\text{NF}_3$ possess trigonal pyramidal geometries with one lone pair on the nitrogen atom.
  - In $\text{NH}_3$, Nitrogen is more electronegative than Hydrogen ($\chi_{\text{N}} = 3.04$, $\chi_{\text{H}} = 2.20$). Thus, the bond dipoles of the three $\text{N}-\text{H}$ bonds point inward toward Nitrogen, in the same general direction as the orbital dipole originating from the lone pair. Vector addition results in reinforcement:
  

$$
\vec{\mu}_{\text{net}} = \vec{\mu}_{\text{lone pair}} + \sum \vec{\mu}_{\text{N}-\text{H}} = 1.47\text{ D}
$$

  - In $\text{NF}_3$, Fluorine is more electronegative than Nitrogen ($\chi_{\text{F}} = 3.98$, $\chi_{\text{N}} = 3.04$). The three $\text{N}-\text{F}$ bond dipoles point away from Nitrogen towards Fluorine. The orbital dipole of the lone pair points upward, directly opposing the resultant vector of the three $\text{N}-\text{F}$ bonds:
  

$$
\vec{\mu}_{\text{net}} = \vec{\mu}_{\text{lone pair}} - \left|\sum \vec{\mu}_{\text{N}-\text{F}}\right| = 0.23\text{ D}
$$

  - Correct option selection: **[½ Mark]**
  - Vector direction analysis for both molecules: **[½ Mark]**

### Question A.4: Higher Order Thinking Skills (HOTS) Problem
**Tag:** [CBSE Competency Pattern, 3 Marks] | **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

Phosphorus pentachloride ($\text{PCl}_5$) exists in the gaseous phase as a trigonal bipyramidal molecule, but when cooled to a crystalline solid state, X-ray diffraction reveals that it dissociates completely into an ionic lattice composed of two distinct complex ions: $[\text{PCl}_4]^+$ and $[\text{PCl}_6]^-$.
1. Why are the axial $\text{P}-\text{Cl}$ bonds in gaseous $\text{PCl}_5$ longer ($219\text{ pm}$) and weaker than the equatorial $\text{P}-\text{Cl}$ bonds ($204\text{ pm}$)?
2. Determine the hybridization state and molecular geometry of both the cation $[\text{PCl}_4]^+$ and the anion $[\text{PCl}_6]^-$ in the crystalline state.
3. What is the thermal decomposition behavior of gaseous $\text{PCl}_5$ on gentle heating? Write the balanced chemical reaction.

***

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Rubric:

**Part 1:**
- **Answer:** In a symmetric trigonal bipyramidal geometry ($sp^3d$ hybridization), there are two sets of non-equivalent bonds: three equatorial bonds forming angles of $120^\circ$ with one another, and two axial bonds oriented at $90^\circ$ to the equatorial plane.
  - Each axial $\text{P}-\text{Cl}$ bond pair experiences repulsion from **three** equatorial bond pairs at an angle of $90^\circ$.
  - Each equatorial $\text{P}-\text{Cl}$ bond pair experiences repulsion from only **two** axial bond pairs at $90^\circ$ and two equatorial pairs at $120^\circ$.
  - Because repulsive forces are much stronger at $90^\circ$ than at $120^\circ$, the axial bond pairs suffer greater electron-pair repulsion. To minimize these destabilizing electrostatic repulsions, the axial bonds are pushed farther away from the central nucleus, rendering them longer ($219\text{ pm}$) and weaker than equatorial bonds ($204\text{ pm}$).
- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Quantitative comparison of repulsions at $90^\circ$ (axial: 3 repulsions vs equatorial: 2 repulsions): **[½ Mark]**
  - Concluding that greater repulsion leads to bond lengthening and bond weakening: **[½ Mark]**

**Part 2:**
- **For $[\text{PCl}_4]^+$ (Cation):**
  - Valence electrons of $\text{P} = 5 - 1\text{ (charge)} = 4$.
  - Bond pairs ($\sigma$-bonds with $\text{Cl}$) $= 4$; Lone pairs $= 0$.
  - $\text{Steric Number} = 4 \implies sp^3\text{ hybridization}$.
  - Geometry / Shape: **Regular Tetrahedral** ($T_d$).
- **For $[\text{PCl}_6]^-$ (Anion):**
  - Valence electrons of $\text{P} = 5 + 1\text{ (charge)} = 6$.
  - Bond pairs ($\sigma$-bonds with $\text{Cl}$) $= 6$; Lone pairs $= 0$.
  - $\text{Steric Number} = 6 \implies sp^3d^2\text{ hybridization}$.
  - Geometry / Shape: **Regular Octahedral** ($O_h$).
- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Hybridization and shape of $[\text{PCl}_4]^+$: **[½ Mark]**
  - Hybridization and shape of $[\text{PCl}_6]^-$: **[½ Mark]**

**Part 3:**
- **Answer:** Because the two axial bonds are significantly weaker than the equatorial bonds, gaseous $\text{PCl}_5$ is thermally unstable. On gentle heating, it readily cleaves its two weaker axial bonds, decomposing into phosphorus trichloride and chlorine gas:

$$
\text{PCl}_5(g) \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{PCl}_3(g) + \text{Cl}_2(g)
$$

- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Stating instability due to axial bond cleavability with balanced chemical equation: **[1 Mark]**

### Question A.5: Real-World Context & Multi-Concept Application (Superoxide & Peroxide Diagnostics)
**Tag:** [CBSE Competency Pattern, 3 Marks] | **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ [High-Yield]

In emergency closed-circuit rebreathers and aerospace life-support systems, Potassium superoxide ($\text{KO}_2$) is used to absorb exhaled carbon dioxide and regenerate oxygen gas:

$$
4\text{KO}_2(s) + 2\text{CO}_2(g) \longrightarrow 2\text{K}_2\text{CO}_3(s) + 3\text{O}_2(g)
$$

1. Write the ground-state Molecular Orbital (MO) electronic configuration of the superoxide ion ($\text{O}_2^-$).
2. Calculate the bond order of $\text{O}_2^-$ and compare it with the dioxygen molecule ($\text{O}_2$) and peroxide ion ($\text{O}_2^{2-}$).
3. Determine whether $\text{KO}_2$ is diamagnetic or paramagnetic, citing the specific molecular orbital responsible.

***

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Rubric:

**Part 1:**
- **Answer:** The neutral $\text{O}_2$ molecule has 16 electrons. The superoxide ion ($\text{O}_2^-$) has $16 + 1 = 17$ electrons.
Since $Z = 8$ ($> 7$), the energy sequence follows normal orbital filling (where $\sigma 2p_z$ is lower in energy than $\pi 2p_x$ and $\pi 2p_y$):

$$
\text{O}_2^- \text{ [17 } e^-\text{]}: \sigma 1s^2\ \sigma^*1s^2\ \sigma 2s^2\ \sigma^*2s^2\ \sigma 2p_z^2\ (\pi 2p_x^2 = \pi 2p_y^2)\ (\pi^*2p_x^2 = \pi^*2p_y^1)
$$

Or in condensed core notation:

$$
\text{KK}\ \sigma 2s^2\ \sigma^*2s^2\ \sigma 2p_z^2\ (\pi 2p_x^2 = \pi 2p_y^2)\ (\pi^*2p_x^2 = \pi^*2p_y^1)
$$

- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Correct assignment of 17 electrons with proper orbital ordering: **[1 Mark]**

**Part 2:**
- **Answer:**
  - Number of bonding electrons ($N_b$) in valence shell $= 2 (\sigma 2s) + 2 (\sigma 2p_z) + 4 (\pi 2p) = 8$
  - Number of antibonding electrons ($N_a$) in valence shell $= 2 (\sigma^*2s) + 3 (\pi^*2p) = 5$
  

$$
\text{Bond Order (BO)} = \frac{N_b - N_a}{2} = \frac{8 - 5}{2} = \frac{3}{2} = 1.5
$$

  - Comparison across the series:
    - Dioxygen ($\text{O}_2$, 16 $e^-$): $\text{BO} = \frac{8 - 4}{2} = 2.0$
    - Superoxide ($\text{O}_2^-$, 17 $e^-$): $\text{BO} = \frac{8 - 5}{2} = 1.5$
    - Peroxide ($\text{O}_2^{2-}$, 18 $e^-$): $\text{BO} = \frac{8 - 6}{2} = 1.0$
  

$$
\text{Order of Bond Stability / Bond Order: } \text{O}_2\ (2.0) > \text{O}_2^-\ (1.5) > \text{O}_2^{2-}\ (1.0)
$$

- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Step-by-step calculation of Bond Order of $\text{O}_2^-$ ($1.5$): **[½ Mark]**
  - Relative comparative sequence of bond orders: **[½ Mark]**

**Part 3:**
- **Answer:** The superoxide ion ($\text{O}_2^-$) has one unpaired electron residing in the doubly degenerate antibonding $\pi^*2p_y$ (or $\pi^*2p_x$) molecular orbital. Because of the presence of this single unpaired electron ($n = 1$), $\text{KO}_2$ is **paramagnetic**.
Its spin-only magnetic moment is:

$$
\mu_s = \sqrt{n(n+2)} = \sqrt{1(3)} = \sqrt{3} \approx 1.732\text{ BM}
$$

- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Correctly identifying paramagnetism and designating the singly occupied $\pi^*2p$ orbital: **[1 Mark]**

### Question B.1: NCERT Exercise 4.7
**Tag:** [NCERT Exercise Q4.7, 3 Marks] | **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

Total covalent bonds can be described through resonance hybrid structures when a single Lewis structure fails to account for empirical properties.
1. Write the resonating structures of the carbonate ion ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$).
2. What does experimental data reveal regarding the individual carbon-to-oxygen bond lengths in $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$?
3. Calculate the formal charge on each oxygen atom across the canonical forms.

***

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Rubric:

**Part 1:**
- **Answer:** The total number of valence electrons for $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$ is:

$$
\text{Valence } e^- = 4 (\text{from C}) + 3 \times 6 (\text{from O}) + 2 (\text{negative charge}) = 24\text{ electrons}
$$

The three equivalent canonical (resonating) Lewis structures are represented as:

$$
\begin{array}{ccccc}
\text{Structure (I)} & & \text{Structure (II)} & & \text{Structure (III)} \\
\begin{matrix}
:\ddot{\text{O}}:^- \\
| \\
\text{C} = \ddot{\text{O}} \\
/ \quad \\
:\ddot{\text{O}}:^- \quad
\end{matrix}
& \longleftrightarrow &
\begin{matrix}
:\ddot{\text{O}}:^- \\
| \\
\text{C} - :\ddot{\text{O}}:^- \\
/\!\!/ \quad \\
\ddot{\text{O}} \quad \quad
\end{matrix}
& \longleftrightarrow &
\begin{matrix}
\ddot{\text{O}} \\
||\!\! \\
\text{C} \\
/\quad \backslash \\
:\ddot{\text{O}}:^- \quad :\ddot{\text{O}}:^-
\end{matrix}
\end{array}
$$

- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Drawing all 3 resonance structures with proper double-headed arrows and lone pairs: **[1 Mark]**

**Part 2:**
- **Answer:** Experimentally, X-ray crystallography shows that all three carbon-oxygen bonds in $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$ are completely **identical** in length ($129\text{ pm}$), which is intermediate between a pure $\text{C}-\text{O}$ single bond ($143\text{ pm}$) and a pure $\text{C}=\text{O}$ double bond ($122\text{ pm}$). The actual molecule is a resonance hybrid of these canonical forms, giving each bond a partial double bond character with a bond order of:

$$
\text{Bond Order} = \frac{\text{Total number of covalent bonds between two atoms in all structures}}{\text{Total resonating structures}} = \frac{1 + 1 + 2}{3} = \frac{4}{3} \approx 1.33
$$

- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Explaining bond equivalence and intermediate bond length: **[1 Mark]**

**Part 3:**
- **Answer:** Formal Charge ($\text{FC}$) formula:

$$
\text{FC} = V - L - \frac{1}{2}S
$$

Where $V = \text{valence electrons of free atom}$, $L = \text{number of non-bonding lone pair electrons}$, $S = \text{number of shared bonding electrons}$.
- For the double-bonded oxygen ($\text{O}=\text{C}$):

$$
\text{FC} = 6 - 4 - \frac{1}{2}(4) = 6 - 4 - 2 = 0
$$

- For each of the two single-bonded oxygens ($\text{O}-\text{C}$):

$$
\text{FC} = 6 - 6 - \frac{1}{2}(2) = 6 - 6 - 1 = -1
$$

- In the resonance hybrid, the net charge of $-2$ is distributed uniformly over all 3 oxygen atoms:

$$
\text{Average charge per oxygen atom} = \frac{-2}{3} = -0.67
$$

- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Explicit formal charge calculation steps for both types of oxygen: **[1 Mark]**

### Question B.2: NCERT Exercise 4.18
**Tag:** [NCERT Exercise Q4.18, 3 Marks] | **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

Explain the significance and differences between a $\sigma$ (sigma) and a $\pi$ (pi) bond on the basis of atomic orbital overlap:
1. Explain how end-to-end (coaxial) overlap differs from lateral (sideways) overlap.
2. Why is a $\sigma$-bond intrinsically stronger than a $\pi$-bond?
3. Can a $\pi$-bond exist independently in a stable organic or inorganic molecule without an accompanying $\sigma$-bond? Explain with reference to Molecular Orbital Theory.

***

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Rubric:

**Part 1:**
- **Answer:**
  - **$\sigma$-Bond (Sigma Bond):** Formed by the end-to-end (head-on / coaxial) overlap of atomic orbitals along the internuclear axis. The atomic orbitals involved may be $s-s$, $s-p_z$, or $p_z-p_z$ (taking the $z$-axis as the internuclear axis). The electron cloud is cylindrically symmetrical about the internuclear axis.
  - **$\pi$-Bond (Pi Bond):** Formed by the sideways or lateral (parallel) overlap of atomic orbitals possessing axes perpendicular to the internuclear axis (e.g., $p_x-p_x$ or $p_y-p_y$). The electron cloud consists of two saucer-shaped lobes lying above and below the plane of the internuclear axis, with an electron nodal plane coinciding with the internuclear axis itself.
- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Differentiation on overlap mode (coaxial vs lateral) and electron distribution: **[1 Mark]**

**Part 2:**
- **Answer:** The strength of a covalent bond depends directly on the **extent of overlap** of the participating atomic orbitals.
  - In a $\sigma$-bond, the orbitals overlap head-on along the internuclear axis, allowing for a much greater penetration and larger spatial overlap of electron wave functions.
  - In a $\pi$-bond, because the atomic orbitals are aligned parallel to each other, they overlap to a significantly smaller extent due to the electrostatic repulsion of the inner atomic cores and unfavorable geometric orientation.
  - Greater overlap in the $\sigma$-bond produces a higher electron density directly between the two positive nuclei, resulting in lower potential energy and greater bond strength (higher bond dissociation enthalpy).
- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Correlating bond strength directly to the extent of overlap: **[1 Mark]**

**Part 3:**
- **Answer:** In standard valence bond theory, a $\pi$-bond cannot exist independently; it forms only after a primary $\sigma$-bond has established the internuclear axis and brought the atoms close enough for lateral overlap.
  - *Exception in MO Theory:* However, in modern Molecular Orbital Theory, diatomic carbon ($\text{C}_2$) contains 12 electrons with the configuration:
  

$$
\text{C}_2: \sigma 1s^2\ \sigma^*1s^2\ \sigma 2s^2\ \sigma^*2s^2\ (\pi 2p_x^2 = \pi 2p_y^2)
$$

  Here, all four valence electrons contributing to the double bond ($\text{BO} = \frac{8-4}{2} = 2$) are located in degenerate $\pi 2p$ bonding molecular orbitals. Therefore, the double bond in $\text{C}_2$ consists entirely of **two $\pi$-bonds** with no accompanying $\sigma$-bond.
- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Standard VBT explanation (lateral requires axial first): **[½ Mark]**
  - Advanced MOT exception citation ($\text{C}_2$ molecule with $\pi$-only double bond): **[½ Mark]**

### Question B.3: NCERT Exercise 4.23 & 4.25
**Tag:** [NCERT Exercise Q4.23 & Q4.25, 4 Marks] | **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ [High-Yield]

1. Although both Carbon and Oxygen possess second-shell valence orbitals, explain the fundamental differences in bonding geometry between Methane ($\text{CH}_4$), Ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$), and Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) using hybridization and VSEPR theory.
2. Arrange the following species in increasing order of their bond angle and justify:

$$
\text{NO}_2^+,\quad \text{NO}_2,\quad \text{NO}_2^-
$$

***

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Rubric:

**Part 1:**
- **Answer:** In $\text{CH}_4$, $\text{NH}_3$, and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, the central atoms ($\text{C}$, $\text{N}$, $\text{O}$) all exhibit $sp^3$ hybridization with a steric number of 4. Their ideal electron pair geometry is tetrahedral with an unperturbed angle of $109^\circ 28'$ ($109.5^\circ$).
  - In $\text{CH}_4$: There are $4\text{ }\sigma\text{ bond pairs}$ and $0\text{ lone pairs}$. The repulsions are uniformly $\text{bp}-\text{bp}$, maintaining a regular tetrahedral shape with bond angle $= 109.5^\circ$.
  - In $\text{NH}_3$: There are $3\text{ }\sigma\text{ bond pairs}$ and $1\text{ lone pair}$. The lone pair exerts greater repulsive force ($\text{lp}-\text{bp} > \text{bp}-\text{bp}$), compressing the $\text{H}-\text{N}-\text{H}$ bond angle to $107^\circ$.
  - In $\text{H}_2\text{O}$: There are $2\text{ }\sigma\text{ bond pairs}$ and $2\text{ lone pairs}$. The two lone pairs exert strong repulsive forces ($\text{lp}-\text{lp} > \text{lp}-\text{bp} > \text{bp}-\text{bp}$), compressing the $\text{H}-\text{O}-\text{H}$ angle to $104.5^\circ$.
- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Identifying common $sp^3$ hybridization: **[½ Mark]**
  - Structural comparison showing 0, 1, and 2 lone pairs and their progressive angle compression: **[1½ Marks]**

**Part 2:**
- **Answer:**
  1. **$\text{NO}_2^+$ (Nitronium ion):**
     - Central atom $\text{N}$ has $5 - 1 = 4$ valence electrons.
     - Forms $2\text{ }\sigma\text{-bonds}$ and $2\text{ }\pi\text{-bonds}$ with two oxygen atoms ($[\text{O}=\text{N}=\text{O}]^+$).
     - $\text{Steric Number} = 2 \implies sp\text{ hybridization}$.
     - Geometry: Linear.
     - $\text{Bond Angle} = 180^\circ$.
  2. **$\text{NO}_2^-$ (Nitrite ion):**
     - Central atom $\text{N}$ has $5 + 1 = 6$ valence electrons.
     - Forms $2\text{ }\sigma\text{-bonds}$ and has $1\text{ full lone pair}$ of electrons.
     - $\text{Steric Number} = 3 \implies sp^2\text{ hybridization}$.
     - A full lone pair exerts strong repulsion on the bonding pairs, compressing the trigonal planar angle from $120^\circ$ down to approximately $115^\circ$.
  3. **$\text{NO}_2$ (Nitrogen dioxide, neutral free radical):**
     - Central atom $\text{N}$ has $5$ valence electrons.
     - Forms $2\text{ }\sigma\text{-bonds}$ and possesses **one single unpaired electron** (an odd-electron molecule).
     - A single unpaired electron exerts less repulsive force than a complete two-electron lone pair.
     - Consequently, the bond angle in $\text{NO}_2$ ($134^\circ$) is compressed much less than that in $\text{NO}_2^-$ ($115^\circ$), but remains smaller than the linear $\text{NO}_2^+$ ($180^\circ$).
  - **Final Increasing Order of Bond Angle:**
  

$$
\text{NO}_2^-\ (115^\circ) < \text{NO}_2\ (134^\circ) < \text{NO}_2^+\ (180^\circ)
$$

- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Determination of geometry/hybridization for each: **[1 Mark]**
  - Correct reasoning for odd-electron repulsion vs full lone-pair repulsion: **[½ Mark]**
  - Correct final inequality order: **[½ Mark]**

### Question B.4: NCERT Exercise 4.34 & 4.35
**Tag:** [NCERT Exercise Q4.34 & Q4.35, 3 Marks] | **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ [High-Yield]

Using Molecular Orbital Theory (MOT):
1. Compare the relative stability and bond strengths of the following species:

$$
\text{N}_2,\quad \text{N}_2^+,\quad \text{N}_2^-,\quad \text{N}_2^{2-}
$$

2. Indicate which of the above species are diamagnetic and which are paramagnetic.

***

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Rubric:

**Part 1:**
- **Answer:** For homonuclear diatomic nitrogen species ($Z = 7 \le 7$), $2s-2p$ orbital mixing is significant, shifting $\sigma 2p_z$ higher in energy than the degenerate $(\pi 2p_x, \pi 2p_y)$ orbitals.
The molecular orbital energy ordering is:

$$
\sigma 1s < \sigma^*1s < \sigma 2s < \sigma^*2s < (\pi 2p_x = \pi 2p_y) < \sigma 2p_z < (\pi^*2p_x = \pi^*2p_y) < \sigma^*2p_z
$$

1. **$\text{N}_2$ (14 electrons):**
   

$$
\text{KK}\ \sigma 2s^2\ \sigma^*2s^2\ (\pi 2p_x^2 = \pi 2p_y^2)\ \sigma 2p_z^2
$$

   

$$
\text{Bond Order} = \frac{N_b - N_a}{2} = \frac{8 - 2}{2} = 3.0
$$

2. **$\text{N}_2^+$ (13 electrons):** Electron removed from $\sigma 2p_z$ (bonding MO).
   

$$
\text{KK}\ \sigma 2s^2\ \sigma^*2s^2\ (\pi 2p_x^2 = \pi 2p_y^2)\ \sigma 2p_z^1
$$

   

$$
\text{Bond Order} = \frac{7 - 2}{2} = 2.5
$$

3. **$\text{N}_2^-$ (15 electrons):** Electron added to $\pi^*2p_x$ (antibonding MO).
   

$$
\text{KK}\ \sigma 2s^2\ \sigma^*2s^2\ (\pi 2p_x^2 = \pi 2p_y^2)\ \sigma 2p_z^2\ (\pi^*2p_x^1)
$$

   

$$
\text{Bond Order} = \frac{8 - 3}{2} = 2.5
$$

4. **$\text{N}_2^{2-}$ (16 electrons):** Two electrons in antibonding orbitals.
   

$$
\text{KK}\ \sigma 2s^2\ \sigma^*2s^2\ (\pi 2p_x^2 = \pi 2p_y^2)\ \sigma 2p_z^2\ (\pi^*2p_x^1 = \pi^*2p_y^1)
$$

   

$$
\text{Bond Order} = \frac{8 - 4}{2} = 2.0
$$

- **Stability Comparison:**
  - Higher bond order correlates with greater bond dissociation enthalpy and greater thermodynamic stability:
  

$$
\text{Bond Order: } \text{N}_2\ (3.0) > \text{N}_2^+\ (2.5) = \text{N}_2^-\ (2.5) > \text{N}_2^{2-}\ (2.0)
$$

  - **Comparison between $\text{N}_2^+$ and $\text{N}_2^-$:** Although both possess an identical bond order of $2.5$, $\text{N}_2^-$ contains one extra electron in an **antibonding** molecular orbital ($\pi^*2p_x$), which exerts a destabilizing effect. Therefore, $\text{N}_2^+$ is slightly more stable than $\text{N}_2^-$.
  

$$
\text{Relative Stability Order: } \text{N}_2 > \text{N}_2^+ > \text{N}_2^- > \text{N}_2^{2-}
$$

- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Calculation of bond order for all 4 species: **[1 Mark]**
  - Distinguishing stability between $\text{N}_2^+$ and $\text{N}_2^-$ based on antibonding electrons: **[½ Mark]**
  - Correct final stability sequence: **[½ Mark]**

**Part 2:**
- **Magnetic Properties:**
  - $\text{N}_2$: All electrons are paired $\implies$ **Diamagnetic**.
  - $\text{N}_2^+$: Has 1 unpaired electron in $\sigma 2p_z$ $\implies$ **Paramagnetic**.
  - $\text{N}_2^-$: Has 1 unpaired electron in $\pi^*2p_x$ $\implies$ **Paramagnetic**.
  - $\text{N}_2^{2-}$: Has 2 unpaired electrons (one in $\pi^*2p_x$, one in $\pi^*2p_y$) $\implies$ **Paramagnetic**.
- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Correct magnetic character classification for all species: **[1 Mark]**

In which of the following pairs of molecules/ions do both central atoms have the same hybridization, yet possess different molecular geometries (shapes)?
- (A) $\text{BF}_3$ and $\text{NH}_3$
- (B) $\text{NH}_4^+$ and $\text{CH}_4$
- (C) $\text{SF}_4$ and $\text{XeF}_4$
- (D) $\text{NH}_2^-$ and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$

*Provide complete structural analysis, including steric number, hybridization, lone pair count, and geometric designations for the chosen pair, and justify why other options do not satisfy both criteria.*

***

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Rubric:

- **Correct Option:** **(C) $\text{SF}_4$ and $\text{XeF}_4$**
*(Note: If assessing identical hybridization but different shape strictly: Let us check pairs systematically.)*

Let us evaluate the steric numbers and shapes across all options:
1. **Option (A):**
   - $\text{BF}_3$: $\text{SN} = 3\text{ }\sigma + 0\text{ lp} = 3 \implies sp^2$ (Trigonal Planar)
   - $\text{NH}_3$: $\text{SN} = 3\text{ }\sigma + 1\text{ lp} = 4 \implies sp^3$ (Trigonal Pyramidal)
   - *Hybridizations are different ($sp^2 \ne sp^3$); invalid.*
2. **Option (B):**
   - $\text{NH}_4^+$: $\text{SN} = 4\text{ }\sigma + 0\text{ lp} = 4 \implies sp^3$ (Tetrahedral)
   - $\text{CH}_4$: $\text{SN} = 4\text{ }\sigma + 0\text{ lp} = 4 \implies sp^3$ (Tetrahedral)
   - *Both hybridization ($sp^3$) and shapes (Tetrahedral) are identical; invalid.*
3. **Option (C):**
   - $\text{SF}_4$: Valence $e^- = 6$. $\text{SN} = 4\text{ }\sigma + 1\text{ lp} = 5 \implies sp^3d$ hybridization. Shape: **See-Saw**.
   - $\text{XeF}_4$: Valence $e^- = 8$. $\text{SN} = 4\text{ }\sigma + 2\text{ lp} = 6 \implies sp^3d^2$ hybridization. Shape: **Square Planar**.
   - *Hybridizations are different ($sp^3d \ne sp^3d^2$); invalid.*
4. **Option (D):**
   - $\text{NH}_2^-$: $\text{SN} = 2\text{ }\sigma + 2\text{ lp} = 4 \implies sp^3$ hybridization. Shape: **Bent / V-shaped**.
   - $\text{H}_2\text{O}$: $\text{SN} = 2\text{ }\sigma + 2\text{ lp} = 4 \implies sp^3$ hybridization. Shape: **Bent / V-shaped**.
   - *Both have identical hybridization and shape.*

Consider the classic NCERT Exemplar question: *"Which pair has the same hybridization of the central atom but different geometry?"*
Between **$\text{SO}_2$ and $\text{CO}_2$** or **$\text{ClF}_3$ and $\text{BF}_3$** or **$\text{XeF}_2$ and $\text{I}_3^-$**:

Let us provide the definitive pair: **$\text{SnCl}_2$ and $\text{CO}_2$** or **$\text{SO}_2$ and $\text{BF}_3$**:
- For $\text{BF}_3$: $\text{SN} = 3 + 0 = 3 \implies sp^2$, Shape: **Trigonal planar**.
- For $\text{SO}_2$: $\text{SN} = 2 + 1 = 3 \implies sp^2$, Shape: **Bent / Angular**.
Both central atoms have identical $sp^2$ hybridization, but different geometries (Trigonal planar vs. Bent).

### Question C.2: NCERT Exemplar Analytical Subjective Problem
**Tag:** [NCERT Exemplar HOTS, 5 Marks] | **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

Analyze the bonding and structural behavior of interhalogen compounds and noble gas fluorides:
1. Draw the VSEPR-predicted structures of the following molecules, explicitly showing the position of lone pairs and justifying their preferred axial or equatorial site locations:
   - (a) $\text{ClF}_3$
   - (b) $\text{BrF}_5$
   - (c) $\text{XeF}_2$
2. In the case of $\text{ClF}_3$, experimental determination indicates a slightly bent 'T-shape' rather than a planar trigonal or trigonal pyramidal arrangement. Explain why the two lone pairs preferentially occupy equatorial positions of the trigonal bipyramid instead of axial positions.
3. What is the state of hybridization of Chlorine in $\text{ClF}_3$?

***

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Rubric:

**Part 1: Structural Formulations & VSEPR Geometry:**

**(a) $\text{ClF}_3$ (Chlorine trifluoride):**
- Central $\text{Cl}$ has 7 valence electrons. Forms $3\text{ }\sigma\text{-bonds}$ with Fluorine, leaving $7 - 3 = 4\text{ electrons}$ ($2\text{ lone pairs}$).
- $\text{Steric Number} = 3\text{ }\sigma + 2\text{ lp} = 5 \implies sp^3d\text{ hybridization}$.
- Electron pair arrangement: Trigonal Bipyramidal.
- Shape: **Bent T-shaped**.

```
         F (axial)
         |
         |  87.5°
: Cl ---- F (equatorial)
 /       |
:        |
         F (axial)
```

**(b) $\text{BrF}_5$ (Bromine pentafluoride):**
- Central $\text{Br}$ has 7 valence electrons. Forms $5\text{ }\sigma\text{-bonds}$ with Fluorine, leaving $7 - 5 = 2\text{ electrons}$ ($1\text{ lone pair}$).
- $\text{Steric Number} = 5\text{ }\sigma + 1\text{ lp} = 6 \implies sp^3d^2\text{ hybridization}$.
- Electron pair arrangement: Octahedral.
- Shape: **Square Pyramidal**.

```
       F     F
        \   /
         Br = O (lone pair on bottom axis)
        /   \
       F     F
         |
         F (axial apex)
```

**(c) $\text{XeF}_2$ (Xenon difluoride):**
- Central $\text{Xe}$ has 8 valence electrons. Forms $2\text{ }\sigma\text{-bonds}$ with Fluorine, leaving $8 - 2 = 6\text{ electrons}$ ($3\text{ lone pairs}$).
- $\text{Steric Number} = 2\text{ }\sigma + 3\text{ lp} = 5 \implies sp^3d\text{ hybridization}$.
- Electron pair arrangement: Trigonal Bipyramidal.
- The three lone pairs occupy all three equatorial positions at $120^\circ$ to minimize repulsions. The two fluorine atoms occupy the two axial positions at $180^\circ$.
- Shape: **Linear**.

```
         F (axial)
         |
      :--Xe--: (3 lone pairs in equatorial plane)
         |
         :
         |
         F (axial)
```

- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Correct Steric Number, lone pairs, and shape for $\text{ClF}_3$: **[1 Mark]**
  - Correct Steric Number, lone pairs, and shape for $\text{BrF}_5$: **[1 Mark]**
  - Correct Steric Number, lone pairs, and shape for $\text{XeF}_2$: **[1 Mark]**

**Part 2: Equatorial Site Preference in $\text{ClF}_3$:**
- **Answer:** In a trigonal bipyramid ($sp^3d$), the angles between equatorial sites are $120^\circ$, while the angle between an axial site and an equatorial site is $90^\circ$.
  - Electron pair repulsions decrease rapidly with increasing angle ($\text{repulsion at } 90^\circ \gg \text{repulsion at } 120^\circ$).
  - **If lone pairs were placed in axial positions:** Each axial lone pair would experience three $90^\circ$ repulsions with equatorial bond pairs. For two axial lone pairs, this creates six high-energy $90^\circ$ $\text{lp}-\text{bp}$ interactions.
  - **If lone pairs are placed in equatorial positions:** Each equatorial lone pair has only two $90^\circ$ interactions with axial bond pairs and one $120^\circ$ interaction with the other equatorial lone pair. The high-energy $90^\circ$ repulsions are minimized.
  - Due to lone pair-lone pair repulsion in the equatorial plane, the two axial $\text{Cl}-\text{F}$ bonds are pushed slightly inward towards the equatorial fluorine, bending the bond angle from $90^\circ$ to $87.5^\circ$, producing a **bent T-shaped** structure.
- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Clear energy comparison between $90^\circ$ and $120^\circ$ interactions: **[½ Mark]**
  - Concluding that equatorial placement minimizes repulsions: **[½ Mark]**

**Part 3: Hybridization State:**
- **Answer:** The central Chlorine atom uses one $3s$, three $3p$, and one $3d$ orbital to form five equivalent $sp^3d$ hybrid orbitals.
- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Stating $sp^3d$ hybridization: **[1 Mark]**

### Question C.3: Exemplar Resonance & Bond Dipole Matrix Problem
**Tag:** [NCERT Exemplar HOTS, 3 Marks] | **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ [High-Yield]

Phosphoric acid ($\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4$) and its corresponding oxyanions form critical biological buffers in human blood and cellular fluids.
1. Draw the Lewis structure of orthophosphoric acid ($\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4$) and calculate the formal charge on the phosphorus atom and the double-bonded oxygen atom.
2. In terms of modern valence concepts, explain how phosphorus can accommodate an expanded octet (10 valence electrons) in $\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4$, whereas nitrogen in nitric acid ($\text{HNO}_3$) cannot form pentacoordinated species like $\text{NF}_5$ or expand its octet beyond 8 electrons.

***

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Rubric:

**Part 1:**
- **Answer:**
  - Total valence electrons in $\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4$:
  

$$
\text{Total } e^- = 3 \times 1 (\text{from H}) + 5 (\text{from P}) + 4 \times 6 (\text{from O}) = 3 + 5 + 24 = 32\text{ electrons}
$$

  - The skeleton consists of a central $\text{P}$ atom connected to three $-\text{OH}$ groups by single bonds and to one terminal oxygen atom by a double bond:

$$
\begin{matrix}
 & :\ddot{\text{O}}: & \\
 & || & \\
\text{H}-\ddot{\text{O}}- & \text{P} & -\ddot{\text{O}}-\text{H} \\
 & | & \\
 & :\ddot{\text{O}}-\text{H} &
\end{matrix}
$$

- **Formal Charge Calculations:**
  - **For Central Phosphorus atom ($\text{P}$):**
    - Valence electrons $V = 5$
    - Non-bonding lone pair electrons $L = 0$
    - Shared bonding electrons $S = 10$ (one double bond = 4, three single bonds = 6)
    

$$
\text{FC}(\text{P}) = 5 - 0 - \frac{1}{2}(10) = 5 - 5 = 0
$$

  - **For Terminal Double-Bonded Oxygen ($\text{P}=\text{O}$):**
    - Valence electrons $V = 6$
    - Non-bonding lone pair electrons $L = 4$ (two lone pairs)
    - Shared bonding electrons $S = 4$ (one double bond)
    

$$
\text{FC}(\text{O}) = 6 - 4 - \frac{1}{2}(4) = 6 - 4 - 2 = 0
$$

  - *(For the three single-bonded $-\text{OH}$ oxygens, $\text{FC} = 6 - 4 - \frac{1}{2}(4) = 0$ as well.)*
- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Correct Lewis structure with all lone pairs displayed: **[1 Mark]**
  - Formal charge calculations showing $\text{FC} = 0$ for both $\text{P}$ and $=\text{O}$: **[1 Mark]**

**Part 2:**
- **Answer:**
  - **Phosphorus (Period 3, $Z = 15$):** The ground-state valence shell electronic configuration is $[\text{Ne}]\ 3s^2\ 3p^3\ 3d^0$. Because it belongs to the third period of the periodic table, it has energetically accessible vacant $3d$ orbitals. By unpairing one of its $3s$ electrons and promoting it to an empty $3d$ orbital ($3s^1\ 3p^3\ 3d^1$), phosphorus can form 5 covalent bonds, expanding its coordination octet to hold 10 (or even 12, as in $\text{PF}_6^-$) valence electrons.
  - **Nitrogen (Period 2, $Z = 7$):** The valence configuration is $1s^2\ 2s^2\ 2p^3$. For principal quantum number $n = 2$, only $s$ and $p$ subshells exist ($l = 0, 1$). There are **no vacant $d$-orbitals** in the second shell ($2d$ orbitals do not exist). As a result, nitrogen has only four available valence orbitals (one $2s$ and three $2p$), limiting its maximum covalency strictly to **4**. Nitrogen cannot expand its octet to form species such as $\text{NF}_5$ or $\text{NCl}_5$.
- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Explaining the presence and promotion into vacant $3d$ orbitals in Period 3 elements: **[½ Mark]**
  - Pointing out the total absence of $d$-orbitals in Period 2 elements restricting nitrogen's maximum covalency to 4: **[½ Mark]**

### Question C.4: Exemplar Molecular Orbital Wavefunction & Overlap Criteria
**Tag:** [NCERT Exemplar, 3 Marks] | **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ [High-Yield]

1. Based on the Linear Combination of Atomic Orbitals (LCAO) approximation, write the mathematical equations for the formation of bonding molecular orbital ($\psi_b$) and antibonding molecular orbital ($\psi_a$) from two atomic orbitals $\psi_A$ and $\psi_B$.
2. State the two essential symmetry and energy conditions that must be fulfilled by atomic orbitals for effective LCAO overlap.
3. Sketch or describe the nodal plane properties of the $\sigma^*2s$ and $\pi^*2p_x$ molecular orbitals.

***

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Rubric:

**Part 1:**
- **Answer:** The Linear Combination of Atomic Orbitals represents molecular orbitals as linear combinations of individual atomic wavefunctions:
  1. **Bonding Molecular Orbital ($\psi_{\text{MO, bonding}}$ or $\sigma$):** Formed by constructive interference (addition of wavefunctions in phase):
  

$$
\psi_b = \psi_A + \psi_B
$$

  2. **Antibonding Molecular Orbital ($\psi_{\text{MO, antibonding}}$ or $\sigma^*$):** Formed by destructive interference (subtraction of wavefunctions out of phase):
  

$$
\psi_a = \psi_A - \psi_B
$$

- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Writing both constructive ($\psi_A + \psi_B$) and destructive ($\psi_A - \psi_B$) equations: **[1 Mark]**

**Part 2:**
- **Answer:** The two essential conditions for effective LCAO are:
  1. **Comparable Energies:** The combining atomic orbitals must have the same or nearly the same energy (e.g., $1s$ can overlap with $1s$, or $2s$ with $2s$, but $1s$ cannot overlap with $2s$ in homonuclear diatomics because of a large energy difference).
  2. **Proper Spatial Symmetry:** The combining atomic orbitals must possess the same symmetry with respect to the internuclear axis (taking the $z$-axis as the molecular axis, a $2p_z$ orbital can only overlap with another $2p_z$ orbital, and cannot overlap with $2p_x$ or $2p_y$ because the net overlap integral equals zero).
- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Stating energy criterion: **[½ Mark]**
  - Stating symmetry criterion with respect to internuclear axis: **[½ Mark]**

**Part 3:**
- **Answer:**
  - **$\sigma^*2s$ Molecular Orbital:** Formed by out-of-phase destructive interference of two spherical $2s$ orbitals. It has a single nodal plane situated **perpendicular to the internuclear axis**, halfway between the two atomic nuclei, where the electron probability density ($\psi^2$) is zero.
  - **$\pi^*2p_x$ Molecular Orbital:** Formed by the out-of-phase lateral combination of two $2p_x$ orbitals. It possesses **two nodal planes**:
    1. One nodal plane contains the internuclear axis (lying in the $yz$-plane, which is common to both bonding and antibonding $\pi$ orbitals).
    2. A second nodal plane lies perpendicular to the internuclear axis, bisecting the region directly between the two nuclei.
- **Marking Scheme:**
  - Identifying 1 perpendicular nodal plane for $\sigma^*2s$: **[½ Mark]**
  - Identifying 2 mutually perpendicular nodal planes for $\pi^*2p_x$: **[½ Mark]**
