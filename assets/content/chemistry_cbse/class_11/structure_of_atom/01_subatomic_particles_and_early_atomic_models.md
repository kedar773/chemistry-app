# PART 1: IN-DEPTH PEDAGOGICAL THEORY & HISTORICAL GENESIS

### 1.1 Dalton's Atomic Theory & The Failure of Indivisibility ⭐⭐⭐
In 1808, John Dalton proposed his Atomic Theory based on the laws of chemical combination (Law of Conservation of Mass, Law of Definite Proportions, Law of Multiple Proportions):
1. Matter consists of indivisible, indestructible particles called **atoms**.
2. Atoms of a given element are identical in mass and chemical properties.
3. Atoms combine in the ratio of small whole numbers to form compounds.

**Experimental Breakdown of Dalton's Postulate:**
Faraday's electrolysis experiments (1830s) provided the first direct quantitative evidence that matter has an electrical structure:

$$
\text{Charge on } 1\text{ mole of electrons} = F = N_A \cdot e \approx 96485.33 \text{ C}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}
$$

This demonstrated that chemical bonding and mass deposition are governed by electrical units, disproving the strict indivisibility of the Daltonian atom.

***

### 1.2 Discovery of the Electron (Cathode Rays) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
The experimental discovery of the electron was achieved using **Cathode Ray Discharge Tubes** by Julius Plücker, William Crookes, and definitively characterized by J.J. Thomson (1897).

#### Discharge Tube Experimental Setup:
* **Apparatus:** A hard glass cylindrical tube fitted with two sealed metal electrodes (cathode: negative terminal; anode: positive terminal).
* **Operating Conditions:**
  * **Pressure:** Reduced from atmospheric ($1 \text{ atm} \approx 760 \text{ torr}$) down to very low pressure:
    

$$
P \approx 10^{-4} \text{ atm to } 10^{-2} \text{ mm Hg} \quad (10^{-2} \text{ torr})
$$

  * **Applied Potential Difference:** Very high voltage:
    

$$
V \approx 10{,}000 \text{ V to } 20{,}000 \text{ V}
$$

* **Visual Phenomenon:**
  * At normal pressure ($1 \text{ atm}$), gases are poor conductors of electricity; no current flows.
  * At $P \sim 10^{-1} \text{ mm Hg}$, the gas begins to glow with a characteristic color depending on the gas used.
  * At $P \sim 10^{-2} \text{ mm Hg}$ to $10^{-4} \text{ mm Hg}$, the glow inside the gas ceases, but current continues to flow. A faint greenish fluorescence appears on the glass wall behind the perforated anode due to bombardment by invisible rays originating from the cathode.

#### Key Characteristics of Cathode Rays (NCERT Specific Points):
1. **Direction of Propagation:** Cathode rays travel in straight lines from the cathode ($-$) towards the anode ($+$).
   * *Proof:* They cast a sharp shadow of an opaque object placed in their path.
2. **Material/Mechanical Nature:** They possess kinetic energy and momentum.
   * *Proof:* They rotate a light paddle wheel mounted on a frictionless spindle placed in their path.
3. **Electric & Magnetic Field Deflection:**
   * In an electric field, the rays deflect toward the positive plate, proving they carry a **negative electric charge**.
   * In a magnetic field applied perpendicularly, they deflect in accordance with Fleming’s Left-Hand Rule.
4. **Universal Nature:** The properties of cathode rays (and their specific charge $e/m_e$) are **independent of**:
   * The nature of the gas inside the tube (whether $\text{H}_2, \text{He}, \text{N}_2$, etc.).
   * The material of the electrodes (whether $\text{Pt}, \text{Fe}, \text{Al}$, etc.).
   * *Deduction:* Electrons are fundamental, universal subatomic constituents of all matter.
5. **Secondary Effects:** They ionize the gases through which they pass, cause fluorescence on phosphors (such as activated zinc sulphide, $\text{ZnS}$), and produce **X-rays** when striking a heavy metal target (such as tungsten or molybdenum).

***

### 1.3 Discovery of the Proton (Anode / Canal Rays) ⭐⭐⭐⭐
In 1886, Eugen Goldstein conducted discharge tube experiments using a **perforated cathode**. He observed luminous rays traveling in the direction opposite to cathode rays (from anode toward cathode), passing through the perforations (canals) of the cathode. These rays were designated as **Canal Rays** or **Anode Rays**.

#### Crucial Contrasts Between Cathode Rays and Anode Rays:

| Parameter | Cathode Rays (Electrons) | Anode Rays (Positive Ions) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Origin** | Emitted directly from cathode surface + gas ionization | Formed **only in the gas space** via ionization of residual gas molecules |
| **Carrier Identity** | Fundamental particle ($e^-$) | Gaseous cations ($\text{M}^{n+}$) formed after electron loss |
| **Dependence on Gas** | **Completely independent** of gas used | **Strongly dependent** on the nature of the residual gas |
| **Charge-to-Mass Ratio ($q/m$)** | Constant: $1.758820 \times 10^{11} \text{ C}\cdot\text{kg}^{-1}$ | Variable; depends on the molar mass $M$ and charge $z$ of the gas ion |
| **Maximum $q/m$ Observed** | Not applicable (fixed) | Maximum when the lightest gas, **Hydrogen ($\text{H}_2$)**, is filled |

* **Definition of Proton:** When hydrogen gas ($\text{H}_2$) is enclosed in the discharge tube, the resulting positive particle has the minimum mass and the highest $q/m$ ratio. This positive particle is the **proton** ($\text{p}$ or $\text{H}^+$):
  

$$
\text{H} \xrightarrow{\text{high energy } e^-} \text{H}^+ + e^-
$$

***

### 1.4 Discovery of the Neutron ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Until 1932, the atom was assumed to consist solely of protons and electrons. However, the mass of atomic nuclei (except $^1\text{H}$) exceeded the total mass of the protons contained within them.

In 1932, **James Chadwick** bombarded a thin foil of **Beryllium ($^9_4\text{Be}$)** with high-energy $\alpha$-particles ($^4_2\text{He}$) emitted from a polonium source. He detected highly penetrating, electrically neutral radiation consisting of particles whose mass was almost identical to that of a proton.

* **Nuclear Transmutation Reaction:**
  

$$
^9_4\text{Be} + ^4_2\text{He} \longrightarrow ^{12}_6\text{C} + ^1_0\text{n}
$$

  *(Also observed with Boron):*
  

$$
^{11}_5\text{B} + ^4_2\text{He} \longrightarrow ^{14}_7\text{N} + ^1_0\text{n}
$$

***

### 2.1 Thomson’s Determination of Specific Charge ($e/m_e$) of an Electron ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### Experimental Principle:
A collimated beam of electrons passes through a region of mutually perpendicular (orthogonal) electric ($\vec{E}$) and magnetic ($\vec{B}$) fields, oriented transversely to the direction of propagation ($\vec{v}$).

```
          (+) Electric Plate
           ─────────────────────
                 │  │  │  │  E-field (downward)
Electron ───►    ▼  ▼  ▼  ▼          ───► Fluorescent Screen
Path             B-field into page (⊗)
           ─────────────────────
          (-) Electric Plate
```

#### Step 1: Force Balance in Crossed Fields (Velocity Selector Condition)
Let the electron travel along the $+x$-direction with velocity $v_x = v$.
* **Electric Force ($\vec{F}_E$):** An electric field $\vec{E}$ directed vertically downward (along $-y$) exerts an upward electrostatic force:
  

$$
F_E = e E \quad (\text{directed in } +y \text{ direction})
$$

* **Magnetic Force ($\vec{F}_B$):** A magnetic field $\vec{B}$ directed perpendicular to $\vec{v}$ (into the page, along $-z$) produces a magnetic Lorentz force:
  

$$
\vec{F}_B = -e (\vec{v} \times \vec{B})
$$

  

$$
F_B = e v B \quad (\text{directed in } -y \text{ direction})
$$

By tuning the magnitudes of $E$ and $B$, the two opposing forces are made exactly equal so that the electron beam undergoes **zero net deflection** and strikes the central point on the screen:

$$
F_E = F_B \implies e E = e v B
$$

$$
\boxed{v = \frac{E}{B}}
$$

#### Step 2: Pure Electric Deflection
Turn off the magnetic field ($B = 0$) while maintaining the uniform electric field $E$ over a plates' length $L$.
* Along the $x$-axis (no acceleration):
  

$$
t = \frac{L}{v} = \frac{L B}{E}
$$

* Along the $y$-axis:
  

$$
a_y = \frac{F_E}{m_e} = \frac{e E}{m_e}
$$

* The transverse displacement $y_1$ at the exit of the electric field plates (starting from $u_y = 0$):
  

$$
y_1 = \frac{1}{2} a_y t^2 = \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{e E}{m_e}\right) \left(\frac{L}{v}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{2} \frac{e}{m_e} \frac{E L^2}{v^2}
$$

* Substituting $v = \frac{E}{B}$:
  

$$
y_1 = \frac{1}{2} \frac{e}{m_e} \frac{E L^2}{\left(\frac{E}{B}\right)^2} = \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{e}{m_e}\right) \frac{L^2 B^2}{E}
$$

* Upon exiting the plates, the electron moves with a constant vertical velocity component $v_y = a_y t = \frac{e E L}{m_e v}$ over a field-free drift distance $D$ to the screen:
  

$$
y_2 = v_y \cdot t_{\text{drift}} = \left(\frac{e E L}{m_e v}\right) \left(\frac{D}{v}\right) = \frac{e E L D}{m_e v^2}
$$

* Total screen deflection $y_{\text{total}} = y_1 + y_2$:
  

$$
y_{\text{total}} = \frac{e}{m_e} \left[\frac{E L^2}{2 v^2} + \frac{E L D}{v^2}\right] = \frac{e}{m_e} \frac{E L}{v^2} \left(\frac{L}{2} + D\right)
$$

* Substituting $v = E/B$:
  

$$
y_{\text{total}} = \frac{e}{m_e} \frac{L B^2}{E} \left(\frac{L}{2} + D\right)
$$

Rearranging to solve for the specific charge:

$$
\boxed{\frac{e}{m_e} = \frac{y_{\text{total}} \cdot E}{L B^2 \left(\frac{L}{2} + D\right)} = 1.758820 \times 10^{11} \text{ C}\cdot\text{kg}^{-1}}
$$

### 2.2 Millikan’s Oil Drop Experiment: Determination of Electronic Charge ($e$) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### Experimental Principle:
Microscopic oil droplets atomized into a chamber pick up electrical charges by colliding with air molecules ionized via an X-ray beam. The motion of an individual drop is tracked through a viewing microscope.

```
                  Atomizer
                     │
            ┌────────▼────────┐
    (+) ────│═══════ • ═══════│  Upper Plate (with pinhole)
            │        │        │
            │   X-rays ──►    │  Ionizing radiation
            │        │        │
            │      • Drop     │
    (-) ────│═════════════════│  Lower Plate
            └─────────────────┘
```

#### Step 1: Terminal Velocity Under Gravity Alone ($E = 0$)
The drop experiences three forces:
1. Downward gravitational force: $F_g = m g = \frac{4}{3} \pi r^3 \rho g$
2. Upward buoyant force of air: $F_b = m_{\text{air}} g = \frac{4}{3} \pi r^3 \sigma g$
3. Upward Stokes' drag force: $F_d = 6 \pi \eta r v_1$
*(where $r$ is drop radius, $\rho$ is oil density, $\sigma$ is air density, $\eta$ is air viscosity, and $v_1$ is downward terminal velocity).*

At terminal velocity:

$$
F_g = F_b + F_d
$$

$$
\frac{4}{3} \pi r^3 (\rho - \sigma) g = 6 \pi \eta r v_1
$$

$$
\boxed{r = \sqrt{\frac{9 \eta v_1}{2(\rho - \sigma)g}}}
$$

This allows exact computation of the radius $r$, and therefore the true mass $m = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \rho$ of the single droplet.

#### Step 2: Motion Under Opposing Electric Field ($E \neq 0$)
An electric field $E = \frac{V}{d}$ is applied vertically across plates separated by distance $d$. The droplet carries a negative charge $q = -ne$.
Adjusting $E$ such that the droplet remains stationary in space:

$$
F_E = F_g - F_b
$$

$$
q E = \frac{4}{3} \pi r^3 (\rho - \sigma) g
$$

$$
\boxed{q = \frac{\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 (\rho - \sigma)g}{E}}
$$

Alternatively, driving the droplet upwards with terminal velocity $v_2$:

$$
q E = \frac{4}{3} \pi r^3 (\rho - \sigma) g + 6 \pi \eta r v_2 = 6\pi\eta r (v_1 + v_2)
$$

$$
\boxed{q = \frac{6 \pi \eta r (v_1 + v_2)}{E}}
$$

#### Quantization of Charge:
Millikan evaluated thousands of drops and observed that the charge $q$ on any drop is an integral multiple of an elementary quantum:

$$
q = n \cdot e \quad \text{where } n = \pm 1, \pm 2, \pm 3, \dots
$$

$$
\boxed{e = 1.6022 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}}
$$

#### Deduction of Rest Mass of the Electron ($m_e$):
Combining Thomson's $e/m_e$ ratio with Millikan's charge $e$:

$$
m_e = \frac{e}{\left(\frac{e}{m_e}\right)} = \frac{1.602176 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}}{1.758820 \times 10^{11} \text{ C}\cdot\text{kg}^{-1}}
$$

$$
\boxed{m_e = 9.10938 \times 10^{-31} \text{ kg} = 9.10938 \times 10^{-28} \text{ g}}
$$

### 2.3 Thomson’s "Plum Pudding" Model of the Atom ⭐⭐⭐
Proposed by J.J. Thomson in 1898:
* **Postulate:** An atom possesses a spherical shape (radius $\sim 10^{-10} \text{ m}$) in which the positive charge is uniformly distributed throughout the sphere.
* **Electron Embedding:** Electrons are embedded into this uniform positive sphere in an arrangement that achieves the most stable electrostatic configuration (analogous to plums/raisins embedded in a pudding, or seeds distributed in a watermelon).
* **Success:** It accounted for the macroscopic electrical neutrality of matter.
* **Failure:** It could not explain the atomic line spectra of elements, nor could it explain the large-angle scattering of $\alpha$-particles observed in Rutherford's experiments.

***

### 2.4 Rutherford’s $\alpha$-Particle Scattering Experiment ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### Experimental Configuration:
* **Source:** Radioactive emitter ($^{214}\text{Bi}$ or Polonium) enclosed in a lead collimator emitting energetic $\alpha$-particles ($\text{He}^{2+}$ ions, $q = +2e, m \approx 4 \text{ u}$, $E_k \sim 5.5 \text{ MeV}$).
* **Target:** Ultra-thin gold foil ($^{197}_{79}\text{Au}$), thickness $\approx 100 \text{ nm}$ ($10^{-7} \text{ m}$, about $1000$ atoms thick).
* **Detector:** Rotatable microscope equipped with a fluorescent screen coated with Zinc Sulphide ($\text{ZnS}$). When an $\alpha$-particle strikes the screen, a microscopic flash of light (scintillation) is produced.

```
 Lead Block Source
    ┌──┐
    │Po├───►  α-beam
    └──┘         │
                 ▼  Thin Gold Foil (~100 nm)
                 │  │
                 │  ├───► Most pass undeflected (~99%)
                 │  │
                 │  └──────► Small angle deflections
                 │
                 └───────────────► Rare bounce back (> 90°)
                                    (1 in ~20,000)
```

#### Observations & Logical Deductions:
1. **Observation 1:** Most of the $\alpha$-particles ($\approx 99\%$) passed straight through the gold foil without any detectable deflection.
   * **Deduction:** The vast majority of the volume of the atom is **empty space**.
2. **Observation 2:** A small fraction of $\alpha$-particles (about $1$ in $8{,}000$) were deflected by small angles ($\theta > 1^\circ$).
   * **Deduction:** The positive charge of the atom is not spread throughout the atom (refuting Thomson's model). It must be concentrated in a very small localized region to exert an intense repulsive electrostatic force on the positively charged $\alpha$-particles.
3. **Observation 3:** Extremely few $\alpha$-particles ($\approx 1$ in $20{,}000$) were deflected by large angles ($>90^\circ$), and occasionally one bounced almost directly backward ($\theta \approx 180^\circ$).
   * **Deduction:** The positive charge and almost the entire mass of the atom are concentrated in an extraordinarily dense, tiny volume at the center, which Rutherford designated as the **nucleus**.

***

### 2.5 Rutherford’s Scattering Formula ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Under the assumptions of:
1. Pure Coulombic repulsion between the point-like positive charge of the nucleus ($+Ze$) and the $\alpha$-particle ($+2e$).
2. The massive gold nucleus ($M_{\text{Au}} \gg m_\alpha$) remains stationary throughout the collision.
3. Classical hyperbolic trajectories.

The number of $\alpha$-particles $N(\theta)$ scattered per unit area at a scattering angle $\theta$ is given by Rutherford’s Scattering Law:

$$
\boxed{N(\theta) \propto \frac{1}{\sin^4\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}}
$$

Or explicitly:

$$
N(\theta) = \frac{N_i \cdot n \cdot t \cdot Z^2 e^4}{(8\pi\varepsilon_0)^2 r^2 E_k^2 \sin^4\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}
$$

* $N_i$: Number of incident $\alpha$-particles
* $n$: Number of target atoms per unit volume
* $t$: Foil thickness
* $Z$: Atomic number of target nucleus
* $E_k$: Kinetic energy of incident $\alpha$-particles
* $r$: Distance from target to detector

***

### 2.6 Derivation: Distance of Closest Approach ($r_0$) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
 α-particle (m, v)                      Nucleus (+Ze)
     (+) ────────────────────────► (●)
     q = +2e                    r = r_0
 (At infinity: PE=0, KE=E_k)    (At r_0: v=0, KE=0, PE=U_max)
```

Consider an $\alpha$-particle of mass $m_\alpha$ and initial velocity $v$ launched directly head-on toward a target nucleus of atomic number $Z$ (impact parameter $b = 0$, scattering angle $\theta = 180^\circ$).

#### Step 1: Conservation of Mechanical Energy
At an infinite distance ($r \to \infty$):
* Electrostatic Potential Energy: $U_\infty = 0$
* Kinetic Energy: $K_\infty = \frac{1}{2} m_\alpha v^2 = E_k$
* Total Energy:
  

$$
E_{\text{total}} = K_\infty + U_\infty = \frac{1}{2} m_\alpha v^2
$$

As the $\alpha$-particle approaches the positively charged nucleus, it is decelerated by the electrostatic Coulomb repulsion until it momentarily comes to rest at the **distance of closest approach** ($r = r_0$).
At $r = r_0$:
* Velocity: $v = 0 \implies K_{r_0} = 0$
* Electrostatic Potential Energy:
  

$$
U_{r_0} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q_\alpha \cdot q_{\text{nucleus}}}{r_0}
$$

  Where $q_\alpha = +2e$ and $q_{\text{nucleus}} = +Ze$:
  

$$
U_{r_0} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{(2e)(Ze)}{r_0} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{2Ze^2}{r_0}
$$

#### Step 2: Equating Initial and Final Energies

$$
E_{\text{total}} = K_{r_0} + U_{r_0}
$$

$$
\frac{1}{2} m_\alpha v^2 = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{2Ze^2}{r_0}
$$

#### Step 3: Explicit Solution for $r_0$

$$
\boxed{r_0 = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{2 Z e^2}{\frac{1}{2}m_\alpha v^2} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{4 Z e^2}{m_\alpha v^2}}
$$

**SI Numerical Constant:**

$$
\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \approx 8.98755 \times 10^9 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2\cdot\text{C}^{-2} \approx 9 \times 10^9 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2\cdot\text{C}^{-2}
$$

**Physical Implication:**
Since the $\alpha$-particle cannot penetrate the nuclear boundary without experiencing non-Coulombic nuclear forces, $r_0$ provides an **upper bound for the nuclear radius**:

$$
R_{\text{nucleus}} \le r_0
$$

For gold ($Z = 79$) with $E_k \approx 5.5 \text{ MeV}$:

$$
r_0 \approx 4.13 \times 10^{-14} \text{ m} \approx 41.3 \text{ fm}
$$

Whereas the actual nuclear radius of gold is $R \approx 1.2 \times 10^{-15} \times (197)^{1/3} \approx 7 \text{ fm}$.

### 2.7 Impact Parameter ($b$) and Trajectory Relationship ⭐⭐⭐⭐
The **impact parameter** $b$ is defined as the perpendicular distance of the initial velocity vector of the $\alpha$-particle from the central line of the nucleus when the particle is far from the atom.

```
                        Impact parameter (b)
                ▲          │
                │          ▼
                │ ───────────────────────────────► Velocity vector
                │          │                     at infinity
   Gold Nucleus (●)        │
                │          │
                ▼          │
```

The mathematical relation derived by Rutherford is:

$$
\boxed{b = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{Z e^2 \cot\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}{\frac{1}{2} m_\alpha v^2} = \frac{r_0}{2} \cot\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}
$$

#### Limiting Cases:
1. **Head-on Collision ($b = 0$):**
   

$$
\cot\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) = 0 \implies \frac{\theta}{2} = 90^\circ \implies \theta = 180^\circ
$$

   The particle retraces its trajectory (rebounds directly backward).
2. **Large Impact Parameter ($b \gg 0$):**
   

$$
\cot\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) \to \infty \implies \frac{\theta}{2} \to 0^\circ \implies \theta \approx 0^\circ
$$

   The $\alpha$-particle experiences virtually no electrostatic deflection and travels undeflected.

### 2.8 Atomic Dimensions and Volume Scaling ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Rutherford's calculations proved:
* **Radius of Atom ($R_A$):** $\approx 10^{-10} \text{ m} = 100 \text{ pm} = 1 \text{ Å} = 0.1 \text{ nm}$
* **Radius of Nucleus ($R_N$):** $\approx 10^{-15} \text{ m}$ to $10^{-14} \text{ m} = 1 \text{ to } 10 \text{ fm}$

#### Ratio of Radii:

$$
\frac{R_A}{R_N} = \frac{10^{-10} \text{ m}}{10^{-15} \text{ m}} = 10^5
$$

The radius of the atom is approximately **$100{,}000$ times larger** than the radius of the nucleus.

#### Ratio of Volumes:
Assuming spherical geometry ($V = \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3$):

$$
\frac{V_{\text{atom}}}{V_{\text{nucleus}}} = \frac{\frac{4}{3}\pi R_A^3}{\frac{4}{3}\pi R_N^3} = \left(\frac{R_A}{R_N}\right)^3 = (10^5)^3 = 10^{15}
$$

$$
\boxed{V_{\text{nucleus}} = 10^{-15} \cdot V_{\text{atom}}}
$$

*Conclusion:* The nucleus occupies only $10^{-13}\%$ (one ten-trillionth of a percent) of the total volume of the atom.

#### Nuclear Density ($\rho_N$) Calculation:
Empirical formula for nuclear radius with mass number $A$:

$$
\boxed{R = R_0 A^{1/3}} \quad \text{where } R_0 \approx 1.2 \times 10^{-15} \text{ m to } 1.33 \times 10^{-15} \text{ m}
$$

Let average nucleon mass $m_0 \approx 1.67 \times 10^{-27} \text{ kg}$:

$$
\text{Nuclear Mass } M_N = A \cdot m_0
$$

$$
\text{Nuclear Volume } V_N = \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3 = \frac{4}{3}\pi (R_0 A^{1/3})^3 = \frac{4}{3}\pi R_0^3 A
$$

$$
\rho_N = \frac{M_N}{V_N} = \frac{A \cdot m_0}{\frac{4}{3}\pi R_0^3 A} = \frac{m_0}{\frac{4}{3}\pi R_0^3}
$$

Substituting $R_0 = 1.2 \times 10^{-15} \text{ m}$:

$$
\rho_N = \frac{1.67 \times 10^{-27}}{\frac{4}{3}\pi (1.2 \times 10^{-15})^3} \approx 2.3 \times 10^{17} \text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^{-3}
$$

$$
\boxed{\rho_N \approx 10^{14} \text{ g}\cdot\text{cm}^{-3} = 10^{17} \text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^{-3}}
$$

*Deduction:* Nuclear density is **constant for all elements**, completely independent of mass number $A$ or atomic number $Z$.

### 2.9 Planetary Analogy and Rutherford’s Classical Orbit ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Rutherford proposed that electrons orbit the dense nucleus in circular paths, akin to planets orbiting the Sun.
For a stable classical orbit of radius $r$ and tangential velocity $v$:

$$
\text{Electrostatic Attraction } = \text{Required Centripetal Force}
$$

$$
\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{Z e^2}{r^2} = \frac{m_e v^2}{r}
$$

$$
\boxed{v = \sqrt{\frac{Z e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 m_e r}}}
$$

#### Orbital Energy Components:
1. **Kinetic Energy ($K$):**
   

$$
K = \frac{1}{2} m_e v^2 = \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{Z e^2}{r}\right) = +\frac{Z e^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0 r}
$$

2. **Potential Energy ($U$):**
   

$$
U = -\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{Z e^2}{r}
$$

3. **Total Mechanical Energy ($E_{\text{total}}$):**
   

$$
E_{\text{total}} = K + U = \frac{Z e^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0 r} - \frac{Z e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r} = -\frac{Z e^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0 r}
$$

$$
\boxed{E_{\text{total}} = -K = \frac{1}{2} U}
$$

*(The negative sign signifies that the electron is electrostatically bound to the nucleus).*

### 2.10 Severe Drawbacks of Rutherford's Model ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### 1. Failure to Explain Atomic Stability (Maxwell’s Classical Electrodynamics)
* According to James Clerk Maxwell's electromagnetic theory, an accelerated charged particle must continuously emit energy in the form of electromagnetic radiation.
* An electron moving in a circular orbit around a nucleus experiences continuous centripetal acceleration ($a = \frac{v^2}{r}$).
* Consequently, the orbiting electron must continuously radiate electromagnetic energy.
* As energy decreases ($E_{\text{total}}$ becomes more negative), the orbital radius $r$ must continuously shrink:
  

$$
r \propto -\frac{1}{E_{\text{total}}}
$$

* The electron would follow an inward spiral path and collapse into the nucleus.

```
        Spiral Collapse:
            .-""""-.
          .'   ..   '.
         /   .'  '.   \
        ;   /  (●) \   ;   (● = Nucleus)
         \   '.  .'   /
          '.   ""   .'
            '-....-'
```

* **Calculation of Collapse Time:** Using the Larmor formula for radiated power ($P = \frac{e^2 a^2}{6\pi\varepsilon_0 c^3}$), the time required for an electron to spiral into the nucleus from $r_0 = 10^{-10} \text{ m}$ is:
  

$$
\boxed{t_{\text{collapse}} \approx 10^{-8} \text{ s}}
$$

* *Contradiction:* Real atoms are remarkably stable and do not collapse.

#### 2. Inability to Explain Discontinuous Atomic Line Spectra
* If the electron radiated energy continuously as its orbital radius steadily contracted, the frequency ($\nu = \frac{v}{2\pi r}$) of the emitted radiation would change continuously.
* Therefore, atoms should emit a **continuous spectrum** containing all wavelengths.
* *Contradiction:* Experimental atomic spectra of hydrogen and other atoms consist of **sharp, discrete line spectra** of precise, quantized frequencies.

#### 3. Unspecified Electronic Structure
Rutherford's model said nothing about how electrons are arranged around the nucleus or the energies associated with their positions.

### Problem 1: Specific Charge of Different Species (Conceptual & Numerical)
**Question:** Calculate the ratio of the specific charge ($e/m$) of an $\alpha$-particle to that of a proton.

**Solution:**
* **Step 1: Identify charge and mass of proton ($p^+$):**
  

$$
q_p = +1 e, \quad m_p = 1 \text{ u} \implies \left(\frac{q}{m}\right)_p = \frac{e}{m_p}
$$

* **Step 2: Identify charge and mass of $\alpha$-particle ($^4_2\text{He}^{2+}$):**
  

$$
q_\alpha = +2 e, \quad m_\alpha \approx 4 m_p
$$

  

$$
\left(\frac{q}{m}\right)_\alpha = \frac{2 e}{4 m_p} = \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{e}{m_p}\right)
$$

* **Step 3: Compute the ratio:**
  

$$
\text{Ratio} = \frac{(q/m)_\alpha}{(q/m)_p} = \frac{\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{e}{m_p}\right)}{\left(\frac{e}{m_p}\right)} = \frac{1}{2} = 1:2
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac{(q/m)_\alpha}{(q/m)_p} = 0.5}
$$

### Problem 2: Rutherford's Angular Scattering Law (NCERT Exemplar Variant)
**Question:** In a Rutherford scattering experiment, the number of $\alpha$-particles scattered at an angle of $60^\circ$ is $5600 \text{ particles per minute}$. Calculate the number of $\alpha$-particles scattered per minute at an angle of $90^\circ$ and at $120^\circ$ under identical conditions.

**Solution:**
From Rutherford’s formula:

$$
N(\theta) \propto \frac{1}{\sin^4\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)} \implies N(\theta) \cdot \sin^4\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) = \text{constant } k
$$

* **Case 1: At $\theta_1 = 60^\circ$:**
  

$$
\frac{\theta_1}{2} = 30^\circ \implies \sin(30^\circ) = \frac{1}{2}
$$

  

$$
N(60^\circ) = \frac{k}{\sin^4(30^\circ)} = \frac{k}{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^4} = 16 k
$$

  

$$
16 k = 5600 \implies k = \frac{5600}{16} = 350
$$

* **Case 2: At $\theta_2 = 90^\circ$:**
  

$$
\frac{\theta_2}{2} = 45^\circ \implies \sin(45^\circ) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
$$

  

$$
\sin^4(45^\circ) = \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^4 = \frac{1}{4}
$$

  

$$
N(90^\circ) = \frac{k}{\sin^4(45^\circ)} = \frac{350}{\frac{1}{4}} = 350 \times 4 = 1400 \text{ particles}\cdot\text{min}^{-1}
$$

* **Case 3: At $\theta_3 = 120^\circ$:**
  

$$
\frac{\theta_3}{2} = 60^\circ \implies \sin(60^\circ) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
$$

  

$$
\sin^4(60^\circ) = \left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^4 = \frac{9}{16}
$$

  

$$
N(120^\circ) = \frac{k}{\sin^4(60^\circ)} = \frac{350}{\frac{9}{16}} = \frac{350 \times 16}{9} = \frac{5600}{9} \approx 622.2 \text{ particles}\cdot\text{min}^{-1}
$$

$$
\boxed{N(90^\circ) = 1400 \text{ min}^{-1}, \quad N(120^\circ) \approx 622 \text{ min}^{-1}}
$$

### Problem 3: Quantitative Closest Approach Calculation
**Question:** An $\alpha$-particle with kinetic energy $E_k = 7.7 \text{ MeV}$ is projected directly towards a gold nucleus ($Z = 79$). Calculate the distance of closest approach ($r_0$) in femtometers ($\text{fm}$). (Take $\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} = 9.0 \times 10^9 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2\cdot\text{C}^{-2}$, $e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$).

**Solution:**
* **Step 1: Convert kinetic energy into SI units (Joules):**
  

$$
1 \text{ eV} = 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J}
$$

  

$$
E_k = 7.7 \text{ MeV} = 7.7 \times 10^6 \text{ eV} = 7.7 \times 10^6 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J} = 1.232 \times 10^{-12} \text{ J}
$$

* **Step 2: Apply the closest approach formula:**
  

$$
r_0 = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{2 Z e^2}{E_k}
$$

  Substitute values:
  

$$
r_0 = \frac{(9.0 \times 10^9) \times 2 \times 79 \times (1.6 \times 10^{-19})^2}{1.232 \times 10^{-12}}
$$

  

$$
r_0 = \frac{9.0 \times 10^9 \times 158 \times 2.56 \times 10^{-38}}{1.232 \times 10^{-12}}
$$

  

$$
r_0 = \frac{3.64032 \times 10^{-26}}{1.232 \times 10^{-12}} = 2.955 \times 10^{-14} \text{ m}
$$

* **Step 3: Convert to femtometers ($1 \text{ fm} = 10^{-15} \text{ m}$):**
  

$$
r_0 = 29.55 \times 10^{-15} \text{ m} = 29.55 \text{ fm}
$$

$$
\boxed{r_0 \approx 29.55 \text{ fm}}
$$

### Problem 4: Nuclear Radius Scaling
**Question:** If the radius of the $^{27}_{13}\text{Al}$ nucleus is measured to be $3.6 \text{ fm}$, calculate the estimated nuclear radius of $^{125}_{52}\text{Te}$.

**Solution:**
* Using the nuclear scaling law:
  

$$
R = R_0 A^{1/3}
$$

* Taking the ratio for two nuclei:
  

$$
\frac{R(\text{Te})}{R(\text{Al})} = \left(\frac{A_{\text{Te}}}{A_{\text{Al}}}\right)^{1/3}
$$

  Given $A_{\text{Te}} = 125$ and $A_{\text{Al}} = 27$:
  

$$
\frac{R(\text{Te})}{3.6 \text{ fm}} = \left(\frac{125}{27}\right)^{1/3} = \frac{5}{3}
$$

  

$$
R(\text{Te}) = 3.6 \times \frac{5}{3} = 1.2 \times 5 = 6.0 \text{ fm}
$$

$$
\boxed{R(\text{Te}) = 6.0 \text{ fm} = 6.0 \times 10^{-15} \text{ m}}
$$

### Problem 5: Millikan's Oil Drop Charge Deduction
**Question:** In an oil drop experiment, the static electric charges measured on five individual oil droplets were:
* Drop 1: $-3.204 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$
* Drop 2: $-6.408 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$
* Drop 3: $-4.806 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$
* Drop 4: $-8.010 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$
* Drop 5: $-1.602 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$
Deduce the elementary unit of charge ($e$).

**Solution:**
* Express each charge as a multiple of the smallest charge ($q_5 = 1.602 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$):
  

$$
\frac{q_1}{q_5} = \frac{-3.204 \times 10^{-19}}{-1.602 \times 10^{-19}} = 2
$$

  

$$
\frac{q_2}{q_5} = \frac{-6.408 \times 10^{-19}}{-1.602 \times 10^{-19}} = 4
$$

  

$$
\frac{q_3}{q_5} = \frac{-4.806 \times 10^{-19}}{-1.602 \times 10^{-19}} = 3
$$

  

$$
\frac{q_4}{q_5} = \frac{-8.010 \times 10^{-19}}{-1.602 \times 10^{-19}} = 5
$$

  

$$
\frac{q_5}{q_5} = 1
$$

* Because all charges are exact integer multiples ($n = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5$) of $1.602 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$, by the principle of charge quantization ($q = ne$):

$$
\boxed{e = 1.602 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}}
$$

# PART 4: EXAMINER TRAPS & COMMON MISTAKES

### Trap 1: The "Origin of Anode Rays" Trap
* **Student Error:** Students write that *"anode rays are emitted directly from the surface of the anode electrode"*, paralleling cathode rays.
* **Examiner Penalty:** Zero marks.
* **Rigorous NCERT Fact:** Anode rays are **never emitted from the anode metal**. High-speed electrons traveling towards the perforated cathode collide with neutral gas atoms/molecules in the tube and knock out electrons, ionizing them:
  

$$
\text{M}(g) + e^- \longrightarrow \text{M}^+(g) + 2e^-
$$

  The resulting positive ions $\text{M}^+$ are repelled by the positive anode and accelerate towards the cathode. Hence, they originate **in the intervening gas space**, NOT from the anode metal.

### Trap 2: Neglecting the Half-Angle in Rutherford's Law
* **Student Error:** Writing $N(\theta) \propto \frac{1}{\sin^4(\theta)}$ instead of $\frac{1}{\sin^4(\theta/2)}$.
* **Numerical Consequence:** For $\theta = 60^\circ$, evaluating $\sin^4(60^\circ) = (\sqrt{3}/2)^4 = 9/16 \approx 0.5625$, whereas the correct term is $\sin^4(30^\circ) = (1/2)^4 = 1/16 = 0.0625$. This creates a **$9\times$ error** in the calculation.

### Trap 3: Closest Approach Nuclear Charge Misidentification
* **Student Error:** Using $Ze$ instead of $2Ze^2$ in the potential energy equation:
  

$$
U = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{Ze^2}{r_0} \quad \text{[WRONG]}
$$

* **Correction:** An $\alpha$-particle is a helium nucleus ($\text{He}^{2+}$) and carries a charge of $q_1 = +2e$, while the target nucleus carries $q_2 = +Ze$. Thus:
  

$$
q_1 \cdot q_2 = (2e)(Ze) = 2Ze^2
$$

  Omitting the factor of $2$ results in an $r_0$ value that is off by exactly half.

### Trap 4: Confusing Atomic Number $Z$ with Mass Number $A$ in Nuclear Radii
* **Student Error:** Using $R = R_0 Z^{1/3}$ instead of $R = R_0 A^{1/3}$.
* **Correction:** Nuclear radius depends on the total number of nucleons (mass number $A$), not just protons (atomic number $Z$).

### Trap 5: Misinterpreting Sign of Potential vs. Kinetic vs. Total Energy
* **Student Error:** Writing $E_{\text{total}} = K + U$ as positive, or treating potential energy as positive.
* **Correction:** The electron-nuclear interaction is attractive, so:
  * Kinetic Energy: $K > 0$
  * Potential Energy: $U < 0$ ($U = 2 E_{\text{total}}$)
  * Total Energy: $E_{\text{total}} < 0$ ($E_{\text{total}} = -K$)
  A negative total energy indicates a bound state.
