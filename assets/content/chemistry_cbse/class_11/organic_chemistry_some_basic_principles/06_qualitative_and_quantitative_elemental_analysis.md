# PART 1: QUALITATIVE ANALYSIS (DETECTION OF ELEMENTS)

Organic compounds primarily contain carbon and hydrogen. In addition, elements such as nitrogen, sulfur, halogens (chlorine, bromine, iodine), and phosphorus may be present. Qualitative analysis establishes the identity of these constituent elements through characteristic chemical transformations.

***

### 1.1 Detection of Carbon and Hydrogen (Copper(II) Oxide Test) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Principle and Molecular Interpretation
The organic substance is mixed thoroughly with dry copper(II) oxide ($\text{CuO}$) and heated strongly in a hard-glass test tube. 
- **Carbon** is oxidized to carbon dioxide gas ($\text{CO}_2$).
- **Hydrogen** is oxidized to water vapor ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$).

Carbon dioxide turns lime water milky due to the precipitation of insoluble calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$). Excess $\text{CO}_2$ turns the solution clear again due to soluble calcium hydrogen carbonate formation.
Water vapors condense on the cooler parts of the test tube and turn anhydrous copper(II) sulfate ($\text{CuSO}_4$, white) into hydrated copper(II) sulfate ($\text{CuSO}_4 \cdot 5\text{H}_2\text{O}$, deep blue).

#### B. Chemical Reactions

**Oxidation of the Organic Compound:**

$$
\text{C} + 2\text{CuO} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{CO}_2 \uparrow + 2\text{Cu}
$$

$$
2\text{H} + \text{CuO} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{H}_2\text{O} \uparrow + \text{Cu}
$$

**Confirmation of Carbon Dioxide:**

$$
\text{CO}_2 + \text{Ca(OH)}_2 \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Insoluble (Milky)}}{\text{CaCO}_3 \downarrow} + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

$$
\text{CaCO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2 \text{ (excess)} \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Soluble (Clear)}}{\text{Ca(HCO}_3)_2}
$$

**Confirmation of Hydrogen:**

$$
\underset{\text{White / Anhydrous}}{\text{CuSO}_4} + 5\text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Deep Blue / Hydrated}}{\text{CuSO}_4 \cdot 5\text{H}_2\text{O}}
$$

### 1.2 Preparation of Lassaigne's Extract (Sodium Fusion Extract - SFE) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Rationale & Need for Fusion
Elements like nitrogen, sulfur, and halogens are covalently bound within organic molecules. Covalent bonds do not ionize in aqueous media; therefore, standard inorganic ionic precipitations and colorimetric tests cannot proceed directly. 

Fusing the organic substrate with molten metallic sodium converts these covalently bound non-metals into water-soluble ionic sodium salts:
- Carbon and Nitrogen $\longrightarrow$ Sodium cyanide ($\text{NaCN}$)
- Sulfur $\longrightarrow$ Sodium sulfide ($\text{Na}_2\text{S}$)
- Halogens ($\text{X} = \text{Cl, Br, I}$) $\longrightarrow$ Sodium halide ($\text{NaX}$)
- Nitrogen and Sulfur together $\longrightarrow$ Sodium thiocyanate ($\text{NaSCN}$)

#### B. Chemical Reactions during Fusion

$$
\text{Na} + \text{C} + \text{N} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{NaCN}
$$

$$
2\text{Na} + \text{S} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Na}_2\text{S}
$$

$$
\text{Na} + \text{X} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{NaX} \quad (\text{X} = \text{Cl, Br, I})
$$

$$
\text{Na} + \text{C} + \text{N} + \text{S} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{NaSCN} \quad (\text{In case of insufficient Na})
$$

> **Crucial Experimental Condition (Excess Sodium):**  
> If sodium is taken in excess during fusion of a compound containing both $\text{N}$ and $\text{S}$, the thiocyanate decomposes into cyanide and sulfide:
> 

$$
\text{NaSCN} + 2\text{Na} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{NaCN} + \text{Na}_2\text{S}
$$

### 1.3 Detection of Nitrogen ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Principle & Step-by-Step Procedure
1. Take $1\text{–}2\text{ mL}$ of Lassaigne’s extract (SFE).
2. Add freshly prepared iron(II) sulfate ($\text{FeSO}_4$) solution.
3. Heat the mixture gently. During this heating under alkaline conditions (from residual $\text{Na}$), $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ reacts with $\text{CN}^-$ to yield hexacyanidoferrate(II) ions $[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]^{4-}$.
4. A few drops of concentrated sulfuric acid ($\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$) are added. Acidification oxidizes some $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ to $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ and prevents precipitation of $\text{Fe(OH)}_2$.
5. The generated $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ reacts with $[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]^{4-}$ to yield a **Prussian blue** precipitate/coloration of iron(III) hexacyanidoferrate(II).

#### B. Chemical Equations

$$
\text{FeSO}_4 + 2\text{NaOH} \longrightarrow \text{Fe(OH)}_2 \downarrow + \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4
$$

$$
\text{Fe(OH)}_2 + 2\text{NaCN} \longrightarrow \text{Fe(CN)}_2 + 2\text{NaOH}
$$

$$
\text{Fe(CN)}_2 + 4\text{NaCN} \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Sodium hexacyanidoferrate(II)}}{\text{Na}_4[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]}
$$

$$
3\text{Na}_4[\text{Fe(CN)}_6] + 4\text{Fe}^{3+} \longrightarrow \underset{\begin{subarray}{c} \text{Prussian Blue} \\ \text{Iron(III) hexacyanidoferrate(II)} \end{subarray}}{\text{Fe}_4[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]_3 \cdot x\text{H}_2\text{O}} + 12\text{Na}^+
$$

#### C. Case of Simultaneous Presence of Nitrogen and Sulfur
If insufficient sodium was used, sodium thiocyanate ($\text{NaSCN}$) forms. Upon addition of $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ ions (without conversion to hexacyanidoferrate), a **blood-red** coloration of iron(III) thiocyanate is formed:

$$
\text{Fe}^{3+} + \text{SCN}^- \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Blood-Red Coloration}}{[\text{Fe(SCN)}]^{2+}} \quad \text{or} \quad \text{Fe(SCN)}_3
$$

***

### 1.4 Detection of Sulfur ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Two independent tests confirm sulfur in the SFE:

#### A. Sodium Nitroprusside Test
A few drops of sodium nitroprusside solution ($\text{Na}_2[\text{Fe(CN)}_5\text{NO}]$) are added to an aliquot of SFE. An intense **deep violet / purple** color confirms sulfur.

$$
\text{Na}_2\text{S} + \underset{\text{Sodium nitroprusside}}{\text{Na}_2[\text{Fe(CN)}_5\text{NO}]} \longrightarrow \underset{\begin{subarray}{c} \text{Sodium thionitroprusside} \\ \text{(Deep Violet / Purple)} \end{subarray}}{\text{Na}_4[\text{Fe(CN)}_5\text{NOS}]}
$$

#### B. Lead Acetate Test
An aliquot of SFE is acidified with dilute acetic acid ($\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$), followed by addition of lead(II) acetate solution. A **black precipitate** of lead(II) sulfide confirms sulfur.

$$
\text{Na}_2\text{S} + (\text{CH}_3\text{COO})_2\text{Pb} \xrightarrow{\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}} \underset{\text{Black Precipitate}}{\text{PbS} \downarrow} + 2\text{CH}_3\text{COONa}
$$

> **Examiner Trap:** Acidification must be performed using **dilute acetic acid**, NOT dilute $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ or $\text{HCl}$. Sulfuric acid precipitates insoluble white lead sulfate ($\text{PbSO}_4 \downarrow$), while hydrochloric acid precipitates white lead chloride ($\text{PbCl}_2 \downarrow$), obscuring the black $\text{PbS}$ observation.

***

### 1.5 Detection of Halogens (Silver Nitrate Test & Layer Test) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Silver Nitrate Test ($\text{AgNO}_3$)
1. **Boiling with Concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$:**  
   Before adding silver nitrate, the SFE **must be boiled with concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$** to decompose any $\text{NaCN}$ or $\text{Na}_2\text{S}$ present. 
   

$$
\text{NaCN} + \text{HNO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{NaNO}_3 + \text{HCN} \uparrow
$$

   

$$
\text{Na}_2\text{S} + 2\text{HNO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\text{NaNO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{S} \uparrow
$$

   *If not decomposed, cyanide and sulfide interfere by giving false precipitates:*
   

$$
\text{NaCN} + \text{AgNO}_3 \longrightarrow \underset{\text{White Precipitate}}{\text{AgCN} \downarrow} + \text{NaNO}_3
$$

   

$$
\text{Na}_2\text{S} + 2\text{AgNO}_3 \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Black Precipitate}}{\text{Ag}_2\text{S} \downarrow} + 2\text{NaNO}_3
$$

2. **Precipitation with $\text{AgNO}_3$:**
   

$$
\text{NaX} + \text{AgNO}_3 \longrightarrow \text{AgX} \downarrow + \text{NaNO}_3
$$

| Halogen | Observation ($\text{AgX}$) | Solubility in Ammonium Hydroxide ($\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Chlorine ($\text{Cl}$)** | **Curdy white** precipitate ($\text{AgCl}$) | Readily completely soluble, forming $[\text{Ag(NH}_3)_2]\text{Cl}$ |
| **Bromine ($\text{Br}$)** | **Pale yellow** precipitate ($\text{AgBr}$) | Sparingly / partially soluble in dilute $\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$ |
| **Iodine ($\text{I}$)** | **Bright yellow** precipitate ($\text{AgI}$) | Completely insoluble in $\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$ |

**Complexation Reaction for $\text{AgCl}$:**

$$
\text{AgCl} + 2\text{NH}_4\text{OH} \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Diamminesilver(I) chloride (Soluble)}}{[\text{Ag(NH}_3)_2]\text{Cl}} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

#### B. Organic Layer Test (for $\text{Br}^-$ and $\text{I}^-$)
When both halogens or trace amounts are present:
1. Acidify SFE with dilute $\text{HNO}_3$ or dilute $\text{HCl}$.
2. Add a small volume of carbon tetrachloride ($\text{CCl}_4$) or chloroform ($\text{CHCl}_3$).
3. Add freshly prepared chlorine water dropwise with vigorous shaking.
4. Chlorine oxidizes bromide to bromine and iodide to iodine:
   

$$
2\text{NaBr} + \text{Cl}_2 \longrightarrow 2\text{NaCl} + \text{Br}_2
$$

   

$$
2\text{NaI} + \text{Cl}_2 \longrightarrow 2\text{NaCl} + \text{I}_2
$$

5. $\text{Br}_2$ dissolves in the organic layer imparting an **orange/reddish-brown** color.
6. $\text{I}_2$ dissolves in the organic layer imparting a **violet/purple** color.

***

### 1.6 Detection of Phosphorus ⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Principle & Procedure
1. The organic compound is fused with an oxidizing agent like sodium peroxide ($\text{Na}_2\text{O}_2$). Phosphorus is oxidized to sodium phosphate:
   

$$
\text{P} \xrightarrow{\text{Na}_2\text{O}_2, \Delta} \text{Na}_3\text{PO}_4
$$

2. The fused mass is extracted with boiling water, filtered, and the filtrate is acidified with concentrated nitric acid ($\text{HNO}_3$).
3. Ammonium molybdate, $(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{MoO}_4$, is added in excess and the mixture warmed gently.
4. Formation of a **canary-yellow precipitate** of ammonium phosphomolybdate confirms phosphorus.

#### B. Chemical Reactions

$$
\text{Na}_3\text{PO}_4 + 3\text{HNO}_3 \longrightarrow \text{H}_3\text{PO}_4 + 3\text{NaNO}_3
$$

$$
\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4 + 12(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{MoO}_4 + 21\text{HNO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \underset{\begin{subarray}{c} \text{Ammonium phosphomolybdate} \\ \text{(Canary-Yellow Precipitate)} \end{subarray}}{(\text{NH}_4)_3[\text{P(Mo}_{3}\text{O}_{10})_4] \downarrow} + 21\text{NH}_4\text{NO}_3 + 12\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

*(Commonly written as $(\text{NH}_4)_3\text{PO}_4 \cdot 12\text{MoO}_3$)*

### 2.1 Carbon and Hydrogen: Liebig’s Combustion Method ⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Experimental Apparatus & Assembly
- A known mass ($m\text{ g}$) of organic compound is placed in a platinum boat and burned in a combustion tube containing granulated $\text{CuO}$ in an atmosphere of pure, dry dioxygen ($\text{O}_2$).
- The combustion products ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and $\text{CO}_2$) pass through a pre-weighed U-tube containing **anhydrous calcium chloride** ($\text{CaCl}_2$) to absorb water.
- Next, gases pass through a pre-weighed U-tube containing concentrated **potassium hydroxide ($\text{KOH}$) solution** (or ascarite/soda-lime) to absorb carbon dioxide.

#### B. Fundamental Derivations

Let:
- Mass of organic compound taken $= m\text{ g}$
- Gain in mass of anhydrous $\text{CaCl}_2$ tube $= m_1\text{ g}$ (mass of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ produced)
- Gain in mass of $\text{KOH}$ tube $= m_2\text{ g}$ (mass of $\text{CO}_2$ produced)

**1. Estimation of Carbon:**

$$
\text{Molar mass of CO}_2 = 44.01\text{ g mol}^{-1} \quad (\text{contains } 12.011\text{ g of Carbon})
$$

$$
44\text{ g of CO}_2 \equiv 12\text{ g of C}
$$

$$
\text{Mass of Carbon in sample} = \frac{12}{44} \times m_2\text{ g}
$$

$$
\boxed{\% \text{ C} = \frac{12}{44} \times \frac{m_2}{m} \times 100\%}
$$

**2. Estimation of Hydrogen:**

$$
\text{Molar mass of H}_2\text{O} = 18.015\text{ g mol}^{-1} \quad (\text{contains } 2.016\text{ g of Hydrogen})
$$

$$
18\text{ g of H}_2\text{O} \equiv 2\text{ g of H}
$$

$$
\text{Mass of Hydrogen in sample} = \frac{2}{18} \times m_1\text{ g}
$$

$$
\boxed{\% \text{ H} = \frac{2}{18} \times \frac{m_1}{m} \times 100\%}
$$

### 2.2 Estimation of Nitrogen

Two standard analytical routes exist for nitrogen estimation:

***

#### Method I: Dumas Method ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

##### A. Principle & Reactions
A known mass ($m\text{ g}$) of the nitrogenous organic compound is mixed with cupric oxide ($\text{CuO}$) and heated in an atmosphere of carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) generated from heating sodium hydrogen carbonate ($\text{NaHCO}_3$) or dry ice.
- Carbon converts to $\text{CO}_2$, hydrogen to $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, and nitrogen to free dinitrogen ($\text{N}_2$) gas.
- Any traces of nitrogen oxides ($\text{NO}_x$) produced are reduced back to elementary $\text{N}_2$ by passing over a **reduced copper gauze** positioned at the delivery end of the tube:

$$
\text{C}_x\text{H}_y\text{N}_z + (2x + y/2)\text{CuO} \longrightarrow x\text{CO}_2 + \frac{y}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O} + \frac{z}{2}\text{N}_2 + (2x + y/2)\text{Cu}
$$

$$
\text{NO}_x + 2x\text{Cu} \longrightarrow x\text{Cu}_2\text{O} + \frac{1}{2}\text{N}_2
$$

The gaseous mixture is collected over concentrated aqueous $\text{KOH}$ ($40\text{–}50\%$) in a graduated nitrometer. 
- $\text{KOH}$ absorbs $\text{CO}_2$ completely: $2\text{KOH} + \text{CO}_2 \longrightarrow \text{K}_2\text{CO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$.
- Water vapor condenses.
- Pure $\text{N}_2$ gas collects in the upper calibrated tube.

##### B. Step-by-Step Derivation
Let:
- Mass of organic compound $= m\text{ g}$
- Volume of $\text{N}_2$ gas collected in nitrometer $= V_1\text{ mL}$ at experimental temperature $T_1\text{ K}$ and atmospheric pressure $P\text{ mmHg}$.
- Aqueous tension (vapor pressure of water) at $T_1\text{ K} = p\text{ mmHg}$.

**Step 1: Determine pressure of dry $\text{N}_2$ gas ($P_1$):**

$$
P_1 = P - p\text{ mmHg}
$$

**Step 2: Convert volume to Standard Temperature and Pressure ($\text{STP}: P_0 = 760\text{ mmHg}, T_0 = 273.15\text{ K}$):**
Using the Ideal Gas Equation:

$$
\frac{P_1 V_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_0 V_0}{T_0} \implies V_0 = \frac{P_1 \times V_1 \times 273}{760 \times T_1}\text{ mL}
$$

**Step 3: Convert STP volume to mass:**

$$
22400\text{ mL of dry N}_2 \text{ at STP has a mass of } 28\text{ g}
$$

$$
\text{Mass of } V_0\text{ mL of N}_2 = \frac{28}{22400} \times V_0\text{ g} = \frac{V_0}{800}\text{ g}
$$

**Step 4: Percentage of Nitrogen:**

$$
\boxed{\% \text{ N} = \frac{28 \times V_0}{22400 \times m} \times 100\% = \frac{V_0}{8 \times m}\%}
$$

***

#### Method II: Kjeldahl's Method ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

##### A. Principle & Chemical Reactions
A known mass ($m\text{ g}$) of organic compound is digested with concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ in a long-necked Kjeldahl flask:
- Anhydrous potassium sulfate ($\text{K}_2\text{SO}_4$) is added to **elevate the boiling point** of sulfuric acid.
- Copper(II) sulfate ($\text{CuSO}_4$) or mercury/selenium powder acts as a **catalytic agent** to accelerate digestion.
- Nitrogen converts quantitatively into **ammonium sulfate** ($(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4$).

**1. Digestion:**

$$
\text{Organic Compound (N)} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \xrightarrow{\text{CuSO}_4, \text{K}_2\text{SO}_4, \Delta} (\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4 + \text{CO}_2 \uparrow + \text{SO}_2 \uparrow + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

**2. Distillation:**
The clarified digest is transferred to a distillation flask, made strongly alkaline with excess sodium hydroxide ($\text{NaOH}$), and steam distilled:

$$
(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4 + 2\text{NaOH} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + 2\text{NH}_3 \uparrow
$$

**3. Absorption & Titration (Back-titration vs Direct Acidimetry):**
The liberated ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$) is quantitatively absorbed in a known volume ($V\text{ mL}$) of standard standard sulfuric acid (molarity $M$ or normality $N_1$):

$$
2\text{NH}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \longrightarrow (\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4
$$

The unreacted residual acid is back-titrated against standard $\text{NaOH}$ (normality $N_2$).

##### B. Mathematical Derivation
Let:
- Mass of organic compound $= m\text{ g}$
- Initial volume of standard acid ($\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$) taken $= V\text{ mL}$ of normality $N_1$
- Volume of standard alkali ($\text{NaOH}$) of normality $N_2$ required for neutralizing unused excess acid $= V_1\text{ mL}$

Using normality equivalence:

$$
\text{Milliequivalents (meq) of unreacted excess acid} = N_2 \times V_1
$$

$$
\text{Total meq of acid taken initially} = N_1 \times V
$$

$$
\text{meq of acid neutralized solely by liberated NH}_3 = (N_1 V - N_2 V_1)
$$

Since:

$$
1000\text{ mL of } 1\text{ N acid} \equiv 1000\text{ meq of acid} \equiv 1\text{ mol of NH}_3 \equiv 14\text{ g of Nitrogen}
$$

$$
1\text{ meq of acid} \equiv \frac{14}{1000}\text{ g of N} = 0.014\text{ g of N}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\text{Mass of Nitrogen in sample} = (N_1 V - N_2 V_1) \times 0.014\text{ g}
$$

$$
\% \text{ N} = \frac{(N_1 V - N_2 V_1) \times 0.014}{m} \times 100\%
$$

$$
\boxed{\% \text{ N} = \frac{1.4 \times [N_1 V - N_2 V_1]}{m} = \frac{1.4 \times (\text{Net meq of acid consumed by NH}_3)}{m}}
$$

If molarity ($M$) is given for diprotic $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$:  
Remember: Normality $= \text{Molarity} \times \text{Basicity} = 2 \times M$.

##### C. Structural Limitations of Kjeldahl's Method ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Kjeldahl's method is **NOT** applicable to:
1. **Nitro compounds ($-\text{NO}_2$):** (e.g., nitrobenzene)
2. **Azo compounds ($-\text{N}=\text{N}-$):** (e.g., azobenzene)
3. **Compounds with nitrogen in a heterocyclic ring:** (e.g., pyridine, quinoline, pyrrole)

**Mechanistic Reason:** Under standard acidic digestion conditions, nitrogen in these structural environments does not convert quantitatively into ammonium sulfate; instead, it escapes as free nitrogen oxides or elemental nitrogen gas.

### 2.3 Estimation of Halogens: Carius Method ⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Principle & Chemical Reactions
A known mass ($m\text{ g}$) of the halogenated organic compound is heated with concentrated nitric acid ($\text{HNO}_3$) in the presence of solid silver nitrate ($\text{AgNO}_3$) in a sealed, thick-walled glass tube (**Carius tube**) inside an iron jacket furnace at $450\text{–}550\text{ K}$.
- Carbon and hydrogen are oxidized to $\text{CO}_2$ and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$.
- The halogen is converted into the corresponding silver halide ($\text{AgX}$):

$$
\text{Organic Halogen} + \text{HNO}_3 + \text{AgNO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{AgX} \downarrow + \text{CO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

The precipitate of $\text{AgX}$ is separated by filtration through a Gooch crucible, washed thoroughly with dilute nitric acid followed by water, dried to constant mass, and weighed.

#### B. Derivations for $\% \text{ Cl}$, $\% \text{ Br}$, $\% \text{ I}$

Let:
- Mass of organic compound $= m\text{ g}$
- Mass of silver halide ($\text{AgX}$) obtained $= m_1\text{ g}$

$$
\text{Molar Masses: } \text{Ag} = 108\text{ g/mol}, \text{Cl} = 35.5\text{ g/mol}, \text{Br} = 80\text{ g/mol}, \text{I} = 127\text{ g/mol}
$$

$$
\text{AgCl} = 143.5\text{ g/mol}, \quad \text{AgBr} = 188\text{ g/mol}, \quad \text{AgI} = 235\text{ g/mol}
$$

**1. Chlorine:**

$$
\boxed{\% \text{ Cl} = \frac{35.5}{143.5} \times \frac{m_1}{m} \times 100\%}
$$

**2. Bromine:**

$$
\boxed{\% \text{ Br} = \frac{80}{188} \times \frac{m_1}{m} \times 100\%}
$$

**3. Iodine:**

$$
\boxed{\% \text{ I} = \frac{127}{235} \times \frac{m_1}{m} \times 100\%}
$$

***

### 2.4 Estimation of Sulfur: Carius Method ⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Principle & Reactions
A known mass ($m\text{ g}$) of sulfur-containing organic compound is heated in a Carius tube with fuming nitric acid ($\text{HNO}_3$) or sodium peroxide ($\text{Na}_2\text{O}_2$).
- Sulfur is oxidized completely to sulfuric acid:

$$
\text{S} + 6\text{HNO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 + 6\text{NO}_2 \uparrow + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

- Excess barium chloride solution ($\text{BaCl}_2$) is added to precipitate insoluble barium sulfate ($\text{BaSO}_4$):

$$
\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 + \text{BaCl}_2 \longrightarrow \underset{\text{White Precipitate}}{\text{BaSO}_4 \downarrow} + 2\text{HCl}
$$

- The precipitate is filtered, washed, dried, ignited, and weighed.

#### B. Derivation
Let:
- Mass of organic compound taken $= m\text{ g}$
- Mass of $\text{BaSO}_4$ precipitate $= m_1\text{ g}$

$$
\text{Molar mass of BaSO}_4 = 137.33 + 32.06 + 4(16.00) = 233.4\text{ g mol}^{-1}
$$

$$
233.4\text{ g of BaSO}_4 \text{ contains } 32\text{ g of Sulfur}
$$

$$
\boxed{\% \text{ S} = \frac{32}{233.4} \times \frac{m_1}{m} \times 100\%}
$$

***

### 2.5 Estimation of Phosphorus ⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Principle & Reactions
A known mass ($m\text{ g}$) of phosphorus-containing organic compound is heated with fuming nitric acid in a Carius tube:

$$
\text{P} \xrightarrow{\text{HNO}_3, \Delta} \text{H}_3\text{PO}_4
$$

Phosphoric acid can be quantitatively determined through two analytical methods:

**Method 1: Precipitation as Ammonium Phosphomolybdate:**
Ammonium molybdate solution in excess $\text{HNO}_3$ precipitates phosphorus as:

$$
\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4 + 12(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{MoO}_4 + 21\text{HNO}_3 \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Molar mass } = 1877\text{ g/mol}}{(\text{NH}_4)_3\text{PO}_4 \cdot 12\text{MoO}_3 \downarrow} + 21\text{NH}_4\text{NO}_3 + 12\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

If mass of $(\text{NH}_4)_3\text{PO}_4 \cdot 12\text{MoO}_3 = m_1\text{ g}$:

$$
\boxed{\% \text{ P} = \frac{31}{1877} \times \frac{m_1}{m} \times 100\%}
$$

**Method 2: Precipitation as Magnesium Pyrophosphate ($\text{Mg}_2\text{P}_2\text{O}_7$):**
Magnesia mixture ($\text{MgCl}_2 + \text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + \text{NH}_4\text{OH}$) is added to yield magnesium ammonium phosphate, which on ignition yields magnesium pyrophosphate:

$$
\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4 + \text{MgCl}_2 + 3\text{NH}_4\text{OH} \longrightarrow \text{MgNH}_4\text{PO}_4 \downarrow + 2\text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

$$
2\text{MgNH}_4\text{PO}_4 \xrightarrow{\Delta \text{ (Ignition)}} \underset{\text{Molar mass } = 222.6\text{ g/mol}}{\text{Mg}_2\text{P}_2\text{O}_7} + 2\text{NH}_3 \uparrow + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

If mass of $\text{Mg}_2\text{P}_2\text{O}_7 = m_1\text{ g}$:

$$
222.6\text{ g of Mg}_2\text{P}_2\text{O}_7 \equiv 2 \times 31 = 62\text{ g of Phosphorus}
$$

$$
\boxed{\% \text{ P} = \frac{62}{222.6} \times \frac{m_1}{m} \times 100\%}
$$

### 2.6 Estimation of Oxygen ⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Method of Difference
Usually, the percentage of oxygen is calculated indirectly:

$$
\boxed{\% \text{ Oxygen} = 100\% - \left[\% \text{ C} + \% \text{ H} + \% \text{ N} + \% \text{ S} + \% \text{ Halogen} + \% \text{ P}\right]}
$$

#### B. Direct Determination (Unterzaucher Method)
A known mass ($m\text{ g}$) of organic compound is pyrolyzed in an atmosphere of pure dinitrogen gas over activated carbon at $1373\text{ K}$ ($1100^\circ\text{C}$). Oxygen quantitatively converts to carbon monoxide:

$$
\text{Compound (O)} \xrightarrow{1373\text{ K}, \text{C}} \text{CO}
$$

The gas is passed over warm iodine pentoxide ($\text{I}_2\text{O}_5$) at $423\text{ K}$, which oxidizes $\text{CO}$ to $\text{CO}_2$ while liberating free iodine:

$$
5\text{CO} + \text{I}_2\text{O}_5 \longrightarrow 5\text{CO}_2 + \text{I}_2
$$

From stoichiometry:

$$
5\text{ mol CO} \equiv 5 \text{ mol O} \equiv 5 \text{ mol CO}_2
$$

$$
1 \text{ mol O} \equiv 1 \text{ mol CO}_2
$$

$$
44\text{ g of CO}_2 \equiv 16\text{ g of Oxygen}
$$

If mass of $\text{CO}_2$ produced $= m_1\text{ g}$:

$$
\boxed{\% \text{ O} = \frac{16}{44} \times \frac{m_1}{m} \times 100\%}
$$

Alternatively, the liberated $\text{I}_2$ can be titrated against standard sodium thiosulfate ($\text{Na}_2\text{S}_2\text{O}_3$).

### Problem 3.1: Liebig’s Method (Carbon & Hydrogen)
**Question:** On complete combustion, $0.246\text{ g}$ of an organic compound gave $0.198\text{ g}$ of carbon dioxide and $0.1014\text{ g}$ of water. Determine the percentage composition of carbon and hydrogen in the compound.

**Solution:**
- Mass of organic compound ($m$) $= 0.246\text{ g}$
- Mass of $\text{CO}_2$ ($m_2$) $= 0.198\text{ g}$
- Mass of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ ($m_1$) $= 0.1014\text{ g}$

1. **Percentage of Carbon:**
   

$$
\% \text{ C} = \frac{12}{44} \times \frac{m_2}{m} \times 100\% = \frac{12}{44} \times \frac{0.198}{0.246} \times 100\%
$$

   

$$
\% \text{ C} = 0.27272 \times 0.80488 \times 100\% = 21.95\%
$$

2. **Percentage of Hydrogen:**
   

$$
\% \text{ H} = \frac{2}{18} \times \frac{m_1}{m} \times 100\% = \frac{2}{18} \times \frac{0.1014}{0.246} \times 100\%
$$

   

$$
\% \text{ H} = 0.11111 \times 0.41220 \times 100\% = 4.58\%
$$

### Problem 3.2: Dumas Method (Nitrogen)
**Question:** In Dumas’ method for estimation of nitrogen, $0.3\text{ g}$ of an organic compound gave $50\text{ mL}$ of nitrogen collected at $300\text{ K}$ and $715\text{ mm}$ pressure. Calculate the percentage composition of nitrogen in the compound. (Aqueous tension at $300\text{ K} = 15\text{ mm}$).

**Solution:**
- Mass of organic substance ($m$) $= 0.3\text{ g}$
- Total barometric pressure ($P$) $= 715\text{ mmHg}$
- Aqueous tension ($p$) $= 15\text{ mmHg}$
- Pressure of dry $\text{N}_2$ ($P_1$) $= P - p = 715 - 15 = 700\text{ mmHg}$
- Experimental volume ($V_1$) $= 50\text{ mL}$
- Experimental temperature ($T_1$) $= 300\text{ K}$

1. **Convert Volume to STP ($P_0 = 760\text{ mmHg}, T_0 = 273\text{ K}$):**
   

$$
V_0 = \frac{P_1 V_1 T_0}{P_0 T_1} = \frac{700 \times 50 \times 273}{760 \times 300} = \frac{9555000}{228000} \approx 41.908\text{ mL}
$$

2. **Percentage of Nitrogen:**
   

$$
\% \text{ N} = \frac{28 \times V_0}{22400 \times m} \times 100\% = \frac{28 \times 41.908}{22400 \times 0.3} \times 100\%
$$

   

$$
\% \text{ N} = \frac{1173.42}{67.2} \approx 17.46\%
$$

### Problem 3.3: Kjeldahl's Method (Back-Titration Archetype)
**Question:** During Kjeldahl's estimation of nitrogen, the ammonia evolved from $0.5\text{ g}$ of an organic compound neutralized $10\text{ mL}$ of $1\text{ M } \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$. Find the percentage of nitrogen in the compound.

**Solution:**
- Mass of compound ($m$) $= 0.5\text{ g}$
- Molarity of $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ $= 1\text{ M}$
- Basicity of $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 = 2 \implies \text{Normality } (N) = 1\text{ M} \times 2 = 2\text{ N}$
- Volume of acid neutralized ($V$) $= 10\text{ mL}$

1. **Equivalents Calculation:**
   

$$
\text{meq of acid neutralized by ammonia} = N \times V = 2 \times 10 = 20\text{ meq}
$$

2. **Percentage of Nitrogen:**
   

$$
\% \text{ N} = \frac{1.4 \times (\text{meq of acid consumed})}{m} = \frac{1.4 \times 20}{0.5} = \frac{28}{0.5} = 56.0\%
$$

### Problem 3.4: Kjeldahl's Method (Excess Acid / Residual Neutralization Archetype)
**Question:** $0.4\text{ g}$ of an organic compound was treated according to Kjeldahl’s method. The ammonia evolved was absorbed in $50\text{ mL}$ of $0.1\text{ N } \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$. The residual acid required $30\text{ mL}$ of $0.1\text{ N } \text{NaOH}$ for complete neutralization. Calculate the percentage of nitrogen in the compound.

**Solution:**
- Mass of compound ($m$) $= 0.4\text{ g}$
- Initial acid: $V_1 = 50\text{ mL}, N_1 = 0.1\text{ N} \implies \text{Initial meq} = 50 \times 0.1 = 5.0\text{ meq}$
- Residual acid neutralized by $\text{NaOH}$: $V_2 = 30\text{ mL}, N_2 = 0.1\text{ N} \implies \text{Residual meq} = 30 \times 0.1 = 3.0\text{ meq}$

1. **Net Acid Neutralized by Liberated Ammonia:**
   

$$
\text{Net meq} = 5.0\text{ meq} - 3.0\text{ meq} = 2.0\text{ meq}
$$

2. **Percentage of Nitrogen:**
   

$$
\% \text{ N} = \frac{1.4 \times 2.0}{0.4} = \frac{2.8}{0.4} = 7.0\%
$$

### Problem 3.5: Carius Method (Halogen)
**Question:** In Carius method of halogen estimation, $0.186\text{ g}$ of an organic compound yielded $0.328\text{ g}$ of $\text{AgBr}$. Find the percentage of bromine in the compound. ($\text{Molar mass: } \text{Ag} = 108\text{ g/mol}, \text{Br} = 80\text{ g/mol}$).

**Solution:**
- Mass of organic substance ($m$) $= 0.186\text{ g}$
- Mass of $\text{AgBr}$ ($m_1$) $= 0.328\text{ g}$
- Molar mass of $\text{AgBr} = 108 + 80 = 188\text{ g mol}^{-1}$

$$
\% \text{ Br} = \frac{80}{188} \times \frac{m_1}{m} \times 100\% = \frac{80}{188} \times \frac{0.328}{0.186} \times 100\%
$$

$$
\% \text{ Br} = 0.42553 \times 1.76344 \times 100\% \approx 75.04\%
$$

### Problem 3.6: Carius Method (Sulfur)
**Question:** In the sulfur estimation of an organic compound by Carius method, $0.157\text{ g}$ of an organic compound gave $0.4813\text{ g}$ of $\text{BaSO}_4$. Calculate the percentage of sulfur present. ($\text{Molar mass: } \text{BaSO}_4 = 233.4\text{ g mol}^{-1}$).

# PART 4: EXAMINER TRAPS & COMMON STUDENT PITFALLS

1. **Aqueous Tension Ignored in Dumas Method:**
   - **Mistake:** Using barometric pressure ($P$) directly in the gas equation without subtracting aqueous tension.
   - **Rule:** Nitrogen is collected over an aqueous $\text{KOH}$ solution and is saturated with water vapor. You **must** subtract aqueous tension:
     

$$
P_{\text{dry } \text{N}_2} = P_{\text{moist}} - p_{\text{aq. tension}}
$$

2. **Molarity vs. Normality Confusion in Kjeldahl Calculations:**
   - **Mistake:** Directly multiplying the molarity of sulfuric acid by its volume without considering basicity ($n\text{-factor} = 2$).
   - **Rule:** $\text{Normality of H}_2\text{SO}_4 = 2 \times \text{Molarity}$. For example, $10\text{ mL}$ of $0.5\text{ M } \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ is equivalent to $10\text{ mL}$ of $1.0\text{ N } \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 = 10\text{ meq}$.

3. **Skipping the Boiling Step with $\text{HNO}_3$ in the Halogen Test:**
   - **Mistake:** Adding $\text{AgNO}_3$ directly to SFE containing nitrogen or sulfur.
   - **Fact:** $\text{CN}^-$ and $\text{S}^{2-}$ react with $\text{Ag}^+$ to form white $\text{AgCN}$ and black $\text{Ag}_2\text{S}$, producing false-positive signals for chlorine. The extract must be boiled with concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$ first.

4. **Kjeldahl Structural Exclusions:**
   - **Mistake:** Assuming all nitrogenous compounds can be analyzed via Kjeldahl's method.
   - **Fact:** Nitro compounds, azo compounds, and nitrogen-containing rings (pyridine, quinoline) do not yield ammonium sulfate under Kjeldahl digestion. Only the Dumas method works universally for all organic nitrogen compounds.

5. **Sulfur Confirmation Acid Selection:**
   - **Mistake:** Using dilute $\text{HCl}$ or $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ instead of $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$ for the lead acetate test.
   - **Fact:** $\text{Pb}^{2+}$ precipitates as white $\text{PbSO}_4$ or $\text{PbCl}_2$, obscuring the black $\text{PbS}$ precipitate. Use only **dilute acetic acid**.

6. **Order of Absorption Tubes in Liebig Combustion:**
   - **Mistake:** Placing the $\text{KOH}$ tube before the $\text{CaCl}_2$ tube.
   - **Fact:** $\text{KOH}$ is hygroscopic and absorbs both $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and $\text{CO}_2$. If placed first, it absorbs both water and carbon dioxide, ruining both measurements. Anhydrous $\text{CaCl}_2$ absorbs water only, leaving $\text{CO}_2$ to pass into the subsequent $\text{KOH}$ tube.

### 5.1 Memory Mnemonics

- **Lassaigne Colors:**
  - **Prussian Blue** $\longrightarrow$ **P**russia has a large **N**avy $\longrightarrow$ **N**itrogen.
  - **Purple / Violet** $\longrightarrow$ **V**iolet / **S**pectrum $\longrightarrow$ **S**ulfur (Nitroprusside).
  - **Blood Red** $\longrightarrow$ Both **N**itrogen and **S**ulfur ($\text{SCN}^-$).

- **Silver Halide Solubility Ladder (Down Group 17):**
  - $\text{AgCl}$ (White) $\longrightarrow$ Completely Soluble in dilute $\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$.
  - $\text{AgBr}$ (Pale Yellow) $\longrightarrow$ Sparingly Soluble in conc. $\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$.
  - $\text{AgI}$ (Bright Yellow) $\longrightarrow$ Completely Insoluble in $\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$.

- **Absorption Train Order:**
  - **C**alcium Chloride first, **P**otassium Hydroxide second ($\mathbf{C}$ before $\mathbf{P}$ in alphabetical order).
  - First absorb $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ ($\text{CaCl}_2$), then absorb $\text{CO}_2$ ($\text{KOH}$).
