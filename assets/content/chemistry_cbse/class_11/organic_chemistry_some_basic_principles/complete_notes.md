## 1. IN-DEPTH PEDAGOGICAL THEORY & CORE CONCEPTS

***

### 1.1 Tetravalence of Carbon and Promotion of Electrons ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
* **Definition & Ground State Configuration:**  
  Carbon has atomic number $Z = 6$. Its ground-state electronic configuration is:
  

$$
1s^2 \, 2s^2 \, 2p_x^1 \, 2p_y^1 \, 2p_z^0
$$

  In its ground state, carbon has only two unpaired electrons in the $2p$ subshell, which would predict divalence.
* **Excitation Phenomenon:**  
  To form four covalent bonds, one electron from the filled $2s$ orbital is promoted to the vacant $2p_z$ orbital:
  

$$
\text{C}^* \ (\text{Excited State}): 1s^2 \, 2s^1 \, 2p_x^1 \, 2p_y^1 \, 2p_z^1
$$

* **Energetics & Thermodynamic Justification:**  
  The promotion energy required to unpair and promote an electron from $2s \to 2p_z$ ($\Delta H \approx +400 \text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$) is compensated by the release of energy during the formation of two additional covalent bonds. Because bond formation is an exothermic process, forming four bonds ($\approx 4 \times 400\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$) confers greater thermodynamic stability than forming two bonds.

***

### 1.2 Concept of Hybridisation and Orbital Overlap ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Pure atomic orbitals ($2s, 2p_x, 2p_y, 2p_z$) have different shapes and directional properties. In methane ($\text{CH}_4$), all four $\text{C}-\text{H}$ bonds are identical in bond length ($108.7\text{ pm}$), bond dissociation enthalpy ($435\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$), and geometry. This equivalence requires **hybridisation**: the intermixing of non-equivalent atomic orbitals of comparable energy to form an equal number of new equivalent hybrid orbitals with identical shape, energy, and directional orientation in space.

#### A. $sp^3$ Hybridisation (Tetrahedral Carbon) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
* **Mixing:** One $2s$ + three $2p$ ($2p_x, 2p_y, 2p_z$) $\longrightarrow$ Four equivalent $sp^3$ hybrid orbitals.
* **Geometry & Bond Angle:** Regular tetrahedron; inter-orbital bond angle $= 109^\circ 28'$ ($109.5^\circ$).
* **Characteristics:**
  * $s$-character: $25\%$ ($\frac{1}{4}$)
  * $p$-character: $75\%$ ($\frac{3}{4}$)
  * Directional orientation: Pointing toward the four corners of a regular tetrahedron with carbon at the center.

#### B. $sp^2$ Hybridisation (Trigonal Planar Carbon) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
* **Mixing:** One $2s$ + two $2p$ (e.g., $2p_x, 2p_y$) $\longrightarrow$ Three equivalent $sp^2$ hybrid orbitals; one unhybridised $2p_z$ orbital remains oriented perpendicular to the hybridisation plane.
* **Geometry & Bond Angle:** Trigonal planar; inter-orbital bond angle $= 120^\circ$.
* **Characteristics:**
  * $s$-character: $33.33\%$ ($\frac{1}{3}$)
  * $p$-character: $66.67\%$ ($\frac{2}{3}$)
  * The unhybridised $2p_z$ orbital participates in sideways (lateral) overlap to produce a $\pi$ (pi) bond.

#### C. $sp$ Hybridisation (Linear Carbon) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
* **Mixing:** One $2s$ + one $2p$ (e.g., $2p_x$) $\longrightarrow$ Two equivalent $sp$ hybrid orbitals; two unhybridised $p$ orbitals ($2p_y, 2p_z$) remain mutually perpendicular to each other and to the internuclear axis.
* **Geometry & Bond Angle:** Linear; inter-orbital bond angle $= 180^\circ$.
* **Characteristics:**
  * $s$-character: $50\%$ ($\frac{1}{2}$)
  * $p$-character: $50\%$ ($\frac{1}{2}$)
  * The two unhybridised $p$ orbitals form two mutually perpendicular $\pi$ bonds.

***

### 1.3 Influence of Hybridisation on Electronegativity, Bond Length, and Bond Strength ⭐⭐⭐⭐
* **Electronegativity:** An $s$ orbital is spherically symmetrical and closer to the nucleus than a $p$ orbital. Greater $s$-character increases the effective nuclear pull on the bonding electrons:
  

$$
\text{Electronegativity of C}: \quad \text{C}(sp) > \text{C}(sp^2) > \text{C}(sp^3)
$$

* **Acidic Nature of Terminal Alkynes:** The terminal hydrogen in ethyne ($\text{H}-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-\text{H}$) is acidic because the high electronegativity of the $sp$-hybridised carbon ($50\%\ s$) stabilizes the conjugate base acetylide anion:
  

$$
\text{H}-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-\text{H} + \text{NaNH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{liq. } \text{NH}_3} \text{H}-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}^- \text{Na}^+ + \text{NH}_3
$$

* **Bond Length & Bond Energy Trends:**
  

$$
\text{Bond Length}: \quad \text{C}_{sp^3}-\text{C}_{sp^3} \ (154\text{ pm}) > \text{C}_{sp^2}-\text{C}_{sp^2} \ (134\text{ pm}) > \text{C}_{sp}-\text{C}_{sp} \ (120\text{ pm})
$$

  

$$
\text{C}-\text{H Bond Length}: \quad \text{C}_{sp^3}-\text{H} \ (111\text{ pm}) > \text{C}_{sp^2}-\text{H} \ (110\text{ pm}) > \text{C}_{sp}-\text{H} \ (106\text{ pm})
$$

  

$$
\text{Bond Strength}: \quad \text{C}\equiv\text{C} \ (835\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}) > \text{C}=\text{C} \ (614\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}) > \text{C}-\text{C} \ (348\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1})
$$

***

### 1.4 Nature of Covalent Bonds: $\sigma$ (Sigma) and $\pi$ (Pi) Bonds ⭐⭐⭐⭐

| Criterion | $\sigma$ (Sigma) Bond | $\pi$ (Pi) Bond |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mode of Overlap** | Coaxial / Head-on along the internuclear axis | Sideways / Collateral / Parallel overlap |
| **Participating Orbitals** | $s-s$, $s-p$, hybrid-hybrid ($sp^3-sp^3$), or hybrid-$s$ | Pure unhybridised $p-p$ or $d-p$ orbitals |
| **Electron Density** | Cylindrically symmetrical along internuclear axis | Two lobes: one above and one below the nodal plane |
| **Extent of Overlap** | Large (stronger bond; higher bond enthalpy) | Small (weaker bond; lower bond enthalpy) |
| **Free Rotation** | Free rotation permitted about $\text{C}-\text{C}$ single axis | Restricted rotation; leads to geometrical isomerism |
| **Independence** | Can exist independently as a single bond | Cannot exist without an underlying $\sigma$ bond |

### 1.5 Structural Representations of Organic Compounds ⭐⭐⭐⭐
1. **Complete (Expanded) Structural Formula:** All covalent bonds are explicitly drawn out as lines (two-electron dash notation).
2. **Condensed Structural Formula:** Identical repeating units are grouped using subscripts and omitting dashes:
   

$$
\text{e.g., Octane: } \text{CH}_3(\text{CH}_2)_6\text{CH}_3
$$

3. **Bond-Line (Skeletal) Formula:**
   * Carbon atoms are not written; they are represented by line vertices and terminal ends.
   * Hydrogen atoms attached to carbon are omitted (implicitly satisfied by tetravalence).
   * Heteroatoms ($\text{O}, \text{N}, \text{S}, \text{X}$) and any hydrogen atoms directly bonded to them ($\text{OH}, \text{NH}_2, \text{SH}$) must be explicitly shown.
4. **Three-Dimensional (Wedge-and-Dash) Representation:**
   * **Solid line (—):** Bond lying in the plane of the paper.
   * **Solid wedge ($\blacktriangleleft$):** Bond projecting forward, out of the plane toward the observer.
   * **Dashed wedge ($\dots$ or $\text{III}$):** Bond projecting backward, away from the observer behind the plane.

## 2. DERIVATIONS, FORMULAS & MATHEMATICAL FRAMEWORK

***

### 2.1 The General Steric Number Formulation for Carbon ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
The steric number ($SN$) determines the hybridisation state of any central atom:

$$
SN = (\text{Number of } \sigma \text{ bonds formed}) + (\text{Number of localized lone pairs on central atom})
$$

For neutral carbon compounds in ground/covalent forms, localized lone pairs $= 0$. Hence:

$$
SN = \sum \sigma\text{-bonds}
$$

* If $SN = 4 \implies sp^3$ hybridisation ($\text{Geometry: Tetrahedral}$; $\theta = 109^\circ 28'$)
* If $SN = 3 \implies sp^2$ hybridisation ($\text{Geometry: Trigonal Planar}$; $\theta = 120^\circ$)
* If $SN = 2 \implies sp$ hybridisation ($\text{Geometry: Linear}$; $\theta = 180^\circ$)

$$
\boxed{\text{Hybridisation Index } (n) = 4 - (\text{Number of } \pi\text{-bonds on that Carbon})}
$$

***

### 2.2 Derivation of Hybrid Orbital Wavefunctions & Tetrahedral Bond Angle ($109^\circ 28'$) ⭐⭐⭐
For an $sp^3$ hybridised carbon, four equivalent hybrid orbitals $\psi_1, \psi_2, \psi_3, \psi_4$ are formed by linear combinations of $2s, 2p_x, 2p_y, 2p_z$:

$$
\psi_i = c_{i1}\phi_{2s} + c_{i2}\phi_{2p_x} + c_{i3}\phi_{2p_y} + c_{i4}\phi_{2p_z}
$$

#### Boundary Conditions & Assumptions:
1. **Orthonormality:** Each orbital is normalized ($\int \psi_i^2 \, d\tau = 1$) and mutually orthogonal ($\int \psi_i \psi_j \, d\tau = 0$ for $i \neq j$).
2. **Equivalence:** All four orbitals contain equal contributions of $s$-character:
   

$$
c_{11}^2 = c_{21}^2 = c_{31}^2 = c_{41}^2 = \frac{1}{4} \implies c_{i1} = \frac{1}{2}
$$

#### Derivation of the Inter-Orbital Angle ($\theta$):
Consider two normalized hybrid orbitals $\psi_1$ and $\psi_2$ oriented at an angle $\theta$ relative to each other:

$$
\psi_1 = \sqrt{\frac{1}{4}}\phi_s + \sqrt{\frac{3}{4}}\phi_{p_1}
$$

$$
\psi_2 = \sqrt{\frac{1}{4}}\phi_s + \sqrt{\frac{3}{4}}\phi_{p_2}
$$

where $\phi_{p_1}$ and $\phi_{p_2}$ are directional $p$-orbitals pointing along unit vectors $\hat{u}_1$ and $\hat{u}_2$, such that $\hat{u}_1 \cdot \hat{u}_2 = \cos\theta$.

Imposing the orthogonality condition $\int \psi_1 \psi_2 \, d\tau = 0$:

$$
\int \left(\frac{1}{2}\phi_s + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\phi_{p_1}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}\phi_s + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\phi_{p_2}\right) d\tau = 0
$$

$$
\frac{1}{4}\underbrace{\int \phi_s^2 \, d\tau}_{1} + \frac{3}{4}\underbrace{\int \phi_{p_1}\phi_{p_2} \, d\tau}_{\cos\theta} = 0
$$

$$
\frac{1}{4} + \frac{3}{4}\cos\theta = 0 \implies 3\cos\theta = -1
$$

$$
\cos\theta = -\frac{1}{3}
$$

$$
\theta = \arccos\left(-\frac{1}{3}\right) \approx 109.4712^\circ = 109^\circ 28'
$$

$$
\boxed{\cos\theta = -\frac{1}{3} \implies \theta = 109^\circ 28'}
$$

***

### 2.3 Calculation of Degree of Unsaturation / Index of Hydrogen Deficiency (IHD) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
The Degree of Unsaturation ($DU$) indicates the total number of $\pi$ bonds and/or rings present in a molecule.

#### Derivation:
For a general hydrocarbon with $C$ carbons and $H$ hydrogens, compared to a fully saturated acyclic alkane with the formula $\text{C}_C\text{H}_{2C+2}$:

$$
DU = \frac{2C + 2 - H}{2}
$$

When heteroatoms are introduced:
* **Monovalent atoms (Halogens, $X = \text{F, Cl, Br, I}$):** Replace one hydrogen $\implies$ add count of $X$ to $H$.
* **Divalent atoms ($\text{O, S}$):** Form two bonds and do not alter the number of hydrogen atoms needed for saturation $\implies$ ignore $\text{O, S}$.
* **Trivalent atoms ($\text{N, P}$):** Bring an extra hydrogen into the saturated chain $\implies$ subtract count of $N$ from $H$.

$$
\boxed{DU = C + 1 - \frac{H + X - N}{2}}
$$

* Where:
  * $C = \text{Number of Carbon atoms}$
  * $H = \text{Number of Hydrogen atoms}$
  * $X = \text{Number of Halogen atoms}$
  * $N = \text{Number of Nitrogen atoms}$

### Problem 1: Systematic Hybridisation and Counting of $\sigma$ and $\pi$ Bonds
**Question:** Determine the total number of $\sigma$ and $\pi$ bonds, and assign the hybridisation state to each carbon atom in:

$$
\text{CH}_2=\text{C}=\text{CH}-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH}
$$

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. **Write the expanded structure showing all single and multiple bonds:**
   ```
         H           H
         |           |
     H - C = C = C - C ≡ C - H
         1   2   3   4     5
   ```
2. **Tabulate Carbon-by-Carbon:**
   * **$\text{C}_1$:** Forms two single bonds to $\text{H}$ ($\sigma$), one double bond to $\text{C}_2$ ($1\sigma, 1\pi$).  
     Total $\sigma = 3$, $\pi = 1$. $\implies \mathbf{sp^2}$
   * **$\text{C}_2$:** Forms two double bonds (one to $\text{C}_1$, one to $\text{C}_3$).  
     Total $\sigma = 2$, $\pi = 2$. $\implies \mathbf{sp}$
   * **$\text{C}_3$:** Forms one double bond to $\text{C}_2$ ($1\sigma, 1\pi$), one single bond to $\text{H}$ ($\sigma$), one single bond to $\text{C}_4$ ($\sigma$).  
     Total $\sigma = 3$, $\pi = 1$. $\implies \mathbf{sp^2}$
   * **$\text{C}_4$:** Forms one single bond to $\text{C}_3$ ($\sigma$), one triple bond to $\text{C}_5$ ($1\sigma, 2\pi$).  
     Total $\sigma = 2$, $\pi = 2$. $\implies \mathbf{sp}$
   * **$\text{C}_5$:** Forms one triple bond to $\text{C}_4$ ($1\sigma, 2\pi$), one single bond to $\text{H}$ ($\sigma$).  
     Total $\sigma = 2$, $\pi = 2$. $\implies \mathbf{sp}$

3. **Summation of Bonds:**
   * Total $\text{C}-\text{H}$ $\sigma$ bonds $= 2 + 1 + 1 = 4$
   * Total $\text{C}-\text{C}$ $\sigma$ bonds $= 4$
   * **Total $\sigma$ bonds $= 8$**
   * Total $\pi$ bonds $= 1 (\text{at } \text{C}_1=\text{C}_2) + 1 (\text{at } \text{C}_2=\text{C}_3) + 2 (\text{at } \text{C}_4\equiv\text{C}_5) = \mathbf{4}$

$$
\boxed{\text{Total } \sigma = 8, \quad \text{Total } \pi = 4}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{C}_1: sp^2, \quad \text{C}_2: sp, \quad \text{C}_3: sp^2, \quad \text{C}_4: sp, \quad \text{C}_5: sp}
$$

### Problem 2: Bond-Line Structural Interconversions
**Question:** Convert the following bond-line representation into condensed and molecular formulas, then calculate its Degree of Unsaturation:

```
        O
       //
      C
     / \
    /   \
   /     \
CH3       N(CH3)2
```
*(4-methylpent-3-en-2-one or an analogous branched unsaturated system)*

Let us take the NCERT exemplar molecule: **4-hydroxy-4-methylpentan-2-one**
```
      OH
      |
(CH3)2C - CH2 - C - CH3
                ||
                O
```
Bond-line:
```
       OH
       |
     /\/\
       ||
       O
```
**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. **Identify the Main Chain & Vertices:**
   * Left terminals: two methyl groups ($\text{C}_5$ and methyl branch on $\text{C}_4$).
   * Carbon-4 is bonded to: two $-\text{CH}_3$ groups, one $-\text{OH}$ group, and the $\text{C}_3$ methylene carbon.
   * Carbon-3: $-\text{CH}_2-$ vertex.
   * Carbon-2: Carbonyl carbon ($\text{C}=\text{O}$).
   * Carbon-1: Terminal $-\text{CH}_3$ group.
2. **Condensed Formula:**
   

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_2\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{OH} \quad \text{or} \quad (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{C(OH)}\text{CH}_2\text{COCH}_3
$$

3. **Molecular Formula:**
   * Count Carbons: $1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 2 = 6$ Carbons $\implies \text{C}_6$
   * Count Hydrogens: $3 + 2 + 6 + 1 (\text{from OH}) = 12$ Hydrogens $\implies \text{H}_{12}$
   * Count Oxygens: $2$ Oxygens $\implies \text{O}_2$
   * **Molecular Formula:** $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_2$
4. **Degree of Unsaturation:**
   

$$
DU = C + 1 - \frac{H + X - N}{2} = 6 + 1 - \frac{12 + 0 - 0}{2} = 7 - 6 = 1
$$

   *Physical Meaning:* The $1$ degree of unsaturation corresponds to the one $\pi$ bond in the $\text{C}=\text{O}$ carbonyl group.

### Problem 3: Planarity & Spatial Conformation in Allenes vs. Dienes
**Question:** Explain why propadiene ($\text{CH}_2=\text{C}=\text{CH}_2$, allene) is a **non-planar** molecule, whereas buta-1,3-diene ($\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}-\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2$) is **planar**.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. **Orbital Analysis of Propadiene ($\text{CH}_2=\text{C}=\text{CH}_2$):**
   * Central carbon ($\text{C}_2$) is $sp$ hybridised with two unhybridised $2p$ orbitals: $2p_y$ and $2p_z$.
   * $\text{C}_1$ is $sp^2$ hybridised; assume its hybrid orbitals lie in the $xy$-plane. Its unhybridised orbital is $2p_z$.
   * The $\pi$ bond between $\text{C}_1$ and $\text{C}_2$ forms via overlap of their respective $2p_z$ orbitals:
     

$$
\pi_1 = 2p_z(\text{C}_1) - 2p_z(\text{C}_2)
$$

   * Central carbon $\text{C}_2$ must use its remaining unhybridised $2p_y$ orbital to form the second $\pi$ bond with $\text{C}_3$.
   * Consequently, $\text{C}_3$ must utilize its $2p_y$ orbital for $\pi$-bonding.
   * This requires the $sp^2$ hybrid orbitals of $\text{C}_3$ (and hence the two attached hydrogens) to lie in the $xz$-plane.
   * **Conclusion:** The terminal $-\text{CH}_2$ planes are perpendicular ($90^\circ$) to each other. Hence, propadiene is **non-planar**.

2. **Orbital Analysis of Buta-1,3-diene ($\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}-\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2$):**
   * All four carbons are $sp^2$ hybridised.
   * Every carbon has its unhybridised $2p_z$ orbital aligned parallel to the others (perpendicular to the common molecular plane).
   * Delocalization occurs across all four centers along the parallel $2p_z$ axes.
   * **Conclusion:** Maximum orbital overlap requires all carbon and hydrogen atoms to lie in the same plane. Hence, buta-1,3-diene is **planar**.

## 4. EXAMINER TRAPS & COMMON MISTAKES

***

> [!WARNING]
> ### Trap 1: Forgetting Implicit Hydrogens at Skeletal Vertices
> * **Error:** When converting a bond-line structure like a cyclopentane ring with an $-\text{OH}$ group to a formula, students often count each ring vertex as $-\text{CH}_2-$, forgetting that the substituted vertex has only ONE hydrogen.
> * **Correct Method:** For every carbon in a neutral species, always apply:
>   

$$
N_{\text{hydrogens}} = 4 - (\text{Number of visible lines attached to that vertex})
$$

> [!WARNING]
> ### Trap 2: Counting Only C-C Bonds and Missing C-H Bonds in $\sigma$-Counting
> * **Error:** When asked for total $\sigma$ bonds in benzene ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_6$), students frequently answer $6$ (counting only $\text{C}-\text{C}$ bonds).
> * **Correct Method:** Benzene has $6 \ (\text{C}-\text{C}) + 6 \ (\text{C}-\text{H}) = \mathbf{12} \ \sigma\text{-bonds}$ and $3 \ \pi\text{-bonds}$.

> [!WARNING]
> ### Trap 3: Lone Pair Miscounting in Heteroatomic Hybrids
> * **Error:** Assigning the nitrogen in pyridine ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_5\text{N}$) or pyrrole ($\text{C}_4\text{H}_5\text{N}$) without checking whether the lone pair is localized or delocalized into the aromatic sextet.
> * **Key Rule:**
>   * In **pyridine**, the nitrogen is double-bonded ($\sigma + \pi$); its lone pair occupies an $sp^2$ hybrid orbital in the molecular plane $\implies \mathbf{sp^2}$.
>   * In **pyrrole**, the nitrogen is single-bonded to three atoms, but its lone pair participates in the aromatic $\pi$-system ($p_z$ orbital) $\implies$ the nitrogen is $\mathbf{sp^2}$ hybridised, **not** $sp^3$.

> [!WARNING]
> ### Trap 4: Bond Angle Distortions from Ideal Values
> * **Error:** Stating that the $\text{H}-\text{C}-\text{H}$ bond angle in ethene ($\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}_2$) is exactly $120^\circ$.
> * **Reality:** Due to $\text{VSEPR}$ repulsion, the double bond ($\text{C}=\text{C}$) repels single bonds more strongly than single bonds repel each other:
>   

$$
\angle \text{C}=\text{C}-\text{H} \approx 121.7^\circ, \quad \angle \text{H}-\text{C}-\text{H} \approx 116.6^\circ
$$

## 5. SPEED HACKS & GOLDEN POINTS

***

### Golden Point 1: $\sigma$-Bond Shortcut for Acyclic/Open-Chain Molecules
For any completely acyclic (open-chain) compound containing $N_{\text{atoms}}$ total atoms:

$$
\boxed{\text{Total } \sigma\text{-bonds} = N_{\text{atoms}} - 1}
$$

* *Example:* For sucrose ($\text{C}_{12}\text{H}_{22}\text{O}_{11}$ open form hypothesis) or pentane ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_{12}$):
  

$$
N_{\text{atoms}} = 5 + 12 = 17 \implies \text{Total } \sigma = 17 - 1 = \mathbf{16}
$$

***

### Golden Point 2: $\sigma$-Bond Shortcut for Cyclic/Ring-Containing Molecules
For a molecule containing $N_{\text{atoms}}$ total atoms and $R$ individual rings:

$$
\boxed{\text{Total } \sigma\text{-bonds} = N_{\text{atoms}} - 1 + R}
$$

* *Example 1 (Cyclohexane, $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}$):* $R = 1$, $N = 18 \implies \sigma = 18 - 1 + 1 = \mathbf{18}$.
* *Example 2 (Benzene, $\text{C}_6\text{H}_6$):* $R = 1$, $N = 12 \implies \sigma = 12 - 1 + 1 = \mathbf{12}$.
* *Example 3 (Bicyclo[2.2.1]heptane, $\text{C}_7\text{H}_{12}$):* $R = 2$, $N = 19 \implies \sigma = 19 - 1 + 2 = \mathbf{20}$.

***

### Golden Point 3: Rapid Hybridisation Identification Matrix

| Environment of Carbon Atom | $\sigma$ Bonds | $\pi$ Bonds | Hybridisation | Geometry | % $s$-character |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Only single bonds ($-\text{C}-$) | 4 | 0 | **$sp^3$** | Tetrahedral | $25\%$ |
| One double bond ($-\text{C}=$) | 3 | 1 | **$sp^2$** | Trigonal Planar | $33.3\%$ |
| Two double bonds ($=\text{C}=$) | 2 | 2 | **$sp$** | Linear | $50\%$ |
| One triple bond ($-\text{C}\equiv$) | 2 | 2 | **$sp$** | Linear | $50\%$ |

***

### Golden Point 4: Allene/Cumulene Odd-Even Stereochemistry Rule
For cumulative polyenes with general formula $R_2\text{C}(=\text{C})_n\text{C}R_2$ where $n$ is the number of double bonds:
* **Even number of consecutive double bonds ($n = 2, 4, 6\dots$):**
  Terminal planes are **mutually perpendicular**.
  

$$
\text{Cannot exhibit Geometrical Isomerism (cis/trans); can show Enantiomerism (Optical Activity) if substituted as } ab\text{C}=\text{C}=\text{C}ab
$$

* **Odd number of consecutive double bonds ($n = 1, 3, 5\dots$):**
  Terminal planes are **coplanar**.
  

$$
\text{Can exhibit Geometrical Isomerism (cis/trans); optically inactive if planar}
$$

# SECTION 1: CLASSIFICATION OF ORGANIC COMPOUNDS

Organic compounds arise primarily from carbon’s unique capabilities of **catenation** (self-linking via covalent bonds, bond energy $\approx 348\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$) and **tetravalency** ($sp^3, sp^2, sp$ hybrid states with bond angles of $109^\circ 28', 120^\circ,$ and $180^\circ$ respectively).

```
                            Organic Compounds
                                    │
         ┌──────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                     ▼
Acyclic / Open Chain                                   Cyclic / Closed Chain
(Aliphatic Compounds)                                  (Ring Compounds)
  ├── Saturated (Alkanes)                                      │
  └── Unsaturated (Alkenes, Alkynes)           ┌───────────────┴───────────────┐
                                               ▼                               ▼
                                       Homocyclic / Carbocyclic          Heterocyclic
                                               │                               │
                                  ┌────────────┴────────────┐             ┌────┴────┐
                                  ▼                         ▼             ▼         ▼
                             Alicyclic                  Aromatic      Alicyclic  Aromatic
                                                            │
                                                   ┌────────┴────────┐
                                                   ▼                 ▼
                                               Benzenoid        Non-Benzenoid
```

***

### 1.1 Acyclic or Open-Chain Compounds (Aliphatic) [⭐⭐⭐⭐]

Compounds containing open chains of carbon atoms, which may be straight or branched. The name *aliphatic* is derived from the Greek word *aleiphar* (meaning fat).

1. **Saturated Acyclic Compounds:** Contain only carbon–carbon single ($\sigma$) bonds.
   

$$
\text{General formula for acyclic alkanes: } \mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n+2}
$$

   

$$
\text{Example: } \mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3} \quad (n\text{-butane})
$$

2. **Unsaturated Acyclic Compounds:** Contain at least one carbon–carbon multiple bond (double bond $\sigma + \pi$ or triple bond $\sigma + 2\pi$).
   

$$
\text{Alkenes: } \mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n} \quad (\text{e.g., } \mathrm{CH_2=CH-CH_3})
$$

   

$$
\text{Alkynes: } \mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n-2} \quad (\text{e.g., } \mathrm{CH\equiv C-CH_3})
$$

***

### 1.2 Cyclic or Closed-Chain Compounds [⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐]

Compounds containing one or more closed rings of atoms.

#### A. Homocyclic or Carbocyclic Compounds
The ring framework consists solely of carbon atoms.
1. **Alicyclic Compounds (Aliphatic Cyclic):** Possess properties resembling aliphatic compounds. Rings may be saturated or unsaturated.
   

$$
\text{General formula for monocyclic cycloalkanes: } \mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n} \quad (n \ge 3)
$$

   Examples: Cyclopropane ($\mathrm{C_3H_6}$), Cyclobutane ($\mathrm{C_4H_8}$), Cyclohexene ($\mathrm{C_6H_{10}}$).

2. **Aromatic Compounds:** Cyclic unsaturated planar systems possessing exceptional thermodynamic stability due to cyclic delocalization of $(4n+2)\,\pi$ electrons (**Hückel’s Rule**, where $n = 0, 1, 2, 3 \dots$).
   - **Benzenoid Aromatic Compounds:** Contain one or more fused or isolated benzene rings.
     - Benzene ($\mathrm{C_6H_6}$, $6\pi\,e^-, n=1$)
     - Naphthalene ($\mathrm{C_{10}H_8}$, $10\pi\,e^-, n=2$)
     - Anthracene ($\mathrm{C_{14}H_{10}}$, $14\pi\,e^-, n=3$)
     - Toluene ($\mathrm{C_6H_5-CH_3}$)
   - **Non-Benzenoid Aromatic Compounds:** Aromatic rings containing no benzene rings.
     - **Tropone** (2,4,6-cycloheptatrien-1-one) and **Tropolone** (contains a 7-membered conjugated ring with $6\pi$ delocalized electrons).
     - **Azulene** ($\mathrm{C_{10}H_8}$, fused 7-membered and 5-membered rings, $10\pi\,e^-$).

#### B. Heterocyclic Compounds
The cyclic ring contains carbon atoms and at least one **heteroatom** (typically $\mathrm{N, O, S}$).
1. **Hetero-alicyclic Compounds:**
   - Oxirane (Ethylene oxide / Epoxide): 3-membered ring with 1 oxygen atom.
   - Tetrahydrofuran ($\mathrm{THF}$, $\mathrm{C_4H_8O}$): 5-membered saturated ring with 1 oxygen atom.
   - Pyrrolidine ($\mathrm{C_4H_9N}$): 5-membered saturated ring with 1 nitrogen atom.
   - Piperidine ($\mathrm{C_5H_{11}N}$): 6-membered saturated ring with 1 nitrogen atom.

2. **Hetero-aromatic Compounds:**
   - Furan ($\mathrm{C_4H_4O}$): 5-membered ring; oxygen contributes one lone pair to complete $6\pi$ delocalized system ($n=1$).
   - Thiophene ($\mathrm{C_4H_4S}$): 5-membered ring; sulfur contributes one lone pair ($6\pi\,e^-$).
   - Pyrrole ($\mathrm{C_4H_5N}$): 5-membered ring; nitrogen lone pair participates in the aromatic sextet ($6\pi\,e^-$).
   - Pyridine ($\mathrm{C_5H_5N}$): 6-membered ring; nitrogen is $sp^2$ hybridized with its lone pair residing in an $sp^2$ orbital orthogonal to the $\pi$-system ($6\pi\,e^-$).

***

### 1.3 Classification Based on Functional Groups [⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐]

A **functional group** is an atom or group of atoms joined in a specific manner that is responsible for the characteristic chemical properties of the organic compound.

A **homologous series** is defined as a family or group of structurally related organic compounds:
1. All members conform to a common general molecular formula.
2. Successive members differ in composition by a constant methylene unit:
   

$$
\Delta(\text{Molecular formula}) = -\mathrm{CH_2}- \implies \Delta M = 14.027\text{ g mol}^{-1} \approx 14\text{ u}
$$

3. All members possess the same functional group and exhibit identical chemical characteristics, with regular gradations in physical properties (e.g., boiling point, melting point, density) due to increasing London dispersion forces with higher molecular mass.

***

# SECTION 2: IUPAC NOMENCLATURE — THE FOUNDATIONAL SYSTEM

The International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry (IUPAC) system provides a systematic, unambiguous name for every organic structure.

### 2.1 The Master Syntax of an IUPAC Name [⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐]

$$
\mathbf{\text{Complete IUPAC Name} = \text{Secondary Prefix} + \text{Primary Prefix} + \text{Word Root} + \text{Primary Suffix} + \text{Secondary Suffix}}
$$

```
┌──────────────────┬─────────────────┬───────────┬────────────────┬──────────────────┐
│ Secondary Prefix │ Primary Prefix  │ Word Root │ Primary Suffix │ Secondary Suffix │
├──────────────────┼─────────────────┼───────────┼────────────────┼──────────────────┤
│ Substituents /   │ "cyclo"         │ Number of │ Degree of      │ Principal        │
│ Non-principal    │ (only present   │ Carbons   │ unsaturation   │ Functional       │
│ functional       │ in alicyclic /  │ in Parent │ (-ane, -ene,   │ Group            │
│ groups           │ cyclic systems) │ Chain     │ -yne)          │ (-ol, -oic acid) │
└──────────────────┴─────────────────┴───────────┴────────────────┴──────────────────┘
```

#### 1. Word Root (Chain Length)
Determined by the number of carbon atoms in the selected **Principal Carbon Chain (PCC)**:
- $\mathrm{C_1}$: Meth-
- $\mathrm{C_2}$: Eth-
- $\mathrm{C_3}$: Prop-
- $\mathrm{C_4}$: But-
- $\mathrm{C_5}$: Pent-
- $\mathrm{C_6}$: Hex-
- $\mathrm{C_7}$: Hept-
- $\mathrm{C_8}$: Oct-
- $\mathrm{C_9}$: Non-
- $\mathrm{C_{10}}$: Dec-
- $\mathrm{C_{11}}$: Undec-
- $\mathrm{C_{12}}$: Dodec-
- $\mathrm{C_{20}}$: Icos-

#### 2. Primary Suffix (Degree of Unsaturation)
- Saturated: **-ane**
- One $\mathrm{C=C}$: **-ene**
- Two $\mathrm{C=C}$: **-adiene** (Word root takes an extra terminal "a": e.g., But**a**-1,3-diene)
- Three $\mathrm{C=C}$: **-atriene**
- One $\mathrm{C\equiv C}$: **-yne**
- Two $\mathrm{C\equiv C}$: **-adiyne**
- One $\mathrm{C=C}$ and one $\mathrm{C\equiv C}$: **-en-yne** (Elide the "e" of "-ene" before vowel/glide "y": e.g., `-en-yne`, not `-ene-yne`).

#### 3. Secondary Suffix (Principal Functional Group)
Appended after the primary suffix to represent the principal functional group.
- Carboxylic acid: **-oic acid** (or **-carboxylic acid**)
- Sulfonic acid: **-sulfonic acid**
- Ester: **-oate** (or **-carboxylate**)
- Acid halide: **-oyl halide**
- Amide: **-amide** (or **-carboxamide**)
- Nitrile: **-nitrile** (or **-carbonitrile**)
- Aldehyde: **-al** (or **-carbaldehyde**)
- Ketone: **-one**
- Alcohol: **-ol**
- Thiol: **-thiol**
- Amine: **-amine**

*Phonetic Elision Rule:* If the secondary suffix begins with a vowel ($a, e, i, o, u$) or $y$, the terminal "e" of the primary suffix is dropped:

$$
\text{Hexane} + \text{-ol} \longrightarrow \text{Hexan-1-ol}
$$

$$
\text{Hexane} + \text{-nitrile} \longrightarrow \text{Hexanenitrile} \quad (\text{"e" is retained because "n" is a consonant})
$$

$$
\text{Hexane} + \text{-dioic acid} \longrightarrow \text{Hexanedioic acid} \quad (\text{"e" is retained before "d"})
$$

#### 4. Primary Prefix
Distinguishes cyclic from acyclic compounds. For monocyclic compounds, the prefix **cyclo-** immediately precedes the word root. (For bicyclic compounds: **bicyclo[a.b.c]-**, for spiro: **spiro[a.b]-**).

#### 5. Secondary Prefix
Indicates the identity and position of substituents (branches) or secondary (inferior) functional groups treated merely as substituents (e.g., $-\mathrm{NO_2}$ *nitro*, $-\mathrm{OR}$ *alkoxy*, $-\mathrm{X}$ *halo*, $-\mathrm{N_3}$ *azido*).

# SECTION 3: PRIORITY TABLE OF FUNCTIONAL GROUPS

When two or more functional groups are present in a polyfunctional organic compound, one is designated as the **Principal Functional Group (PFG)** based on IUPAC seniority. All remaining functional groups are treated as **substituents (prefixes)**.

### Master Priority Order (Decreasing Seniority: Top to Bottom) [⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐]

$$
\begin{aligned}
-\mathrm{COOH} > -\mathrm{SO_3H} > -\mathrm{(CO)_2O} > -\mathrm{COOR} > -\mathrm{COX} > -\mathrm{CONH_2} > -\mathrm{CN} > -\mathrm{NC} \\
> -\mathrm{CHO} > \mathrm{>C=O} > -\mathrm{OH} > -\mathrm{SH} > -\mathrm{NH_2} > \mathrm{>C=C<} > -\mathrm{C\equiv C-}
\end{aligned}
$$

| Class | Functional Group Structure | Prefix (Substituent) | Suffix (When C is counted in PCC) | Special Suffix (When C is not in PCC) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Carboxylic acid** | $-\mathrm{COOH}$ | carboxy- | **-oic acid** | -carboxylic acid |
| **Sulfonic acid** | $-\mathrm{SO_3H}$ | sulfo- | **-sulfonic acid** | -sulfonic acid |
| **Anhydride** | $-\mathrm{CO-O-CO}-$ | — | **-oic anhydride** | -carboxylic anhydride |
| **Ester** | $-\mathrm{COOR}$ | alkoxycarbonyl- or carbalkoxy- | **alkyl ... -oate** | alkyl ... -carboxylate |
| **Acid Halide** | $-\mathrm{COX}$ ($\mathrm{X=Cl, Br, I}$) | halocarbonyl- / haloformyl- | **-oyl halide** | -carbonyl halide |
| **Amide** | $-\mathrm{CONH_2}$ | carbamoyl- | **-amide** | -carboxamide |
| **Nitrile** | $-\mathrm{C\equiv N}$ | cyano- | **-nitrile** | -carbonitrile |
| **Isonitrile** | $-\mathrm{N\equiv C}$ | isocyano- | **-isonitrile** | — |
| **Aldehyde** | $-\mathrm{CHO}$ | formyl- / oxo- | **-al** | -carbaldehyde |
| **Ketone** | $\mathrm{>C=O}$ | oxo- / keto- | **-one** | — |
| **Alcohol** | $-\mathrm{OH}$ | hydroxy- | **-ol** | — |
| **Thiol** | $-\mathrm{SH}$ | mercapto- / sulfanyl- | **-thiol** | — |
| **Amine** | $-\mathrm{NH_2}$ | amino- | **-amine** | — |
| **Alkene** | $-\mathrm{CH=CH}-$ | — | **-ene** | — |
| **Alkyne** | $-\mathrm{C\equiv C}-$ | — | **-yne** | — |

> [!NOTE]
> **Substituents that are ALWAYS Prefixes (Never take Suffixes):**
> - Halogens ($-\mathrm{F}$: fluoro, $-\mathrm{Cl}$: chloro, $-\mathrm{Br}$: bromo, $-\mathrm{I}$: iodo)
> - Nitro ($-\mathrm{NO_2}$)
> - Nitroso ($-\mathrm{NO}$)
> - Alkoxy ($-\mathrm{OR}$, e.g., $-\mathrm{OCH_3}$ methoxy, $-\mathrm{OC_2H_5}$ ethoxy)
> - Alkyl ($-\mathrm{R}$)
> - Phenyl ($-\mathrm{C_6H_5}$)
> - Diazo ($-\mathrm{N_2^+}$)

# SECTION 4: STEP-BY-STEP RULES FOR IUPAC NOMENCLATURE

### 4.1 Selection of the Principal Carbon Chain (PCC) [⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐]

Follow this strict hierarchical priority order to select the PCC:
1. **Maximum number of Principal Functional Groups (PFGs).**
2. **Maximum number of multiple bonds** (double and triple bonds combined, regardless of chain length).
3. **Maximum length of the continuous carbon chain** (maximum number of carbon atoms).
4. **Maximum number of double bonds.**
5. **Maximum number of substituents (side chains).**
6. **Lowest locant set for substituents.**

***

### 4.2 The Lowest Locant Rule vs. "Lowest Sum Rule" [⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐]

> [!CAUTION]
> **Debunking the "Lowest Sum Rule":**
> NCERT and modern IUPAC regulations state that the **Lowest Set of Locants** rule strictly overrides any "sum of locants" calculation.
>
> When comparing two different numbering paths, compare the locant sets **term-by-term in increasing numerical order**. The correct set is the one that has the lower number at the **first point of difference**.
> 
> *Example:* Locant set $(2, 7, 8)$ vs $(3, 4, 5)$.
> - First point of comparison: $2$ vs $3$.
> - Since $2 < 3$, the set $(2, 7, 8)$ wins instantly at the first point of difference, even though $2+7+8=17$ while $3+4+5=12$. The sum is completely irrelevant.

#### Hierarchical Priority for Numbering the PCC:
1. **Principal Functional Group (PFG)** gets lowest possible locant.
2. **Multiple bonds ($\mathrm{C=C}$ and $\mathrm{C\equiv C}$)** get lowest locants.
3. **Substituents / Prefixes** get lowest locants at first point of difference.
4. **Alphabetical priority:** If identical locant sets result from both directions, the substituent that comes first alphabetically receives the lower number.

***

### 4.3 Double Bonds vs. Triple Bonds Tie-Breaker [⭐⭐⭐⭐]

When a double bond and a triple bond are equidistant from the two ends of the chain:
- The **double bond receives lower locant preference** over the triple bond.
- Mnemonic: *"ene"* precedes *"yne"* alphabetically.

$$
\mathrm{\overset{1}{C}H_2=\overset{2}{C}H-\overset{3}{C}H_2-\overset{4}{C}\equiv\overset{5}{C}H} \implies \text{Pent-1-en-4-yne} \quad (\mathbf{Correct})
$$

$$
\mathrm{\overset{5}{C}H_2=\overset{4}{C}H-\overset{3}{C}H_2-\overset{2}{C}\equiv\overset{1}{C}H} \implies \text{Pent-4-en-1-yne} \quad (\mathbf{Incorrect})
$$

- Note: If they are **not** equidistant, the multiple bond closest to either end takes the lowest locant regardless of whether it is double or triple:

$$
\mathrm{\overset{1}{C}H\equiv\overset{2}{C}-\overset{3}{C}H_2-\overset{4}{C}H=\overset{5}{C}H-\overset{6}{C}H_3} \implies \text{Hex-4-en-1-yne} \quad (\text{Locant set } 1,4 \text{ beats } 2,5)
$$

***

### 4.4 Special IUPAC 1993 Rule for Identical Terminal Carbon-Containing Groups [⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐]

If an unbranched chain contains **three or more identical terminal carbon-containing functional groups** (such as $-\mathrm{COOH}$, $-\mathrm{CHO}$, $-\mathrm{CN}$, $-\mathrm{CONH_2}$):
- **None** of the carbons of these functional groups are included in the principal carbon chain.
- The parent hydrocarbon chain is named, and special suffixes (**-tricarboxylic acid, -tricarbaldehyde, -tricarbonitrile**) are appended.

#### Case Study: Citric Acid Analogue

$$
\mathrm{HOOC-CH_2-CH(COOH)-CH_2-COOH}
$$

- Old convention (obsolete): Carbons included in chain $\to$ 3-carboxy-1,5-pentanedioic acid.
- **Modern IUPAC (NCERT):** The three $-\mathrm{COOH}$ carbons are excluded from the main chain.
  

$$
\text{Parent chain} = \mathrm{-CH_2-CH-CH_2-} \quad (3\text{ carbons: propane})
$$

  

$$
\mathbf{\text{Correct IUPAC Name: Propane-1,2,3-tricarboxylic acid}}
$$

#### Case Study: 1,2,3-Tricyano compound

$$
\mathrm{NC-CH_2-CH(CN)-CH_2-CN}
$$

$$
\mathbf{\text{Correct IUPAC Name: Propane-1,2,3-tricarbonitrile}}
$$

***

### 4.5 Naming of Complex (Branched) Substituents [⭐⭐⭐⭐]

When a substituent carbon chain is itself branched:
1. Carbon-1 ($1'$) of the side chain is **always the carbon atom attached directly to the principal chain**.
2. The complex substituent name is enclosed entirely within **parentheses**.
3. Multiplying prefixes like *bis-*, *tris-*, *tetrakis-* are used when multiple identical complex substituents are present.
4. In alphabetical ordering:
   - Prefixes **iso-** and **neo-** are considered part of the fundamental alkyl name (i.e., **i** is alphabetized).
   - Prefixes **sec-**, **tert-** separated by a hyphen are **not** alphabetized (alphabetized by the main root: e.g., *sec*-butyl is alphabetized under **b**).
   - Multiplying prefixes (*di-, tri-, tetra-*) for simple substituents are **ignored** in alphabetization.
   - However, for complex branched substituents inside parentheses, multiplying prefixes within the substituent (e.g., *dimethyl*) **are** alphabetized!

```
Common vs. Systematic Names of Complex Alkyl Groups:
- Isopropyl:     -(1-methylethyl)
- Isobutyl:      -(2-methylpropyl)
- sec-Butyl:     -(1-methylpropyl)
- tert-Butyl:    -(1,1-dimethylethyl)
- Neopentyl:     -(2,2-dimethylpropyl)
- Isopentyl:     -(3-methylbutyl)
```

# SECTION 5: EXHAUSTIVE NOMENCLATURE BY FUNCTIONAL GROUP CLASS

### 5.1 Alkanes, Alkenes, Alkynes
- **Example 5.1.1:**
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-C(CH_3)_2-CH(C_2H_5)-CH_2-CH_3}
$$

  - Longest chain: 6 carbons (Hexane).
  - Numbering from left gives locants $2,2,3$; numbering from right gives $4,5,5$. Lower locant set: $2,2,3$.
  - Substituents: 2,2-dimethyl and 3-ethyl.
  - Alphabetical: ethyl before methyl.
  - **Name:** $\mathbf{\text{3-Ethyl-2,2-dimethylhexane}}$

- **Example 5.1.2:**
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_2=CH-CH(CH_3)-C\equiv CH}
$$

  - Numbering: Double bond starts at 1, triple bond at 5 vs triple bond at 1, double bond at 4.
  - Direction 1: locants for multiple bonds = 1 (ene), 4 (yne). Direction 2: locants = 1 (yne), 4 (ene).
  - Multiple bond locants tie ($1,4$). Double bond wins tie-breaker!
  - Left carbon is $\mathrm{C_1}$.
  - Substituent: 3-methyl.
  - **Name:** $\mathbf{\text{3-Methylpent-1-en-4-yne}}$

***

### 5.2 Halogen & Nitro Derivatives
Always named as prefixes (*halo-* and *nitro-*).
- **Example 5.2.1:**
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH(Cl)-CH(Br)-CH_3}
$$

  - Numbering from either side yields locants $2,3$.
  - Alphabetical priority: 2-bromo precedes 3-chloro.
  - **Name:** $\mathbf{\text{2-Bromo-3-chlorobutane}}$

- **Example 5.2.2:**
  

$$
\mathrm{O_2N-CH_2-CH=CH-CH_3}
$$

  - Multiple bond takes priority over prefix ($-\mathrm{NO_2}$).
  - Double bond gets locant 2 from right: $\mathrm{\overset{4}{C}H_2(NO_2)-\overset{3}{C}H=\overset{2}{C}H-\overset{1}{C}H_3}$.
  - **Name:** $\mathbf{\text{4-Nitrobut-2-ene}}$

***

### 5.3 Alcohols ($-\mathrm{OH}$) and Thiols ($-\mathrm{SH}$)
Suffix: **-ol**, **-thiol**. Prefix: **hydroxy-**, **mercapto-** (or **sulfanyl-**).
- **Example 5.3.1:**
  

$$
\mathrm{HO-CH_2-CH_2-CH(OH)-CH_3}
$$

  - Numbering from right: locants $2,4$ for $-\mathrm{OH}$.
  - Numbering from left: locants $1,3$ for $-\mathrm{OH}$.
  - Lower locants: $1,3$.
  - **Name:** $\mathbf{\text{Butane-1,3-diol}}$ (Terminal "e" of butane retained because "d" is a consonant).

- **Example 5.3.2:**
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_2=CH-CH(OH)-CH_3}
$$

  - Functional group ($-\mathrm{OH}$) has higher priority than double bond.
  - Numbering from right gives $-\mathrm{OH}$ locant 2.
  - Chain: 4 carbons (but-3-en-2-ol).
  - **Name:** $\mathbf{\text{But-3-en-2-ol}}$

***

### 5.4 Carbonyl Compounds: Aldehydes ($-\mathrm{CHO}$) & Ketones ($\mathrm{>C=O}$)
- Aldehyde suffix: **-al**; Ketone suffix: **-one**.
- Aldehyde is higher in priority than ketone. When both are present:
  - $-\mathrm{CHO}$ is principal ($\implies$ suffix **-al**).
  - $\mathrm{>C=O}$ is secondary ($\implies$ prefix **oxo-**).
- **Example 5.4.1:**
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CO-CH_2-CH_2-CHO}
$$

  - Carbons: $\mathrm{\overset{5}{C}H_3-\overset{4}{C}O-\overset{3}{C}H_2-\overset{2}{C}H_2-\overset{1}{C}HO}$
  - Principal functional group: $-\mathrm{CHO}$ at $\mathrm{C_1}$.
  - Substituent: oxo at $\mathrm{C_4}$.
  - **Name:** $\mathbf{\text{4-Oxopentanal}}$

- **Example 5.4.2 (Dialdehyde):**
  

$$
\mathrm{OHC-CH_2-CH_2-CH_2-CHO}
$$

  - **Name:** $\mathbf{\text{Pentanedial}}$

***

### 5.5 Carboxylic Acids & Acid Derivatives
- **Carboxylic Acid:** $\mathrm{CH_3-CH(OH)-COOH} \implies \mathbf{\text{2-Hydroxypropanoic acid}}$ (Lactic acid).
- **Ester:** Named as *alkyl alkanoate*. The alkyl group attached directly to oxygen ($-\mathrm{O-R}$) is written **first** as an independent separate word.
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-COO-CH_3}
$$

  - Alkyl group on oxygen: methyl.
  - Acyl chain: 3 carbons (propanoate).
  - **Name:** $\mathbf{\text{Methyl propanoate}}$
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-COO-CH_2-CH_2-Cl}
$$

  - Alkyl group on oxygen: 2-chloroethyl.
  - Acyl group: ethanoate.
  - **Name:** $\mathbf{\text{2-Chloroethyl ethanoate}}$
- **Acid Halide:** $\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-COCl} \implies \mathbf{\text{Propanoyl chloride}}$
- **Amide:**
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CONH-CH_2-CH_3}
$$

  - Nitrogen-substituted alkyl group: prefix $N$-ethyl.
  - Acyl parent: ethanamide.
  - **Name:** $\mathbf{N\text{-Ethylethanamide}}$

***

### 5.6 Amines ($-\mathrm{NH_2}, -\mathrm{NHR}, -\mathrm{NR_2}$)
Suffix: **-amine**. Prefix: **amino-**.
- Secondary/tertiary amines are named as $N$-substituted derivatives:
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-NH-CH(CH_3)_2}
$$

  - Longest carbon chain attached to nitrogen: propane (3 carbons).
  - Position of amine on chain: locant 2 (propan-2-amine).
  - Substituent on nitrogen: methyl ($N$-methyl).
  - **Name:** $\mathbf{N\text{-Methylpropan-2-amine}}$
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-N(C_2H_5)-CH_2-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3}
$$

  - Longest chain: butane (butan-1-amine).
  - Substituents on nitrogen: ethyl and methyl.
  - **Name:** $\mathbf{N\text{-Ethyl-}N\text{-methylbutan-1-amine}}$

# SECTION 6: AROMATIC COMPOUNDS NOMENCLATURE

### 6.1 Monosubstituted Benzene Derivatives
Common names accepted by IUPAC:
- Methylbenzene $\to$ **Toluene**
- Methoxybenzene $\to$ **Anisole**
- Aminobenzene $\to$ **Aniline**
- Hydroxybenzene $\to$ **Phenol**
- Benzenecarboxylic acid $\to$ **Benzoic acid**
- Benzenecarbaldehyde $\to$ **Benzaldehyde**
- Benzenesulfonic acid $\to$ **Benzenesulfonic acid**
- Acetophenone $\to$ **1-Phenylethan-1-one** (Methyl phenyl ketone)

When benzene is a substituent, $\mathrm{C_6H_5}-$ is called **Phenyl**. The $\mathrm{C_6H_5-CH_2}-$ group is called **Benzyl**.

***

### 6.2 Disubstituted & Polysubstituted Benzenes [⭐⭐⭐⭐]
1. **Disubstituted positional prefixes (Common names only):**
   - $1,2$-substitution: **ortho- ($o$-)**
   - $1,3$-substitution: **meta- ($m$-)**
   - $1,4$-substitution: **para- ($p$-)**
   *(IUPAC requires numbers: $1,2-$, $1,3-$, and $1,4-$).*

2. **Polysubstituted Systems:**
   - If a group imparts a base name (e.g., $-\mathrm{OH}$ imparts *phenol*, $-\mathrm{NH_2}$ imparts *aniline*), that principal group automatically defines carbon **$\mathrm{C_1}$**.
   - Number the remaining carbons to give substituents the lowest possible locants.
   

#### Exemplar Case Studies:
- **2-Chloro-4-nitrophenol:**
  $-\mathrm{OH}$ defines $\mathrm{C_1}$. Numbering toward $-\mathrm{Cl}$ gives locant 2, and $-\mathrm{NO_2}$ gets 4.
- **2,4,6-Tribromoaniline:**
  $-\mathrm{NH_2}$ is on $\mathrm{C_1}$. Bromines are at $2,4,6$.
- **When no group confers a special base name:**
  Number according to the lowest locant set; if locants tie, use alphabetical priority.
  

$$
\text{1-Chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene}
$$

  Locants $1,2,4$ beat $1,3,4$ or $1,4,6$.

# SECTION 7: HIGH-YIELD NCERT EXEMPLAR & APPLICATION PROBLEMS

### Problem 7.1 (NCERT Exemplar)
**Write the IUPAC name of the following compound:**

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH(OH)-CH_2-CO-CH_3}
$$

- **Step 1: Identify functional groups.**
  Ketone ($\mathrm{>C=O}$) and secondary alcohol ($-\mathrm{OH}$).
- **Step 2: Seniority.**
  Ketone > Alcohol. Thus, ketone is the Principal Functional Group (suffix **-one**). Alcohol is a substituent (prefix **hydroxy-**).
- **Step 3: Longest continuous carbon chain containing PFG.**
  5 carbons $\implies$ Pentan-.
- **Step 4: Numbering the chain.**
  - Left-to-right: $\mathrm{C_2}$ has $-\mathrm{OH}$, $\mathrm{C_4}$ has $\mathrm{=O}$.
  - Right-to-left: $\mathrm{C_2}$ has $\mathrm{=O}$, $\mathrm{C_4}$ has $-\mathrm{OH}$.
  - PFG ($\mathrm{=O}$) must receive the lowest locant $\implies \mathrm{C_2}$.
- **Step 5: Assemble the components:**
  - Prefix: 4-hydroxy
  - Root: pentan
  - Suffix: 2-one

$$
\mathbf{\text{Answer: 4-Hydroxypentan-2-one}}
$$

***

### Problem 7.2 (NCERT Advanced Problem)
**Provide the IUPAC name for:**

$$
\mathrm{CH_2=CH-CH_2-CH(CH=CH_2)-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3}
$$

- **Step 1: Determine the longest chain with maximum number of multiple bonds.**
  - Straight chain from left to right: contains 7 carbons but only ONE double bond.
  - Chain incorporating both double bonds:
    

$$
\mathrm{\overset{1}{C}H_2=\overset{2}{C}H-\overset{3}{C}H_2-\overset{4}{C}H(CH_2CH_2CH_3)-\overset{5}{C}H=\overset{6}{C}H_2}
$$

    Contains 6 carbons and **TWO double bonds**. Multiple bond count strictly takes priority over length!
- **Step 2: Numbering.**
  Symmetrical multiple bonds at positions $1$ and $5$ from both sides.
  - Left-to-right: locant for substituent at $\mathrm{C_4}$.
  - Right-to-left: locant for substituent at $\mathrm{C_3}$.
  - Right-to-left wins lowest locant rule for substituent!
  

$$
\mathrm{\overset{6}{C}H_2=\overset{5}{C}H-\overset{4}{C}H_2-\overset{3}{C}H(CH_2CH_2CH_3)-\overset{2}{C}H=\overset{1}{C}H_2}
$$

- **Step 3: Identify substituent:** At $\mathrm{C_3}$, we have a propyl group ($-\mathrm{CH_2CH_2CH_3}$).
- **Step 4: Assemble name:**

$$
\mathbf{\text{Answer: 3-Propylhexa-1,5-diene}}
$$

***

### Problem 7.3 (NCERT Cyclic Compound)
**Name the compound:**

$$
\text{Cyclohex-2-en-1-ol vs Cyclohex-1-en-3-ol}
$$

- Functional groups: $-\mathrm{OH}$ (alcohol) and $\mathrm{C=C}$ (double bond).
- $-\mathrm{OH}$ outranks $\mathrm{C=C} \implies -\mathrm{OH}$ carbon is strictly assigned **$\mathrm{C_1}$**.
- Now number around the ring towards the double bond to give it the lowest possible locant:
  - Path A: $\mathrm{C_1(OH) \to C_2(=CH-) \to C_3(=CH-)}$ (Double bond locant = 2).
  - Path B: $\mathrm{C_1(OH) \to C_6 \to C_5(=CH-) \to C_4(=CH-)}$ (Double bond locant = 5).

$$
\mathbf{\text{Answer: Cyclohex-2-en-1-ol}}
$$

***

### Problem 7.4 (Bifunctional Ester)
**Name the compound:**

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-O-CO-CH_2-CH_2-COOH}
$$

- Principal group: $-\mathrm{COOH}$ outranks $-\mathrm{COOR}$.
- Principal chain: 4 carbons ($\mathrm{\overset{4}{C}OOR-\overset{3}{C}H_2-\overset{2}{C}H_2-\overset{1}{C}OOH}$).
- Suffix: **-butanedioic acid** (or substituent at $\mathrm{C_4}$).
- When an ester group is treated as a substituent with its carbonyl pointing toward the chain: $-\mathrm{COOCH_3}$ is **methoxycarbonyl**.

$$
\mathbf{\text{Answer: 4-Methoxy-4-oxobutanoic acid (or 3-(methoxycarbonyl)propanoic acid)}}
$$

# SECTION 8: EXAMINER TRAPS & FREQUENT MISTAKES

> [!WARNING]
> ### The Top 6 Board & Competitive Exam Traps:
>
> 1. **The "e" Elision Blunder:**
>    - Writing `Propane-1-ol` $\to$ **WRONG** (terminal "e" must drop before "o"). Correct: **Propan-1-ol**.
>    - Writing `Propandioic acid` $\to$ **WRONG** ("e" is retained before consonant "d"). Correct: **Propanedioic acid**.
>    - Writing `But-2-en-4-yne` $\to$ **WRONG** ("e" dropped before "y"). Correct: **But-1-en-3-yne**.
>
> 2. **Punctuation Rules Violations:**
>    - Numbers are separated from numbers by **commas** (e.g., $2,3$-).
>    - Numbers are separated from words by **hyphens** (e.g., -methyl-).
>    - No spaces are allowed anywhere in an IUPAC name (it must be written as a continuous single word), with the **sole exception of Esters** (e.g., `Ethyl ethanoate`) and **Acid Anhydrides/Acids** (e.g., `Ethanoic acid`).
>
> 3. **The Sum Rule Myth:**
>    - Evaluating the sum of locants instead of the **first point of difference**. A locant set of $2,7,8$ beats $3,4,5$ unconditionally because $2 < 3$.
>
> 4. **Counting Carbons of Functional Groups Twice:**
>    - In $\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CN}$, students often name it "Propylnitrile".
>    - **Reality:** The carbon of $-\mathrm{CN}$ is already carbon-1 of the chain! The name is **Propanenitrile**.
>    - For $\mathrm{CH_3-CHO}$, naming it "Methyl aldehyde" or "Methanal" $\to$ **Ethan-1-al** (contains 2 carbons!).
>
> 5. **Alphabetizing Multiplying Prefixes:**
>    - In a compound with `diethyl` and `trimethyl`, students alphabetize `d` before `t`.
>    - **Reality:** Simple multiplying prefixes (*di, tri, tetra*) are **completely ignored** in alphabetization. Compare **ethyl (e)** vs **methyl (m)**. Ethyl comes first regardless of multiplying prefixes!
>    - *Exception:* Complex prefixes containing multiplying prefixes within parentheses (e.g., `(1,2-dimethylpropyl)`) **do** consider the 'd'.
>
> 6. **Principal Functional Group Carbon in Cyclic Compounds:**
>    - Ring carbon attached to $-\mathrm{COOH}$ cannot include the carbonyl carbon inside the ring.
>    - Cyclohexane with $-\mathrm{COOH}$ is **NOT** "Cyclohexanoic acid"!
>    - **Reality:** The special suffix must be used: **Cyclohexanecarboxylic acid**.

### 9.3 Degree of Unsaturation (Double Bond Equivalent - DBE)
Before naming an unknown structure, compute its DBE to instantly predict rings and multiple bonds:

$$
\mathbf{\text{DBE} = C + 1 - \frac{H}{2} - \frac{X}{2} + \frac{N}{2}}
$$

Where:
- $C = \text{Number of Carbon atoms}$
- $H = \text{Number of Hydrogen atoms}$
- $X = \text{Number of Halogen atoms } (\mathrm{F, Cl, Br, I})$
- $N = \text{Number of Nitrogen atoms}$
*(Oxygen and Sulfur are bivalent and do not affect DBE).*

#### Interpretation:
- $\text{DBE} = 0 \implies \text{Completely saturated, open chain.}$
- $\text{DBE} = 1 \implies \text{One double bond OR one ring.}$
- $\text{DBE} = 2 \implies \text{One triple bond, two double bonds, two rings, or one double bond + one ring.}$
- $\text{DBE} \ge 4 \implies \text{Strong indicator of a Benzene ring (1 ring + 3 double bonds = 4 DBE).}$

## SUMMARY CHECKLIST FOR WRITING IUPAC NAMES IN EXAMS
1. Locate the **Principal Functional Group** (highest priority).
2. Trace the **Longest Continuous Carbon Chain** containing the PFG and maximum multiple bonds.
3. Number from the end that gives **PFG $\to$ Multiple Bonds $\to$ Lowest Locant Set for Substituents $\to$ Alphabetical Priority**.
4. Group identical substituents using prefixes (*di-, tri-*, etc.).
5. Arrange all secondary prefixes in **strict alphabetical order**.
6. Check for **vowel elision** between the primary suffix and secondary suffix (*drop "e" before a, e, i, o, u, y*).
7. Ensure all punctuation is correct (**commas** between digits, **hyphens** between letters and digits, no illegal spaces).

# 1. FOUNDATIONAL THEORY & CLASSIFICATION OVERVIEW

### Definition of Isomerism ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
The phenomenon of existence of two or more chemical compounds having the **same molecular formula** but **different physical and chemical properties** is termed **isomerism**. The individual compounds are called **isomers** (Greek: *isos* = equal, *meros* = parts).

### Root Causes of Isomeric Variation
1. Difference in connectivity/linkage of atoms (Constitutional/Structural Isomerism).
2. Difference in three-dimensional spatial orientation of atoms or groups with identical connectivity (Stereoisomerism).

```
                              ISOMERISM
               Same Molecular Formula, Different Properties

(Constitutional: Different Connectivity)            (Identical Connectivity, Different 3D Arrangement)
         │                                                   │
  ┌──────┴──────┬──────────┬──────────┬──────────┐    ┌──────┴──────┐
  │             │          │          │          │    │             │
Chain       Position   Functional  Metamerism Tautomerism Geometrical  Optical
Isomerism   Isomerism   Group      Isomerism              Isomerism  Isomerism
                       Isomerism              (Prototropy) (cis/trans) (Chiral)
```

***

## 1.1 Degree of Unsaturation / Double Bond Equivalent (DBE) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Before sketching structural isomers for an unknown molecular formula, calculate the **Index of Hydrogen Deficiency (IHD)** or **Double Bond Equivalent (DBE)**. DBE determines the total number of $\pi$-bonds and/or rings present in the molecule.

### General Formula
For an organic compound with general molecular formula $\mathrm{C}_c \mathrm{H}_h \mathrm{N}_n \mathrm{O}_o \mathrm{X}_x$ (where $\mathrm{X} = \mathrm{F, Cl, Br, I}$):

$$
\mathrm{DBE} = c + 1 - \frac{h}{2} - \frac{x}{2} + \frac{n}{2}
$$

*(Note: Bivalent atoms like Oxygen $\mathrm{O}$ and Sulfur $\mathrm{S}$ do not alter the DBE equation).*

### Structural Implications of DBE
- $\mathrm{DBE} = 0 \implies$ Fully saturated acyclic hydrocarbon/derivative (Alkanes, Alcohols, Ethers, Amines).
- $\mathrm{DBE} = 1 \implies$ One double bond ($\mathrm{C=C}$, $\mathrm{C=O}$) **OR** one monocyclic ring.
- $\mathrm{DBE} = 2 \implies$ One triple bond ($\mathrm{C\equiv C}$, $\mathrm{C\equiv N}$), **OR** two double bonds, **OR** one double bond + one ring, **OR** a bicyclic system.
- $\mathrm{DBE} \ge 4 \implies$ High probability of a benzene ring ($\text{Benzene core} = 1\text{ ring} + 3\ \pi\text{-bonds} \implies \mathrm{DBE} = 4$).

***

# 2. STRUCTURAL (CONSTITUTIONAL) ISOMERISM

Structural isomers are molecules sharing an identical molecular formula whose atoms are connected in a different order (different structural formulae).

***

## 2.1 Chain (Skeletal) Isomerism ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **NCERT Definition:** When two or more compounds have similar molecular formula but different carbon skeletons (straight vs. branched chains), they are referred to as chain isomers.
- **Minimum Carbons Required:**
  - Alkanes: Minimum **4 Carbons** ($\mathrm{C_4H_{10}}$).
  - Alkenes: Minimum **4 Carbons** ($\mathrm{C_4H_8}$).
  - Alkynes: Minimum **5 Carbons** ($\mathrm{C_5H_8}$).

### Case Study: $\mathrm{C_5H_{12}}$ (Pentane Isomers)

$$
\mathrm{DBE} = 5 + 1 - \frac{12}{2} = 0
$$

1. **$n$-Pentane (Pentane):**
   

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3}
$$

   - Boiling point: $309.1\ \mathrm{K}$ ($36.1^\circ\mathrm{C}$)
2. **Isopentane (2-Methylbutane):**
   

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH(CH_3)-CH_2-CH_3}
$$

   - Boiling point: $301.0\ \mathrm{K}$ ($28.0^\circ\mathrm{C}$)
3. **Neopentane (2,2-Dimethylpropane):**
   

$$
\mathrm{C(CH_3)_4}
$$

   - Boiling point: $282.5\ \mathrm{K}$ ($9.5^\circ\mathrm{C}$)

> **Physical Law (Surface Area & Van der Waals Forces):**
> Branching converts the extended, elongated molecular shape into a more compact, spherical geometry. A sphere possesses the minimum surface-area-to-volume ratio. 
> 

$$
\text{Extent of Branching} \uparrow \implies \text{Surface Area} \downarrow \implies \text{Magnitude of London Dispersion Forces} \downarrow \implies \text{Boiling Point} \downarrow
$$

***

## 2.2 Position Isomerism ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **NCERT Definition:** When two or more compounds differ in the position of a substituent atom, functional group, or multiple bond (double/triple bond) on the same carbon skeleton, they are called position isomers.
- **Critical Requirement:** The parent carbon backbone must remain **identical** in size and connectivity.

### Classic Exemplar Pairs
1. **Dihaloalkanes ($\mathrm{C_3H_6Cl_2}$):**
   - 1,1-Dichloropropane: $\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CHCl_2}$ (gem-dichloride)
   - 1,2-Dichloropropane: $\mathrm{CH_3-CH(Cl)-CH_2Cl}$ (vic-dichloride)
   - 1,3-Dichloropropane: $\mathrm{Cl-CH_2-CH_2-CH_2-Cl}$
   - 2,2-Dichloropropane: $\mathrm{CH_3-CCl_2-CH_3}$
   *(Here, 1,1- and 2,2- are position isomers; 1,2- and 1,3- are position isomers).*

2. **Alkenyl and Alkynyl Systems:**
   - But-1-ene ($\mathrm{CH_2=CH-CH_2-CH_3}$) and But-2-ene ($\mathrm{CH_3-CH=CH-CH_3}$).
   - Pent-1-yne ($\mathrm{CH\equiv C-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3}$) and Pent-2-yne ($\mathrm{CH_3-C\equiv C-CH_2-CH_3}$).

3. **Disubstituted Benzene Rings ($\mathrm{C_6H_4X_2}$):**
   - 1,2-Dichlorobenzene (*ortho*)
   - 1,3-Dichlorobenzene (*meta*)
   - 1,4-Dichlorobenzene (*para*)

***

## 2.3 Functional Group Isomerism ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Compounds having identical molecular formulae but possessing different functional groups exhibit functional group isomerism.

### Canonical Functional Pairs

#### 1. Alcohols and Ethers ($\mathrm{C}_n \mathrm{H}_{2n+2} \mathrm{O}$, $\mathrm{DBE}=0$)
- **Formula:** $\mathrm{C_2H_6O}$
  - Ethanol: $\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-OH}$ (Intermolecular H-bonding, b.p. $\approx 351\ \mathrm{K}$, reacts with $\mathrm{Na}$ to release $\mathrm{H_2(g)}$).
  - Methoxymethane: $\mathrm{CH_3-O-CH_3}$ (Dipole-dipole interaction only, b.p. $\approx 249\ \mathrm{K}$, inert to $\mathrm{Na}$).

#### 2. Aldehydes and Ketones ($\mathrm{C}_n \mathrm{H}_{2n} \mathrm{O}$, $\mathrm{DBE}=1$)
- **Formula:** $\mathrm{C_3H_6O}$
  - Propanal: $\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CHO}$ (Gives positive Tollen's and Fehling's tests).
  - Propanone: $\mathrm{CH_3-CO-CH_3}$ (Negative Tollen's test; forms crystalline bisulfite adduct).
  - Allyl alcohol: $\mathrm{CH_2=CH-CH_2-OH}$ (Unsaturated alcohol isomer).
  - Prop-1-en-2-ol: $\mathrm{CH_2=C(OH)-CH_3}$ (Enol isomer).
  - Oxetane / Methyloxirane: Cyclic ether isomers.

#### 3. Carboxylic Acids and Esters ($\mathrm{C}_n \mathrm{H}_{2n} \mathrm{O}_2$, $\mathrm{DBE}=1$)
- **Formula:** $\mathrm{C_2H_4O_2}$
  - Ethanoic acid: $\mathrm{CH_3COOH}$ (Acidic, effervescence with $\mathrm{NaHCO_3}$).
  - Methyl methanoate: $\mathrm{HCOOCH_3}$ (Ester, sweet fruity odor, non-acidic).

#### 4. Cyanides and Isocyanides ($\mathrm{C}_n \mathrm{H}_{2n-1} \mathrm{N}$, $\mathrm{DBE}=2$)
- **Formula:** $\mathrm{C_2H_3N}$
  - Ethanenitrile (Acetonitrile): $\mathrm{CH_3-C\equiv N}$
  - Methyl isocyanide (Methyl carbylamine): $\mathrm{CH_3-\overset{+}{N}\equiv \overset{-}{C}}$

#### 5. Nitroalkanes and Alkyl Nitrites ($\mathrm{R-NO_2}$ vs $\mathrm{R-ONO}$)
- **Formula:** $\mathrm{C_2H_5NO_2}$
  - Nitroethane: $\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-NO_2}$
  - Ethyl nitrite: $\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-O-N=O}$

#### 6. Primary, Secondary, and Tertiary Amines ($\mathrm{C}_n \mathrm{H}_{2n+3} \mathrm{N}$, $\mathrm{DBE}=0$)
- **Formula:** $\mathrm{C_3H_9N}$
  - Propan-1-amine ($1^\circ$): $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2NH_2}$
  - $N$-Methylethanamine ($2^\circ$): $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2NHCH_3}$
  - $N,N$-Dimethylmethanamine ($3^\circ$): $(\mathrm{CH_3})_3\mathrm{N}$

***

## 2.4 Metamerism ⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **NCERT Definition:** It arises due to an **unequal distribution of alkyl groups on either side of the same polyvalent functional group** (polyvalent heteroatoms like $-\mathrm{O}-$, $-\mathrm{S}-$, $-\mathrm{NH}-$, $-\mathrm{CO}-$, $-\mathrm{COO}-$).

### Case Study: Ethers of $\mathrm{C_4H_{10}O}$ ($\mathrm{DBE}=0$)
Polyvalent bridge: $-\mathrm{O}-$
1. **Ethoxyethane (Diethyl ether):**
   

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-O-CH_2-CH_3} \quad (\text{Ethyl} + \text{Ethyl})
$$

2. **1-Methoxypropane (Methyl propyl ether):**
   

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-O-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3} \quad (\text{Methyl} + \text{Propyl})
$$

3. **2-Methoxypropane (Methyl isopropyl ether):**
   

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-O-CH(CH_3)_2} \quad (\text{Methyl} + \text{Isopropyl})
$$

> **Hierarchy Priority Alert:**
> Between Diethyl ether and 1-Methoxypropane, the relationship is **strictly metamerism**. 
> Between 1-Methoxypropane and 2-Methoxypropane, the relationship can be classified as both **metamerism** and **position isomerism**. Under NCERT examination conventions:
> 

$$
\mathbf{Metamerism} > \mathbf{Position\ Isomerism}
$$

### Case Study: Ketones of $\mathrm{C_5H_{10}O}$ ($\mathrm{DBE}=1$)
Polyvalent bridge: $-\mathrm{C(=O)}-$
- Pentan-2-one: $\mathrm{CH_3-CO-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3}$ (Methyl + Propyl)
- Pentan-3-one: $\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CO-CH_2-CH_3}$ (Ethyl + Ethyl)
These are metamers.

***

## 2.5 Tautomerism (Dynamic Isomerism / Desmotropism) ⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Definition:** A special type of functional isomerism where two interconvertible constitutional isomers exist in rapid dynamic equilibrium, differing fundamentally in the point of attachment of a mobile hydrogen atom (proton) accompanied by a shift of $\pi$-electron density.
- **Prototropy:** The migration of a proton ($\mathrm{H^+}$) from one atom to another within the polyatomic framework.
- **Essential Structural Criterion:** The presence of at least one acidic $\alpha$-hydrogen ($\alpha\text{-}\mathrm{H}$) attached to an $sp^3$-hybridized carbon adjacent to an electron-withdrawing carbonyl or unsaturated group.

### The Keto-Enol System

$$
\mathrm{\underset{Keto\ form}{R-CH_2-\overset{\overset{O}{\parallel}}{C}-R'} \ \ \rightleftharpoons\ \ \underset{Enol\ form}{R-CH=\overset{\overset{OH}{\vert}}{C}-R'}}
$$

### Reaction Mechanism (Step-by-Step)

#### (A) Base-Catalyzed Enolization
- **Step 1: Deprotonation by base ($:\!\mathrm{B}$) generating resonance-stabilized enolate ion:**
  

$$
\mathrm{R-CH_2-\overset{\overset{O}{\parallel}}{C}-R' + :B \ \rightleftharpoons\ \left[ R-\bar{C}H-\overset{\overset{O}{\parallel}}{C}-R' \ \longleftrightarrow\ R-CH=\overset{\overset{O^-}{\vert}}{C}-R' \right] + H-B^+}
$$

- **Step 2: Protonation at the oxygen atom by conjugate acid ($\mathrm{H-B^+}$):**
  

$$
\mathrm{R-CH=\overset{\overset{O^-}{\vert}}{C}-R' + H-B^+ \ \rightleftharpoons\ R-CH=\overset{\overset{OH}{\vert}}{C}-R' + :B}
$$

#### (B) Acid-Catalyzed Enolization
- **Step 1: Protonation of the carbonyl oxygen:**
  

$$
\mathrm{R-CH_2-\overset{\overset{O}{\parallel}}{C}-R' + H^+ \ \rightleftharpoons\ R-CH_2-\overset{\overset{OH^+}{\parallel}}{C}-R'}
$$

- **Step 2: Elimination of $\alpha$-proton by solvent water ($\mathrm{H_2O}$):**
  

$$
\mathrm{R-CH_2-\overset{\overset{OH^+}{\parallel}}{C}-R' + H_2O \ \rightleftharpoons\ R-CH=\overset{\overset{OH}{\vert}}{C}-R' + H_3O^+}
$$

```
Base-Catalyzed:
  O                      O                       O⁻                     OH
  ║                      ║                       │                      │
R-C-CH₂-R'  + :B ⇌ [ R-C-CH-R'  ⟷  R-C=CH-R' ] + HB⁺ ⇌ R-C=CH-R' + :B
                         (Carbanion)             (Enolate)

Acid-Catalyzed:
  O                        OH⁺                     OH
  ║                        ║                       │
R-C-CH₂-R'  + H⁺ ⇌ R-C-CH₂-R'  + H₂O ⇌ R-C=CH-R' + H₃O⁺
```

### Factors Governing Percentage Enol Content
In simple monocarbonyls (acetone, acetaldehyde), the keto form dominates ($\approx 99.9\%$) because the bond dissociation energy of $\mathrm{C=O}$ ($\approx 745\ \mathrm{kJ\cdot mol^{-1}}$) is significantly greater than that of $\mathrm{C=C}$ ($\approx 610\ \mathrm{kJ\cdot mol^{-1}}$).

However, enol content rises dramatically under specific structural stabilization mechanisms:

#### 1. Intramolecular Hydrogen Bonding & Quasi-Aromatic Ring Formation ($\beta$-Dicarbonyls)
In **Acetylacetone (Pentane-2,4-dione)**, the enol content reaches $\approx 76\%$ in liquid phase and $>90\%$ in non-polar gas phase:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-\overset{\overset{O}{\parallel}}{C}-CH_2-\overset{\overset{O}{\parallel}}{C}-CH_3 \ \ \rightleftharpoons\ \ CH_3-\overset{\overset{O}{\parallel}}{C}-CH=\overset{\overset{OH}{\vert}}{C}-CH_3}
$$

The enol form forms a highly stable **6-membered pseudo-ring** through strong intramolecular hydrogen bonding ($\mathrm{O-H\cdots O=C}$) conjugated with the carbon-carbon double bond.

#### 2. Aromatization (Driving Force $\Delta H^\circ \ll 0$)
In **Cyclohexa-2,4-dien-1-one**, the enol tautomer is **Phenol**:

$$
\text{Keto form } (0\%) \rightleftharpoons \text{Phenol } (100\%)
$$

The enolization confers **aromatic stabilization resonance energy** ($\approx 150.6\ \mathrm{kJ\cdot mol^{-1}}$), forcing the equilibrium completely to the enol side.

***

## 2.6 Ring-Chain Isomerism ⭐⭐⭐
Molecules possessing the same molecular formula can exist as an open-chain compound or a cyclic compound.
- **Alkenes and Cycloalkanes ($\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n}$):**
  Propene ($\mathrm{CH_3-CH=CH_2}$) and Cyclopropane ($\mathrm{C_3H_6}$).
- **Alkynes, Alkadiens and Cycloalkenes ($\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n-2}$):**
  But-1-yne, Buta-1,3-diene, and Cyclobutene ($\mathrm{C_4H_6}$).

# 3. STEREOISOMERISM

Compounds with the same molecular formula and identical atom-to-atom connectivity, but different spatial orientations of their atoms in three dimensions.

$$
\mathrm{Stereoisomerism} = \begin{cases} \text{Conformational (Interconvertible by single bond rotation)} \\ \text{Configurational (Interconvertible only by bond breaking/making)} \begin{cases} \text{Geometrical} \\ \text{Optical} \end{cases} \end{cases}
$$

***

## 3.1 Geometrical Isomerism (cis / trans & E / Z) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### Fundamental Prerequisites for Geometrical Isomerism
1. **Restricted Rotation:** Must possess a site of hindered rotation (e.g., $\mathrm{C=C}$ $\pi$-bond, cyclic ring system, or cumulative double bonds).
2. **Non-Identical Ligands on Each End:** Each carbon atom involved in the hindered rotation axis must be independently attached to **two distinct atoms or groups**.

$$
\mathrm{C(ab)=C(cd)} \implies \text{Shows Geometrical Isomerism if } a \neq b \text{ and } c \neq d
$$

$$
\mathrm{C(aa)=C(cd)} \implies \text{No Geometrical Isomerism}
$$

```
    a         c             a         d
     \       /               \       /
      C  ═  C                 C  ═  C
     /       \               /       \
    b         d             b         c
     (Isomer 1)                  (Isomer 2)
```

***

### Alkene Systems: cis-trans Nomenclature

Consider **But-2-ene** ($\mathrm{CH_3-CH=CH-CH_3}$):

$$
\begin{array}{cc}
\begin{array}{c}
\mathrm{H_3C} \qquad \mathrm{CH_3} \\
\diagdown \quad \diagup \\
\mathrm{C} = \mathrm{C} \\
\diagup \quad \diagdown \\
\mathrm{H} \qquad\quad \mathrm{H}
\end{array}
&
\begin{array}{c}
\mathrm{H_3C} \qquad\quad \mathrm{H} \\
\diagdown \quad \diagup \\
\mathrm{C} = \mathrm{C} \\
\diagup \quad \diagdown \\
\mathrm{H} \qquad \mathrm{CH_3}
\end{array}
\\
cis-But-2-ene & trans-But-2-ene
\end{array}
$$

### Physical Properties Comparison: cis vs. trans

| Physical Property | *cis*-Isomer | *trans*-Isomer | Physical / Thermodynamic Reason |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dipole Moment ($\mu$)** | **Higher** ($\mu > 0$) | **Lower / Zero** ($\mu \approx 0$) | Individual bond vectors reinforce in *cis*; cancel by centrosymmetric opposition in *trans*. |
| **Boiling Point ($T_\text{b}$)** | **Higher** | **Lower** | Stronger dipole-dipole attractions in *cis* require greater thermal energy to vaporize. |
| **Melting Point ($T_\text{m}$)** | **Lower** | **Higher** | *trans*-isomer possesses higher molecular symmetry, allowing tighter, denser crystal lattice packing. |
| **Solubility (in polar solvents)**| **Higher** | **Lower** | Higher permanent dipole moment promotes superior solvation in polar media. |
| **Stability** | **Lower** | **Higher** | Steric repulsion (van der Waals strain) between bulky *cis* groups raises its enthalpy ($\Delta H_f^\circ$). |

$$
\mu_{\text{cis}} = \sqrt{\mu_1^2 + \mu_2^2 + 2\mu_1\mu_2\cos\theta} \quad (\theta = 60^\circ)
$$

$$
\mu_{\text{trans}} = \sqrt{\mu_1^2 + \mu_2^2 + 2\mu_1\mu_2\cos(180^\circ)} = |\mu_1 - \mu_2| = 0 \quad (\text{if } \mu_1 = \mu_2)
$$

***

### The CIP (Cahn-Ingold-Prelog) Priority Rules & E/Z System
When alkenes have tri- or tetra-substituted frameworks where *cis/trans* is ambiguous, IUPAC dictates the **$E/Z$ convention** ($E = \text{Entgegen}$, opposite; $Z = \text{Zusammen}$, together).

#### CIP Sequence Rules:
1. **Rule 1 (Atomic Number):** Higher atomic number takes precedence at the atom directly attached to the double bond carbon:
   

$$
\mathrm{I} > \mathrm{Br} > \mathrm{Cl} > \mathrm{F} > \mathrm{O} > \mathrm{N} > \mathrm{C} > \mathrm{H} > \text{lone pair}
$$

2. **Rule 2 (Isotopes):** If atomic numbers are identical, the isotope of higher mass number takes priority:
   

$$
{}^3\mathrm{H} \ (\mathrm{T}) > {}^2\mathrm{H} \ (\mathrm{D}) > {}^1\mathrm{H} \ (\mathrm{H}); \qquad {}^{14}\mathrm{C} > {}^{13}\mathrm{C} > {}^{12}\mathrm{C}
$$

3. **Rule 3 (Exploration Along Chain):** If the first atoms are identical, proceed along the path of highest priority atoms until the first point of difference is found:
   

$$
-\mathrm{CH_2-CH_3} \ (\text{bonded to } \mathrm{C, H, H}) \quad \text{loses to} \quad -\mathrm{CH(CH_3)_2} \ (\text{bonded to } \mathrm{C, C, H})
$$

4. **Rule 4 (Multiple Bonds):** Multiply-bonded atoms are duplicated or triplicated:
   

$$
-\mathrm{CH=O} \equiv -\mathrm{CH(O, O)}; \qquad -\mathrm{C\equiv N} \equiv -\mathrm{C(N, N, N)}; \qquad -\mathrm{COOH} \equiv -\mathrm{C(O, O, OH)}
$$

- **$Z$-Isomer:** High-priority groups are on the **same side** of the reference double bond axis.
- **$E$-Isomer:** High-priority groups are on **opposite sides** of the reference double bond axis.

***

### Geometrical Isomerism in Cycloalkanes
Ring-carbon single bonds cannot rotate $360^\circ$ without rupturing the ring. Hence, substituted cycloalkanes show geometrical isomerism:

**1,2-Dimethylcyclopropane:**
- *cis*-1,2-Dimethylcyclopropane: Both $-\mathrm{CH_3}$ groups point above the ring plane.
- *trans*-1,2-Dimethylcyclopropane: One $-\mathrm{CH_3}$ group points above, the other below the ring plane.

***

## 3.2 Optical Isomerism ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Definition:** Stereoisomers that possess identical physical properties (b.p., m.p., density, refractive index) but differ in their behavior toward plane-polarized light (PPL) are called **optical isomers**.

### Polarimeter & Optical Activity Terminology
- **Plane-Polarized Light (PPL):** Ordinary light consists of electromagnetic oscillations in all perpendicular planes. Passing through a **Nicol prism** (calcite $\mathrm{CaCO_3}$), it emerges oscillating in only a single plane.
- **Optical Activity:** The property of a substance to rotate the plane of polarization of PPL.
- **Dextrorotatory ($d$ or $+$):** Rotates the plane of PPL clockwise.
- **Laevorotatory ($l$ or $-$):** Rotates the plane of PPL counter-clockwise.

```
Ordinary Light        Nicol Prism        Plane-Polarized Light (PPL)      Chiral Sample         Rotated PPL
     │ * │                 │                         │                          │                    /
  ───┼───┼───    ───>   [CALCITE]     ───>           │               ───>     [CHIRAL]   ───>       /
     │ * │                 │                         │                          │                  /
(All directions)                                (Single plane)                                (Rotated by angle α)
```

### Specific Rotation Formula
The angle of optical rotation ($\alpha$) measured on a polarimeter depends on the path length, concentration, temperature, and wavelength of light used:

$$
[\alpha]_\lambda^T = \frac{\alpha}{l \times c}
$$

**Where:**
- $[\alpha]_\lambda^T$ = Specific rotation (in degrees per unit path-length and concentration; standard unit: ${}^\circ \cdot \mathrm{dm^{-1}\cdot g^{-1}\cdot mL}$)
- $\alpha$ = Observed rotation in degrees ($^\circ$)
- $l$ = Path length of polarimeter tube in **decimeters** ($\mathrm{dm}$, where $1\ \mathrm{dm} = 10\ \mathrm{cm}$)
- $c$ = Concentration of the solute in **grams per milliliter** ($\mathrm{g\cdot mL^{-1}}$)

***

### Molecular Chirality & Symmetry Elements
- **Chiral / Asymmetric Carbon:** An $sp^3$-hybridized carbon atom bonded to **four different atoms or groups**: $\mathrm{C(a)(b)(c)(d)}$.
- **Criterion for Optical Activity:** The necessary and sufficient condition for a molecule to exhibit optical activity is **chirality** (absence of any element of improper symmetry):
  1. **Plane of Symmetry ($\sigma$ or POS):** An imaginary plane that bisects a molecule into two halves that are mirror images of each other.
  2. **Center of Symmetry ($C_i$ or COS):** A point in space such that any line drawn through it from any atom encounters an identical atom at an equal distance on the opposite side.

$$
\text{Absence of both POS and COS} \implies \text{Chiral Molecule} \implies \text{Optically Active}
$$

***

### Enantiomers, Diastereomers, and Meso Compounds

#### 1. Enantiomers ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Definition:** Non-superimposable mirror-image stereoisomers.
- **Properties:**
  - Possess identical chemical reactivities with achiral reagents.
  - Possess identical physical constants (m.p., b.p., density, solubility).
  - Rotate plane-polarized light by **equal magnitudes but in opposite directions** ($+ \alpha$ and $- \alpha$).
  - Display distinct biological/pharmacological actions with chiral enzymes and receptors.

#### 2. Diastereomers ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Definition:** Stereoisomers that are **not mirror images** of each other.
- **Properties:**
  - Occur when a molecule contains two or more stereocenters and the configuration of at least one (but not all) is inverted.
  - Possess **different physical properties** (melting points, boiling points, solubilities, dipole moments).
  - Can be separated by standard fractional crystallization or distillation.

#### 3. Meso Compounds ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Definition:** A compound containing two or more stereocenters that is superimposable on its mirror image due to an **internal plane of symmetry (POS)** or center of symmetry (COS).
- **Net Optical Rotation:** **Zero ($[\alpha] = 0^\circ$)**.
- **Cause:** **Internal Compensation** (the optical rotation of one half of the molecule is cancelled by the equal and opposite rotation of the other half).

***

### Exemplar Optical Case Study: Tartaric Acid

$$
\mathrm{HOOC-CH(OH)-CH(OH)-COOH}
$$

Number of asymmetric carbons: $n = 2$ (Both stereocenters possess identical ligands: $-\mathrm{H}$, $-\mathrm{OH}$, $-\mathrm{COOH}$).

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
\begin{array}{c}
\mathrm{COOH} \\
\vert \\
\mathrm{H}-\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{OH} \\
\vert \\
\mathrm{HO}-\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{H} \\
\vert \\
\mathrm{COOH}
\end{array}
&
\begin{array}{c}
\mathrm{COOH} \\
\vert \\
\mathrm{HO}-\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{H} \\
\vert \\
\mathrm{H}-\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{OH} \\
\vert \\
\mathrm{COOH}
\end{array}
&
\begin{array}{c}
\mathrm{COOH} \\
\vert \\
\mathrm{H}-\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{OH} \\
\vert ---\text{POS}--- \\
\mathrm{H}-\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{OH} \\
\vert \\
\mathrm{COOH}
\end{array}
\\
(+)-Tartaric acid & (-)-Tartaric acid & meso-Tartaric acid \\
\text{(Optically Active)} & \text{(Optically Active)} & \text{(Optically Inactive, Internal Compensation)}
\end{array}
$$

- Pair $(+)$ and $(-)$ are **Enantiomers**.
- Pair $(+)$ and *meso* are **Diastereomers**.
- Pair $(-)$ and *meso* are **Diastereomers**.

#### Racemic Mixture / Modification ($dl$ or $\pm$)
An equimolar mixture of two enantiomers ($50\%\ d$ and $50\%\ l$). It is optically inactive due to **External Compensation** (the rotation caused by molecules of one enantiomer is cancelled by molecules of the opposite enantiomer). A racemic mixture can be separated into pure enantiomers via **optical resolution**.

### Example 1: Exhaustive Structural Enumeration
**Problem:** Write the condensed and bond-line structural formulas of all structural isomers of the formula $\mathrm{C_6H_{14}}$. Classify them as chain or position isomers.

**Solution:**

$$
\mathrm{DBE} = 6 + 1 - \frac{14}{2} = 0 \implies \text{Acyclic saturated alkane}
$$

1. **6-Carbon chain:**
   

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CH_2-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3} \quad \text{(Hexane)}
$$

2. **5-Carbon chain with one methyl branch:**
   - Attach $-\mathrm{CH_3}$ at C2:
     

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH(CH_3)-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3} \quad \text{(2-Methylpentane)}
$$

   - Attach $-\mathrm{CH_3}$ at C3:
     

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CH(CH_3)-CH_2-CH_3} \quad \text{(3-Methylpentane)}
$$

3. **4-Carbon chain with two methyl branches:**
   - Both methyls at C2:
     

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-C(CH_3)_2-CH_2-CH_3} \quad \text{(2,2-Dimethylbutane)}
$$

   - Methyls at C2 and C3:
     

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH(CH_3)-CH(CH_3)-CH_3} \quad \text{(2,3-Dimethylbutane)}
$$

**Total structural isomers = 5.**
- Relationship between 2-Methylpentane and 3-Methylpentane: **Position Isomers**.
- Relationship between Hexane and 2-Methylpentane: **Chain Isomers**.
- Relationship between 2,2-Dimethylbutane and 2,3-Dimethylbutane: **Position Isomers**.

***

### Example 2: Differentiating Isomer Types (NCERT Exemplar Archetype)
**Problem:** An organic liquid 'A' with formula $\mathrm{C_3H_8O}$ reacts with metallic sodium to liberate a gas that burns with a 'pop' sound. Another liquid 'B' of identical formula does not react with sodium.
(a) Identify the functional groups in A and B.
(b) Write their IUPAC names and state their isomeric relationship.
(c) Identify the position isomer of compound A.

**Solution:**

$$
\mathrm{DBE} = 3 + 1 - \frac{8}{2} = 0
$$

1. $\mathrm{C_3H_8O}$ with $\mathrm{DBE}=0$ represents an alcohol or an ether.
2. Reaction with $\mathrm{Na}$ indicates active hydrogen from an alcohol:
   

$$
2\mathrm{R-OH} + 2\mathrm{Na} \longrightarrow 2\mathrm{R-ONa} + \mathrm{H_2}\uparrow
$$

   Hence, **A is an alcohol**, and **B is an ether**.
3. **Identification:**
   - Compound A: Propan-1-ol ($\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CH_2-OH}$)
   - Compound B: Methoxyethane ($\mathrm{CH_3-O-CH_2-CH_3}$)
   - Relationship between A and B: **Functional Group Isomers**.
4. Position isomer of compound A:
   Move the $-\mathrm{OH}$ group from C1 to C2 along the same propane chain:
   

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH(OH)-CH_3} \quad \text{(Propan-2-ol)}
$$

***

### Example 3: Geometrical Isomerism Identification
**Problem:** Which of the following compounds exhibit geometrical isomerism?
(i) But-1-ene
(ii) 1,2-Dichloroethene
(iii) 1-Chlorobut-1-ene
(iv) 2-Methylbut-2-ene

**Solution Analysis:**
- **(i) But-1-ene ($\mathrm{CH_2=CH-CH_2-CH_3}$):**
  C1 is attached to two identical hydrogen atoms ($\mathrm{H, H}$). **Does not exhibit GI.**
- **(ii) 1,2-Dichloroethene ($\mathrm{CHCl=CHCl}$):**
  C1 has $\mathrm{-H}$ and $\mathrm{-Cl}$ ($\mathrm{H} \neq \mathrm{Cl}$); C2 has $\mathrm{-H}$ and $\mathrm{-Cl}$ ($\mathrm{H} \neq \mathrm{Cl}$). **Exhibits GI (*cis* and *trans*).**
- **(iii) 1-Chlorobut-1-ene ($\mathrm{CH(Cl)=CH-CH_2-CH_3}$):**
  C1 has $\mathrm{-H}$ and $\mathrm{-Cl}$; C2 has $\mathrm{-H}$ and $-\mathrm{CH_2CH_3}$. Both carbons have two distinct ligands. **Exhibits GI (*cis* and *trans*).**
- **(iv) 2-Methylbut-2-ene ($\mathrm{(CH_3)_2C=CH-CH_3}$):**
  C2 is attached to two identical methyl groups ($-\mathrm{CH_3}, -\mathrm{CH_3}$). **Does not exhibit GI.**

***

### Example 4: Quantitative Polarimetry Calculation
**Problem:** A solution containing $1.50\ \mathrm{g}$ of an optically active natural product in $20.0\ \mathrm{mL}$ of ethanol was placed in a $2.00\ \mathrm{dm}$ polarimeter tube. The observed rotation at $20^\circ\mathrm{C}$ using the sodium D-line was $+3.60^\circ$. Calculate the specific rotation of the solute.

**Step-by-Step Derivation:**
1. **List given parameters:**
   - Observed rotation, $\alpha = +3.60^\circ$
   - Tube length, $l = 2.00\ \mathrm{dm}$
   - Solute mass, $m = 1.50\ \mathrm{g}$
   - Solution volume, $V = 20.0\ \mathrm{mL}$

2. **Compute concentration ($c$):**
   

$$
c = \frac{m}{V} = \frac{1.50\ \mathrm{g}}{20.0\ \mathrm{mL}} = 0.075\ \mathrm{g\cdot mL^{-1}}
$$

3. **Apply the Biot-Fresnel equation:**
   

$$
[\alpha]_\mathrm{D}^{20} = \frac{\alpha}{l \times c}
$$

   

$$
[\alpha]_\mathrm{D}^{20} = \frac{+3.60^\circ}{2.00\ \mathrm{dm} \times 0.075\ \mathrm{g\cdot mL^{-1}}} = \frac{+3.60^\circ}{0.150} = +24.0^\circ
$$

$$
\mathbf{[\alpha]_\mathrm{D}^{20} = +24.0^\circ \cdot dm^{-1}\cdot g^{-1}\cdot mL}
$$

# 6. EXAMINER TRAPS & FREQUENT STUDENT PITFALLS

### 1. The Carbon Chain Identity Trap
- **Mistake:** Classifying Pentan-1-ol and 2-Methylbutan-1-ol as position isomers because the $-\mathrm{OH}$ group is on terminal carbons.
- **Correction:** The parent chain changed from 5 carbons to 4 carbons. They are **chain isomers**, not position isomers. Position isomerism requires the parent carbon framework to remain unchanged.

### 2. Metamerism vs. Position Isomerism Overlap
- **Mistake:** Labeling Pentan-2-one and Pentan-3-one exclusively as position isomers.
- **Correction:** Under NCERT guidelines, changing the alkyl groups flanking a bivalent functional group ($-\mathrm{CO}-$) is primarily **metamerism**. Always write metamerism first in board examinations.

### 3. Conflating Internal vs. External Compensation
- **Mistake:** Stating that a racemic mixture is inactive due to internal compensation.
- **Correction:**
  - *Meso* compounds $\implies$ **Internal Compensation** (within the same molecule).
  - Racemic mixtures $\implies$ **External Compensation** (between different enantiomer molecules).

### 4. Unit Errors in Polarimetry Calculations
- **Mistake:** Substituting tube length in centimeters ($\mathrm{cm}$) or concentration in $\mathrm{mol\cdot L^{-1}}$.
- **Correction:** Path length must be converted to **decimeters** ($10\ \mathrm{cm} = 1\ \mathrm{dm}$). Concentration must be in $\mathbf{g\cdot mL^{-1}}$ or $\mathbf{g\cdot cm^{-3}}$.

### 5. The Terminal Alkene Fallacy in Geometrical Isomerism
- **Mistake:** Assuming any alkene with a $\mathrm{C=C}$ bond shows *cis/trans* isomerism.
- **Correction:** Terminal alkenes ($\mathrm{R-CH=CH_2}$) have two hydrogen atoms on the terminal carbon ($\mathrm{C1}$) and can **never** exhibit geometrical isomerism.

# 1. Introduction & Reaction Anatomy

An organic reaction is a step-by-step sequence of elementary processes involving bond cleavage, redistribution of electron density, and formation of new bonds.

$$
\text{Substrate} + \text{Attacking Reagent} \xrightarrow{[\text{Solvent, Temp, Catalyst}]} [\text{Transition State(s)} / \text{Intermediates}] \longrightarrow \text{Products} + \text{By-products}
$$

- **Substrate**: The reactant supplying carbon to the newly formed bond.
- **Attacking Reagent**: The chemical species (electron-rich or electron-deficient) that attacks the substrate.
- **Reaction Mechanism**: A sequential, detailed description of bond breaking, bond formation, electron movements (curved arrow notations), energetic pathways, and intermediate geometries.

```
Curved Arrow Formalism (NCERT Standard):
  Double-barbed arrow ( ↷ ): Represents transfer of an electron PAIR (2 e⁻).
  Fish-hook / single-barbed arrow ( ↾ ): Represents transfer of a SINGLE electron (1 e⁻).
```

# 2. Fission of a Covalent Bond

### Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (Core Foundation)

Covalent bonds break in one of two distinct thermodynamic pathways depending on the electronegativity difference ($\Delta \chi$), solvent polarity, and the presence of external initiators (heat/light/peroxides).

```
                      ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
                      │        Covalent Bond Fission            │
                      └────────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                           │
                 ┌─────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┐
                 ▼                                                   ▼
       ┌───────────────────┐                               ┌───────────────────┐
       │ Heterolytic       │                               │ Homolytic         │
       │ Cleavage          │                               │ Cleavage          │
       └─────────┬─────────┘                               └─────────┬─────────┘
                 │                                                   │
     ┌───────────┴───────────┐                                       ▼
     ▼                       ▼                               Free Radicals
Carbocation              Carbanion                           (Neutral odd e⁻)
(C⁺, 6e⁻)                (C⁻, 8e⁻)
```

## 2.1 Heterolytic Cleavage (Heterolysis)

Heterolytic fission occurs when a covalent bond between two atoms breaks unsymmetrically such that the shared pair of electrons remains entirely with one of the fragments.

### Case A: Electronegativity of $Z > \text{C}$ ($\chi_Z > \chi_C$)
The more electronegative atom takes the bonding electron pair, generating a **carbocation** (carbonium ion) and an anion:

$$
\mathrm{R_3C - Z \xrightarrow{\text{Polar Solvent}} R_3C^{\oplus} + :Z^{\ominus}}
$$

### Case B: Electronegativity of $\text{C} > Y$ ($\chi_C > \chi_Y$)
Carbon is more electronegative than atom $Y$ (e.g., organometallic compounds where $Y = \mathrm{Mg}, \mathrm{Li}$), generating a **carbanion** and a cation:

$$
\mathrm{R_3C - Y \xrightarrow{\text{Aprotic/Polar Solvent}} R_3C^{\ominus} + Y^{\oplus}}
$$

### Energetics & Solvation:
Heterolysis requires high heterolytic bond dissociation energy ($\Delta H_{\text{heterolysis}}^\circ$). In the gas phase, this process is strictly unfavorable due to charge separation:

$$
\Delta H_{\text{heterolysis}}^\circ \gg \Delta H_{\text{homolysis}}^\circ
$$

In solution, heterolysis is facilitated by the **dielectric constant ($\varepsilon_r$)** and the **solvation enthalpy ($\Delta H_{\text{solv}}^\circ$)** of the solvent:

$$
\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H_{\text{cleavage}}^\circ - \Delta H_{\text{solvation}}^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ
$$

A higher solvent dielectric constant ($\varepsilon_r(\mathrm{H_2O}) \approx 78.4$) significantly lowers electrostatic attraction between ions according to Coulomb's Law:

$$
F = \frac{q_1 q_2}{4\pi \varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r r^2}
$$

***

## 2.2 Homolytic Cleavage (Homolysis)

Homolytic fission occurs when a covalent bond breaks symmetrically such that each departing atom or group retains one electron of the shared pair, forming **free radicals**.

$$
\mathrm{A - B \xrightarrow{h\nu \text{ or } \Delta \text{ or Peroxide}} A^\bullet + B^\bullet}
$$

$$
\mathrm{H_3C - CH_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta \ (> 500^\circ\mathrm{C})} H_3C^\bullet + ^\bullet CH_3}
$$

### Ideal Conditions for Homolysis (Mnemonic: `HELP`):
- **H**: Heat / High temperature ($T > 400^\circ\mathrm{C}$)
- **E**: Electricity / Electrolysis (e.g., Kolbe's electrolytic synthesis)
- **L**: Light / Photochemical radiation ($h\nu$, UV light)
- **P**: Peroxides ($\mathrm{R-O-O-R}$, benzoyl peroxide) or Non-polar solvents ($\mathrm{CCl_4}, \mathrm{CS_2}$)

***

## 2.3 Direct Comparison: Heterolysis vs. Homolysis

| Parameter | Homolytic Cleavage | Heterolytic Cleavage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Electron Distribution** | Symmetrical (1 electron to each) | Unsymmetrical (both electrons to one atom) |
| **Species Produced** | Neutral Free Radicals | Oppositely charged ions (Cation & Anion) |
| **Arrow Type** | Fish-hook arrow ($\curvearrowright$ single-headed) | Curved arrow ($\curvearrowright$ double-headed) |
| **Favorable Conditions** | Non-polar solvent, gas phase, $h\nu$, peroxides | Polar protic/aprotic solvents, presence of acid/base |
| **Energy Required** | Lower ($\Delta H_{\text{homolysis}}^\circ$) | Higher in gas phase; mitigated in solution by solvation |

***

# 3. Reactive Intermediates

### Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (Exam Decider)

Intermediates are short-lived, highly reactive, low-concentration chemical species that lie at local energy minima on a reaction coordinate diagram ($\tau \sim 10^{-12}\text{ to } 10^{-6}\text{ s}$).

***

## 3.1 Carbocations ($\mathrm{C^\oplus}$)

### Structure and Bonding:
- **Carbon Valence Electrons**: 6 electrons around carbon (sextet, electron-deficient, open shell).
- **Hybridization**: $sp^2$ hybridized.
- **Geometry**: Trigonal planar ($120^\circ$ bond angles).
- **Empty Orbital**: Unhybridized vacant $2p_z$ orbital oriented perpendicular to the molecular plane.
- **Magnetic Nature**: Diamagnetic (all 6 valence electrons are paired).

```
             Empty 2p_z orbital
                   ▲
                   │   (+)
                   │
              R    │    R
               \   │   /
                \  │  /
                 C ───
                   │
                   │
                   ▼
                   R
        [Planar: Bond angle = 120°]
```

### Order of Stability:

$$
\text{Tropylium} > \text{Triphenylmethyl} > \text{Diphenylmethyl} > \text{Benzyl} \approx \text{Allyl} > 3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ > \text{Methyl} > \text{Vinyl} > \text{Phenyl}
$$

$$
\mathrm{\left(CH_3\right)_3C^\oplus > \left(CH_3\right)_2CH^\oplus > CH_3CH_2^\oplus > CH_3^\oplus}
$$

### Factors Governing Carbocation Stability:
1. **Aromaticity**: The cycloheptatrienyl (tropylium) cation has $(4n + 2) = 6\ \pi$-electrons in an unbroken ring of $sp^2$ carbons, conferring exceptional aromatic stability.
2. **Resonance / Conjugation**: Overlap of the empty $p$-orbital with adjacent $\pi$-bonds or lone pairs (e.g., benzyl and allyl cations).
3. **Hyperconjugation**: $\sigma_{\mathrm{C-H}} \to p_{\text{vacant}}$ delocalization. Stability is directly proportional to the number of $\alpha$-hydrogens ($N_{\alpha\text{-H}}$):
   - $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3C^\oplus}$: $9\ \alpha\text{-H}$
   - $\mathrm{(CH_3)_2CH^\oplus}$: $6\ \alpha\text{-H}$
   - $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2^\oplus}$: $3\ \alpha\text{-H}$
   - $\mathrm{CH_3^\oplus}$: $0\ \alpha\text{-H}$
4. **Inductive Effect**: $+I$ alkyl groups disperse positive charge across the framework.

***

## 3.2 Carbanions ($\mathrm{C^\ominus}$)

### Structure and Bonding:
- **Carbon Valence Electrons**: 8 electrons (octet complete: 3 bonding pairs + 1 lone pair).
- **Hybridization**: Typically $sp^3$ (when localized); planar $sp^2$ if the lone pair participates in resonance.
- **Geometry**: Trigonal pyramidal (distorted tetrahedral due to lone pair–bond pair repulsions, bond angle $\sim 107^\circ$).
- **Magnetic Nature**: Diamagnetic.
- **Stereochemical Feature**: Rapid pyramidal inversion ("umbrella inversion" or amine-like inversion) with a low inversion barrier ($\approx 25\text{ to } 30\text{ kJ/mol}$), making isolated chiral aliphatic carbanions rapidly racemize unless constrained in a ring.

```
                   :  (Lone pair)
                   ▲
                   │
                   C (-)
                 / | \
                /  |  \
               R   R   R
        [Trigonal Pyramidal, sp³]
```

### Order of Stability:
Alkyl carbanions display the inverse stability trend of carbocations because $+I$ groups destabilize an already electron-rich center:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3^\ominus > CH_3CH_2^\ominus > \left(CH_3\right)_2CH^\ominus > \left(CH_3\right)_3C^\ominus}
$$

$$
\text{Methyl} > 1^\circ > 2^\circ > 3^\circ
$$

### Factors Governing Carbanion Stability:
1. **$s$-Character of the Anionic Carbon**: Greater $s$-character holds electrons closer to the nucleus, increasing stability:

$$
\mathrm{H-C\equiv C^\ominus \ (sp,\ 50\%\ s) > H_2C=CH^\ominus \ (sp^2,\ 33.3\%\ s) > CH_3-CH_2^\ominus \ (sp^3,\ 25\%\ s)}
$$

2. **Resonance Delocalization**: Highly stabilized by adjacent electron-withdrawing groups (carbonyl, cyano, nitro):

$$
\mathrm{CH_2=CH-CH_2^\ominus \longleftrightarrow ^\ominus CH_2-CH=CH_2}
$$

3. **Inductive Effect**: $-I$ groups stabilize by dispersing negative charge; $+I$ groups destabilize.

***

## 3.3 Carbon Free Radicals ($\mathrm{C^\bullet}$)

### Structure and Bonding:
- **Carbon Valence Electrons**: 7 electrons (odd-electron/paramagnetic species).
- **Hybridization**: $sp^2$ (shallow planar or rapidly inverting planar/shallow pyramid).
- **Geometry**: Planar (trigonal planar with the unpaired electron localized in the unhybridized $2p_z$ orbital).
- **Magnetic Nature**: Paramagnetic (unpaired electron; detected via ESR/EPR spectroscopy).

```
             Unpaired electron (•)
                   ▲
                   │
              R    │    R
               \   │   /
                \  │  /
                 C ───
                   │
                   │
                   ▼
                   R
        [Planar: Bond angle ≈ 120°]
```

### Order of Stability:

$$
\text{Triphenylmethyl} > \text{Diphenylmethyl} > \text{Benzyl} \approx \text{Allyl} > 3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ > \text{Methyl} > \text{Vinyl}
$$

$$
\mathrm{\left(CH_3\right)_3C^\bullet > \left(CH_3\right)_2CH^\bullet > CH_3CH_2^\bullet > CH_3^\bullet}
$$

### Stabilization Mechanisms:
1. **Hyperconjugation**: $\sigma_{\mathrm{C-H}} \to$ half-filled $p$-orbital interaction. Stability scales with the number of $\alpha$-hydrogens.
2. **Resonance**: Allylic and benzylic radicals distribute the single electron over multiple carbon centers.

***

## 3.4 Overview of Major Reactive Intermediates

| Feature | Carbocation ($\mathrm{R_3C^\oplus}$) | Carbanion ($\mathrm{R_3C^\ominus}$) | Free Radical ($\mathrm{R_3C^\bullet}$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Total valence electrons** | 6 | 8 | 7 |
| **Electron status** | Deficient (Electrophilic) | Rich (Nucleophilic) | Deficient (Odd $e^-$) |

| **Molecular Geometry** | Trigonal planar | Trigonal pyramidal (if $sp^3$) | Trigonal planar |
| **Bond Angle** | $120^\circ$ | $\approx 107^\circ$ | $120^\circ$ |
| **Magnetic Character** | Diamagnetic | Diamagnetic | Paramagnetic |
| **Stability Order (Alkyl)** | $3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ > \mathrm{\overset{\bullet}{C}H_3}$ | $\mathrm{\overset{\ominus}{C}H_3} > 1^\circ > 2^\circ > 3^\circ$ | $3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ > \mathrm{CH_3^\bullet}$ |

# 4. Attacking Reagents: Electrophiles and Nucleophiles

### Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Reagents are classified based on their electron density and propensity to accept or donate electron pairs during a reaction.

***

## 4.1 Electrophiles ($\mathrm{E^\oplus}$ / Electron-Seeking Reagents)

Species that are electron-deficient and can accept an electron pair from a substrate to form a covalent bond. They act as **Lewis acids**.

### Classification:
1. **Positively Charged Electrophiles**:
   

$$
\mathrm{H^\oplus,\ Cl^\oplus,\ Br^\oplus,\ I^\oplus,\ NO_2^\oplus,\ NO^\oplus,\ R_3C^\oplus,\ R-\overset{\oplus}{C}=O}
$$

   *(Caution: $\mathrm{Na^+,\ K^+,\ Ca^{2+},\ NH_4^+}$ are NOT electrophiles; their octets are complete and they cannot accept an electron pair).*
2. **Neutral Electrophiles with Incomplete Octets**:
   

$$
\mathrm{BF_3,\ BCl_3,\ AlCl_3,\ FeCl_3,\ ZnCl_2,\ :CH_2 \text{ (carbene)},\ :NH \text{ (nitrene)}}
$$

3. **Neutral Molecules with Polarized Multiple Bonds** (Central atom accepts electrons into an empty $d$-orbital or an antibonding $\pi^*$ orbital):
   

$$
\mathrm{CO_2 \ (O=C=O),\ SO_3,\ SO_2,\ R-CHO,\ R_2C=O,\ R-COCl}
$$

4. **Compounds with Expandable Valence Shells** (Possess vacant $d$-orbitals):
   

$$
\mathrm{PCl_5,\ SF_4,\ SiCl_4,\ SnCl_4}
$$

***

## 4.2 Nucleophiles ($\mathrm{Nu^\ominus}$ / Nucleus-Seeking Reagents)

Species that possess at least one unshared pair of electrons and can donate this electron pair to an electron-deficient center to form a covalent bond. They act as **Lewis bases**.

### Classification:
1. **Negatively Charged Nucleophiles**:
   

$$
\mathrm{H^\ominus \ (hydride),\ OH^\ominus,\ RO^\ominus,\ HS^\ominus,\ RS^\ominus,\ Cl^\ominus,\ Br^\ominus,\ I^\ominus,\ CN^\ominus,\ CH_3^\ominus}
$$

2. **Neutral Nucleophiles with Lone Pairs**:
   

$$
\mathrm{H_2\ddot{O},\ R-\ddot{O}-H,\ R-\ddot{O}-R,\ \ddot{N}H_3,\ R\ddot{N}H_2,\ R_2\ddot{N}H,\ R_3\ddot{N},\ R-\ddot{S}-H,\ \ddot{P}H_3}
$$

3. **$\pi$-Electron Rich Donors**:
   

$$
\mathrm{CH_2=CH_2,\ HC\equiv CH,\ \text{Benzene } (C_6H_6)}
$$

4. **Organometallic Reagents** (Carbon acts as carbanionic nucleophile):
   

$$
\mathrm{R-MgX \ (\text{Grignard Reagent}),\ R-Li,\ R_2CuLi \ (\text{Gilman Reagent})}
$$

***

## 4.3 Ambiphilic & Ambident Species

- **Ambiphilic Reagents**: Molecules containing both an electron-deficient and an electron-rich site, allowing them to act as both an electrophile and a nucleophile depending on reaction conditions.
  - Examples: $\mathrm{H_2O}$, $\mathrm{R-OH}$, $\mathrm{R-C\equiv N}$, $\mathrm{R-CHO}$.
- **Ambident Nucleophiles**: Nucleophiles that can donate electron pairs from two distinct nucleophilic atoms:
  - Cyanide / Isocyanide: $[:\mathrm{C \equiv N}:]^\ominus \implies \text{Attack via C} \to \text{Nitrile}; \text{Attack via N} \to \text{Isonitrile}$
  - Nitrite / Nitro: $[:\ddot{\mathrm{O}}-\ddot{\mathrm{N}}=\ddot{\mathrm{O}}]^\ominus \implies \text{Attack via O} \to \text{Alkyl nitrite}; \text{Attack via N} \to \text{Nitroalkane}$

***

# 5. Electronic Displacements in Covalent Bonds

Electron displacement effects govern the stability of intermediates, ground-state charge distribution, and dynamic reactivity of organic molecules.

```
               Electronic Displacement Effects
                              │
         ┌────────────────────┴────────────────────┐
         ▼                                         ▼
   Permanent Effects                        Temporary Effects
   (Operate in ground state)               (Require attacking reagent)
         │                                         │
   ┌─────┼─────────────┐                           ▼
   │     │             │                   Electromeric Effect
   ▼     ▼             ▼                        (E-effect)
Inductive Resonance Hyperconjugation
(I-effect) (R/M-effect) (Baker-Nathan)
```

***

## 5.1 Inductive Effect ($I$-Effect)

### Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### Fundamental Definition & Characteristics:
The **inductive effect** is the permanent polarization of a $\sigma$-bond arising from a difference in electronegativities between two covalently bonded atoms, causing a partial displacement of $\sigma$-electrons along a saturated carbon chain.

1. **Permanent Phenomenon**: Inherent property of the molecule in its ground electronic state.
2. **$\sigma$-Framework Exclusive**: Occurs purely through $\sigma$-bonds; $\pi$-electrons are not displaced.
3. **Distance-Dependent Fall-Off**: Electron transmission diminishes rapidly with distance. It is generally negligible beyond the 3rd carbon:

$$
\delta_1 + > \delta_2 + > \delta_3 + \gg \delta_4 + \approx 0
$$

$$
\mathrm{C_3^{\delta\delta\delta+} - C_2^{\delta\delta+} - C_1^{\delta+} - Cl^{\delta-}}
$$

4. **Reference Standard**: Hydrogen is taken as the arbitrary zero reference ($\mathrm{-H} = 0$).

***

### Classification of Inductive Groups:

#### Negative Inductive Effect ($-I$ Effect):
Electron-withdrawing groups (EWGs) pull $\sigma$-electron density away from carbon:

$$
\mathrm{-\overset{\oplus}{N}F_3 > -\overset{\oplus}{N}R_3 > -\overset{\oplus}{N}H_3 > -NO_2 > -SO_3H > -CN > -COOH > -F > -Cl > -Br > -I > -OR > -OH > -C\equiv CH > -NH_2 > -C_6H_5 > -CH=CH_2 > -H}
$$

#### Positive Inductive Effect ($+I$ Effect):
Electron-donating groups (EDGs) release $\sigma$-electron density toward carbon:

$$
\mathrm{-O^\ominus > -COO^\ominus > -CR_3 \ (3^\circ) > -CHR_2 \ (2^\circ) > -CH_2R \ (1^\circ) > -CD_3 > -CH_3 > -T > -D > -H}
$$

*(Note: Isotopic effect: Tritium ($-\mathrm{T}$) > Deuterium ($-\mathrm{D}$) > Protium ($-\mathrm{H}$) in $+I$ because heavier isotopes have a lower zero-point vibrational energy, leading to a shorter, stronger, more compact $\mathrm{C-D}$ bond that releases electron density more readily).*

***

### Quantitative Applications of the Inductive Effect:

### 1. Acid Strength of Carboxylic Acids ($\mathrm{R-COOH}$):

$$
\mathrm{R-COOH \xrightleftharpoons{K_a} R-COO^\ominus + H^\oplus}
$$

$$
K_a = \frac{[\mathrm{R-COO^\ominus}][\mathrm{H^\oplus}]}{[\mathrm{R-COOH}]}, \quad \mathrm{p}K_a = -\log_{10} K_a
$$

- A lower $\mathrm{p}K_a$ corresponds to higher acidity.
- **$-I$ groups** stabilize the conjugate base ($\mathrm{R-COO^\ominus}$) by dispersing the negative charge, shifting the equilibrium to the right ($\uparrow K_a, \downarrow \mathrm{p}K_a$).
- **$+I$ groups** concentrate negative charge on the carboxylate ion, destabilizing it ($\downarrow K_a, \uparrow \mathrm{p}K_a$).

$$
\mathrm{F-CH_2-COOH > Cl-CH_2-COOH > Br-CH_2-COOH > I-CH_2-COOH > CH_3-COOH}
$$

$$
\mathrm{CCl_3COOH > CHCl_2COOH > CH_2ClCOOH > CH_3COOH}
$$

### 2. Acid Strength Governed by Distance, Number, and Strength (DNPS Rule):
1. **D (Distance)**: A $-I$ group closer to $-\mathrm{COOH}$ exerts a stronger acidifying effect.
   

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CH(Cl)-COOH > CH_3-CH(Cl)-CH_2-COOH > CH_2(Cl)-CH_2-CH_2-COOH}
$$

2. **N (Number)**: Multiple $-I$ groups reinforce one another.
   

$$
\mathrm{Cl_3C-COOH > Cl_2CH-COOH > ClCH_2-COOH}
$$

3. **P / S (Power / Strength)**: More electronegative atoms exert a stronger effect at the same distance.
   

$$
\mathrm{F-CH_2-COOH > Cl-CH_2-COOH > Br-CH_2-COOH}
$$

***

## 5.2 Electromeric Effect ($E$-Effect)

### Priority: ⭐⭐⭐

### Definition & Characteristics:
The **electromeric effect** is a temporary displacement of a shared pair of $\pi$-electrons to one of the bonded atoms under the influence of an attacking reagent.

1. **Temporary Effect**: Operates only in the presence of an attacking reagent. Once the reagent is removed, the molecule reverts to its ground electronic state.
2. **Occurs in Unsaturated Systems**: Requires multiple bonds ($\mathrm{C=C},\ \mathrm{C\equiv C},\ \mathrm{C=O},\ \mathrm{C\equiv N}$).

***

### Types of Electromeric Effects:

#### +E Effect (Positive Electromeric Effect):
The $\pi$-electrons are transferred to the atom to which the attacking reagent attaches.

$$
\mathrm{CH_2=CH_2 + H^\oplus \longrightarrow CH_3-\overset{\oplus}{C}H_2}
$$

```
    ↷ 
H₂C = CH₂  +  H⁺  ───►  H₃C ── C⁺H₂
     └─── H⁺ attaches here
```

#### -E Effect (Negative Electromeric Effect):
The $\pi$-electrons are transferred to an atom other than the one to which the attacking reagent attaches.

$$
\mathrm{\left(CH_3\right)_2C=O + CN^\ominus \longrightarrow \left(CH_3\right)_2C(CN)-O^\ominus}
$$

```
       ↷
(CH₃)₂C = O  +  :CN⁻  ───►  (CH₃)₂C ── O⁻
      │                            │
      └─────── CN attaches here ───┘
```

***

## 5.3 Resonance / Mesomeric Effect ($R$/$M$-Effect)

### Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### Definition:
Resonance occurs when a molecule cannot be accurately represented by a single Lewis structure, but can be described as a hybrid of two or more contributing canonical structures that differ only in the distribution of $\pi$-electrons and non-bonding electrons.

The **Mesomeric Effect ($M$)** is the permanent electron transmission operating between $\pi$-bonds or between a $\pi$-bond and a lone pair on an adjacent conjugated atom.

***

### Conditions for Resonance (Conjugation Systems):
1. **$\pi-\sigma-\pi$ conjugation**: $\mathrm{CH_2=CH-CH=CH_2}$
2. **$\pi-\sigma-$ Lone pair**: $\mathrm{CH_2=CH-\ddot{O}H}$
3. **$\pi-\sigma-$ Vacant $p$-orbital (Carbocation)**: $\mathrm{CH_2=CH-\overset{\oplus}{C}H_2}$
4. **$\pi-\sigma-$ Odd electron (Free Radical)**: $\mathrm{CH_2=CH-\dot{C}H_2}$
5. **$\pi-\sigma-$ Carbanion**: $\mathrm{CH_2=CH-\overset{\ominus}{C}H_2}$
6. **Lone pair $-\sigma-$ Vacant orbital**: $\mathrm{CH_3-\ddot{O}-\overset{\oplus}{C}H_2 \longleftrightarrow CH_3-\overset{\oplus}{O}=CH_2}$

***

### Canonical Rules: Stability of Resonating Structures
To evaluate the relative contribution of canonical forms to the resonance hybrid:

1. **Octet Completeness**: Structures with complete octets for all second-period atoms ($\mathrm{C, N, O, F}$) contribute far more than structures with incomplete octets.
2. **Number of Covalent Bonds**: Structures with more covalent bonds are generally more stable.
3. **Charge Separation**: Neutral molecules are more stable than dipolar/charge-separated forms.
4. **Electronegativity / Charge Alignment**: Negative charges are more stable on more electronegative atoms ($\mathrm{O, N, F}$); positive charges are more stable on more electropositive atoms ($\mathrm{C}$).
5. **Like Charges Distance**: Structures with like charges on adjacent atoms are highly destabilized.

***

### Resonance Energy:
Resonance energy is the energy difference between the real resonance hybrid and the most stable theoretical canonical structure:

$$
E_{\text{resonance}} = \Delta H^\circ_{\mathrm{f,\ calculated\ (most\ stable\ canonical)}} - \Delta H^\circ_{\mathrm{f,\ experimental\ (hybrid)}}
$$

For benzene, the theoretical heat of hydrogenation of 1,3,5-cyclohexatriene is $3 \times (-119.5\text{ kJ/mol}) = -358.5\text{ kJ/mol}$. The experimental heat of hydrogenation of benzene is $-208.2\text{ kJ/mol}$:

$$
E_{\text{resonance}}(\text{Benzene}) = -358.5 - (-208.2) = -150.3\text{ kJ/mol} \quad (\approx 36\text{ kcal/mol})
$$

***

### Types of Mesomeric/Resonance Effects:

#### +R / +M Effect (Electron Donating via Resonance):
Substituent transfers lone-pair electrons into the conjugated $\pi$-system:

$$
\mathrm{-O^\ominus > -NR_2 > -NHR > -NH_2 > -OH > -OR > -NHCOR > -OCOR > -Ph > -F > -Cl > -Br > -I}
$$

**Resonance in Phenol ($+R$):**

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Canonical I:} & \quad \mathrm{C_6H_5-OH} \\
\text{Canonical II:} & \quad \text{Ortho-carbanion, oxygen has positive formal charge} \\
\text{Canonical III:} & \quad \text{Para-carbanion, oxygen has positive formal charge} \\
\text{Canonical IV:} & \quad \text{Ortho-carbanion, oxygen has positive formal charge} \\
\text{Canonical V:} & \quad \text{Equivalent to Canonical I with alternating double bonds}
\end{aligned}
$$

As a result of $+R$, electron density increases specifically at the **ortho-** and **para-** positions, directing electrophilic aromatic substitution to those sites.

#### -R / -M Effect (Electron Withdrawing via Resonance):
Substituent pulls $\pi$-electrons out of the conjugated $\pi$-system:

$$
\mathrm{-NO_2 > -CN > -SO_3H > -CHO > >C=O > -COCl > -COOR > -COOH > -CONH_2}
$$

**Resonance in Nitrobenzene ($-R$):**
Electron density is withdrawn from the ring, leaving the **ortho-** and **para-** positions positively polarized ($\delta+$). The **meta-** position retains comparatively higher electron density, making $-R$ groups **meta-directing** and **ring-deactivating** toward electrophiles.

***

## 5.4 Hyperconjugation (Baker-Nathan Effect / No-Bond Resonance)

### Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### Physical Definition:
**Hyperconjugation** is the stabilizing interaction resulting from the overlap of electrons in a $\sigma$-orbital (typically $\mathrm{C-H}$ or $\mathrm{C-C}$) with an adjacent empty or partially filled $p$-orbital, or with an adjacent $\pi^*$ antibonding molecular orbital.

- It is a **permanent effect**.
- Also called **$\sigma \to p$ or $\sigma \to \pi^*$ delocalization**.
- Coined **"no-bond resonance"** because the contributing structures show no covalent bond between the carbon and the $\alpha$-proton:

$$
\mathrm{H-C(\alpha) - C^\oplus \longleftrightarrow H^\oplus \quad C=C}
$$

***

### 1. Hyperconjugation in Carbocations ($\sigma_{\mathrm{C-H}} \to p_{\text{vacant}}$):

```
       H                            H
       │                            │
   H ─ C ─ C⁺H₂      ◄────►     H ─ C = CH₂     H⁺
       │                            │
       H                            H
   (Canonical I)                (Canonical II: "No-bond")
```

The total number of hyperconjugative structures ($N_{\text{structures}}$) is:

$$
N_{\text{structures}} = N_{\alpha\text{-H}} + 1
$$

- **Tert-butyl carbocation** ($\mathrm{(CH_3)_3C^\oplus}$): $9\ \alpha\text{-H} \implies 10$ structures.
- **Isopropyl carbocation** ($\mathrm{(CH_3)_2CH^\oplus}$): $6\ \alpha\text{-H} \implies 7$ structures.
- **Ethyl carbocation** ($\mathrm{CH_3CH_2^\oplus}$): $3\ \alpha\text{-H} \implies 4$ structures.
- **Methyl carbocation** ($\mathrm{CH_3^\oplus}$): $0\ \alpha\text{-H} \implies 1$ structure (no hyperconjugative stabilization).

$$
\text{Stability: } \mathrm{\left(CH_3\right)_3C^\oplus > \left(CH_3\right)_2CH^\oplus > CH_3CH_2^\oplus > CH_3^\oplus}
$$

***

### 2. Hyperconjugation in Alkenes ($\sigma_{\mathrm{C-H}} \to \pi^*$):

The $\sigma$-bonding electrons of the $\alpha\mathrm{-C-H}$ bond delocalize into the adjacent empty $\pi^*$ antibonding orbital of the double bond.

- **Direct Consequence 1 (Bond Lengths)**: The nominal single $\mathrm{C-C}$ bond acquires partial double-bond character, becoming shorter than a standard single bond ($\approx 1.49\text{ Å}$ vs $1.54\text{ Å}$). The $\mathrm{C=C}$ double bond lengthens slightly ($\approx 1.35\text{ Å}$ vs $1.34\text{ Å}$).
- **Direct Consequence 2 (Heat of Hydrogenation, $\Delta H_{\text{hydro}}^\circ$)**: Greater hyperconjugation stabilizes the alkene, lowering its heat of hydrogenation:

$$
\text{Stability of Alkene} \propto N_{\alpha\text{-H}} \propto \frac{1}{\Delta H_{\text{hydro}}^\circ}
$$

```
Comparison of Alkene Stability by α-Hydrogens:

R₂C=CR₂   >   R₂C=CHR   >   R₂C=CH₂   ~   RCH=CHR (trans)   >   RCH=CHR (cis)   >   RCH=CH₂
(12 α-H)      (9 α-H)       (6 α-H)           (6 α-H)               (6 α-H)         (3 α-H)
```

***

### 3. Hyperconjugation in Free Radicals ($\sigma_{\mathrm{C-H}} \to \text{half-filled } p$):

The $\alpha\mathrm{-C-H}$ bond $\sigma$-electrons interact with the singly occupied $p$-orbital of the radical:

$$
\mathrm{\left(CH_3\right)_3C^\bullet \ (9\ \alpha\text{-H}) > \left(CH_3\right)_2\dot{C}H \ (6\ \alpha\text{-H}) > CH_3\dot{C}H_2 \ (3\ \alpha\text{-H}) > \dot{C}H_3 \ (0\ \alpha\text{-H})}
$$

***

### 4. Reverse Hyperconjugation (Sacrificial/Negative Hyperconjugation):
Occurs when $\alpha$-carbons are attached to strongly electronegative halogens, such as in the trichloromethyl group ($-\mathrm{CCl_3}$), interacting with an adjacent $\pi$-system:

$$
\sigma_{p} \to \sigma^*_{\mathrm{C-Cl}}
$$

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5-CCl_3} \implies \text{Deactivates the benzene ring toward electrophilic attack and directs meta.}
$$

# 6. Integrated Applications & Stability Hierarchies

### The Priority Override Rule:
When evaluating competing electronic effects on reaction intermediates, use the following operational hierarchy:

$$
\text{Aromaticity} > \text{Complete Octet (Resonance)} > \text{Equivalent Resonance} > \text{Resonance }(M/R) > \text{Hyperconjugation }(H) > \text{Inductive Effect }(I)
$$

$$
\mathbf{\text{A} > \mathbf{O} > \mathbf{R} > \mathbf{H} > \mathbf{I}}
$$

***

### Case 1: The Halogen Paradox in Haloarenes
Halogens ($-\mathrm{F}, -\mathrm{Cl}, -\mathrm{Br}, -\mathrm{I}$) show competing effects:
- **Resonance ($+R$)**: Donates a lone pair into the aromatic $\pi$-system, stabilizing ortho- and para- intermediates during electrophilic substitution.
- **Inductive Effect ($-I$)**: Strong electronegativity withdraws $\sigma$-electron density, deactivating the aromatic ring overall.

**The Net Result**:
- The **inductive effect ($-I$)** dominates the energy of activation, deactivating the benzene ring toward electrophilic substitution compared to unsubstituted benzene.
- The **resonance effect ($+R$)** controls the regiochemical outcome, directing the incoming electrophile to **ortho-** and **para-** positions.

***

### Case 2: Carbocation Stability Override (Octet vs. Inductive)
Consider the methoxymethyl cation vs. the tert-butyl cation:

$$
\mathbf{A:} \quad \mathrm{CH_3-\ddot{O}-CH_2^\oplus \longleftrightarrow CH_3-\overset{\oplus}{O}=CH_2} \quad (\text{Every atom has an octet})
$$

$$
\mathbf{B:} \quad \mathrm{(CH_3)_3C^\oplus} \quad (\text{Sextet carbon, 9 }\alpha\text{-H atoms, Hyperconjugation})
$$

**Stability**: $\mathbf{A} \gg \mathbf{B}$.
Even though oxygen is more electronegative than carbon, in Structure $\mathbf{A}$ the $+R$ lone-pair donation produces an all-octet canonical contributor ($\mathrm{CH_3-\overset{\oplus}{O}=CH_2}$). In $\mathbf{B}$, the positive carbon remains an electron sextet. An octet structure always overrides hyperconjugation and inductive effects.

**Question:** Explain the hybridization of carbon in $\mathrm{CH_3^\oplus}$. What is the geometry, and what happens when the 1-propyl carbocation ($\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CH_2^\oplus}$) is formed during an addition reaction?

**Solution:**

### Problem 2: Resonance Energy and Quantitative Heat of Hydrogenation
**Question:** The experimental enthalpy of hydrogenation ($\Delta H_{\text{hydro}}^\circ$) of cyclohexene is $-119.5\text{ kJ/mol}$. If the experimental heat of hydrogenation of benzene is measured to be $-208.2\text{ kJ/mol}$, calculate the resonance energy of benzene.

**Step-by-Step Calculation:**

\Delta H_{\text{hydro}}^\circ(\text{Cyclohexene}) = -119.5\text{ kJ/mol}

$$
\Delta H_{\text{hydro, theoretical}}^\circ = 3 \times (-119.5\text{ kJ/mol}) = -358.5\text{ kJ/mol}

The actual experimental value for benzene:

$$
\Delta H_{\text{hydro, experimental}}^\circ = -208.2\text{ kJ/mol}

$$
\text{Resonance Energy} = |\Delta H_{\text{hydro, theoretical}}^\circ| - |\Delta H_{\text{hydro, experimental}}^\circ|
$$

$$
\text{Resonance Energy} = |-358.5| - |-208.2| = 358.5 - 208.2 = \mathbf{150.3\text{ kJ/mol}} \quad \left(\approx \mathbf{35.92\text{ kcal/mol}}\right)

$$
\boxed{\text{Resonance Energy of Benzene} = 150.3\text{ kJ/mol}}

# 8. Examiner Traps & Common Mistakes

***

### Trap 1: Confusing Lewis Bases with Nucleophiles
- **Basicity**: A **thermodynamic** property measured by the equilibrium constant ($K_b$ or $\mathrm{p}K_a$ of the conjugate acid) for abstracting a proton ($\mathrm{H^\oplus}$).
- **Nucleophilicity**: A **kinetic** property measured by the rate constant ($k$) of attack at an electron-deficient carbon center.
- *Example*: Sterically hindered bases such as **tert-butoxide** ($\mathrm{(CH_3)_3C-O^\ominus}$) are strong bases (conjugate acid $\mathrm{p}K_a \approx 18$), but poor nucleophiles because steric bulk impedes attack at carbon centers.

***

### Trap 2: Counting $\beta$-Hydrogens for Hyperconjugation
Students often count all hydrogens on a molecule rather than strictly counting the **$\alpha$-hydrogens** (hydrogens bonded to the $sp^3$ carbon directly adjacent to the $sp^2$ center).
- In the tert-butyl cation $\mathrm{[(CH_3)_3C^\oplus]}$, the hydrogens are directly on $\alpha$-carbons $\implies 9\ \alpha\text{-H}$.
- In the neopentyl cation $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3C-CH_2^\oplus}$, the adjacent $sp^3$ carbon is bonded to three methyl groups with **zero** hydrogens directly attached to it ($0\ \alpha\text{-H}$). The 9 methyl hydrogens are $\beta$-hydrogens and **cannot** stabilize the carbocation via hyperconjugation!

***

### Trap 3: Assuming Halogens are Always Ortho/Para Directing AND Activating
Halogens are **ortho/para directors**, but they are **deactivating**. Students often assume that all ortho/para directors must be ring activators. Because the $-I$ effect of halogens is stronger than their $+R$ effect, they withdraw net electron density while still directing incoming electrophiles ortho/para.

***

### Trap 4: Ignoring Octets in Resonance Forms
Students frequently prioritize electronegativity over octet completion.
- *Incorrect*: Assuming $\mathrm{CH_3-\overset{\oplus}{O}=CH_2}$ is less stable than $\mathrm{CH_3-\ddot{O}-CH_2^\oplus}$ because oxygen has a positive formal charge.
- *Correct*: $\mathrm{CH_3-\overset{\oplus}{O}=CH_2}$ is the major contributor because every atom has a **complete octet**. Octet completion consistently takes precedence over formal charge placement.

***

### Trap 5: Misidentifying Species with Positive Charges as Electrophiles
Not all positively charged species can accept electrons:
- $\mathrm{H_3O^\oplus}$, $\mathrm{NH_4^\oplus}$, and $\mathrm{Na^\oplus}$ are **not** electrophiles. Their valence shells are completely filled, and they have no available empty orbitals to accept a lone pair.

***

### Golden Rule 1: The `A-O-R-H-I` Stability Test
To evaluate carbocation, carbanion, or radical stability in under 10 seconds:
1. **A**: Is the system **Aromatic**? (e.g., Tropylium cation $\implies$ extreme stability).
2. **O**: Does a canonical form give every atom a complete **Octet**?
3. **R**: Is there **Resonance** delocalization ($\pi$-conjugation, benzylic/allylic)?
4. **H**: How many **Hyperconjugative** $\alpha$-hydrogens are there?
5. **I**: Does the **Inductive effect** ($\pm I$) disperse or concentrate charge?

***

### Golden Rule 2: The `HELP` Mnemonic for Homolysis
Whenever a reaction involves:
- **H**eat ($T > 400^\circ\mathrm{C}$)
- **E**lectricity (Kolbe electrolysis)
- **L**ight ($h\nu$, UV)
- **P**eroxides ($\mathrm{R_2O_2}$)
Expect **homolytic bond cleavage** and a **free radical** pathway, not carbocations or carbanions.

***

### Golden Rule 3: Fast $s$-Character Carbanion Checklist
Calculate the hybrid state of the carbanionic carbon to quickly evaluate relative stability:

$$
\mathrm{C(sp) \ (50\%\ s)} > \mathrm{C(sp^2) \ (33.3\%\ s)} > \mathrm{C(sp^3) \ (25\%\ s)}
$$

$$
\mathrm{HC\equiv C^\ominus \ (\mathrm{p}K_a \approx 25) > CH_2=CH^\ominus \ (\mathrm{p}K_a \approx 44) > CH_3-CH_2^\ominus \ (\mathrm{p}K_a \approx 50)}
$$

***

### Golden Rule 4: The `DNPS` Priority for Inductive Effects
When comparing the acid-strengthening effect of substituents:

$$
\text{Distance} > \text{Number} > \text{Power (Electronegativity)}
$$

*Example*: One fluorine located at the 2-position ($\alpha$) increases acidity more than two fluorines at the 4-position ($\gamma$), because the inductive effect drops off steeply with distance.

# Methods of Purification of Organic Compounds

# Organic Chemistry: Some Basic Principles and Techniques

## Topic: Methods of Purification of Organic Compounds

***

### Master Flowchart & Pedagogical Overview

Once an organic compound is extracted from a natural source or synthesized in the laboratory, it is almost invariably contaminated with impurities, unreacted starting materials, secondary by-products, or solvents. Purification isolates the compound in its purest physical state prior to qualitative and quantitative elemental analysis.

```
Purification Methods of Organic Compounds
 │
 ├── 1. Sublimation (Solid phase $\rightleftharpoons$ Gas phase)
 ├── 2. Crystallisation (Differential solubility in a chosen solvent across temperatures)
 ├── 3. Distillation (Volatile liquids; differences in vapor pressures / boiling points)
 │    ├── (a) Simple Distillation ($\Delta T_b \ge 25\text{--}30\text{ K}$)
 │    ├── (b) Fractional Distillation ($\Delta T_b < 25\text{ K}$; fractionating columns)
 │    ├── (c) Vacuum Distillation / Distillation under Reduced Pressure (Decomposes at/below normal $T_b$)
 │    └── (d) Steam Distillation (Steam volatile, immiscible with water, high $P^\circ$ near $373\text{ K}$)
 ├── 4. Differential Extraction (Liquid-Liquid Extraction using immiscible solvents & partition)
 └── 5. Chromatography (Differential distribution between stationary & mobile phases)
      ├── (a) Adsorption Chromatography
      │    ├── Column Chromatography
      │    └── Thin Layer Chromatography (TLC)
      └── (b) Partition Chromatography
           └── Paper Chromatography
```

***

## 1. Sublimation

### 1.1 Priority & Fundamental Principle
- **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐
- **Principle:** Certain substances, upon heating at atmospheric pressure, transition directly from the solid state to the vapor state without passing through the intermediate liquid state. Upon cooling, the vapors condense directly back into the solid state.
- **Thermodynamic Condition:** Occurs when the triple-point pressure of the substance is higher than the prevailing external atmospheric pressure, or when the substance exerts a significant vapor pressure well below its melting point.

$$
\text{Solid} + \Delta H_{\text{sub}} \rightleftharpoons \text{Vapour}
$$

$$
\Delta H_{\text{sub}} = \Delta H_{\text{fus}} + \Delta H_{\text{vap}}
$$

### 1.2 Laboratory Procedure & Setup
1. The impure powdered substance is placed in a dry porcelain evaporating dish.
2. The dish is covered with a perforated filter paper and an inverted glass funnel.
3. The open stem of the funnel is plugged with a loose plug of cotton wool to prevent the escape of vapors.
4. The dish is heated gently on a sand bath. 
5. The vapors rise through the perforations, make contact with the cold inner walls of the funnel, and deposit as pure crystals (**sublimate**). Non-sublimable impurities remain behind in the dish.

### 1.3 High-Yield Examples & Exemplar Systems
- **Sublimable Organic Compounds:**
  - Naphthalene ($\text{C}_{10}\text{H}_8$)
  - Anthracene ($\text{C}_{14}\text{H}_{10}$)
  - Benzoic acid ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOH}$)
  - Camphor ($\text{C}_{10}\text{H}_{16}\text{O}$)
  - Phthalic anhydride ($\text{C}_8\text{H}_4\text{O}_3$)
  - Anthraquinone
- **Common Binary Separations:**
  - $\text{Camphor} + \text{NaCl}$
  - $\text{Benzoic acid} + \text{Sand}$
  - $\text{Naphthalene} + \text{Urea}$

***

## 2. Crystallisation

### 2.1 Priority & Fundamental Principle
- **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Principle:** Based on the difference in the solubilities of the organic compound and its impurities in a suitably chosen solvent over a range of temperatures.
- The desired compound must be **sparingly soluble at room temperature** but **highly soluble at elevated temperatures** (near the solvent's boiling point). Impurities must either be completely insoluble (removed by hot filtration) or remain soluble even when the solution is chilled (remaining in the mother liquor).

### 2.2 Criteria for an Ideal Crystallisation Solvent
1. High solvent power for the solute at high temperatures; very low solvent power at low temperatures.
2. Must **not** react chemically with the solute.
3. Must dissolve impurities either not at all, or to such an extent that they remain in solution upon cooling.
4. Volatile enough to be easily removed from the purified crystals (moderate boiling point).

### 2.3 Step-by-Step Laboratory Protocol
1. **Preparation of Solution:** Minimum volume of boiling solvent is added portion-wise to the impure substance until it just dissolves, forming a saturated solution at its boiling point.
2. **Decolourisation (if colored impurities are present):** A small pinch of activated charcoal (animal charcoal) is added. Activated charcoal possesses an immense surface area with active sites that selectively adsorb high-molecular-weight colored impurities. The solution is brought to a brief simmer.
3. **Hot Filtration:** The hot solution is filtered rapidly through a fluted filter paper in a hot-water funnel to remove insoluble impurities and charcoal. Fluting increases the effective surface area and filtration speed, preventing premature crystallisation inside the funnel stem.
4. **Crystallisation (Nucleation & Crystal Growth):** The clear filtrate is allowed to cool slowly and undisturbed to room temperature, then chilled in an ice bath. Slow cooling ensures large, well-defined, pure crystals without trapped solvent.
5. **Isolation & Washing:** Crystals are collected using vacuum filtration on a **Büchner funnel** connected to a suction flask. Crystals are washed with a minimum volume of ice-cold solvent to wash off adhering mother liquor.
6. **Drying:** Crystals are dried between folds of filter paper, in a desiccator over anhydrous $\text{CaCl}_2$ or concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$, or inside a vacuum oven below the melting point of the compound.

### 2.4 Fractional Crystallisation
When two solutes $A$ and $B$ are present in comparable quantities and have different solubilities in the same solvent:
- The less soluble compound ($A$) crystallises out first upon partial concentration and cooling.
- The mother liquor is concentrated further and cooled to isolate compound $B$.

***

## 3. Distillation

Distillation is employed to separate:
1. Volatile liquids from non-volatile impurities.
2. Liquids having different boiling points.

The process involves two coupled phase transitions:

$$
\text{Liquid} \xrightarrow{\text{Vaporisation}} \text{Vapour} \xrightarrow{\text{Condensation}} \text{Liquid (Distillate)}
$$

```
          Atmospheric Boiling Point Criterion:
          
          P_vapour(T_b) = P_external = 1.01325 bar (1 atm)
```

***

### 3.1 Simple Distillation
- **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Condition:** Applicable to stable liquids that do not decompose at or near their boiling points, containing:
  - Non-volatile impurities, OR
  - Liquid mixtures where the components differ significantly in their boiling points:

$$
\Delta T_b \ge 25\text{ to }30\text{ K}
$$

#### Exemplar Separations:
1. **Chloroform** ($T_b = 334\text{ K} = 61^\circ\text{C}$) and **Aniline** ($T_b = 457\text{ K} = 184^\circ\text{C}$):
   

$$
\Delta T_b = 457 - 334 = 123\text{ K}
$$

2. **Ether** ($T_b = 308\text{ K} = 35^\circ\text{C}$) and **Toluene** ($T_b = 384\text{ K} = 111^\circ\text{C}$):
   

$$
\Delta T_b = 76\text{ K}
$$

3. **Benzene** ($T_b = 353\text{ K} = 80^\circ\text{C}$) and **Nitrobenzene** ($T_b = 484\text{ K} = 211^\circ\text{C}$):
   

$$
\Delta T_b = 131\text{ K}
$$

***

### 3.2 Fractional Distillation
- **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Condition:** Separation of two or more miscible, volatile liquids whose boiling point difference is small:

$$
\Delta T_b < 25\text{ K}
$$

#### Mechanism & Mathematical Basis
In a mixture of two volatile miscible liquids $A$ and $B$ (where $A$ is more volatile: $P_A^\circ > P_B^\circ \implies T_{b,A} < T_{b,B}$), Raoult's Law describes the liquid-vapor equilibrium:

$$
P_A = P_A^\circ x_A, \quad P_B = P_B^\circ x_B
$$

$$
P_{\text{total}} = P_A + P_B = P_A^\circ x_A + P_B^\circ (1 - x_A)
$$

The mole fraction of component $A$ in the vapor phase ($y_A$) is given by Dalton's law:

$$
y_A = \frac{P_A}{P_{\text{total}}} = \frac{P_A^\circ x_A}{P_A^\circ x_A + P_B^\circ (1 - x_A)}
$$

Because $P_A^\circ > P_B^\circ$:

$$
\frac{y_A}{y_B} = \left(\frac{P_A^\circ}{P_B^\circ}\right) \left(\frac{x_A}{x_B}\right) \implies \frac{y_A}{y_B} > \frac{x_A}{x_B}
$$

*The vapor phase is always richer in the more volatile component ($A$) than the liquid phase in equilibrium with it.*

#### Role of the Fractionating Column
A fractionating column provides a large cooling surface area for repeated, successive cycles of condensation and revaporisation along its height.
- Vapors ascending the column condense on the internal packing/indentations.
- The descending liquid condensate (**reflux**) is stripped of volatile components by ascending hot vapors.
- Each condensation-revaporisation cycle corresponds to one **theoretical plate**.
- By the time vapors reach the top of the column, they consist virtually of 100% pure low-boiling component $A$.

```
Top of Column: Vapour reaches T_b of lower-boiling component (Pure A distills)
      ▲
      │  Multiple condensation-revaporisation equilibria
      │  (Equated to Theoretical Plates: N)
      │
Bottom of Column / Still Pot: High-boiling liquid B concentrates
```

#### Industrial Applications:
1. Separation of crude oil fractions in petroleum refineries.
2. Separation of liquid air ($T_b(\text{N}_2) = 77.3\text{ K}$, $T_b(\text{Ar}) = 87.3\text{ K}$, $T_b(\text{O}_2) = 90.2\text{ K}$).
3. Separation of acetone ($T_b = 329\text{ K}$) and methyl alcohol ($T_b = 338\text{ K}$), where $\Delta T_b = 9\text{ K}$.

***

### 3.3 Vacuum Distillation (Distillation Under Reduced Pressure)
- **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Principle:** The boiling point of a liquid is the temperature at which its saturated vapor pressure equals the external pressure:

$$
P_{\text{vapour}}(T_b) = P_{\text{ext}}
$$

If $P_{\text{ext}}$ is lowered using a vacuum pump or water aspirator, $P_{\text{vapour}}$ achieves equality with $P_{\text{ext}}$ at a significantly lower temperature.
- **Thermodynamic Basis (Clausius-Clapeyron Equation):**

$$
\ln\left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right) = -\frac{\Delta H_{\text{vap}}}{R}\left(\frac{1}{T_2} - \frac{1}{T_1}\right)
$$

Since $\Delta H_{\text{vap}} > 0$, as $P_{\text{ext}}$ decreases ($P_2 < P_1$), the boiling temperature must decrease ($T_2 < T_1$).

#### Critical Target Compounds:
1. **Glycerol (Glycerine, $\text{CH}_2\text{OH-CHOH-CH}_2\text{OH}$):**
   - Normal boiling point: $T_b = 563\text{ K}$ ($290^\circ\text{C}$) at $760\text{ mm Hg}$ (decomposes near this temperature into acrolein and char).
   - Under reduced pressure: Distills smoothly without decomposition at **$453\text{ K}$ ($180^\circ\text{C}$) under $12\text{ mm Hg}$**.
2. **Concentration of Spent-Lye in Soap Industry:** Recovery of crude glycerol from soap-spent lyes.
3. **Hydrogen Peroxide ($\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$):** Concentrated by vacuum distillation to avoid explosive thermal disproportionation:

$$
2\text{H}_2\text{O}_2(l) \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(l) + \text{O}_2(g)
$$

### 3.4 Steam Distillation
- **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Criterion for Application:** Used to purify substances which:
  1. Are completely insoluble or virtually immiscible with water.
  2. Are volatile in steam.
  3. Possess a non-negligible vapor pressure ($\ge 10\text{--}15\text{ mm Hg}$) near $373\text{ K}$.
  4. Contain non-volatile impurities.

#### Rigorous Mathematical Derivation of Steam Distillation

Consider an immiscible binary system of water ($w$) and an organic liquid ($o$). Because the two liquid phases are mutually insoluble, each exerts its own saturated vapor pressure independently of the mole fraction of the other:

$$
P_{\text{total}} = P_w^\circ + P_o^\circ
$$

The mixture boils at a temperature $T_{\text{mixture}}$ at which:

$$
P_{\text{total}} = P_w^\circ + P_o^\circ = P_{\text{atm}} = 1.013\text{ bar} = 760\text{ mm Hg}
$$

Because $P_w^\circ < 760\text{ mm Hg}$ and $P_o^\circ < 760\text{ mm Hg}$ individually:

$$
T_{\text{mixture}} < T_{b,w}\,(373\text{ K}) \quad \text{and} \quad T_{\text{mixture}} < T_{b,o}
$$

*The mixture boils at a temperature lower than the normal boiling point of both water and the organic liquid.* This protects temperature-sensitive high-boiling compounds from thermal degradation.

##### Composition of the Distillate:
Assuming ideal gas behavior for both vapors in the distillation head:

$$
P_w^\circ V = n_w R T = \left(\frac{w_w}{M_w}\right) R T
$$

$$
P_o^\circ V = n_o R T = \left(\frac{w_o}{M_o}\right) R T
$$

Dividing the two equations:

$$
\frac{P_o^\circ}{P_w^\circ} = \frac{n_o}{n_w} = \frac{\left(\frac{w_o}{M_o}\right)}{\left(\frac{w_w}{M_w}\right)}
$$

Rearranging to determine the mass ratio of organic substance to water in the distillate:

$$
\boxed{\frac{w_o}{w_w} = \frac{P_o^\circ \cdot M_o}{P_w^\circ \cdot M_w}}
$$

Where:
- $w_o, w_w$ = masses of organic compound and water collected in the distillate receiver.
- $M_o, M_w$ = molar masses of the organic compound and water ($M_w = 18.015\text{ g mol}^{-1}$).
- $P_o^\circ, P_w^\circ$ = saturated vapor pressures of the pure components at the distillation temperature ($T_{\text{mixture}}$).

```
Proportion of Organic Substance in Distillate:
Mass fraction: % w_o = [ w_o / (w_o + w_w) ] * 100
Even if P_o° is relatively small, a large molar mass M_o ensures 
a substantial recovery yield of the organic component per unit mass of steam.
```

#### Canonical NCERT Examples:
- **Aniline** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2$): Normal $T_b = 457\text{ K}$; steam distills at $\approx 371.5\text{ K}$ ($98.5^\circ\text{C}$).
- **Nitrobenzene** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NO}_2$): Normal $T_b = 484\text{ K}$; steam distills at $\approx 372\text{ K}$ ($99^\circ\text{C}$).
- **o-Nitrophenol vs. p-Nitrophenol separation:**
  - *o-Nitrophenol* possesses **intramolecular hydrogen bonding** (chelation) $\implies$ non-associated, high volatility $\implies$ **steam volatile**.
  - *p-Nitrophenol* possesses **intermolecular hydrogen bonding** $\implies$ high association, low vapor pressure $\implies$ **steam non-volatile**.
- Essential oils (citronella, eucalyptus, turpentine oil, peppermint).

## 4. Differential Extraction (Liquid-Liquid Extraction)

### 4.1 Priority & Fundamental Principle
- **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Principle:** An organic compound present in an aqueous solution is recovered by shaking it with an immiscible organic solvent in which the compound is markedly more soluble.
- Governed by **Nernst's Distribution Law (Partition Law):**
  At a constant temperature, a solute distributes itself between two non-miscible solvents in a constant ratio of concentrations, provided the solute exists in the same molecular state in both phases:

$$
K_D = \frac{C_{\text{organic}}}{C_{\text{aqueous}}} = \frac{[\text{Solute}]_{\text{org}}}{[\text{Solute}]_{\text{aq}}}
$$

Where $K_D$ is the **Distribution Coefficient** or **Partition Coefficient**.

### 4.2 Mathematical Derivation: Multi-Step vs. Single-Step Extraction
Let:
- $W$ = Initial mass of organic solute dissolved in volume $V$ of aqueous solution.
- $v$ = Volume of extracting organic solvent used in each successive extraction.
- $x_1$ = Mass of solute remaining unextracted in the aqueous phase after the **1st extraction**.

$$
\text{Concentration in aqueous phase: } C_{aq,1} = \frac{x_1}{V}
$$

$$
\text{Mass extracted into organic phase: } W - x_1
$$

$$
\text{Concentration in organic phase: } C_{org,1} = \frac{W - x_1}{v}
$$

Applying Nernst's Distribution Law ($K = \frac{C_{\text{aq}}}{C_{\text{org}}}$ where $K = 1/K_D$):

$$
K = \frac{\frac{x_1}{V}}{\frac{W - x_1}{v}} = \frac{x_1 \cdot v}{(W - x_1)V}
$$

$$
K(W - x_1)V = x_1 \cdot v \implies K W V - K x_1 V = x_1 v
$$

$$
x_1(K V + v) = K W V \implies x_1 = W \left[ \frac{K V}{K V + v} \right]
$$

For the **2nd extraction** using another fresh portion $v$ of the organic solvent:

$$
x_2 = x_1 \left[ \frac{K V}{K V + v} \right] = W \left[ \frac{K V}{K V + v} \right]^2
$$

By mathematical induction, after **$n$ successive extractions** each using volume $v$:

$$
\boxed{x_n = W \left[ \frac{K V}{K V + v} \right]^n}
$$

$$
\text{Total mass of solute extracted: } W_{\text{extracted}} = W - x_n = W \left( 1 - \left[ \frac{K V}{K V + v} \right]^n \right)
$$

#### Inescapable Mathematical Conclusion:
Since $\frac{KV}{KV + v} < 1$, as $n \to \infty$ for a fixed total solvent volume $V_{\text{total}} = n \cdot v$, the value of $\left[\frac{KV}{KV + v}\right]^n$ diminishes exponentially.

$$
\boxed{\text{Multiple extractions with smaller solvent volumes are far more efficient than a single extraction with the entire volume.}}
$$

## 5. Chromatography

- **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Definition (IUPAC):** A physical method of separation in which the components to be separated are distributed between two phases, one of which is stationary (**stationary phase**) while the other (**mobile phase**) moves in a definite direction.

```
Chromatographic Techniques
 │
 ├── 1. Adsorption Chromatography (Stationary phase: Solid adsorbent)
 │    ├── Column Chromatography
 │    └── Thin Layer Chromatography (TLC)
 │
 └── 2. Partition Chromatography (Stationary phase: Liquid trapped on support)
      └── Paper Chromatography
```

***

### 5.1 Adsorption Chromatography
Based on the differential adsorption of various components of a mixture on a suitable solid adsorbent surface.
- **Common Adsorbents:** Activated Alumina ($\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$), Silica gel ($\text{SiO}_2 \cdot x\text{H}_2\text{O}$), Florisil, Cellulose powder.
- Molecules with stronger affinity for the polar stationary phase are adsorbed strongly and move slowly. Molecules with weaker affinity remain predominantly in the non-polar mobile phase and elute rapidly.

#### A. Column Chromatography
- **Setup:** A vertical glass tube equipped with a stopcock at the bottom, packed uniformly with an adsorbent (stationary phase) suspended in a suitable liquid.
- **Elution Process:** 
  1. The mixture dissolved in the minimum volume of the least polar solvent is introduced onto the top of the column.
  2. The mobile phase (**eluent**) is passed down continuously.
  3. Solutes separate into discrete horizontal bands based on their adsorption affinity.
  4. The components are collected separately at the base (**eluate**).

$$
\text{Order of Elution: Weakly adsorbed (faster) } \longrightarrow \text{ Strongly adsorbed (slower)}
$$

```
          [ Top of Column ]
                 │
                 ▼
          ┌─────────────┐
          │  Band C     │  <-- Most strongly adsorbed (Most polar)
          ├─────────────┤
          │  Band B     │  <-- Moderately adsorbed
          ├─────────────┤
          │  Band A     │  <-- Most weakly adsorbed (Elutes FIRST)
          └─────────────┘
                 │
                 ▼
          [ Stopcock / Eluate ]
```

#### B. Thin Layer Chromatography (TLC)
- **Stationary Phase:** A thin ($0.2\text{ mm}$) layer of adsorbent (silica gel or alumina) spread onto a glass, aluminium, or plastic plate ($8 \times 2\text{ cm}$), known as a **chromaplate**.
- **Execution:**
  1. The sample spot is applied $\approx 1\text{--}2\text{ cm}$ above the bottom edge (base line).
  2. The plate is placed vertically in a closed development chamber saturated with solvent vapors.
  3. The mobile phase rises via capillary action.
  4. When the solvent front reaches $\approx 80\text{--}90\%$ of the plate height, the plate is removed, and the solvent front is marked immediately.

##### Quantification: Retardation Factor ($R_f$)

$$
\boxed{R_f = \frac{\text{Distance moved by the substance from base line } (x)}{\text{Distance moved by the solvent front from base line } (y)}}
$$

- **Properties of $R_f$:**
  - Dimensionless number.
  - $0 \le R_f \le 1$.
  - Under fixed conditions (temperature, stationary phase, exact solvent composition), $R_f$ is an intrinsic physical constant for a given solute.
  - Highly adsorbed (polar) compounds $\implies$ Low $R_f$.
  - Weakly adsorbed (non-polar) compounds $\implies$ High $R_f$.

```
     Solvent Front ---------------------------  Top (y)
                            o   Compound A (x_A) -> Higher R_f (Less Polar)
                            
                            o   Compound B (x_B) -> Lower R_f (More Polar)
     Base Line     ---•-------•---------------  Bottom (Origin)
```

##### Visualisation of Spots:
- **Coloured spots:** Detected visually.
- **Colorless spots:**
  - **UV Light:** Fluorescent indicator embedded in silica plate glows under UV ($254\text{ nm}$); spots appear as dark quenching zones.
  - **Iodine Chamber:** Plates exposed to $\text{I}_2$ vapor; unsaturated/aromatic compounds form transient brown charge-transfer complexes.
  - **Ninhydrin Spray:** Reacts with amino acids to produce an intense purple/blue Ruhemann's complex.
  - **$\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ Spray & Charring:** Spraying with concentrated sulfuric acid followed by heating chars organic compounds into visible black carbon spots.

***

### 5.2 Partition Chromatography
- **Primary Type:** **Paper Chromatography**.
- **Stationary Phase:** Water molecules trapped inside the cellulose network of chromatography paper (Whatman No. 1 paper). The cellulose acts only as an inert structural support holding the stationary liquid phase.
- **Mobile Phase:** An organic solvent or solvent mixture that flows over the paper.
- **Mechanism:** Separation is achieved through continuous **liquid-liquid partition** of components between the mobile liquid phase and the stationary water phase.

```
+---------------------------+-------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
| Parameter                 | Adsorption Chromatography     | Partition Chromatography          |
+---------------------------+-------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
| Primary Mechanism         | Surface Adsorption (Solid-Gas | Liquid-Liquid Partition           |
|                           | or Solid-Liquid)              | Equilibrium                       |
| Stationary Phase          | Solid (Silica gel, Alumina)   | Liquid (Water bound in cellulose) |
| Representative Techniques | TLC, Column Chromatography    | Paper Chromatography              |
| Retardation Metric        | R_f (governed by adsorption)  | R_f (governed by partition K_D)   |
+---------------------------+-------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
```

## 6. High-Yield Solved Problems & Exemplar Archetypes

### Problem 1: Steam Distillation Mass & Percentage Recovery (Board/Entrance Classic)
**Statement:** An organic liquid $O$ is immiscible with water and purified via steam distillation at an atmospheric pressure of $760\text{ mm Hg}$. The mixture boils at $371.5\text{ K}$ ($98.5^\circ\text{C}$). At this temperature, the vapor pressure of pure water is $710\text{ mm Hg}$. The molecular mass of the organic compound is $123\text{ g mol}^{-1}$.
1. Determine the vapor pressure of the organic compound at the boiling point of the mixture.
2. Calculate the mass ratio of the organic compound to water in the collected distillate.
3. Calculate the percentage by mass of the organic substance present in the distillate.

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Calculate $P_o^\circ$**
According to Dalton's law of partial pressures for an immiscible system:

$$
P_{\text{total}} = P_w^\circ + P_o^\circ
$$

$$
760\text{ mm Hg} = 710\text{ mm Hg} + P_o^\circ
$$

$$
\mathbf{P_o^\circ = 760 - 710 = 50\text{ mm Hg}}
$$

**Step 2: Calculate Mass Ratio $\frac{w_o}{w_w}$**
Using the steam distillation relationship:

$$
\frac{w_o}{w_w} = \frac{P_o^\circ \cdot M_o}{P_w^\circ \cdot M_w}
$$

Given:
- $P_o^\circ = 50\text{ mm Hg}$
- $P_w^\circ = 710\text{ mm Hg}$
- $M_o = 123\text{ g mol}^{-1}$
- $M_w = 18.0\text{ g mol}^{-1}$

$$
\frac{w_o}{w_w} = \frac{50 \times 123}{710 \times 18.0} = \frac{6150}{12780} \approx \mathbf{0.4812}
$$

**Step 3: Calculate Percentage of Organic Compound in Distillate**

$$
\% w_o = \left( \frac{w_o}{w_o + w_w} \right) \times 100 = \left( \frac{\frac{w_o}{w_w}}{\frac{w_o}{w_w} + 1} \right) \times 100
$$

$$
\% w_o = \left( \frac{0.4812}{0.4812 + 1} \right) \times 100 = \left( \frac{0.4812}{1.4812} \right) \times 100 \approx \mathbf{32.49\%}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Answer: } P_o^\circ = 50\text{ mm Hg}, \quad \frac{w_o}{w_w} = 0.4812, \quad \% w_o = 32.49\%}
$$

***

### Problem 2: Multi-Step vs. Single-Step Differential Extraction
**Statement:** An aqueous solution contains $6.0\text{ g}$ of an organic substance in $100\text{ mL}$ of water. The partition coefficient of the compound between diethyl ether and water is:

$$
K_D = \frac{C_{\text{ether}}}{C_{\text{water}}} = 4.0
$$

Compare the mass of solute extracted using:
- **Case A:** A single extraction with $100\text{ mL}$ of diethyl ether.
- **Case B:** Two successive extractions using $50\text{ mL}$ of diethyl ether each.

**Solution:**

Define partition coefficient in terms of water to ether:

$$
K = \frac{C_{\text{water}}}{C_{\text{ether}}} = \frac{1}{K_D} = \frac{1}{4.0} = 0.25
$$

**Case A: Single Extraction ($V = 100\text{ mL}$, $v = 100\text{ mL}$, $n = 1$)**

$$
x_1 = W \left[ \frac{K V}{K V + v} \right]
$$

$$
x_1 = 6.0 \left[ \frac{0.25 \times 100}{(0.25 \times 100) + 100} \right] = 6.0 \left[ \frac{25}{25 + 100} \right] = 6.0 \times \frac{25}{125} = 6.0 \times 0.20 = 1.20\text{ g remaining}
$$

$$
\text{Mass extracted} = W - x_1 = 6.0\text{ g} - 1.20\text{ g} = \mathbf{4.80\text{ g}}
$$

**Case B: Two Successive Extractions ($V = 100\text{ mL}$, $v = 50\text{ mL}$, $n = 2$)**

$$
x_2 = W \left[ \frac{K V}{K V + v} \right]^2
$$

$$
K V + v = (0.25 \times 100) + 50 = 25 + 50 = 75
$$

$$
x_2 = 6.0 \left[ \frac{25}{75} \right]^2 = 6.0 \left[ \frac{1}{3} \right]^2 = \frac{6.0}{9} = \mathbf{0.67\text{ g remaining}}
$$

$$
\text{Mass extracted} = W - x_2 = 6.0\text{ g} - 0.67\text{ g} = \mathbf{5.33\text{ g}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Efficiency Gain: Multiple extraction recovers } 5.33\text{ g } (88.8\%) \text{ vs. single extraction } 4.80\text{ g } (80.0\%)}}
$$

### Problem 3: TLC Analysis & $R_f$ Calculation (Exemplar Archetype)
**Statement:** In a thin-layer chromatography experiment using silica gel and an ethyl acetate-hexane ($1:4$) solvent system:
- Distance moved by solvent front from the pencil line $= 12.0\text{ cm}$.
- Distance moved by compound $X = 3.6\text{ cm}$.
- Distance moved by compound $Y = 8.4\text{ cm}$.
1. Calculate the $R_f$ values of components $X$ and $Y$.
2. State which compound is more polar and justify based on chromatographic principles.

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Calculate $R_f$**

$$
R_f(X) = \frac{\text{Distance moved by } X}{\text{Distance moved by solvent front}} = \frac{3.6\text{ cm}}{12.0\text{ cm}} = \mathbf{0.30}
$$

$$
R_f(Y) = \frac{\text{Distance moved by } Y}{\text{Distance moved by solvent front}} = \frac{8.4\text{ cm}}{12.0\text{ cm}} = \mathbf{0.70}
$$

**Step 2: Polarity Assessment**
- The stationary phase (silica gel) contains active, exposed polar silanol groups ($-\text{Si-OH}$).
- Compound $X$ has a significantly lower $R_f$ ($0.30$), indicating it is held more firmly on the stationary phase due to stronger dipole-dipole interactions or hydrogen bonding.
- Therefore, **Compound $X$ is more polar** than Compound $Y$.

## 8. Golden Points, Speed Hacks & Mnemonics

### 1. The Separation Selection Rule-of-Thumb
- Solid with high vapor pressure $\implies$ **Sublimation**
- Solid with temperature-dependent solubility $\implies$ **Crystallisation**
- Liquid mixture with $\Delta T_b \ge 25\text{ K}$ $\implies$ **Simple Distillation**
- Liquid mixture with $\Delta T_b < 25\text{ K}$ $\implies$ **Fractional Distillation**
- Liquid decomposing below normal $T_b$ $\implies$ **Vacuum Distillation**
- Water-immiscible, steam-volatile liquid $\implies$ **Steam Distillation**
- Differential distribution between phases $\implies$ **Chromatography**

### 2. High-Yield Isomer Separation: The Nitrophenols
- **ortho-Nitrophenol:** Intramolecular H-bonding (chelation) $\implies$ Lower boiling point, steam-volatile $\implies$ Separated via **Steam Distillation**.
- **para-Nitrophenol:** Intermolecular H-bonding $\implies$ Associated polymeric network, higher boiling point, non-steam-volatile $\implies$ Remains in the distillation flask.

### 3. Chromatography Classification Mnemonic: **"P-P & A-T-C"**
- **P-P:** **P**aper chromatography is **P**artition.
- **A-T-C:** **A**dsorption chromatography comprises **T**LC and **C**olumn.

### 4. Partition Coefficient ($K$) Definition Watch
Always double-check how the problem defines the distribution ratio:
- If defined as $K_D = \frac{C_{\text{org}}}{C_{\text{aq}}}$, then:
  

$$
x_n = W \left( \frac{V_{\text{aq}}}{V_{\text{aq}} + K_D \cdot v_{\text{org}}} \right)^n
$$

- If defined as $K = \frac{C_{\text{aq}}}{C_{\text{org}}}$, then:
  

$$
x_n = W \left( \frac{K \cdot V_{\text{aq}}}{K \cdot V_{\text{aq}} + v_{\text{org}}} \right)^n
$$

*Both yield identical results; verify whether $K$ sits in the numerator or alongside the organic volume.*

## Master Lecture Notes: Qualitative and Quantitative Elemental Analysis of Organic Compounds

***

# PART 1: QUALITATIVE ANALYSIS (DETECTION OF ELEMENTS)

Organic compounds primarily contain carbon and hydrogen. In addition, elements such as nitrogen, sulfur, halogens (chlorine, bromine, iodine), and phosphorus may be present. Qualitative analysis establishes the identity of these constituent elements through characteristic chemical transformations.

***

### 1.1 Detection of Carbon and Hydrogen (Copper(II) Oxide Test) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Principle and Molecular Interpretation
The organic substance is mixed thoroughly with dry copper(II) oxide ($\text{CuO}$) and heated strongly in a hard-glass test tube. 
- **Carbon** is oxidized to carbon dioxide gas ($\text{CO}_2$).
- **Hydrogen** is oxidized to water vapor ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$).

Carbon dioxide turns lime water milky due to the precipitation of insoluble calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$). Excess $\text{CO}_2$ turns the solution clear again due to soluble calcium hydrogen carbonate formation.
Water vapors condense on the cooler parts of the test tube and turn anhydrous copper(II) sulfate ($\text{CuSO}_4$, white) into hydrated copper(II) sulfate ($\text{CuSO}_4 \cdot 5\text{H}_2\text{O}$, deep blue).

#### B. Chemical Reactions

**Oxidation of the Organic Compound:**

$$
\text{C} + 2\text{CuO} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{CO}_2 \uparrow + 2\text{Cu}
$$

$$
2\text{H} + \text{CuO} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{H}_2\text{O} \uparrow + \text{Cu}
$$

**Confirmation of Carbon Dioxide:**

$$
\text{CO}_2 + \text{Ca(OH)}_2 \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Insoluble (Milky)}}{\text{CaCO}_3 \downarrow} + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

$$
\text{CaCO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2 \text{ (excess)} \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Soluble (Clear)}}{\text{Ca(HCO}_3)_2}
$$

**Confirmation of Hydrogen:**

$$
\underset{\text{White / Anhydrous}}{\text{CuSO}_4} + 5\text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Deep Blue / Hydrated}}{\text{CuSO}_4 \cdot 5\text{H}_2\text{O}}
$$

***

### 1.2 Preparation of Lassaigne's Extract (Sodium Fusion Extract - SFE) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Rationale & Need for Fusion
Elements like nitrogen, sulfur, and halogens are covalently bound within organic molecules. Covalent bonds do not ionize in aqueous media; therefore, standard inorganic ionic precipitations and colorimetric tests cannot proceed directly. 

Fusing the organic substrate with molten metallic sodium converts these covalently bound non-metals into water-soluble ionic sodium salts:
- Carbon and Nitrogen $\longrightarrow$ Sodium cyanide ($\text{NaCN}$)
- Sulfur $\longrightarrow$ Sodium sulfide ($\text{Na}_2\text{S}$)
- Halogens ($\text{X} = \text{Cl, Br, I}$) $\longrightarrow$ Sodium halide ($\text{NaX}$)
- Nitrogen and Sulfur together $\longrightarrow$ Sodium thiocyanate ($\text{NaSCN}$)

#### B. Chemical Reactions during Fusion

$$
\text{Na} + \text{C} + \text{N} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{NaCN}
$$

$$
2\text{Na} + \text{S} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Na}_2\text{S}
$$

$$
\text{Na} + \text{X} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{NaX} \quad (\text{X} = \text{Cl, Br, I})
$$

$$
\text{Na} + \text{C} + \text{N} + \text{S} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{NaSCN} \quad (\text{In case of insufficient Na})
$$

> **Crucial Experimental Condition (Excess Sodium):**  
> If sodium is taken in excess during fusion of a compound containing both $\text{N}$ and $\text{S}$, the thiocyanate decomposes into cyanide and sulfide:
> 

$$
\text{NaSCN} + 2\text{Na} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{NaCN} + \text{Na}_2\text{S}
$$

***

### 1.3 Detection of Nitrogen ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Principle & Step-by-Step Procedure
1. Take $1\text{–}2\text{ mL}$ of Lassaigne’s extract (SFE).
2. Add freshly prepared iron(II) sulfate ($\text{FeSO}_4$) solution.
3. Heat the mixture gently. During this heating under alkaline conditions (from residual $\text{Na}$), $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ reacts with $\text{CN}^-$ to yield hexacyanidoferrate(II) ions $[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]^{4-}$.
4. A few drops of concentrated sulfuric acid ($\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$) are added. Acidification oxidizes some $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ to $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ and prevents precipitation of $\text{Fe(OH)}_2$.
5. The generated $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ reacts with $[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]^{4-}$ to yield a **Prussian blue** precipitate/coloration of iron(III) hexacyanidoferrate(II).

#### B. Chemical Equations

$$
\text{FeSO}_4 + 2\text{NaOH} \longrightarrow \text{Fe(OH)}_2 \downarrow + \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4
$$

$$
\text{Fe(OH)}_2 + 2\text{NaCN} \longrightarrow \text{Fe(CN)}_2 + 2\text{NaOH}
$$

$$
\text{Fe(CN)}_2 + 4\text{NaCN} \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Sodium hexacyanidoferrate(II)}}{\text{Na}_4[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]}
$$

$$
3\text{Na}_4[\text{Fe(CN)}_6] + 4\text{Fe}^{3+} \longrightarrow \underset{\begin{subarray}{c} \text{Prussian Blue} \\ \text{Iron(III) hexacyanidoferrate(II)} \end{subarray}}{\text{Fe}_4[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]_3 \cdot x\text{H}_2\text{O}} + 12\text{Na}^+
$$

#### C. Case of Simultaneous Presence of Nitrogen and Sulfur
If insufficient sodium was used, sodium thiocyanate ($\text{NaSCN}$) forms. Upon addition of $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ ions (without conversion to hexacyanidoferrate), a **blood-red** coloration of iron(III) thiocyanate is formed:

$$
\text{Fe}^{3+} + \text{SCN}^- \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Blood-Red Coloration}}{[\text{Fe(SCN)}]^{2+}} \quad \text{or} \quad \text{Fe(SCN)}_3
$$

***

### 1.4 Detection of Sulfur ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Two independent tests confirm sulfur in the SFE:

#### A. Sodium Nitroprusside Test
A few drops of sodium nitroprusside solution ($\text{Na}_2[\text{Fe(CN)}_5\text{NO}]$) are added to an aliquot of SFE. An intense **deep violet / purple** color confirms sulfur.

$$
\text{Na}_2\text{S} + \underset{\text{Sodium nitroprusside}}{\text{Na}_2[\text{Fe(CN)}_5\text{NO}]} \longrightarrow \underset{\begin{subarray}{c} \text{Sodium thionitroprusside} \\ \text{(Deep Violet / Purple)} \end{subarray}}{\text{Na}_4[\text{Fe(CN)}_5\text{NOS}]}
$$

#### B. Lead Acetate Test
An aliquot of SFE is acidified with dilute acetic acid ($\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$), followed by addition of lead(II) acetate solution. A **black precipitate** of lead(II) sulfide confirms sulfur.

$$
\text{Na}_2\text{S} + (\text{CH}_3\text{COO})_2\text{Pb} \xrightarrow{\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}} \underset{\text{Black Precipitate}}{\text{PbS} \downarrow} + 2\text{CH}_3\text{COONa}
$$

> **Examiner Trap:** Acidification must be performed using **dilute acetic acid**, NOT dilute $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ or $\text{HCl}$. Sulfuric acid precipitates insoluble white lead sulfate ($\text{PbSO}_4 \downarrow$), while hydrochloric acid precipitates white lead chloride ($\text{PbCl}_2 \downarrow$), obscuring the black $\text{PbS}$ observation.

***

### 1.5 Detection of Halogens (Silver Nitrate Test & Layer Test) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Silver Nitrate Test ($\text{AgNO}_3$)
1. **Boiling with Concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$:**  
   Before adding silver nitrate, the SFE **must be boiled with concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$** to decompose any $\text{NaCN}$ or $\text{Na}_2\text{S}$ present. 
   

$$
\text{NaCN} + \text{HNO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{NaNO}_3 + \text{HCN} \uparrow
$$

   

$$
\text{Na}_2\text{S} + 2\text{HNO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\text{NaNO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{S} \uparrow
$$

   *If not decomposed, cyanide and sulfide interfere by giving false precipitates:*
   

$$
\text{NaCN} + \text{AgNO}_3 \longrightarrow \underset{\text{White Precipitate}}{\text{AgCN} \downarrow} + \text{NaNO}_3
$$

   

$$
\text{Na}_2\text{S} + 2\text{AgNO}_3 \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Black Precipitate}}{\text{Ag}_2\text{S} \downarrow} + 2\text{NaNO}_3
$$

2. **Precipitation with $\text{AgNO}_3$:**
   

$$
\text{NaX} + \text{AgNO}_3 \longrightarrow \text{AgX} \downarrow + \text{NaNO}_3
$$

| Halogen | Observation ($\text{AgX}$) | Solubility in Ammonium Hydroxide ($\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Chlorine ($\text{Cl}$)** | **Curdy white** precipitate ($\text{AgCl}$) | Readily completely soluble, forming $[\text{Ag(NH}_3)_2]\text{Cl}$ |
| **Bromine ($\text{Br}$)** | **Pale yellow** precipitate ($\text{AgBr}$) | Sparingly / partially soluble in dilute $\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$ |
| **Iodine ($\text{I}$)** | **Bright yellow** precipitate ($\text{AgI}$) | Completely insoluble in $\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$ |

**Complexation Reaction for $\text{AgCl}$:**

$$
\text{AgCl} + 2\text{NH}_4\text{OH} \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Diamminesilver(I) chloride (Soluble)}}{[\text{Ag(NH}_3)_2]\text{Cl}} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

#### B. Organic Layer Test (for $\text{Br}^-$ and $\text{I}^-$)
When both halogens or trace amounts are present:
1. Acidify SFE with dilute $\text{HNO}_3$ or dilute $\text{HCl}$.
2. Add a small volume of carbon tetrachloride ($\text{CCl}_4$) or chloroform ($\text{CHCl}_3$).
3. Add freshly prepared chlorine water dropwise with vigorous shaking.
4. Chlorine oxidizes bromide to bromine and iodide to iodine:
   

$$
2\text{NaBr} + \text{Cl}_2 \longrightarrow 2\text{NaCl} + \text{Br}_2
$$

   

$$
2\text{NaI} + \text{Cl}_2 \longrightarrow 2\text{NaCl} + \text{I}_2
$$

5. $\text{Br}_2$ dissolves in the organic layer imparting an **orange/reddish-brown** color.
6. $\text{I}_2$ dissolves in the organic layer imparting a **violet/purple** color.

### 1.6 Detection of Phosphorus ⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Principle & Procedure
1. The organic compound is fused with an oxidizing agent like sodium peroxide ($\text{Na}_2\text{O}_2$). Phosphorus is oxidized to sodium phosphate:
   

$$
\text{P} \xrightarrow{\text{Na}_2\text{O}_2, \Delta} \text{Na}_3\text{PO}_4
$$

2. The fused mass is extracted with boiling water, filtered, and the filtrate is acidified with concentrated nitric acid ($\text{HNO}_3$).
3. Ammonium molybdate, $(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{MoO}_4$, is added in excess and the mixture warmed gently.
4. Formation of a **canary-yellow precipitate** of ammonium phosphomolybdate confirms phosphorus.

#### B. Chemical Reactions

$$
\text{Na}_3\text{PO}_4 + 3\text{HNO}_3 \longrightarrow \text{H}_3\text{PO}_4 + 3\text{NaNO}_3
$$

$$
\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4 + 12(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{MoO}_4 + 21\text{HNO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \underset{\begin{subarray}{c} \text{Ammonium phosphomolybdate} \\ \text{(Canary-Yellow Precipitate)} \end{subarray}}{(\text{NH}_4)_3[\text{P(Mo}_{3}\text{O}_{10})_4] \downarrow} + 21\text{NH}_4\text{NO}_3 + 12\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

*(Commonly written as $(\text{NH}_4)_3\text{PO}_4 \cdot 12\text{MoO}_3$)*

### 2.1 Carbon and Hydrogen: Liebig’s Combustion Method ⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Experimental Apparatus & Assembly
- A known mass ($m\text{ g}$) of organic compound is placed in a platinum boat and burned in a combustion tube containing granulated $\text{CuO}$ in an atmosphere of pure, dry dioxygen ($\text{O}_2$).
- The combustion products ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and $\text{CO}_2$) pass through a pre-weighed U-tube containing **anhydrous calcium chloride** ($\text{CaCl}_2$) to absorb water.
- Next, gases pass through a pre-weighed U-tube containing concentrated **potassium hydroxide ($\text{KOH}$) solution** (or ascarite/soda-lime) to absorb carbon dioxide.

#### B. Fundamental Derivations

Let:
- Mass of organic compound taken $= m\text{ g}$
- Gain in mass of anhydrous $\text{CaCl}_2$ tube $= m_1\text{ g}$ (mass of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ produced)
- Gain in mass of $\text{KOH}$ tube $= m_2\text{ g}$ (mass of $\text{CO}_2$ produced)

**1. Estimation of Carbon:**

$$
\text{Molar mass of CO}_2 = 44.01\text{ g mol}^{-1} \quad (\text{contains } 12.011\text{ g of Carbon})
$$

$$
44\text{ g of CO}_2 \equiv 12\text{ g of C}
$$

$$
\text{Mass of Carbon in sample} = \frac{12}{44} \times m_2\text{ g}
$$

$$
\boxed{\% \text{ C} = \frac{12}{44} \times \frac{m_2}{m} \times 100\%}
$$

**2. Estimation of Hydrogen:**

$$
\text{Molar mass of H}_2\text{O} = 18.015\text{ g mol}^{-1} \quad (\text{contains } 2.016\text{ g of Hydrogen})
$$

$$
18\text{ g of H}_2\text{O} \equiv 2\text{ g of H}
$$

$$
\text{Mass of Hydrogen in sample} = \frac{2}{18} \times m_1\text{ g}
$$

$$
\boxed{\% \text{ H} = \frac{2}{18} \times \frac{m_1}{m} \times 100\%}
$$

### 2.2 Estimation of Nitrogen

Two standard analytical routes exist for nitrogen estimation:

***

#### Method I: Dumas Method ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

##### A. Principle & Reactions
A known mass ($m\text{ g}$) of the nitrogenous organic compound is mixed with cupric oxide ($\text{CuO}$) and heated in an atmosphere of carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) generated from heating sodium hydrogen carbonate ($\text{NaHCO}_3$) or dry ice.
- Carbon converts to $\text{CO}_2$, hydrogen to $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, and nitrogen to free dinitrogen ($\text{N}_2$) gas.
- Any traces of nitrogen oxides ($\text{NO}_x$) produced are reduced back to elementary $\text{N}_2$ by passing over a **reduced copper gauze** positioned at the delivery end of the tube:

$$
\text{C}_x\text{H}_y\text{N}_z + (2x + y/2)\text{CuO} \longrightarrow x\text{CO}_2 + \frac{y}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O} + \frac{z}{2}\text{N}_2 + (2x + y/2)\text{Cu}
$$

$$
\text{NO}_x + 2x\text{Cu} \longrightarrow x\text{Cu}_2\text{O} + \frac{1}{2}\text{N}_2
$$

The gaseous mixture is collected over concentrated aqueous $\text{KOH}$ ($40\text{–}50\%$) in a graduated nitrometer. 
- $\text{KOH}$ absorbs $\text{CO}_2$ completely: $2\text{KOH} + \text{CO}_2 \longrightarrow \text{K}_2\text{CO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$.
- Water vapor condenses.
- Pure $\text{N}_2$ gas collects in the upper calibrated tube.

##### B. Step-by-Step Derivation
Let:
- Mass of organic compound $= m\text{ g}$
- Volume of $\text{N}_2$ gas collected in nitrometer $= V_1\text{ mL}$ at experimental temperature $T_1\text{ K}$ and atmospheric pressure $P\text{ mmHg}$.
- Aqueous tension (vapor pressure of water) at $T_1\text{ K} = p\text{ mmHg}$.

**Step 1: Determine pressure of dry $\text{N}_2$ gas ($P_1$):**

$$
P_1 = P - p\text{ mmHg}
$$

**Step 2: Convert volume to Standard Temperature and Pressure ($\text{STP}: P_0 = 760\text{ mmHg}, T_0 = 273.15\text{ K}$):**
Using the Ideal Gas Equation:

$$
\frac{P_1 V_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_0 V_0}{T_0} \implies V_0 = \frac{P_1 \times V_1 \times 273}{760 \times T_1}\text{ mL}
$$

**Step 3: Convert STP volume to mass:**

$$
22400\text{ mL of dry N}_2 \text{ at STP has a mass of } 28\text{ g}
$$

$$
\text{Mass of } V_0\text{ mL of N}_2 = \frac{28}{22400} \times V_0\text{ g} = \frac{V_0}{800}\text{ g}
$$

**Step 4: Percentage of Nitrogen:**

$$
\boxed{\% \text{ N} = \frac{28 \times V_0}{22400 \times m} \times 100\% = \frac{V_0}{8 \times m}\%}
$$

***

#### Method II: Kjeldahl's Method ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

##### A. Principle & Chemical Reactions
A known mass ($m\text{ g}$) of organic compound is digested with concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ in a long-necked Kjeldahl flask:
- Anhydrous potassium sulfate ($\text{K}_2\text{SO}_4$) is added to **elevate the boiling point** of sulfuric acid.
- Copper(II) sulfate ($\text{CuSO}_4$) or mercury/selenium powder acts as a **catalytic agent** to accelerate digestion.
- Nitrogen converts quantitatively into **ammonium sulfate** ($(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4$).

**1. Digestion:**

$$
\text{Organic Compound (N)} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \xrightarrow{\text{CuSO}_4, \text{K}_2\text{SO}_4, \Delta} (\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4 + \text{CO}_2 \uparrow + \text{SO}_2 \uparrow + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

**2. Distillation:**
The clarified digest is transferred to a distillation flask, made strongly alkaline with excess sodium hydroxide ($\text{NaOH}$), and steam distilled:

$$
(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4 + 2\text{NaOH} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + 2\text{NH}_3 \uparrow
$$

**3. Absorption & Titration (Back-titration vs Direct Acidimetry):**
The liberated ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$) is quantitatively absorbed in a known volume ($V\text{ mL}$) of standard standard sulfuric acid (molarity $M$ or normality $N_1$):

$$
2\text{NH}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \longrightarrow (\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4
$$

The unreacted residual acid is back-titrated against standard $\text{NaOH}$ (normality $N_2$).

##### B. Mathematical Derivation
Let:
- Mass of organic compound $= m\text{ g}$
- Initial volume of standard acid ($\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$) taken $= V\text{ mL}$ of normality $N_1$
- Volume of standard alkali ($\text{NaOH}$) of normality $N_2$ required for neutralizing unused excess acid $= V_1\text{ mL}$

Using normality equivalence:

$$
\text{Milliequivalents (meq) of unreacted excess acid} = N_2 \times V_1
$$

$$
\text{Total meq of acid taken initially} = N_1 \times V
$$

$$
\text{meq of acid neutralized solely by liberated NH}_3 = (N_1 V - N_2 V_1)
$$

Since:

$$
1000\text{ mL of } 1\text{ N acid} \equiv 1000\text{ meq of acid} \equiv 1\text{ mol of NH}_3 \equiv 14\text{ g of Nitrogen}
$$

$$
1\text{ meq of acid} \equiv \frac{14}{1000}\text{ g of N} = 0.014\text{ g of N}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\text{Mass of Nitrogen in sample} = (N_1 V - N_2 V_1) \times 0.014\text{ g}
$$

$$
\% \text{ N} = \frac{(N_1 V - N_2 V_1) \times 0.014}{m} \times 100\%
$$

$$
\boxed{\% \text{ N} = \frac{1.4 \times [N_1 V - N_2 V_1]}{m} = \frac{1.4 \times (\text{Net meq of acid consumed by NH}_3)}{m}}
$$

If molarity ($M$) is given for diprotic $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$:  
Remember: Normality $= \text{Molarity} \times \text{Basicity} = 2 \times M$.

##### C. Structural Limitations of Kjeldahl's Method ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Kjeldahl's method is **NOT** applicable to:
1. **Nitro compounds ($-\text{NO}_2$):** (e.g., nitrobenzene)
2. **Azo compounds ($-\text{N}=\text{N}-$):** (e.g., azobenzene)
3. **Compounds with nitrogen in a heterocyclic ring:** (e.g., pyridine, quinoline, pyrrole)

**Mechanistic Reason:** Under standard acidic digestion conditions, nitrogen in these structural environments does not convert quantitatively into ammonium sulfate; instead, it escapes as free nitrogen oxides or elemental nitrogen gas.

### 2.3 Estimation of Halogens: Carius Method ⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Principle & Chemical Reactions
A known mass ($m\text{ g}$) of the halogenated organic compound is heated with concentrated nitric acid ($\text{HNO}_3$) in the presence of solid silver nitrate ($\text{AgNO}_3$) in a sealed, thick-walled glass tube (**Carius tube**) inside an iron jacket furnace at $450\text{–}550\text{ K}$.
- Carbon and hydrogen are oxidized to $\text{CO}_2$ and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$.
- The halogen is converted into the corresponding silver halide ($\text{AgX}$):

$$
\text{Organic Halogen} + \text{HNO}_3 + \text{AgNO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{AgX} \downarrow + \text{CO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

The precipitate of $\text{AgX}$ is separated by filtration through a Gooch crucible, washed thoroughly with dilute nitric acid followed by water, dried to constant mass, and weighed.

#### B. Derivations for $\% \text{ Cl}$, $\% \text{ Br}$, $\% \text{ I}$

Let:
- Mass of organic compound $= m\text{ g}$
- Mass of silver halide ($\text{AgX}$) obtained $= m_1\text{ g}$

$$
\text{Molar Masses: } \text{Ag} = 108\text{ g/mol}, \text{Cl} = 35.5\text{ g/mol}, \text{Br} = 80\text{ g/mol}, \text{I} = 127\text{ g/mol}
$$

$$
\text{AgCl} = 143.5\text{ g/mol}, \quad \text{AgBr} = 188\text{ g/mol}, \quad \text{AgI} = 235\text{ g/mol}
$$

**1. Chlorine:**

$$
\boxed{\% \text{ Cl} = \frac{35.5}{143.5} \times \frac{m_1}{m} \times 100\%}
$$

**2. Bromine:**

$$
\boxed{\% \text{ Br} = \frac{80}{188} \times \frac{m_1}{m} \times 100\%}
$$

**3. Iodine:**

$$
\boxed{\% \text{ I} = \frac{127}{235} \times \frac{m_1}{m} \times 100\%}
$$

### 2.4 Estimation of Sulfur: Carius Method ⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Principle & Reactions
A known mass ($m\text{ g}$) of sulfur-containing organic compound is heated in a Carius tube with fuming nitric acid ($\text{HNO}_3$) or sodium peroxide ($\text{Na}_2\text{O}_2$).
- Sulfur is oxidized completely to sulfuric acid:

$$
\text{S} + 6\text{HNO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 + 6\text{NO}_2 \uparrow + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

- Excess barium chloride solution ($\text{BaCl}_2$) is added to precipitate insoluble barium sulfate ($\text{BaSO}_4$):

$$
\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 + \text{BaCl}_2 \longrightarrow \underset{\text{White Precipitate}}{\text{BaSO}_4 \downarrow} + 2\text{HCl}
$$

- The precipitate is filtered, washed, dried, ignited, and weighed.

#### B. Derivation
Let:
- Mass of organic compound taken $= m\text{ g}$
- Mass of $\text{BaSO}_4$ precipitate $= m_1\text{ g}$

$$
\text{Molar mass of BaSO}_4 = 137.33 + 32.06 + 4(16.00) = 233.4\text{ g mol}^{-1}
$$

$$
233.4\text{ g of BaSO}_4 \text{ contains } 32\text{ g of Sulfur}
$$

$$
\boxed{\% \text{ S} = \frac{32}{233.4} \times \frac{m_1}{m} \times 100\%}
$$

### 2.5 Estimation of Phosphorus ⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Principle & Reactions
A known mass ($m\text{ g}$) of phosphorus-containing organic compound is heated with fuming nitric acid in a Carius tube:

$$
\text{P} \xrightarrow{\text{HNO}_3, \Delta} \text{H}_3\text{PO}_4
$$

Phosphoric acid can be quantitatively determined through two analytical methods:

**Method 1: Precipitation as Ammonium Phosphomolybdate:**
Ammonium molybdate solution in excess $\text{HNO}_3$ precipitates phosphorus as:

$$
\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4 + 12(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{MoO}_4 + 21\text{HNO}_3 \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Molar mass } = 1877\text{ g/mol}}{(\text{NH}_4)_3\text{PO}_4 \cdot 12\text{MoO}_3 \downarrow} + 21\text{NH}_4\text{NO}_3 + 12\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

If mass of $(\text{NH}_4)_3\text{PO}_4 \cdot 12\text{MoO}_3 = m_1\text{ g}$:

$$
\boxed{\% \text{ P} = \frac{31}{1877} \times \frac{m_1}{m} \times 100\%}
$$

**Method 2: Precipitation as Magnesium Pyrophosphate ($\text{Mg}_2\text{P}_2\text{O}_7$):**
Magnesia mixture ($\text{MgCl}_2 + \text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + \text{NH}_4\text{OH}$) is added to yield magnesium ammonium phosphate, which on ignition yields magnesium pyrophosphate:

$$
\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4 + \text{MgCl}_2 + 3\text{NH}_4\text{OH} \longrightarrow \text{MgNH}_4\text{PO}_4 \downarrow + 2\text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

$$
2\text{MgNH}_4\text{PO}_4 \xrightarrow{\Delta \text{ (Ignition)}} \underset{\text{Molar mass } = 222.6\text{ g/mol}}{\text{Mg}_2\text{P}_2\text{O}_7} + 2\text{NH}_3 \uparrow + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

If mass of $\text{Mg}_2\text{P}_2\text{O}_7 = m_1\text{ g}$:

$$
222.6\text{ g of Mg}_2\text{P}_2\text{O}_7 \equiv 2 \times 31 = 62\text{ g of Phosphorus}
$$

$$
\boxed{\% \text{ P} = \frac{62}{222.6} \times \frac{m_1}{m} \times 100\%}
$$

### 2.6 Estimation of Oxygen ⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Method of Difference
Usually, the percentage of oxygen is calculated indirectly:

$$
\boxed{\% \text{ Oxygen} = 100\% - \left[\% \text{ C} + \% \text{ H} + \% \text{ N} + \% \text{ S} + \% \text{ Halogen} + \% \text{ P}\right]}
$$

#### B. Direct Determination (Unterzaucher Method)
A known mass ($m\text{ g}$) of organic compound is pyrolyzed in an atmosphere of pure dinitrogen gas over activated carbon at $1373\text{ K}$ ($1100^\circ\text{C}$). Oxygen quantitatively converts to carbon monoxide:

$$
\text{Compound (O)} \xrightarrow{1373\text{ K}, \text{C}} \text{CO}
$$

The gas is passed over warm iodine pentoxide ($\text{I}_2\text{O}_5$) at $423\text{ K}$, which oxidizes $\text{CO}$ to $\text{CO}_2$ while liberating free iodine:

$$
5\text{CO} + \text{I}_2\text{O}_5 \longrightarrow 5\text{CO}_2 + \text{I}_2
$$

From stoichiometry:

$$
5\text{ mol CO} \equiv 5 \text{ mol O} \equiv 5 \text{ mol CO}_2
$$

$$
1 \text{ mol O} \equiv 1 \text{ mol CO}_2
$$

$$
44\text{ g of CO}_2 \equiv 16\text{ g of Oxygen}
$$

If mass of $\text{CO}_2$ produced $= m_1\text{ g}$:

$$
\boxed{\% \text{ O} = \frac{16}{44} \times \frac{m_1}{m} \times 100\%}
$$

Alternatively, the liberated $\text{I}_2$ can be titrated against standard sodium thiosulfate ($\text{Na}_2\text{S}_2\text{O}_3$).

### Problem 3.1: Liebig’s Method (Carbon & Hydrogen)
**Question:** On complete combustion, $0.246\text{ g}$ of an organic compound gave $0.198\text{ g}$ of carbon dioxide and $0.1014\text{ g}$ of water. Determine the percentage composition of carbon and hydrogen in the compound.

**Solution:**
- Mass of organic compound ($m$) $= 0.246\text{ g}$
- Mass of $\text{CO}_2$ ($m_2$) $= 0.198\text{ g}$
- Mass of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ ($m_1$) $= 0.1014\text{ g}$

1. **Percentage of Carbon:**
   

$$
\% \text{ C} = \frac{12}{44} \times \frac{m_2}{m} \times 100\% = \frac{12}{44} \times \frac{0.198}{0.246} \times 100\%
$$

   

$$
\% \text{ C} = 0.27272 \times 0.80488 \times 100\% = 21.95\%
$$

2. **Percentage of Hydrogen:**
   

$$
\% \text{ H} = \frac{2}{18} \times \frac{m_1}{m} \times 100\% = \frac{2}{18} \times \frac{0.1014}{0.246} \times 100\%
$$

   

$$
\% \text{ H} = 0.11111 \times 0.41220 \times 100\% = 4.58\%
$$

### Problem 3.2: Dumas Method (Nitrogen)
**Question:** In Dumas’ method for estimation of nitrogen, $0.3\text{ g}$ of an organic compound gave $50\text{ mL}$ of nitrogen collected at $300\text{ K}$ and $715\text{ mm}$ pressure. Calculate the percentage composition of nitrogen in the compound. (Aqueous tension at $300\text{ K} = 15\text{ mm}$).

**Solution:**
- Mass of organic substance ($m$) $= 0.3\text{ g}$
- Total barometric pressure ($P$) $= 715\text{ mmHg}$
- Aqueous tension ($p$) $= 15\text{ mmHg}$
- Pressure of dry $\text{N}_2$ ($P_1$) $= P - p = 715 - 15 = 700\text{ mmHg}$
- Experimental volume ($V_1$) $= 50\text{ mL}$
- Experimental temperature ($T_1$) $= 300\text{ K}$

1. **Convert Volume to STP ($P_0 = 760\text{ mmHg}, T_0 = 273\text{ K}$):**
   

$$
V_0 = \frac{P_1 V_1 T_0}{P_0 T_1} = \frac{700 \times 50 \times 273}{760 \times 300} = \frac{9555000}{228000} \approx 41.908\text{ mL}
$$

2. **Percentage of Nitrogen:**
   

$$
\% \text{ N} = \frac{28 \times V_0}{22400 \times m} \times 100\% = \frac{28 \times 41.908}{22400 \times 0.3} \times 100\%
$$

   

$$
\% \text{ N} = \frac{1173.42}{67.2} \approx 17.46\%
$$

### Problem 3.3: Kjeldahl's Method (Back-Titration Archetype)
**Question:** During Kjeldahl's estimation of nitrogen, the ammonia evolved from $0.5\text{ g}$ of an organic compound neutralized $10\text{ mL}$ of $1\text{ M } \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$. Find the percentage of nitrogen in the compound.

**Solution:**
- Mass of compound ($m$) $= 0.5\text{ g}$
- Molarity of $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ $= 1\text{ M}$
- Basicity of $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 = 2 \implies \text{Normality } (N) = 1\text{ M} \times 2 = 2\text{ N}$
- Volume of acid neutralized ($V$) $= 10\text{ mL}$

1. **Equivalents Calculation:**
   

$$
\text{meq of acid neutralized by ammonia} = N \times V = 2 \times 10 = 20\text{ meq}
$$

2. **Percentage of Nitrogen:**
   

$$
\% \text{ N} = \frac{1.4 \times (\text{meq of acid consumed})}{m} = \frac{1.4 \times 20}{0.5} = \frac{28}{0.5} = 56.0\%
$$

### Problem 3.4: Kjeldahl's Method (Excess Acid / Residual Neutralization Archetype)
**Question:** $0.4\text{ g}$ of an organic compound was treated according to Kjeldahl’s method. The ammonia evolved was absorbed in $50\text{ mL}$ of $0.1\text{ N } \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$. The residual acid required $30\text{ mL}$ of $0.1\text{ N } \text{NaOH}$ for complete neutralization. Calculate the percentage of nitrogen in the compound.

**Solution:**
- Mass of compound ($m$) $= 0.4\text{ g}$
- Initial acid: $V_1 = 50\text{ mL}, N_1 = 0.1\text{ N} \implies \text{Initial meq} = 50 \times 0.1 = 5.0\text{ meq}$
- Residual acid neutralized by $\text{NaOH}$: $V_2 = 30\text{ mL}, N_2 = 0.1\text{ N} \implies \text{Residual meq} = 30 \times 0.1 = 3.0\text{ meq}$

1. **Net Acid Neutralized by Liberated Ammonia:**
   

$$
\text{Net meq} = 5.0\text{ meq} - 3.0\text{ meq} = 2.0\text{ meq}
$$

2. **Percentage of Nitrogen:**
   

$$
\% \text{ N} = \frac{1.4 \times 2.0}{0.4} = \frac{2.8}{0.4} = 7.0\%
$$

# PART 4: EXAMINER TRAPS & COMMON STUDENT PITFALLS

1. **Aqueous Tension Ignored in Dumas Method:**
   - **Mistake:** Using barometric pressure ($P$) directly in the gas equation without subtracting aqueous tension.
   - **Rule:** Nitrogen is collected over an aqueous $\text{KOH}$ solution and is saturated with water vapor. You **must** subtract aqueous tension:
     

$$
P_{\text{dry } \text{N}_2} = P_{\text{moist}} - p_{\text{aq. tension}}
$$

2. **Molarity vs. Normality Confusion in Kjeldahl Calculations:**
   - **Mistake:** Directly multiplying the molarity of sulfuric acid by its volume without considering basicity ($n\text{-factor} = 2$).
   - **Rule:** $\text{Normality of H}_2\text{SO}_4 = 2 \times \text{Molarity}$. For example, $10\text{ mL}$ of $0.5\text{ M } \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ is equivalent to $10\text{ mL}$ of $1.0\text{ N } \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 = 10\text{ meq}$.

3. **Skipping the Boiling Step with $\text{HNO}_3$ in the Halogen Test:**
   - **Mistake:** Adding $\text{AgNO}_3$ directly to SFE containing nitrogen or sulfur.
   - **Fact:** $\text{CN}^-$ and $\text{S}^{2-}$ react with $\text{Ag}^+$ to form white $\text{AgCN}$ and black $\text{Ag}_2\text{S}$, producing false-positive signals for chlorine. The extract must be boiled with concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$ first.

4. **Kjeldahl Structural Exclusions:**
   - **Mistake:** Assuming all nitrogenous compounds can be analyzed via Kjeldahl's method.
   - **Fact:** Nitro compounds, azo compounds, and nitrogen-containing rings (pyridine, quinoline) do not yield ammonium sulfate under Kjeldahl digestion. Only the Dumas method works universally for all organic nitrogen compounds.

5. **Sulfur Confirmation Acid Selection:**
   - **Mistake:** Using dilute $\text{HCl}$ or $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ instead of $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$ for the lead acetate test.
   - **Fact:** $\text{Pb}^{2+}$ precipitates as white $\text{PbSO}_4$ or $\text{PbCl}_2$, obscuring the black $\text{PbS}$ precipitate. Use only **dilute acetic acid**.

6. **Order of Absorption Tubes in Liebig Combustion:**
   - **Mistake:** Placing the $\text{KOH}$ tube before the $\text{CaCl}_2$ tube.
   - **Fact:** $\text{KOH}$ is hygroscopic and absorbs both $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and $\text{CO}_2$. If placed first, it absorbs both water and carbon dioxide, ruining both measurements. Anhydrous $\text{CaCl}_2$ absorbs water only, leaving $\text{CO}_2$ to pass into the subsequent $\text{KOH}$ tube.

#### Question A.2.1: Carbocation Stability & Hyperconjugation
**Tags:** [CBSE Competency Pattern, Assertion-Reason, 1 Mark]  
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

- **Assertion (A):** The tert-butyl carbocation, $\left(\text{CH}_3\right)_3\text{C}^+$, is thermodynamically more stable than the isopropyl carbocation, $\left(\text{CH}_3\right)_2\text{CH}^+$.
- **Reason (R):** The tert-butyl carbocation possesses $9$ $\alpha$-hydrogens capable of undergoing hyperconjugation ($C_{\alpha}-H$ $\sigma \to p$ delocalization), compared to only $6$ in the isopropyl carbocation.

**Answer:** **(a)**  
**CBSE Marking Scheme / Step Justification:**
- Assertion is true: Alkyl carbocation stability follows $3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ$. **[½ Mark]**
- Reason is true and explicitly explains stability via hyperconjugation: delocalization of $\sigma$-electrons of $C_{\alpha}-H$ bonds into the vacant unhybridized $2p$-orbital of the carbocationic center disperses the positive charge. Greater number of hyperconjugative structures ($n+1 = 10$ vs $7$) directly dictates greater thermodynamic stability. **[½ Mark]**

#### Question A.2.2: Inductive vs Electromeric Effect
**Tags:** [CBSE Competency Pattern, Assertion-Reason, 1 Mark]  
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ [High-Yield]

- **Assertion (A):** The inductive effect is a permanent electron displacement effect operating through $\sigma$-bonds, whereas the electromeric effect is a temporary electron displacement occurring only in the presence of an attacking reagent in multiple bonds.
- **Reason (R):** Inductive effect involves the complete transfer of a shared pair of $\pi$-electrons to one of the bonded atoms.

**Answer:** **(c)**  
**CBSE Marking Scheme / Step Justification:**
- Assertion is true: Inductive effect persists in the ground state along $\sigma$-bonds; electromeric effect requires an attacking reagent and vanishes upon its removal. **[½ Mark]**
- Reason is false: The inductive effect involves only the *partial shifting/polarization* of $\sigma$-electrons, never complete transfer. Complete transfer of $\pi$-electrons characterizes the electromeric effect. **[½ Mark]**

#### Question A.2.4: Halogen Detection by Lassaigne’s Test
**Tags:** [CBSE Competency Pattern, Assertion-Reason, 1 Mark]  
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ [High-Yield]

- **Assertion (A):** In Lassaigne’s test for halogens, the sodium fusion extract is boiled with concentrated nitric acid ($\text{HNO}_3$) prior to the addition of silver nitrate ($\text{AgNO}_3$).
- **Reason (R):** Concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$ decomposes any sodium cyanide ($\text{NaCN}$) and sodium sulfide ($\text{Na}_2\text{S}$) present, which would otherwise interfere by forming white $\text{AgCN}$ or black $\text{Ag}_2\text{S}$ precipitates.

**Answer:** **(a)**  
**CBSE Marking Scheme / Step Justification:**
- Assertion is true: Boiling with $\text{HNO}_3$ is an essential step when $\text{N}$ or $\text{S}$ are present. **[½ Mark]**
- Reason is true: The chemical reactions are:
  

$$
\text{NaCN} + \text{HNO}_3 \to \text{NaNO}_3 + \text{HCN}\uparrow
$$

  

$$
\text{Na}_2\text{S} + 2\text{HNO}_3 \to 2\text{NaNO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{S}\uparrow

#### Problem A.3.2: Relative Acidities and Electronic Effects
**Tags:** [CBSE Competency Pattern, HOTS, 3 Marks]  
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

Arrange the following sets of organic compounds in increasing order of their acidic strength ($K_a$). Justify each placement using resonance, inductive ($-I/+I$), and steric/orbital concepts:
1. $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$, $\text{FCH}_2\text{COOH}$, $\text{ClCH}_2\text{COOH}$, $\text{O}_2\text{N}-\text{CH}_2\text{COOH}$
2. Phenol, 4-Nitrophenol, 4-Methoxyphenol, 4-Methylphenol
3. Ethyne, Ethene, Ethane, Propyne

***

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Scheme:

##### Set 1: Substituted Acetic Acids

$$
\mathbf{\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} < \text{ClCH}_2\text{COOH} < \text{FCH}_2\text{COOH} < \text{O}_2\text{N}-\text{CH}_2\text{COOH}}
$$

- **Justification:** Acidity depends on the stability of the conjugate base (carboxylate anion, $\text{RCOO}^-$). 
- Electron-withdrawing groups ($-I$) disperse the negative charge across the carboxylate group, stabilizing the conjugate base.
- Electronegativity order is $\text{F} > \text{Cl}$, so $-\text{CH}_2\text{F}$ exerts a stronger $-I$ effect than $-\text{CH}_2\text{Cl}$.
- The $-\text{NO}_2$ group exerts both powerful $-I$ and $-M$ inductive/field effects (strongest electron-withdrawing group in the set), stabilizing $\text{O}_2\text{NCH}_2\text{COO}^-$ most effectively.
- $\text{CH}_3-$ exerts an electron-donating $+I$ effect, which destabilizes the anion.  
  **Marking Rubric:**
  - Correct order: **[½ Mark]**
  - Explanation referencing $-I$ effect and conjugate base stabilization: **[½ Mark]**

##### Set 2: Substituted Phenols

$$
\mathbf{\text{4-Methoxyphenol} < \text{4-Methylphenol} < \text{Phenol} < \text{4-Nitrophenol}}
$$

- **Justification:** 
  - In **4-nitrophenol**, the $-\text{NO}_2$ group at the *para* position stabilizes the phenoxide ion via both strong $-I$ and strong $-R$ (resonance delocalization of negative charge directly onto the oxygens of $-\text{NO}_2$).
  - In **phenol**, resonance stabilization of the phenoxide ion occurs only over the phenyl ring.
  - In **4-methylphenol**, the $-\text{CH}_3$ group destabilizes the phenoxide ion through $+I$ and hyperconjugation.
  - In **4-methoxyphenol**, although the methoxy oxygen is electronegative ($-I$), its lone pair donates electrons via strong resonance ($+R$ effect), which increases electron density at the phenoxide oxygen, significantly destabilizing the conjugate base. Since $+R > -I$, 4-methoxyphenol is the weakest acid.  
  **Marking Rubric:**
  - Correct order: **[½ Mark]**
  - Justification explicitly balancing $+R$ vs $-I$ for $-\text{OCH}_3$ and $-R$ for $-\text{NO}_2$: **[½ Mark]**

##### Set 3: Hydrocarbon Acidities

$$
\mathbf{\text{Ethane} < \text{Ethene} < \text{Propyne} < \text{Ethyne}}
$$

- **Justification:**
  - Acidity is governed by the hybridization of the carbon bearing the hydrogen:
    

$$
\text{sp (50\% s-character)} > \text{sp}^2 \text{ (33.3\% s-character)} > \text{sp}^3 \text{ (25\% s-character)}
$$

  - Higher $s$-character pulls electron density closer to the nucleus, making the conjugate carbanion more stable.
  - Both ethyne and propyne feature $sp$-hybridized $\text{C}-\text{H}$ bonds. However, in **propyne** ($\text{CH}_3-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH}$), the methyl group has an electron-donating $+I$ inductive effect, which destabilizes the acetylide conjugate base ($\text{CH}_3-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}^\ominus$) relative to ethyne's conjugate base ($\text{H}-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}^\ominus$). Thus, $\text{Ethyne} > \text{Propyne}$.  
  **Marking Rubric:**
  - Correct order: **[½ Mark]**
  - Justification based on percentage $s$-character and $+I$ destabilization in propyne: **[½ Mark]**

### Question B.2: Quantitative Estimation of Elements (Carius and Kjeldahl Methods)
**Tags:** [NCERT Exercise Q12.37 & Q12.38, 4 Marks]  
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

1. In a Carius determination of sulfur, $0.378\text{ g}$ of an organic compound gave $0.699\text{ g}$ of barium sulfate ($\text{BaSO}_4$). Calculate the percentage of sulfur present in the compound. (Atomic masses: $\text{Ba} = 137$, $\text{S} = 32$, $\text{O} = 16$). *(2 Marks)*

2. During nitrogen estimation by Kjeldahl’s method, the ammonia evolved from $0.50\text{ g}$ of an organic compound neutralized $50.0\text{ mL}$ of $0.1\text{ M }\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$. ***

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Scheme:

##### 1. Percentage of Sulfur by Carius Method:
- **Molar Mass of $\text{BaSO}_4$:**
  

$$
M(\text{BaSO}_4) = 137 + 32 + (4 \times 16) = 137 + 32 + 64 = 233\text{ g/mol}

  $233\text{ g of BaSO}_4$ contains $32\text{ g of Sulfur}$.
- **Mass of Sulfur in Sample:**
  

$$
\text{Mass of S} = \frac{32}{233} \times \text{Mass of BaSO}_4 = \frac{32}{233} \times 0.699\text{ g}
$$

  

$$
\text{Mass of S} = 32 \times 0.00300 = 0.096\text{ g}
$$

- **Percentage of Sulfur:**
  

$$
\% \text{ S} = \left(\frac{\text{Mass of S}}{\text{Mass of organic compound}}\right) \times 100
$$

  

$$
\% \text{ S} = \left(\frac{0.096\text{ g}}{0.378\text{ g}}\right) \times 100 = 25.3968\% \approx \mathbf{25.40\%}
$$

  - Calculation of mass of sulfur: **[½ Mark]**
  - Correct final percentage with rounding to two decimals ($25.40\%$): **[1 Mark]**

##### 2. Percentage of Nitrogen by Kjeldahl's Method:
- **Total Initial Acid Added:**
  

$$
\text{Volume of } 0.1\text{ M }\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 = 50.0\text{ mL}
$$

  

$$
\text{Normality of } \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 = \text{Molarity} \times \text{Basicity} = 0.1\text{ M} \times 2 = 0.2\text{ N}
$$

  

$$
\text{Total milliequivalents (meq) of acid taken} = N_1 V_1 = 0.2\text{ N} \times 50.0\text{ mL} = 10.0\text{ meq}
$$

- **Back Titration with $\text{NaOH}$:**
  

$$
\text{Normality of } \text{NaOH} = 0.1\text{ M} \times 1 = 0.1\text{ N}
$$

  

$$
\text{Volume of } \text{NaOH} = 60.0\text{ mL}
$$

  

$$
\text{meq of unreacted acid} = N_2 V_2 = 0.1\text{ N} \times 60.0\text{ mL} = 6.0\text{ meq}

$$
\text{meq of acid neutralized by }\text{NH}_3 = 10.0 - 6.0 = 4.0\text{ meq}

$$
\implies \text{meq of }\text{NH}_3 \text{ evolved} = 4.0\text{ meq}
$$

$$
\% \text{ N} = \frac{1.4 \times (\text{meq of acid consumed})}{\text{Mass of sample in grams}}
$$

  

$$
\% \text{ N} = \frac{1.4 \times 4.0}{0.50} = \frac{5.6}{0.50} = \mathbf{11.20\%}
$$

### Question C.1: Fission of Covalent Bonds and Reaction Intermediates
**Tags:** [NCERT Exemplar, Analytical Subjective, 3 Marks]

1. Differentiate between homolytic and heterolytic cleavage of a covalent bond. Under what experimental reaction conditions is homolysis favored over heterolysis? *(1 Mark)*

$$
\dot{\text{C}}\text{H}_3,\quad (\text{CH}_3)_3\dot{\text{C}},\quad \text{CH}_3\dot{\text{C}}\text{H}_2,\quad (\text{CH}_3)_2\dot{\text{C}}\text{H}
$$

   *(1 Mark)*
3. Identify the electrophilic center in the molecule **methyl 2-oxopropanoate** ($\text{CH}_3-\text{CO}-\text{COOCH}_3$). When treated with a cyanide ion ($\text{CN}^-$), which carbonyl carbon is preferentially attacked? Justify your choice based on electronic factors. *(1 Mark)*

***

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Scheme:

##### 1. Homolytic vs. Heterolytic Fission:
- **Homolytic Fission:** A symmetrical cleavage where each departing atom retains one of the two shared bonding electrons, producing neutral, odd-electron species called **free radicals**:
  

$$
\text{A}-\text{B} \xrightarrow{h\nu \text{ or } \Delta} \dot{\text{A}} + \dot{\text{B}}
$$

- **Heterolytic Fission:** An asymmetrical cleavage where the more electronegative fragment retains both bonding electrons, yielding an anion and a cation:
  

$$
\text{A}-\text{B} \longrightarrow \text{A}^\oplus + :\text{B}^\ominus
$$

- **Conditions Favoring Homolysis:** High temperatures in vapor phase, non-polar solvents, ultraviolet/visible irradiation ($h\nu$), or presence of radical initiators like organic peroxides ($\text{R}-\text{O}-\text{O}-\text{R}$).  
  **Marking Rubric:**
  - Clear structural/electron distribution distinction: **[½ Mark]**
  - Mentioning conditions ($h\nu$, peroxides, non-polar phase): **[½ Mark]**

##### 2. Free Radical Stability Order & Hyperconjugation:

$$
\mathbf{\dot{\text{C}}\text{H}_3 < \text{CH}_3\dot{\text{C}}\text{H}_2 < (\text{CH}_3)_2\dot{\text{C}}\text{H} < (\text{CH}_3)_3\dot{\text{C}}}
$$

- **Explanation:**
  - Free radicals are electron-deficient species (the central carbon has 7 valence electrons and an half-filled $2p$-orbital).
  - Alkyl groups stabilize the radical center through **hyperconjugation**: delocalization of $\sigma(\text{C}_\alpha-\text{H})$ bonding electrons into the half-filled $2p$-orbital.
  - Number of hyperconjugative $\alpha$-hydrogens:
    - $\dot{\text{C}}\text{H}_3$: $0$ $\alpha$-H
    - Ethyl radical ($\text{CH}_3\dot{\text{C}}\text{H}_2$): $3$ $\alpha$-H
    - Isopropyl radical ($(\text{CH}_3)_2\dot{\text{C}}\text{H}$): $6$ $\alpha$-H
    - Tert-butyl radical ($(\text{CH}_3)_3\dot{\text{C}}$): $9$ $\alpha$-H
  - Greater number of hyperconjugative structures leads to greater dispersal of the unpaired electron and greater radical stability.  
  **Marking Rubric:**
  - Correct order: **[½ Mark]**
  - Explicit count of $\alpha$-H and $\sigma\text{–}p$ delocalization explanation: **[½ Mark]**

##### 3. Electrophilic Site Selection in $\text{CH}_3-\text{CO}-\text{COOCH}_3$:
- The molecule contains two carbonyl groups:
  - **Ketone carbonyl:** $\text{C}_2$ ($-\text{CO}-$)
  - **Ester carbonyl:** $\text{C}_1$ ($-\text{COOCH}_3$)
- **Preferential Attack:** The nucleophile ($\text{CN}^-$) attacks the **ketone carbonyl carbon ($\text{C}_2$)**.
- **Justification:**
  - In the ester group ($-\text{COOCH}_3$), the adjacent methoxy oxygen atom has lone pairs that undergo $+R$ resonance donation into the carbonyl carbon:
    

$$
-\text{C}(=\text{O})-\ddot{\text{O}}\text{CH}_3 \longleftrightarrow -\text{C}(\text{O}^\ominus)=\overset{\oplus}{\text{O}}\text{CH}_3
$$

    This delocalization reduces the partial positive charge ($\delta^+$) on the ester carbonyl carbon.
  - The ketone carbonyl carbon does not benefit from $+R$ stabilization; it experiences only the electron-withdrawing inductive effect of the adjacent ester group. Thus, $\text{C}_2$ has a much higher electrophilicity ($\delta^+$) and reacts preferentially with the nucleophile.  
  **Marking Rubric:**
  - Correctly identifying the ketone carbonyl carbon ($\text{C}_2$): **[½ Mark]**
  - Explaining ester resonance ($+R$ donation by ester oxygen) lowering electrophilicity of $\text{C}_1$: **[½ Mark]**

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Scheme:

##### 1. Fundamental Rules of Resonance Stability:
1. **Octet Rule:** Structures with complete valence octets for all second-row elements ($\text{C}, \text{N}, \text{O}, \text{F}$) are more stable than structures with incomplete octets.
2. **Covalent Bond Count:** Structures with a greater number of covalent bonds are more stable.
3. **Electronegativity and Charge Separation:** Structures without formal charges are most stable. Among charged structures:
   - Negative charge residing on more electronegative atoms ($\text{O}, \text{N}, \text{X}$) is more stable.
   - Positive charge on more electropositive/less electronegative atoms ($\text{C}$) is more stable.
   - Minimal charge separation between opposite formal charges corresponds to higher stability.  
  **Marking Rubric:**
  - Three correctly stated rules (octet, bond count, charge placement): **[1½ Marks; ½ Mark each]**

##### 2. Stability Evaluations:

- **Pair (i):**
  

$$
\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}-\ddot{\text{Cl}}: \quad (\text{Structure A}) \quad \text{vs} \quad \overset{\ominus}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2-\text{CH}=\overset{\oplus}{\text{Cl}}: \quad (\text{Structure B})
$$

  - **More Stable:** **Structure A**.
  - **Reason:** Structure A is an uncharged neutral molecule with complete octets and no separation of charge. Structure B has charge separation, and puts a positive formal charge on electronegative chlorine while having a carbanionic carbon.  
  **Marking Rubric:** Structure choice + justification: **[½ Mark]**

- **Pair (ii):**
  

$$
\text{CH}_3-\overset{\oplus}{\text{C}}=\ddot{\text{O}} \quad (\text{Structure C}) \quad \text{vs} \quad \text{CH}_3-\text{C}\equiv\overset{\oplus}{\text{O}}: \quad (\text{Structure D})
$$

  - **More Stable:** **Structure D**.
  - **Reason:** In Structure C, the carbon atom has an incomplete octet (sextet of electrons; only 6 valence electrons). In Structure D, both carbon and oxygen possess complete valence shells ($8$ electrons each) and it contains an additional covalent bond (triple bond vs double bond). Complete octet configuration overrides the electronegativity rule.  
  **Marking Rubric:** Structure choice + complete octet overriding rule: **[½ Mark]**

- **Set (iii):**
  

$$
\mathbf{I: }\ \text{CH}_2=\text{CH}-\text{CH}=\text{O} \quad > \quad \mathbf{II: }\ \overset{\oplus}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2-\text{CH}=\text{CH}-\text{O}^\ominus \quad > \quad \mathbf{III: }\ \overset{\ominus}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2-\text{CH}=\text{CH}-\text{O}^\oplus
$$

  - **Relative Order:** $\mathbf{I} > \mathbf{II} > \mathbf{III}$.
  - **Reason:** 
    - Structure $\mathbf{I}$ is neutral with all octets complete (most stable).
    - Structure $\mathbf{II}$ has opposite charges separated, with the negative charge residing on the more electronegative oxygen atom and positive charge on carbon.
    - Structure $\mathbf{III}$ is least stable because it places a positive charge on the highly electronegative oxygen and a negative charge on carbon, which is energetically unfavorable.  
  **Marking Rubric:** Correct order + rationale for $\text{II}$ vs $\text{III}$: **[1 Mark]**

- **Definition:** Resonance energy is the difference in potential energy between the actual real resonance hybrid of a molecule and its most stable theoretical Lewis canonical structure:
  

$$
\text{Resonance Energy} = E(\text{most stable canonical form}) - E(\text{resonance hybrid})
$$

- **Calculation from Heats of Hydrogenation:**
  - Cyclohexene contains $1$ $\text{C}=\text{C}$ double bond:
    

$$
\Delta H^\circ_{\text{hydro}}(\text{cyclohexene}) = -119.5\text{ kJ/mol}
$$

  - Theoretical non-delocalized model of benzene (hypothetical cyclohexa-1,3,5-triene with 3 isolated double bonds):
    

$$
\Delta H^\circ_{\text{calc}} = 3 \times (-119.5\text{ kJ/mol}) = -358.5\text{ kJ/mol}
$$

  - Actual experimental enthalpy of hydrogenation for benzene:
    

$$
\Delta H^\circ_{\text{exp}} = -208.0\text{ kJ/mol}
$$

  - **Resonance Energy Calculation:**
    

$$
\text{Resonance Energy} = |\Delta H^\circ_{\text{calc}}| - |\Delta H^\circ_{\text{exp}}|
$$

    

$$
\text{Resonance Energy} = 358.5\text{ kJ/mol} - 208.0\text{ kJ/mol} = \mathbf{150.5\text{ kJ/mol}}
$$

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Scheme:

##### 1. Chemical Separation of Benzoic Acid and Naphthalene:
- **Principle:** Benzoic acid contains a carboxylic acid ($-\text{COOH}$) functional group and reacts with weak bases, whereas naphthalene is a non-polar hydrocarbon and is chemically inert to weak bases.
- **Procedure:**
  1. Dissolve the solid mixture in an organic solvent immiscible with water (e.g., diethyl ether or dichloromethane).
  2. Transfer to a separating funnel and shake with an aqueous sodium bicarbonate solution ($5\text{–}10\% \text{ NaHCO}_3$).
  3. Benzoic acid reacts to form water-soluble sodium benzoate, partitioning into the lower aqueous layer:
     

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOH} + \text{NaHCO}_3\text{(aq)} \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COO}^\ominus\text{Na}^\oplus\text{(aq)} + \text{CO}_2\uparrow + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

  4. Naphthalene remains in the organic layer. Separate the two layers.
  5. **Isolating Benzoic Acid:** Acidify the aqueous layer with dilute $\text{HCl}$. Benzoic acid precipitates out as white crystals, which are filtered, washed with cold water, and recrystallized:
     

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COO}^\ominus\text{Na}^\oplus + \text{HCl}\text{(aq)} \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOH}\downarrow\text{(s)} + \text{NaCl}\text{(aq)}
$$

  6. **Isolating Naphthalene:** Evaporate the organic solvent from the organic layer to obtain pure naphthalene, which can be further purified by sublimation.  
  **Marking Rubric:**
  - Chemical extraction logic using $\text{NaHCO}_3$: **[½ Mark]**
  - Balanced equations for salt formation and acid regeneration: **[½ Mark]**
  - Isolation and purification steps for both compounds: **[½ Mark]**

##### 2. Steam Distillation vs Vacuum Distillation:
- **Criteria for Steam Distillation:**
  Used for substances that:
  1. Are practically insoluble/immiscible in water.
  2. Are steam-volatile.
  3. Possess non-volatile impurities.
- **Governing Mathematical Principle:**
  According to Dalton's Law of Partial Pressures for two immiscible liquids:
  

$$
P_{\text{total}} = p_{\text{water}} + p_{\text{organic}} = P_{\text{external}}
$$

  The mixture boils when the sum of their individual vapor pressures equals atmospheric pressure ($1\text{ atm}$). Because $p_{\text{water}} > 0$, $p_{\text{organic}} < P_{\text{external}}$, which means the organic liquid vaporizes and codistills at a temperature **lower** than its normal atmospheric boiling point, preventing thermal decomposition.
- **Ratio of Masses Distilled:**
  

$$
\frac{w_1}{w_2} = \frac{p_1 M_1}{p_2 M_2}
$$

  *(where $w_1, w_2$ are masses; $p_1, p_2$ are vapor pressures; and $M_1, M_2$ are molar masses).*  
  **Marking Rubric:**
  - Conditions for steam distillation: **[½ Mark]**
  - Dalton's law formulation and boiling point reduction explanation: **[½ Mark]**
  - Mass-to-vapor pressure mathematical relationship: **[½ Mark]**

##### 3. Distillation under Reduced Pressure (Vacuum Distillation):
- **Principle:** The boiling point of a liquid is the temperature at which its vapor pressure equals the external ambient pressure. By decreasing the pressure above the liquid surface using a vacuum pump, the liquid boils at a significantly lower temperature than its normal boiling point, allowing distillation below its thermal decomposition temperature.
- **Industrial Example:** Purification of **Glycerol** (Glycerin). Glycerol boils with decomposition at $290^\circ\text{C}$ under $760\text{ mm Hg}$, but distills smoothly without decomposition at $180^\circ\text{C}$ under a reduced pressure of $12\text{ mm Hg}$.  
  **Marking Rubric:**
  - Relationship between reduced external pressure and lower boiling point: **[½ Mark]**
  - Glycerol example with representative boiling point reduction data: **[½ Mark]**

### 1.1 Degree of Unsaturation / Index of Hydrogen Deficiency (Double Bond Equivalent - DBE)

$$
\text{DBE} = c + 1 - \frac{h + x - n}{2}

### 1.3 Rapid Reaction Intermediates & Mechanism Identifier

## 3. Golden Points to Remember (NCERT In-Text Lines & High-Yield Truths)

### 3.1 Electronic Effects: Exact Distinctions
1. **Inductive Effect ($I$):** Permanent effect transmitted through $\sigma$-bonds. Decreases sharply with distance and is practically negligible beyond the 3rd carbon atom. Alkyl groups act as electron-donating groups ($+I$) due to electron-releasing polarizability.
2. **Resonance / Mesomeric Effect ($R$ or $M$):** Permanent effect involving complete delocalization of $\pi$-electrons or lone pairs through conjugated systems.
   * $+R$ groups (electron-donating via lone pair): $-\ddot{\text{O}}\text{H}, -\ddot{\text{N}}\text{H}_2, -\ddot{\text{O}}\text{R}, -\ddot{\text{X}}:$
   * $-R$ groups (electron-withdrawing via vacant orbital/electronegative multiple bond): $-\text{NO}_2, -\text{C}\equiv\text{N}, -\text{CHO}, >\text{C}=\text{O}, -\text{COOH}$
3. **Electromeric Effect ($E$):** **Temporary** effect operative only in the presence of an attacking reagent.
   * $+E$ (Addition towards reagent): Multiple bond electrons shift towards the atom where reagent gets attached.
   * $-E$ (Addition away from reagent): Multiple bond electrons shift away from the atom to which attacking reagent binds (e.g., addition of $\text{CN}^-$ to carbonyls).
4. **Hyperconjugation ($\sigma\text{-p}$ or $\sigma\text{-}\pi^*$ Conjugation):**
   * Delocalization of $\sigma$-electrons of $\text{C}-\text{H}$ bond of an alkyl group directly attached to an atom of unsaturated system or an atom with an unshared p-orbital (carbocation/radical).
   * Total hyperconjugative structures for carbocations/alkenes $= n + 1$ (where $n$ is the number of $\alpha\text{-hydrogens}$).

### 3.3 Separation & Purification Techniques
* **Steam Distillation:** Applied when substances are steam volatile and **immiscible with water** (e.g., aniline, o-nitrophenol). At the boiling point: $P_{\text{total}} = P_{\text{organic}} + P_{\text{water}} = P_{\text{atm}}$. Hence, the organic liquid boils at a temperature *lower* than its normal boiling point.
* **Distillation under Reduced Pressure (Vacuum Distillation):** Used for liquids having high boiling points and those that **decompose at or below their boiling points** (e.g., glycerol decomposes at its normal b.p. of $290^\circ\text{C}$; distilled at $180^\circ\text{C}$ under $12\,\text{mm}\,\text{Hg}$).
* **Differential Extraction:** Based on differing partition coefficients; uses a separating funnel with two immiscible solvent layers.
* **Thin-Layer Chromatography (TLC):**
  

$$
R_f = \frac{\text{Distance moved by substance from baseline}}{\text{Distance moved by solvent front from baseline}}
$$

  *(Adsorbent: Silica gel or Alumina. Stationary phase: Solid; Mobile phase: Liquid).*

| **Halogens (Carius)** | $\% \text{X} = \frac{M_{\text{atomic}}(\text{X}) \times m_{\text{AgX}} \times 100}{M_{\text{molar}}(\text{AgX}) \times m}$ | $M_{\text{AgCl}} = 143.5$, $M_{\text{AgBr}} = 188$, $M_{\text{AgI}} = 235\,\text{g/mol}$. |
| **Sulfur (Carius)** | $\% \text{S} = \frac{32 \times m_{\text{BaSO}_4} \times 100}{233 \times m}$ | $\text{BaSO}_4$ formed by adding $\text{BaCl}_2$ after oxidation with fuming $\text{HNO}_3$. |
| **Phosphorus** | $\% \text{P} = \frac{62 \times m_{\text{Mg}_2\text{P}_2\text{O}_7} \times 100}{222 \times m}$ | Alternatively: $\% \text{P} = \frac{31 \times m_{(\text{NH}_4)_3\text{PO}_4 \cdot 12\text{MoO}_3} \times 100}{1877 \times m}$. |
| **Oxygen** | $\% \text{O} = 100 - (\% \text{C} + \% \text{H} + \dots)$ | Modern method: $\% \text{O} = \frac{32 \times m_{\text{CO}_2} \times 100}{88 \times m}$ via iodine pentoxide oxidation. |

### 4.2 Essential IUPAC Nomenclature Checklist for Board Evaluation
1. **Priority of Principal Functional Groups:**
   

$$
-\text{COOH} > -\text{SO}_3\text{H} > -\text{COOR} > -\text{COCl} > -\text{CONH}_2 > -\text{CN} > -\text{CHO} > >\text{C}=\text{O} > -\text{OH} > -\text{NH}_2 > >\text{C}=\text{C}< > -\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-
$$

2. **Double Bond vs Triple Bond Tie-Breaker:**
   * When both are equidistant from chain ends, **double bond receives lower locant**:
     

$$
\text{CH}\equiv\text{C}-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 \implies \text{Pent-1-en-4-yne (NOT Pent-4-en-1-yne)}
$$

   * Suffix vowel dropping rule: terminal "e" of "-ene" is dropped before "-yne" $\rightarrow$ `-en-yne`.
3. **Special Carbon-Containing Functional Groups (Directly on ring / 3 or more on same chain):**
   * $\text{CH}_2(\text{COOH})-\text{CH}(\text{COOH})-\text{CH}_2(\text{COOH})$ is named:
     

$$
\mathbf{\text{Propane-1,2,3-tricarboxylic acid}} \quad (\text{NOT 3-carboxypentanedioic acid})
$$
