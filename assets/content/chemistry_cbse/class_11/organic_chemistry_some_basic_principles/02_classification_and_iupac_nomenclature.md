# SECTION 1: CLASSIFICATION OF ORGANIC COMPOUNDS

Organic compounds arise primarily from carbon’s unique capabilities of **catenation** (self-linking via covalent bonds, bond energy $\approx 348\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$) and **tetravalency** ($sp^3, sp^2, sp$ hybrid states with bond angles of $109^\circ 28', 120^\circ,$ and $180^\circ$ respectively).

```
                            Organic Compounds
                                    │
         ┌──────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                     ▼
Acyclic / Open Chain                                   Cyclic / Closed Chain
(Aliphatic Compounds)                                  (Ring Compounds)
  ├── Saturated (Alkanes)                                      │
  └── Unsaturated (Alkenes, Alkynes)           ┌───────────────┴───────────────┐
                                               ▼                               ▼
                                       Homocyclic / Carbocyclic          Heterocyclic
                                               │                               │
                                  ┌────────────┴────────────┐             ┌────┴────┐
                                  ▼                         ▼             ▼         ▼
                             Alicyclic                  Aromatic      Alicyclic  Aromatic
                                                            │
                                                   ┌────────┴────────┐
                                                   ▼                 ▼
                                               Benzenoid        Non-Benzenoid
```

***

### 1.1 Acyclic or Open-Chain Compounds (Aliphatic) [⭐⭐⭐⭐]

Compounds containing open chains of carbon atoms, which may be straight or branched. The name *aliphatic* is derived from the Greek word *aleiphar* (meaning fat).

1. **Saturated Acyclic Compounds:** Contain only carbon–carbon single ($\sigma$) bonds.
   

$$
\text{General formula for acyclic alkanes: } \mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n+2}
$$

   

$$
\text{Example: } \mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3} \quad (n\text{-butane})
$$

2. **Unsaturated Acyclic Compounds:** Contain at least one carbon–carbon multiple bond (double bond $\sigma + \pi$ or triple bond $\sigma + 2\pi$).
   

$$
\text{Alkenes: } \mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n} \quad (\text{e.g., } \mathrm{CH_2=CH-CH_3})
$$

   

$$
\text{Alkynes: } \mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n-2} \quad (\text{e.g., } \mathrm{CH\equiv C-CH_3})
$$

***

### 1.2 Cyclic or Closed-Chain Compounds [⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐]

Compounds containing one or more closed rings of atoms.

#### A. Homocyclic or Carbocyclic Compounds
The ring framework consists solely of carbon atoms.
1. **Alicyclic Compounds (Aliphatic Cyclic):** Possess properties resembling aliphatic compounds. Rings may be saturated or unsaturated.
   

$$
\text{General formula for monocyclic cycloalkanes: } \mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n} \quad (n \ge 3)
$$

   Examples: Cyclopropane ($\mathrm{C_3H_6}$), Cyclobutane ($\mathrm{C_4H_8}$), Cyclohexene ($\mathrm{C_6H_{10}}$).

2. **Aromatic Compounds:** Cyclic unsaturated planar systems possessing exceptional thermodynamic stability due to cyclic delocalization of $(4n+2)\,\pi$ electrons (**Hückel’s Rule**, where $n = 0, 1, 2, 3 \dots$).
   - **Benzenoid Aromatic Compounds:** Contain one or more fused or isolated benzene rings.
     - Benzene ($\mathrm{C_6H_6}$, $6\pi\,e^-, n=1$)
     - Naphthalene ($\mathrm{C_{10}H_8}$, $10\pi\,e^-, n=2$)
     - Anthracene ($\mathrm{C_{14}H_{10}}$, $14\pi\,e^-, n=3$)
     - Toluene ($\mathrm{C_6H_5-CH_3}$)
   - **Non-Benzenoid Aromatic Compounds:** Aromatic rings containing no benzene rings.
     - **Tropone** (2,4,6-cycloheptatrien-1-one) and **Tropolone** (contains a 7-membered conjugated ring with $6\pi$ delocalized electrons).
     - **Azulene** ($\mathrm{C_{10}H_8}$, fused 7-membered and 5-membered rings, $10\pi\,e^-$).

#### B. Heterocyclic Compounds
The cyclic ring contains carbon atoms and at least one **heteroatom** (typically $\mathrm{N, O, S}$).
1. **Hetero-alicyclic Compounds:**
   - Oxirane (Ethylene oxide / Epoxide): 3-membered ring with 1 oxygen atom.
   - Tetrahydrofuran ($\mathrm{THF}$, $\mathrm{C_4H_8O}$): 5-membered saturated ring with 1 oxygen atom.
   - Pyrrolidine ($\mathrm{C_4H_9N}$): 5-membered saturated ring with 1 nitrogen atom.
   - Piperidine ($\mathrm{C_5H_{11}N}$): 6-membered saturated ring with 1 nitrogen atom.

2. **Hetero-aromatic Compounds:**
   - Furan ($\mathrm{C_4H_4O}$): 5-membered ring; oxygen contributes one lone pair to complete $6\pi$ delocalized system ($n=1$).
   - Thiophene ($\mathrm{C_4H_4S}$): 5-membered ring; sulfur contributes one lone pair ($6\pi\,e^-$).
   - Pyrrole ($\mathrm{C_4H_5N}$): 5-membered ring; nitrogen lone pair participates in the aromatic sextet ($6\pi\,e^-$).
   - Pyridine ($\mathrm{C_5H_5N}$): 6-membered ring; nitrogen is $sp^2$ hybridized with its lone pair residing in an $sp^2$ orbital orthogonal to the $\pi$-system ($6\pi\,e^-$).

***

### 1.3 Classification Based on Functional Groups [⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐]

A **functional group** is an atom or group of atoms joined in a specific manner that is responsible for the characteristic chemical properties of the organic compound.

A **homologous series** is defined as a family or group of structurally related organic compounds:
1. All members conform to a common general molecular formula.
2. Successive members differ in composition by a constant methylene unit:
   

$$
\Delta(\text{Molecular formula}) = -\mathrm{CH_2}- \implies \Delta M = 14.027\text{ g mol}^{-1} \approx 14\text{ u}
$$

3. All members possess the same functional group and exhibit identical chemical characteristics, with regular gradations in physical properties (e.g., boiling point, melting point, density) due to increasing London dispersion forces with higher molecular mass.

# SECTION 2: IUPAC NOMENCLATURE — THE FOUNDATIONAL SYSTEM

The International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry (IUPAC) system provides a systematic, unambiguous name for every organic structure.

### 2.1 The Master Syntax of an IUPAC Name [⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐]

$$
\mathbf{\text{Complete IUPAC Name} = \text{Secondary Prefix} + \text{Primary Prefix} + \text{Word Root} + \text{Primary Suffix} + \text{Secondary Suffix}}
$$

```
┌──────────────────┬─────────────────┬───────────┬────────────────┬──────────────────┐
│ Secondary Prefix │ Primary Prefix  │ Word Root │ Primary Suffix │ Secondary Suffix │
├──────────────────┼─────────────────┼───────────┼────────────────┼──────────────────┤
│ Substituents /   │ "cyclo"         │ Number of │ Degree of      │ Principal        │
│ Non-principal    │ (only present   │ Carbons   │ unsaturation   │ Functional       │
│ functional       │ in alicyclic /  │ in Parent │ (-ane, -ene,   │ Group            │
│ groups           │ cyclic systems) │ Chain     │ -yne)          │ (-ol, -oic acid) │
└──────────────────┴─────────────────┴───────────┴────────────────┴──────────────────┘
```

#### 1. Word Root (Chain Length)
Determined by the number of carbon atoms in the selected **Principal Carbon Chain (PCC)**:
- $\mathrm{C_1}$: Meth-
- $\mathrm{C_2}$: Eth-
- $\mathrm{C_3}$: Prop-
- $\mathrm{C_4}$: But-
- $\mathrm{C_5}$: Pent-
- $\mathrm{C_6}$: Hex-
- $\mathrm{C_7}$: Hept-
- $\mathrm{C_8}$: Oct-
- $\mathrm{C_9}$: Non-
- $\mathrm{C_{10}}$: Dec-
- $\mathrm{C_{11}}$: Undec-
- $\mathrm{C_{12}}$: Dodec-
- $\mathrm{C_{20}}$: Icos-

#### 2. Primary Suffix (Degree of Unsaturation)
- Saturated: **-ane**
- One $\mathrm{C=C}$: **-ene**
- Two $\mathrm{C=C}$: **-adiene** (Word root takes an extra terminal "a": e.g., But**a**-1,3-diene)
- Three $\mathrm{C=C}$: **-atriene**
- One $\mathrm{C\equiv C}$: **-yne**
- Two $\mathrm{C\equiv C}$: **-adiyne**
- One $\mathrm{C=C}$ and one $\mathrm{C\equiv C}$: **-en-yne** (Elide the "e" of "-ene" before vowel/glide "y": e.g., `-en-yne`, not `-ene-yne`).

#### 3. Secondary Suffix (Principal Functional Group)
Appended after the primary suffix to represent the principal functional group.
- Carboxylic acid: **-oic acid** (or **-carboxylic acid**)
- Sulfonic acid: **-sulfonic acid**
- Ester: **-oate** (or **-carboxylate**)
- Acid halide: **-oyl halide**
- Amide: **-amide** (or **-carboxamide**)
- Nitrile: **-nitrile** (or **-carbonitrile**)
- Aldehyde: **-al** (or **-carbaldehyde**)
- Ketone: **-one**
- Alcohol: **-ol**
- Thiol: **-thiol**
- Amine: **-amine**

*Phonetic Elision Rule:* If the secondary suffix begins with a vowel ($a, e, i, o, u$) or $y$, the terminal "e" of the primary suffix is dropped:

$$
\text{Hexane} + \text{-ol} \longrightarrow \text{Hexan-1-ol}
$$

$$
\text{Hexane} + \text{-nitrile} \longrightarrow \text{Hexanenitrile} \quad (\text{"e" is retained because "n" is a consonant})
$$

$$
\text{Hexane} + \text{-dioic acid} \longrightarrow \text{Hexanedioic acid} \quad (\text{"e" is retained before "d"})
$$

#### 4. Primary Prefix
Distinguishes cyclic from acyclic compounds. For monocyclic compounds, the prefix **cyclo-** immediately precedes the word root. (For bicyclic compounds: **bicyclo[a.b.c]-**, for spiro: **spiro[a.b]-**).

#### 5. Secondary Prefix
Indicates the identity and position of substituents (branches) or secondary (inferior) functional groups treated merely as substituents (e.g., $-\mathrm{NO_2}$ *nitro*, $-\mathrm{OR}$ *alkoxy*, $-\mathrm{X}$ *halo*, $-\mathrm{N_3}$ *azido*).

# SECTION 3: PRIORITY TABLE OF FUNCTIONAL GROUPS

When two or more functional groups are present in a polyfunctional organic compound, one is designated as the **Principal Functional Group (PFG)** based on IUPAC seniority. All remaining functional groups are treated as **substituents (prefixes)**.

### Master Priority Order (Decreasing Seniority: Top to Bottom) [⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐]

$$
\begin{aligned}
-\mathrm{COOH} > -\mathrm{SO_3H} > -\mathrm{(CO)_2O} > -\mathrm{COOR} > -\mathrm{COX} > -\mathrm{CONH_2} > -\mathrm{CN} > -\mathrm{NC} \\
> -\mathrm{CHO} > \mathrm{>C=O} > -\mathrm{OH} > -\mathrm{SH} > -\mathrm{NH_2} > \mathrm{>C=C<} > -\mathrm{C\equiv C-}
\end{aligned}
$$

| Class | Functional Group Structure | Prefix (Substituent) | Suffix (When C is counted in PCC) | Special Suffix (When C is not in PCC) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Carboxylic acid** | $-\mathrm{COOH}$ | carboxy- | **-oic acid** | -carboxylic acid |
| **Sulfonic acid** | $-\mathrm{SO_3H}$ | sulfo- | **-sulfonic acid** | -sulfonic acid |
| **Anhydride** | $-\mathrm{CO-O-CO}-$ | — | **-oic anhydride** | -carboxylic anhydride |
| **Ester** | $-\mathrm{COOR}$ | alkoxycarbonyl- or carbalkoxy- | **alkyl ... -oate** | alkyl ... -carboxylate |
| **Acid Halide** | $-\mathrm{COX}$ ($\mathrm{X=Cl, Br, I}$) | halocarbonyl- / haloformyl- | **-oyl halide** | -carbonyl halide |
| **Amide** | $-\mathrm{CONH_2}$ | carbamoyl- | **-amide** | -carboxamide |
| **Nitrile** | $-\mathrm{C\equiv N}$ | cyano- | **-nitrile** | -carbonitrile |
| **Isonitrile** | $-\mathrm{N\equiv C}$ | isocyano- | **-isonitrile** | — |
| **Aldehyde** | $-\mathrm{CHO}$ | formyl- / oxo- | **-al** | -carbaldehyde |
| **Ketone** | $\mathrm{>C=O}$ | oxo- / keto- | **-one** | — |
| **Alcohol** | $-\mathrm{OH}$ | hydroxy- | **-ol** | — |
| **Thiol** | $-\mathrm{SH}$ | mercapto- / sulfanyl- | **-thiol** | — |
| **Amine** | $-\mathrm{NH_2}$ | amino- | **-amine** | — |
| **Alkene** | $-\mathrm{CH=CH}-$ | — | **-ene** | — |
| **Alkyne** | $-\mathrm{C\equiv C}-$ | — | **-yne** | — |

> [!NOTE]
> **Substituents that are ALWAYS Prefixes (Never take Suffixes):**
> - Halogens ($-\mathrm{F}$: fluoro, $-\mathrm{Cl}$: chloro, $-\mathrm{Br}$: bromo, $-\mathrm{I}$: iodo)
> - Nitro ($-\mathrm{NO_2}$)
> - Nitroso ($-\mathrm{NO}$)
> - Alkoxy ($-\mathrm{OR}$, e.g., $-\mathrm{OCH_3}$ methoxy, $-\mathrm{OC_2H_5}$ ethoxy)
> - Alkyl ($-\mathrm{R}$)
> - Phenyl ($-\mathrm{C_6H_5}$)
> - Diazo ($-\mathrm{N_2^+}$)

# SECTION 4: STEP-BY-STEP RULES FOR IUPAC NOMENCLATURE

### 4.1 Selection of the Principal Carbon Chain (PCC) [⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐]

Follow this strict hierarchical priority order to select the PCC:
1. **Maximum number of Principal Functional Groups (PFGs).**
2. **Maximum number of multiple bonds** (double and triple bonds combined, regardless of chain length).
3. **Maximum length of the continuous carbon chain** (maximum number of carbon atoms).
4. **Maximum number of double bonds.**
5. **Maximum number of substituents (side chains).**
6. **Lowest locant set for substituents.**

***

### 4.2 The Lowest Locant Rule vs. "Lowest Sum Rule" [⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐]

> [!CAUTION]
> **Debunking the "Lowest Sum Rule":**
> NCERT and modern IUPAC regulations state that the **Lowest Set of Locants** rule strictly overrides any "sum of locants" calculation.
>
> When comparing two different numbering paths, compare the locant sets **term-by-term in increasing numerical order**. The correct set is the one that has the lower number at the **first point of difference**.
> 
> *Example:* Locant set $(2, 7, 8)$ vs $(3, 4, 5)$.
> - First point of comparison: $2$ vs $3$.
> - Since $2 < 3$, the set $(2, 7, 8)$ wins instantly at the first point of difference, even though $2+7+8=17$ while $3+4+5=12$. The sum is completely irrelevant.

#### Hierarchical Priority for Numbering the PCC:
1. **Principal Functional Group (PFG)** gets lowest possible locant.
2. **Multiple bonds ($\mathrm{C=C}$ and $\mathrm{C\equiv C}$)** get lowest locants.
3. **Substituents / Prefixes** get lowest locants at first point of difference.
4. **Alphabetical priority:** If identical locant sets result from both directions, the substituent that comes first alphabetically receives the lower number.

***

### 4.3 Double Bonds vs. Triple Bonds Tie-Breaker [⭐⭐⭐⭐]

When a double bond and a triple bond are equidistant from the two ends of the chain:
- The **double bond receives lower locant preference** over the triple bond.
- Mnemonic: *"ene"* precedes *"yne"* alphabetically.

$$
\mathrm{\overset{1}{C}H_2=\overset{2}{C}H-\overset{3}{C}H_2-\overset{4}{C}\equiv\overset{5}{C}H} \implies \text{Pent-1-en-4-yne} \quad (\mathbf{Correct})
$$

$$
\mathrm{\overset{5}{C}H_2=\overset{4}{C}H-\overset{3}{C}H_2-\overset{2}{C}\equiv\overset{1}{C}H} \implies \text{Pent-4-en-1-yne} \quad (\mathbf{Incorrect})
$$

- Note: If they are **not** equidistant, the multiple bond closest to either end takes the lowest locant regardless of whether it is double or triple:

$$
\mathrm{\overset{1}{C}H\equiv\overset{2}{C}-\overset{3}{C}H_2-\overset{4}{C}H=\overset{5}{C}H-\overset{6}{C}H_3} \implies \text{Hex-4-en-1-yne} \quad (\text{Locant set } 1,4 \text{ beats } 2,5)
$$

***

### 4.4 Special IUPAC 1993 Rule for Identical Terminal Carbon-Containing Groups [⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐]

If an unbranched chain contains **three or more identical terminal carbon-containing functional groups** (such as $-\mathrm{COOH}$, $-\mathrm{CHO}$, $-\mathrm{CN}$, $-\mathrm{CONH_2}$):
- **None** of the carbons of these functional groups are included in the principal carbon chain.
- The parent hydrocarbon chain is named, and special suffixes (**-tricarboxylic acid, -tricarbaldehyde, -tricarbonitrile**) are appended.

#### Case Study: Citric Acid Analogue

$$
\mathrm{HOOC-CH_2-CH(COOH)-CH_2-COOH}
$$

- Old convention (obsolete): Carbons included in chain $\to$ 3-carboxy-1,5-pentanedioic acid.
- **Modern IUPAC (NCERT):** The three $-\mathrm{COOH}$ carbons are excluded from the main chain.
  

$$
\text{Parent chain} = \mathrm{-CH_2-CH-CH_2-} \quad (3\text{ carbons: propane})
$$

  

$$
\mathbf{\text{Correct IUPAC Name: Propane-1,2,3-tricarboxylic acid}}
$$

#### Case Study: 1,2,3-Tricyano compound

$$
\mathrm{NC-CH_2-CH(CN)-CH_2-CN}
$$

$$
\mathbf{\text{Correct IUPAC Name: Propane-1,2,3-tricarbonitrile}}
$$

***

### 4.5 Naming of Complex (Branched) Substituents [⭐⭐⭐⭐]

When a substituent carbon chain is itself branched:
1. Carbon-1 ($1'$) of the side chain is **always the carbon atom attached directly to the principal chain**.
2. The complex substituent name is enclosed entirely within **parentheses**.
3. Multiplying prefixes like *bis-*, *tris-*, *tetrakis-* are used when multiple identical complex substituents are present.
4. In alphabetical ordering:
   - Prefixes **iso-** and **neo-** are considered part of the fundamental alkyl name (i.e., **i** is alphabetized).
   - Prefixes **sec-**, **tert-** separated by a hyphen are **not** alphabetized (alphabetized by the main root: e.g., *sec*-butyl is alphabetized under **b**).
   - Multiplying prefixes (*di-, tri-, tetra-*) for simple substituents are **ignored** in alphabetization.
   - However, for complex branched substituents inside parentheses, multiplying prefixes within the substituent (e.g., *dimethyl*) **are** alphabetized!

```
Common vs. Systematic Names of Complex Alkyl Groups:
- Isopropyl:     -(1-methylethyl)
- Isobutyl:      -(2-methylpropyl)
- sec-Butyl:     -(1-methylpropyl)
- tert-Butyl:    -(1,1-dimethylethyl)
- Neopentyl:     -(2,2-dimethylpropyl)
- Isopentyl:     -(3-methylbutyl)
```

# SECTION 5: EXHAUSTIVE NOMENCLATURE BY FUNCTIONAL GROUP CLASS

### 5.1 Alkanes, Alkenes, Alkynes
- **Example 5.1.1:**
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-C(CH_3)_2-CH(C_2H_5)-CH_2-CH_3}
$$

  - Longest chain: 6 carbons (Hexane).
  - Numbering from left gives locants $2,2,3$; numbering from right gives $4,5,5$. Lower locant set: $2,2,3$.
  - Substituents: 2,2-dimethyl and 3-ethyl.
  - Alphabetical: ethyl before methyl.
  - **Name:** $\mathbf{\text{3-Ethyl-2,2-dimethylhexane}}$

- **Example 5.1.2:**
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_2=CH-CH(CH_3)-C\equiv CH}
$$

  - Numbering: Double bond starts at 1, triple bond at 5 vs triple bond at 1, double bond at 4.
  - Direction 1: locants for multiple bonds = 1 (ene), 4 (yne). Direction 2: locants = 1 (yne), 4 (ene).
  - Multiple bond locants tie ($1,4$). Double bond wins tie-breaker!
  - Left carbon is $\mathrm{C_1}$.
  - Substituent: 3-methyl.
  - **Name:** $\mathbf{\text{3-Methylpent-1-en-4-yne}}$

***

### 5.2 Halogen & Nitro Derivatives
Always named as prefixes (*halo-* and *nitro-*).
- **Example 5.2.1:**
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH(Cl)-CH(Br)-CH_3}
$$

  - Numbering from either side yields locants $2,3$.
  - Alphabetical priority: 2-bromo precedes 3-chloro.
  - **Name:** $\mathbf{\text{2-Bromo-3-chlorobutane}}$

- **Example 5.2.2:**
  

$$
\mathrm{O_2N-CH_2-CH=CH-CH_3}
$$

  - Multiple bond takes priority over prefix ($-\mathrm{NO_2}$).
  - Double bond gets locant 2 from right: $\mathrm{\overset{4}{C}H_2(NO_2)-\overset{3}{C}H=\overset{2}{C}H-\overset{1}{C}H_3}$.
  - **Name:** $\mathbf{\text{4-Nitrobut-2-ene}}$

***

### 5.3 Alcohols ($-\mathrm{OH}$) and Thiols ($-\mathrm{SH}$)
Suffix: **-ol**, **-thiol**. Prefix: **hydroxy-**, **mercapto-** (or **sulfanyl-**).
- **Example 5.3.1:**
  

$$
\mathrm{HO-CH_2-CH_2-CH(OH)-CH_3}
$$

  - Numbering from right: locants $2,4$ for $-\mathrm{OH}$.
  - Numbering from left: locants $1,3$ for $-\mathrm{OH}$.
  - Lower locants: $1,3$.
  - **Name:** $\mathbf{\text{Butane-1,3-diol}}$ (Terminal "e" of butane retained because "d" is a consonant).

- **Example 5.3.2:**
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_2=CH-CH(OH)-CH_3}
$$

  - Functional group ($-\mathrm{OH}$) has higher priority than double bond.
  - Numbering from right gives $-\mathrm{OH}$ locant 2.
  - Chain: 4 carbons (but-3-en-2-ol).
  - **Name:** $\mathbf{\text{But-3-en-2-ol}}$

***

### 5.4 Carbonyl Compounds: Aldehydes ($-\mathrm{CHO}$) & Ketones ($\mathrm{>C=O}$)
- Aldehyde suffix: **-al**; Ketone suffix: **-one**.
- Aldehyde is higher in priority than ketone. When both are present:
  - $-\mathrm{CHO}$ is principal ($\implies$ suffix **-al**).
  - $\mathrm{>C=O}$ is secondary ($\implies$ prefix **oxo-**).
- **Example 5.4.1:**
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CO-CH_2-CH_2-CHO}
$$

  - Carbons: $\mathrm{\overset{5}{C}H_3-\overset{4}{C}O-\overset{3}{C}H_2-\overset{2}{C}H_2-\overset{1}{C}HO}$
  - Principal functional group: $-\mathrm{CHO}$ at $\mathrm{C_1}$.
  - Substituent: oxo at $\mathrm{C_4}$.
  - **Name:** $\mathbf{\text{4-Oxopentanal}}$

- **Example 5.4.2 (Dialdehyde):**
  

$$
\mathrm{OHC-CH_2-CH_2-CH_2-CHO}
$$

  - **Name:** $\mathbf{\text{Pentanedial}}$

***

### 5.5 Carboxylic Acids & Acid Derivatives
- **Carboxylic Acid:** $\mathrm{CH_3-CH(OH)-COOH} \implies \mathbf{\text{2-Hydroxypropanoic acid}}$ (Lactic acid).
- **Ester:** Named as *alkyl alkanoate*. The alkyl group attached directly to oxygen ($-\mathrm{O-R}$) is written **first** as an independent separate word.
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-COO-CH_3}
$$

  - Alkyl group on oxygen: methyl.
  - Acyl chain: 3 carbons (propanoate).
  - **Name:** $\mathbf{\text{Methyl propanoate}}$
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-COO-CH_2-CH_2-Cl}
$$

  - Alkyl group on oxygen: 2-chloroethyl.
  - Acyl group: ethanoate.
  - **Name:** $\mathbf{\text{2-Chloroethyl ethanoate}}$
- **Acid Halide:** $\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-COCl} \implies \mathbf{\text{Propanoyl chloride}}$
- **Amide:**
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CONH-CH_2-CH_3}
$$

  - Nitrogen-substituted alkyl group: prefix $N$-ethyl.
  - Acyl parent: ethanamide.
  - **Name:** $\mathbf{N\text{-Ethylethanamide}}$

***

### 5.6 Amines ($-\mathrm{NH_2}, -\mathrm{NHR}, -\mathrm{NR_2}$)
Suffix: **-amine**. Prefix: **amino-**.
- Secondary/tertiary amines are named as $N$-substituted derivatives:
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-NH-CH(CH_3)_2}
$$

  - Longest carbon chain attached to nitrogen: propane (3 carbons).
  - Position of amine on chain: locant 2 (propan-2-amine).
  - Substituent on nitrogen: methyl ($N$-methyl).
  - **Name:** $\mathbf{N\text{-Methylpropan-2-amine}}$
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-N(C_2H_5)-CH_2-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3}
$$

  - Longest chain: butane (butan-1-amine).
  - Substituents on nitrogen: ethyl and methyl.
  - **Name:** $\mathbf{N\text{-Ethyl-}N\text{-methylbutan-1-amine}}$

***

# SECTION 6: AROMATIC COMPOUNDS NOMENCLATURE

### 6.1 Monosubstituted Benzene Derivatives
Common names accepted by IUPAC:
- Methylbenzene $\to$ **Toluene**
- Methoxybenzene $\to$ **Anisole**
- Aminobenzene $\to$ **Aniline**
- Hydroxybenzene $\to$ **Phenol**
- Benzenecarboxylic acid $\to$ **Benzoic acid**
- Benzenecarbaldehyde $\to$ **Benzaldehyde**
- Benzenesulfonic acid $\to$ **Benzenesulfonic acid**
- Acetophenone $\to$ **1-Phenylethan-1-one** (Methyl phenyl ketone)

When benzene is a substituent, $\mathrm{C_6H_5}-$ is called **Phenyl**. The $\mathrm{C_6H_5-CH_2}-$ group is called **Benzyl**.

***

### 6.2 Disubstituted & Polysubstituted Benzenes [⭐⭐⭐⭐]
1. **Disubstituted positional prefixes (Common names only):**
   - $1,2$-substitution: **ortho- ($o$-)**
   - $1,3$-substitution: **meta- ($m$-)**
   - $1,4$-substitution: **para- ($p$-)**
   *(IUPAC requires numbers: $1,2-$, $1,3-$, and $1,4-$).*

2. **Polysubstituted Systems:**
   - If a group imparts a base name (e.g., $-\mathrm{OH}$ imparts *phenol*, $-\mathrm{NH_2}$ imparts *aniline*), that principal group automatically defines carbon **$\mathrm{C_1}$**.
   - Number the remaining carbons to give substituents the lowest possible locants.
   

#### Exemplar Case Studies:
- **2-Chloro-4-nitrophenol:**
  $-\mathrm{OH}$ defines $\mathrm{C_1}$. Numbering toward $-\mathrm{Cl}$ gives locant 2, and $-\mathrm{NO_2}$ gets 4.
- **2,4,6-Tribromoaniline:**
  $-\mathrm{NH_2}$ is on $\mathrm{C_1}$. Bromines are at $2,4,6$.
- **When no group confers a special base name:**
  Number according to the lowest locant set; if locants tie, use alphabetical priority.
  

$$
\text{1-Chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene}
$$

  Locants $1,2,4$ beat $1,3,4$ or $1,4,6$.

# SECTION 7: HIGH-YIELD NCERT EXEMPLAR & APPLICATION PROBLEMS

### Problem 7.1 (NCERT Exemplar)
**Write the IUPAC name of the following compound:**

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH(OH)-CH_2-CO-CH_3}
$$

- **Step 1: Identify functional groups.**
  Ketone ($\mathrm{>C=O}$) and secondary alcohol ($-\mathrm{OH}$).
- **Step 2: Seniority.**
  Ketone > Alcohol. Thus, ketone is the Principal Functional Group (suffix **-one**). Alcohol is a substituent (prefix **hydroxy-**).
- **Step 3: Longest continuous carbon chain containing PFG.**
  5 carbons $\implies$ Pentan-.
- **Step 4: Numbering the chain.**
  - Left-to-right: $\mathrm{C_2}$ has $-\mathrm{OH}$, $\mathrm{C_4}$ has $\mathrm{=O}$.
  - Right-to-left: $\mathrm{C_2}$ has $\mathrm{=O}$, $\mathrm{C_4}$ has $-\mathrm{OH}$.
  - PFG ($\mathrm{=O}$) must receive the lowest locant $\implies \mathrm{C_2}$.
- **Step 5: Assemble the components:**
  - Prefix: 4-hydroxy
  - Root: pentan
  - Suffix: 2-one

$$
\mathbf{\text{Answer: 4-Hydroxypentan-2-one}}
$$

***

### Problem 7.2 (NCERT Advanced Problem)
**Provide the IUPAC name for:**

$$
\mathrm{CH_2=CH-CH_2-CH(CH=CH_2)-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3}
$$

- **Step 1: Determine the longest chain with maximum number of multiple bonds.**
  - Straight chain from left to right: contains 7 carbons but only ONE double bond.
  - Chain incorporating both double bonds:
    

$$
\mathrm{\overset{1}{C}H_2=\overset{2}{C}H-\overset{3}{C}H_2-\overset{4}{C}H(CH_2CH_2CH_3)-\overset{5}{C}H=\overset{6}{C}H_2}
$$

    Contains 6 carbons and **TWO double bonds**. Multiple bond count strictly takes priority over length!
- **Step 2: Numbering.**
  Symmetrical multiple bonds at positions $1$ and $5$ from both sides.
  - Left-to-right: locant for substituent at $\mathrm{C_4}$.
  - Right-to-left: locant for substituent at $\mathrm{C_3}$.
  - Right-to-left wins lowest locant rule for substituent!
  

$$
\mathrm{\overset{6}{C}H_2=\overset{5}{C}H-\overset{4}{C}H_2-\overset{3}{C}H(CH_2CH_2CH_3)-\overset{2}{C}H=\overset{1}{C}H_2}
$$

- **Step 3: Identify substituent:** At $\mathrm{C_3}$, we have a propyl group ($-\mathrm{CH_2CH_2CH_3}$).
- **Step 4: Assemble name:**

$$
\mathbf{\text{Answer: 3-Propylhexa-1,5-diene}}
$$

***

### Problem 7.3 (NCERT Cyclic Compound)
**Name the compound:**

$$
\text{Cyclohex-2-en-1-ol vs Cyclohex-1-en-3-ol}
$$

- Functional groups: $-\mathrm{OH}$ (alcohol) and $\mathrm{C=C}$ (double bond).
- $-\mathrm{OH}$ outranks $\mathrm{C=C} \implies -\mathrm{OH}$ carbon is strictly assigned **$\mathrm{C_1}$**.
- Now number around the ring towards the double bond to give it the lowest possible locant:
  - Path A: $\mathrm{C_1(OH) \to C_2(=CH-) \to C_3(=CH-)}$ (Double bond locant = 2).
  - Path B: $\mathrm{C_1(OH) \to C_6 \to C_5(=CH-) \to C_4(=CH-)}$ (Double bond locant = 5).

$$
\mathbf{\text{Answer: Cyclohex-2-en-1-ol}}
$$

***

### Problem 7.4 (Bifunctional Ester)
**Name the compound:**

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-O-CO-CH_2-CH_2-COOH}
$$

- Principal group: $-\mathrm{COOH}$ outranks $-\mathrm{COOR}$.
- Principal chain: 4 carbons ($\mathrm{\overset{4}{C}OOR-\overset{3}{C}H_2-\overset{2}{C}H_2-\overset{1}{C}OOH}$).
- Suffix: **-butanedioic acid** (or substituent at $\mathrm{C_4}$).
- When an ester group is treated as a substituent with its carbonyl pointing toward the chain: $-\mathrm{COOCH_3}$ is **methoxycarbonyl**.

$$
\mathbf{\text{Answer: 4-Methoxy-4-oxobutanoic acid (or 3-(methoxycarbonyl)propanoic acid)}}
$$

# SECTION 8: EXAMINER TRAPS & FREQUENT MISTAKES

> [!WARNING]
> ### The Top 6 Board & Competitive Exam Traps:
>
> 1. **The "e" Elision Blunder:**
>    - Writing `Propane-1-ol` $\to$ **WRONG** (terminal "e" must drop before "o"). Correct: **Propan-1-ol**.
>    - Writing `Propandioic acid` $\to$ **WRONG** ("e" is retained before consonant "d"). Correct: **Propanedioic acid**.
>    - Writing `But-2-en-4-yne` $\to$ **WRONG** ("e" dropped before "y"). Correct: **But-1-en-3-yne**.
>
> 2. **Punctuation Rules Violations:**
>    - Numbers are separated from numbers by **commas** (e.g., $2,3$-).
>    - Numbers are separated from words by **hyphens** (e.g., -methyl-).
>    - No spaces are allowed anywhere in an IUPAC name (it must be written as a continuous single word), with the **sole exception of Esters** (e.g., `Ethyl ethanoate`) and **Acid Anhydrides/Acids** (e.g., `Ethanoic acid`).
>
> 3. **The Sum Rule Myth:**
>    - Evaluating the sum of locants instead of the **first point of difference**. A locant set of $2,7,8$ beats $3,4,5$ unconditionally because $2 < 3$.
>
> 4. **Counting Carbons of Functional Groups Twice:**
>    - In $\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CN}$, students often name it "Propylnitrile".
>    - **Reality:** The carbon of $-\mathrm{CN}$ is already carbon-1 of the chain! The name is **Propanenitrile**.
>    - For $\mathrm{CH_3-CHO}$, naming it "Methyl aldehyde" or "Methanal" $\to$ **Ethan-1-al** (contains 2 carbons!).
>
> 5. **Alphabetizing Multiplying Prefixes:**
>    - In a compound with `diethyl` and `trimethyl`, students alphabetize `d` before `t`.
>    - **Reality:** Simple multiplying prefixes (*di, tri, tetra*) are **completely ignored** in alphabetization. Compare **ethyl (e)** vs **methyl (m)**. Ethyl comes first regardless of multiplying prefixes!
>    - *Exception:* Complex prefixes containing multiplying prefixes within parentheses (e.g., `(1,2-dimethylpropyl)`) **do** consider the 'd'.
>
> 6. **Principal Functional Group Carbon in Cyclic Compounds:**
>    - Ring carbon attached to $-\mathrm{COOH}$ cannot include the carbonyl carbon inside the ring.
>    - Cyclohexane with $-\mathrm{COOH}$ is **NOT** "Cyclohexanoic acid"!
>    - **Reality:** The special suffix must be used: **Cyclohexanecarboxylic acid**.

### 9.3 Degree of Unsaturation (Double Bond Equivalent - DBE)
Before naming an unknown structure, compute its DBE to instantly predict rings and multiple bonds:

$$
\mathbf{\text{DBE} = C + 1 - \frac{H}{2} - \frac{X}{2} + \frac{N}{2}}
$$

Where:
- $C = \text{Number of Carbon atoms}$
- $H = \text{Number of Hydrogen atoms}$
- $X = \text{Number of Halogen atoms } (\mathrm{F, Cl, Br, I})$
- $N = \text{Number of Nitrogen atoms}$
*(Oxygen and Sulfur are bivalent and do not affect DBE).*

#### Interpretation:
- $\text{DBE} = 0 \implies \text{Completely saturated, open chain.}$
- $\text{DBE} = 1 \implies \text{One double bond OR one ring.}$
- $\text{DBE} = 2 \implies \text{One triple bond, two double bonds, two rings, or one double bond + one ring.}$
- $\text{DBE} \ge 4 \implies \text{Strong indicator of a Benzene ring (1 ring + 3 double bonds = 4 DBE).}$

## SUMMARY CHECKLIST FOR WRITING IUPAC NAMES IN EXAMS
1. Locate the **Principal Functional Group** (highest priority).
2. Trace the **Longest Continuous Carbon Chain** containing the PFG and maximum multiple bonds.
3. Number from the end that gives **PFG $\to$ Multiple Bonds $\to$ Lowest Locant Set for Substituents $\to$ Alphabetical Priority**.
4. Group identical substituents using prefixes (*di-, tri-*, etc.).
5. Arrange all secondary prefixes in **strict alphabetical order**.
6. Check for **vowel elision** between the primary suffix and secondary suffix (*drop "e" before a, e, i, o, u, y*).
7. Ensure all punctuation is correct (**commas** between digits, **hyphens** between letters and digits, no illegal spaces).
