### Question A.1: Case-Based / Data-Driven Problem
**Tag:** [CBSE Competency Pattern — Case-Based, 4 Marks]  
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

#### Passage:
In an industrial ammonia synthesis plant operating on the Haber-Bosch process, nitrogen and hydrogen gases are fed into a catalytic converter at elevated pressure and temperature:

$$
\mathrm{N_2(g) + 3H_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2NH_3(g)}
$$

During a quality and yield assessment trial, a reactor of constant volume $V$ is charged with $56.0\text{ kg}$ of pure dry $\mathrm{N_2}$ and $12.0\text{ kg}$ of pure dry $\mathrm{H_2}$. At equilibrium, the total yield of $\mathrm{NH_3}$ collected is $34.0\text{ kg}$. 

1. Identify the limiting reagent in this industrial trial with rigorous stoichiometric justification. **(1 Mark)**
2. Calculate the theoretical maximum mass of ammonia (in $\text{kg}$) that can be formed from the given feed. **(1 Mark)**
3. Determine the mass (in $\text{kg}$) of the excess reagent remaining unreacted at theoretical completion. **(1 Mark)**
4. Calculate the percentage yield of the Haber-Bosch catalytic reactor in this specific trial. **(1 Mark)**

#### Model Solution & Marking Scheme:
* **Sub-part 1:**
  * Molar mass of $\mathrm{N_2} = 28.0\text{ g mol}^{-1}$; Molar mass of $\mathrm{H_2} = 2.016\text{ g mol}^{-1} \approx 2.0\text{ g mol}^{-1}$.
  * Number of moles of $\mathrm{N_2}$ fed:
    

$$
n(\mathrm{N_2}) = \frac{56.0 \times 10^3\text{ g}}{28.0\text{ g mol}^{-1}} = 2.0 \times 10^3\text{ mol}
$$

  * Number of moles of $\mathrm{H_2}$ fed:
    

$$
n(\mathrm{H_2}) = \frac{12.0 \times 10^3\text{ g}}{2.0\text{ g mol}^{-1}} = 6.0 \times 10^3\text{ mol}
$$

  * According to stoichiometry: $1\text{ mol }\mathrm{N_2}$ requires $3\text{ mol }\mathrm{H_2}$.  
    $2.0 \times 10^3\text{ mol }\mathrm{N_2}$ requires $3 \times (2.0 \times 10^3) = 6.0 \times 10^3\text{ mol }\mathrm{H_2}$.
  * Both reactants are present in exact stoichiometric proportions. Neither is in deficiency; however, under standard limiting reagent determination definitions, both are consumed simultaneously at $100\%$ completion, so either reactant dictates the maximum yield.  
    *(**Marking Rubric:** $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for calculating moles of both gases, $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for comparing stoichiometric ratio and stating stoichiometric balance).*

* **Sub-part 2:**
  * From stoichiometry: $1\text{ mol }\mathrm{N_2} \equiv 2\text{ mol }\mathrm{NH_3}$.
  * Theoretical moles of $\mathrm{NH_3}$:
    

$$
n(\mathrm{NH_3})_{\text{theo}} = 2 \times 2.0 \times 10^3\text{ mol} = 4.0 \times 10^3\text{ mol}
$$

  * Molar mass of $\mathrm{NH_3} = 14.0 + 3(1.0) = 17.0\text{ g mol}^{-1}$.
  * Maximum mass of $\mathrm{NH_3}$:
    

$$
m(\mathrm{NH_3})_{\text{theo}} = 4.0 \times 10^3\text{ mol} \times 17.0\text{ g mol}^{-1} = 68.0 \times 10^3\text{ g} = 68.0\text{ kg}
$$

  
    *(**Marking Rubric:** $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for mole-to-mass relation, $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for correct calculation with units: $68.0\text{ kg}$).*

* **Sub-part 3:**
  * Since both reactants are in exact stoichiometric equivalence ($1:3$ molar ratio), the theoretical excess reagent remaining is $0.0\text{ kg}$.  
    *(**Marking Rubric:** $1$ Mark for deducing zero excess reagent remaining at complete theoretical reaction).*

* **Sub-part 4:**
  * Percentage Yield formula:
    

$$
\text{Percentage Yield} = \frac{\text{Actual Yield}}{\text{Theoretical Yield}} \times 100
$$

  * Substitution:
    

$$
\text{Percentage Yield} = \frac{34.0\text{ kg}}{68.0\text{ kg}} \times 100 = 50.0\%
$$

  
    *(**Marking Rubric:** $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for percentage yield formula, $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for final value: $50.0\%$).*

### Question A.2: Assertion-Reason Problems
**Tag:** [CBSE Competency Pattern — AR, 1 Mark each]  
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ [High-Yield]

**Directions for Q(i) to Q(iv):**  
In the following questions, a statement of Assertion (A) is followed by a statement of Reason (R). Choose the correct option:  
- (a) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).  
- (b) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is NOT the correct explanation of (A).  
- (c) (A) is true, but (R) is false.  
- (d) (A) is false, but (R) is true.

***

#### Q.2(i)
* **Assertion (A):** The empirical formula of glucose is $\mathrm{CH_2O}$, whereas its molecular formula is $\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}$.  
* **Reason (R):** The molecular formula of a compound is always an integral multiple of its empirical formula, defined by $\text{Molecular Formula} = n \times \text{Empirical Formula}$, where $n = \frac{\text{Molar Mass}}{\text{Empirical Formula Mass}}$.

**Answer:** **(a)**  
**Step-by-Step Explanation & Marking:**  
* The atomic ratio of $\mathrm{C:H:O}$ in glucose is $6:12:6 = 1:2:1$, giving the simplest whole-number ratio empirical formula $\mathrm{CH_2O}$ (Empirical Formula mass $= 12 + 2 + 16 = 30\text{ g mol}^{-1}$).  
* Molecular mass $= 180\text{ g mol}^{-1}$. Therefore, $n = \frac{180}{30} = 6$. The reason correctly accounts for the mathematical derivation of the molecular formula from the empirical formula.  
*(**Marking Rubric:** $1$ Mark for option (a) with valid reasoning).*

***

#### Q.2(ii)
* **Assertion (A):** One molal ($\mathrm{1\ m}$) aqueous solution of urea is more concentrated than one molar ($\mathrm{1\ M}$) aqueous solution of urea at $298\text{ K}$ (density of water $= 1.00\text{ g mL}^{-1}$).  
* **Reason (R):** Molality depends on the mass of the solvent, whereas molarity depends on the total volume of the solution, which includes solute volume.

**Answer:** **(d)**  
**Step-by-Step Explanation & Marking:**  
* In a $1\text{ M}$ solution, $1\text{ mole}$ of urea is dissolved in $1000\text{ mL}$ of solution.  
  

$$
\text{Mass of solution} = V \times d \approx 1000\text{ mL} \times 1.02\text{ g/mL} > 1000\text{ g}
$$

  Solvent mass $= \text{Mass of solution} - \text{Mass of solute} (60\text{ g}) < 1000\text{ g}$.  
  Hence, $1\text{ M}$ solution contains $1\text{ mole}$ of solute in LESS than $1000\text{ g}$ of water, which makes its effective molality $> 1\text{ m}$.  
* Consequently, a $1\text{ M}$ solution is **more concentrated** than a $1\text{ m}$ solution, making Assertion (A) false. Reason (R) is fundamentally a true statement.  
*(**Marking Rubric:** $1$ Mark for option (d)).*

***

#### Q.2(iii)
* **Assertion (A):** When $20\text{ mL}$ of $\mathrm{H_2(g)}$ reacts with $10\text{ mL}$ of $\mathrm{O_2(g)}$ at constant temperature and pressure, water vapor produced occupies $20\text{ mL}$.  
* **Reason (R):** Gases combine or are produced in a chemical reaction in a simple whole-number ratio by volume, provided all gases are at the same temperature and pressure (Gay-Lussac’s Law).

**Answer:** **(a)**  
**Step-by-Step Explanation & Marking:**  
* Reaction equation:
  

$$
2\mathrm{H_2(g)} + \mathrm{O_2(g)} \rightarrow 2\mathrm{H_2O(g)}
$$

* By Gay-Lussac’s Law of Combining Volumes:
  

$$
2\text{ volumes of }\mathrm{H_2} + 1\text{ volume of }\mathrm{O_2} \rightarrow 2\text{ volumes of }\mathrm{H_2O(g)}
$$

* $20\text{ mL}$ of $\mathrm{H_2}$ reacts with $10\text{ mL}$ of $\mathrm{O_2}$ to yield precisely $20\text{ mL}$ of $\mathrm{H_2O(g)}$. The reason states Gay-Lussac's Law, which directly explains the observation.  
*(**Marking Rubric:** $1$ Mark for option (a)).*

***

#### Q.2(iv)
* **Assertion (A):** The number of significant figures in $0.00250$ is 3, while in $2.50 \times 10^{-3}$ it is also 3.  
* **Reason (R):** Zeros preceding the first non-zero digit are not significant, whereas trailing zeros after a decimal point are significant.

**Answer:** **(a)**  
**Step-by-Step Explanation & Marking:**  
* For $0.00250$: Leading zeros ($0.00$) indicate decimal position and are non-significant; the digits $2$ and $5$ are significant; the trailing zero after the decimal is significant $\rightarrow 3$ significant figures.  
* For scientific notation $N \times 10^x$: only digits in $N$ are counted ($2.50 \rightarrow 3$ significant figures). Reason gives the correct governing rule.  
*(**Marking Rubric:** $1$ Mark for option (a)).*

### Question A.3: HOTS & Multi-Concept Application Problem
**Tag:** [CBSE Competency Pattern — HOTS Analytical, 5 Marks]  
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

#### Problem Statement:
An organic liquid compound containing Carbon, Hydrogen, and Nitrogen is widely used in polymer resin synthesis. Complete combustion of $0.2100\text{ g}$ of this compound yields $0.4611\text{ g}$ of $\mathrm{CO_2}$ and $0.2831\text{ g}$ of $\mathrm{H_2O}$. 

In a separate Kjeldahl experiment, the nitrogen present in $0.1575\text{ g}$ of the same compound was converted quantitatively into $\mathrm{NH_3}$. This liberated ammonia was absorbed in $50.00\text{ mL}$ of $0.1000\text{ M }\mathrm{HCl}$. The unreacted excess acid required $27.50\text{ mL}$ of $0.1000\text{ M }\mathrm{NaOH}$ for complete neutralization.

Given that the vapor density of the compound is $30.05$:
1. Determine the percentage composition of $\mathrm{C}$, $\mathrm{H}$, and $\mathrm{N}$ in the organic compound. **(2 Marks)**
2. Calculate its empirical formula. **(1 Mark)**
3. Deduce its molecular formula. **(1 Mark)**
4. If $5.00\text{ g}$ of this compound is dissolved in $250.0\text{ mL}$ of water to form a solution of density $1.02\text{ g mL}^{-1}$, determine its molality ($\mathrm{m}$). **(1 Mark)**

#### Model Solution & Marking Scheme:

* **Step 1: Percentage of Carbon and Hydrogen**
  * Mass of Carbon in $\mathrm{CO_2}$:
    

$$
\% \mathrm{C} = \frac{12.011}{44.01} \times \frac{0.4611\text{ g}}{0.2100\text{ g}} \times 100 = \frac{0.12584}{0.2100} \times 100 = 59.92\%
$$

  * Mass of Hydrogen in $\mathrm{H_2O}$:
    

$$
\% \mathrm{H} = \frac{2 \times 1.008}{18.016} \times \frac{0.2831\text{ g}}{0.2100\text{ g}} \times 100 = \frac{0.03168}{0.2100} \times 100 = 15.08\%
$$

  *(**Marking Rubric:** $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for $\% \mathrm{C} = 59.92\%$, $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for $\% \mathrm{H} = 15.08\%$).*

* **Step 2: Percentage of Nitrogen via Kjeldahl Analysis**
  * Total moles of $\mathrm{HCl}$ added:
    

$$
n(\mathrm{HCl})_{\text{initial}} = 50.00 \times 10^{-3}\text{ L} \times 0.1000\text{ mol L}^{-1} = 5.000 \times 10^{-3}\text{ mol}
$$

  * Moles of $\mathrm{NaOH}$ required for back-titration:
    

$$
n(\mathrm{NaOH}) = 27.50 \times 10^{-3}\text{ L} \times 0.1000\text{ mol L}^{-1} = 2.750 \times 10^{-3}\text{ mol}
$$

  * Moles of unreacted $\mathrm{HCl} = n(\mathrm{NaOH}) = 2.750 \times 10^{-3}\text{ mol}$.
  * Moles of $\mathrm{HCl}$ neutralized by evolved $\mathrm{NH_3}$:
    

$$
n(\mathrm{HCl})_{\text{reacted}} = (5.000 - 2.750) \times 10^{-3} = 2.250 \times 10^{-3}\text{ mol}
$$

  * Since $\mathrm{NH_3 + HCl \rightarrow NH_4Cl}$, moles of $\mathrm{N} = 2.250 \times 10^{-3}\text{ mol}$.
  * Mass of Nitrogen:
    

$$
m(\mathrm{N}) = 2.250 \times 10^{-3}\text{ mol} \times 14.007\text{ g mol}^{-1} = 0.03152\text{ g}
$$

  * Percentage of Nitrogen:
    

$$
\% \mathrm{N} = \frac{0.03152\text{ g}}{0.1575\text{ g}} \times 100 = 25.02\%
$$

  * Verification of Total Percentage:
    

$$
\sum \% = 59.92 + 15.08 + 25.02 = 100.02\% \approx 100\% \quad (\text{No oxygen present})
$$

  *(**Marking Rubric:** $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for mole back-titration equation, $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for $\% \mathrm{N} = 25.0\%$).*

* **Step 3: Empirical Formula Calculation**

| Element | Percentage | Atomic Mass | Moles of Element | Simplest Molar Ratio | Whole Number Ratio |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| $\mathrm{C}$ | $59.92$ | $12.01$ | $\frac{59.92}{12.01} = 4.989$ | $\frac{4.989}{1.786} = 2.79 \approx 2.8$ | - |
| $\mathrm{H}$ | $15.08$ | $1.008$ | $\frac{15.08}{1.008} = 14.96$ | $\frac{14.96}{1.786} = 8.376$ | - |
| $\mathrm{N}$ | $25.00$ | $14.01$ | $\frac{25.00}{14.01} = 1.784$ | $\frac{1.784}{1.784} = 1.00$ | - |

  * Let us evaluate the integer multiple:
    

$$
\text{Ratio } \mathrm{C:H:N} \approx 2.8 : 8.4 : 1 \Rightarrow \text{Multiply by } 5: 14 : 42 : 5
$$

  * Looking at the direct small integer relation:
    

$$
\mathrm{C} = \frac{59.92}{12.01} = 4.99 \approx 5.0
$$

    

$$
\mathrm{H} = \frac{15.08}{1.008} = 14.96 \approx 15.0
$$

    

$$
\mathrm{N} = \frac{25.00}{14.01} = 1.785
$$

    If we check ethylenediamine $\mathrm{C_2H_8N_2}$ ($M = 60.1$):
    $\% \mathrm{C} = \frac{24}{60.1} = 39.9\%$.  
    Here: Molar mass $= 2 \times \text{Vapor Density} = 2 \times 30.05 = 60.10\text{ g mol}^{-1}$.  
    Let the formula be $\mathrm{C}_x\mathrm{H}_y\mathrm{N}_z$:  
    

$$
x = \frac{60.10 \times 0.5992}{12.01} = 3.00
$$

    

$$
y = \frac{60.10 \times 0.1508}{1.008} = 9.00
$$

    

$$
z = \frac{60.10 \times 0.2500}{14.01} = 1.07 \approx 1
$$

    *(Accounting for analytical rounding error: Propylamine / Isopropylamine, $\mathrm{C_3H_9N}$, Molar mass $= 36.03 + 9.07 + 14.01 = 59.11\text{ g mol}^{-1}$)*.  
    Empirical Formula $= \mathrm{C_3H_9N}$.  
    *(**Marking Rubric:** $1$ Mark for empirical formula derivation).*

* **Step 4: Molecular Formula**
  * $\text{Empirical Formula Mass} = 3(12.01) + 9(1.008) + 14.01 = 59.10\text{ g mol}^{-1}$.
  * $\text{Molecular Mass} = 2 \times \text{Vapor Density} = 2 \times 30.05 = 60.10\text{ g mol}^{-1}$.
  * $n = \frac{60.10}{59.10} \approx 1$.
  * Molecular Formula $= (\mathrm{C_3H_9N})_1 = \mathrm{C_3H_9N}$.  
    *(**Marking Rubric:** $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for relation $\text{Molar Mass} = 2 \times \text{V.D.}$, $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for final molecular formula).*

* **Step 5: Molality of Solution**
  * Mass of solute $= 5.00\text{ g}$. Moles of solute:
    

$$
n_2 = \frac{5.00\text{ g}}{59.1\text{ g mol}^{-1}} = 0.0846\text{ mol}
$$

  * Volume of solution $= 250.0\text{ mL}$.  
  * Mass of solution $= 250.0\text{ mL} \times 1.02\text{ g mL}^{-1} = 255.0\text{ g}$.
  * Mass of solvent ($w_1$) $= 255.0\text{ g} - 5.00\text{ g} = 250.0\text{ g} = 0.250\text{ kg}$.
  * Molality ($m$):
    

$$
m = \frac{n_2}{w_1\text{ (kg)}} = \frac{0.0846\text{ mol}}{0.250\text{ kg}} = 0.338\text{ mol kg}^{-1} = 0.338\text{ m}
$$

  
    *(**Marking Rubric:** $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for calculating mass of solvent correctly, $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for final molality with unit).*

### Question B.1: NCERT Exercise 1.24 (Limiting Reagent & Residual Reactants)
**Tag:** [NCERT Exercise 1.24, 3 Marks]  
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

#### Problem Statement:
Dinitrogen and dihydrogen react with each other to produce ammonia according to the chemical equation:

$$
\mathrm{N_2(g) + 3H_2(g) \rightarrow 2NH_3(g)}
$$

1. Calculate the mass of ammonia produced if $2.00 \times 10^3\text{ g}$ of dinitrogen reacts with $1.00 \times 10^3\text{ g}$ of dihydrogen.
2. Will any of the two reactants remain unreacted?
3. If yes, which one and what would be its mass?

#### Model Solution & Marking Scheme:
* **Step 1: Mole calculation of reactants**
  

$$
n(\mathrm{N_2}) = \frac{2.00 \times 10^3\text{ g}}{28.0\text{ g mol}^{-1}} = 71.43\text{ mol}
$$

  

$$
n(\mathrm{H_2}) = \frac{1.00 \times 10^3\text{ g}}{2.016\text{ g mol}^{-1}} = 496.0\text{ mol}
$$

  *(**Marking Rubric:** $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for determining initial moles).*

* **Step 2: Identification of Limiting Reagent**
  * According to the balanced equation, $1\text{ mol of }\mathrm{N_2}$ requires $3\text{ mol of }\mathrm{H_2}$.
  * For $71.43\text{ mol of }\mathrm{N_2}$, required moles of $\mathrm{H_2}$:
    

$$
n(\mathrm{H_2})_{\text{required}} = 3 \times 71.43 = 214.29\text{ mol}
$$

  * Available moles of $\mathrm{H_2} = 496.0\text{ mol} > 214.29\text{ mol}$.
  * Therefore, $\mathrm{N_2}$ is the **limiting reagent** and $\mathrm{H_2}$ is the **excess reagent**.  
    *(**Marking Rubric:** $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for stoichiometric comparison and specifying limiting reagent).*

* **Step 3: Mass of $\mathrm{NH_3}$ Produced**
  * $1\text{ mol }\mathrm{N_2}$ produces $2\text{ mol }\mathrm{NH_3}$.
  * Moles of $\mathrm{NH_3}$ formed:
    

$$
n(\mathrm{NH_3}) = 2 \times 71.43 = 142.86\text{ mol}
$$

  * Mass of $\mathrm{NH_3}$:
    

$$
m(\mathrm{NH_3}) = 142.86\text{ mol} \times 17.03\text{ g mol}^{-1} = 2432.9\text{ g} \approx 2.43 \times 10^3\text{ g} \ (2.43\text{ kg})
$$

  
    *(**Marking Rubric:** $1$ Mark for mass of $\mathrm{NH_3}$ with correct significant figures).*

* **Step 4: Residual Unreacted Reagent**
  * Yes, $\mathrm{H_2}$ remains unreacted.
  * Moles of unreacted $\mathrm{H_2}$:
    

$$
n(\mathrm{H_2})_{\text{excess}} = 496.0\text{ mol} - 214.29\text{ mol} = 281.71\text{ mol}
$$

  * Mass of unreacted $\mathrm{H_2}$:
    

$$
m(\mathrm{H_2}) = 281.71\text{ mol} \times 2.016\text{ g mol}^{-1} = 567.9\text{ g} \approx 5.68 \times 10^2\text{ g}
$$

  
    *(**Marking Rubric:** $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for naming $\mathrm{H_2}$, $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for final mass: $568\text{ g}$).*

### Question B.2: NCERT Exercise 1.36 (Stoichiometry of Precipitation)
**Tag:** [NCERT Exercise 1.36, 3 Marks]  
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ [High-Yield]

#### Problem Statement:
Chlorine is prepared in the laboratory by treating manganese dioxide ($\mathrm{MnO_2}$) with aqueous hydrochloric acid according to the reaction:

$$
4\mathrm{HCl(aq)} + \mathrm{MnO_2(s)} \rightarrow 2\mathrm{H_2O(l)} + \mathrm{MnCl_2(aq)} + \mathrm{Cl_2(g)}
$$

How many grams of $\mathrm{HCl}$ react completely with $5.0\text{ g}$ of manganese dioxide? (Atomic mass of $\mathrm{Mn} = 54.94\text{ u}$, $\mathrm{O} = 16.00\text{ u}$, $\mathrm{Cl} = 35.45\text{ u}$, $\mathrm{H} = 1.008\text{ u}$).

#### Model Solution & Marking Scheme:
* **Step 1: Calculate Molar Masses**
  

$$
M(\mathrm{MnO_2}) = 54.94 + 2(16.00) = 86.94\text{ g mol}^{-1}
$$

  

$$
M(\mathrm{HCl}) = 1.008 + 35.45 = 36.458\text{ g mol}^{-1}
$$

  
  *(**Marking Rubric:** $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for correct molar masses).*

* **Step 2: Molar Stoichiometry Ratio**
  From the balanced chemical equation:
  

$$
1\text{ mol }\mathrm{MnO_2} \equiv 86.94\text{ g}
$$

  

$$
4\text{ mol }\mathrm{HCl} \equiv 4 \times 36.458\text{ g} = 145.832\text{ g}
$$

  
  *(**Marking Rubric:** $1$ Mark for quantitative molar link between $\mathrm{MnO_2}$ and $\mathrm{HCl}$).*

* **Step 3: Mass Calculation**
  

$$
86.94\text{ g of }\mathrm{MnO_2}\text{ reacts with } 145.832\text{ g of }\mathrm{HCl}
$$

  Therefore, $5.0\text{ g of }\mathrm{MnO_2}$ reacts with:
  

$$
m(\mathrm{HCl}) = \frac{145.832\text{ g}}{86.94\text{ g}} \times 5.0\text{ g} = 8.387\text{ g} \approx 8.4\text{ g}
$$

  
  *(**Marking Rubric:** $1$ Mark for step calculation, $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for final answer: $8.4\text{ g}$ with units).*

### Question B.3: NCERT Exercise 1.8 & 1.12 (Concentration Interconversion)
**Tag:** [NCERT Exercise Combined Standard, 3 Marks]  
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

#### Problem Statement:
An aqueous solution of commercial concentrated nitric acid is labeled as $69\%$ by mass of $\mathrm{HNO_3}$ and has a density of $1.41\text{ g mL}^{-1}$.
1. Calculate the molarity of this nitric acid sample.
2. What volume of this concentrated acid is required to prepare $250.0\text{ mL}$ of $0.500\text{ M }\mathrm{HNO_3}$ solution?

#### Model Solution & Marking Scheme:
* **Step 1: Molarity Determination**
  * Let mass of solution $= 100\text{ g}$.
  * Mass of solute ($\mathrm{HNO_3}$) $= 69.0\text{ g}$.
  * Molar mass of $\mathrm{HNO_3} = 1.008 + 14.007 + 3(16.00) = 63.015\text{ g mol}^{-1} \approx 63.02\text{ g mol}^{-1}$.
  * Moles of $\mathrm{HNO_3}$:
    

$$
n = \frac{69.0\text{ g}}{63.02\text{ g mol}^{-1}} = 1.095\text{ mol}
$$

  * Volume of $100\text{ g}$ solution:
    

$$
V = \frac{\text{Mass}}{\text{Density}} = \frac{100\text{ g}}{1.41\text{ g mL}^{-1}} = 70.92\text{ mL} = 0.07092\text{ L}
$$

  * Molarity ($M_1$):
    

$$
M_1 = \frac{n}{V\text{ (L)}} = \frac{1.095\text{ mol}}{0.07092\text{ L}} = 15.44\text{ M} \quad \left(\text{or } M = \frac{\% \times d \times 10}{M_w} = \frac{69 \times 1.41 \times 10}{63.02} = 15.44\text{ M}\right)
$$

  
    *(**Marking Rubric:** $1$ Mark for calculating moles and volume, $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for obtaining $15.44\text{ M}$).*

* **Step 2: Dilution Equation**
  * Applying the dilution formula:
    

$$
M_1 V_1 = M_2 V_2
$$

  * Where $M_1 = 15.44\text{ M}$, $M_2 = 0.500\text{ M}$, $V_2 = 250.0\text{ mL}$.
  * Solving for $V_1$:
    

$$
V_1 = \frac{M_2 V_2}{M_1} = \frac{0.500\text{ M} \times 250.0\text{ mL}}{15.44\text{ M}} = \frac{125.0}{15.44} = 8.096\text{ mL} \approx 8.10\text{ mL}
$$

  
    *(**Marking Rubric:** $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for dilution formula, $1$ Mark for final value: $8.10\text{ mL}$).*

### Question C.1: NCERT Exemplar MCQ (Avogadro’s Number & Particulate Count)
**Tag:** [NCERT Exemplar Multiple Choice, 1 Mark]  
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ [High-Yield]

#### Problem Statement:
Which of the following contains the maximum number of atoms?
* (a) $1.0\text{ g}$ of $\mathrm{Ag(s)}$ [Atomic mass $= 108\text{ u}$]
* (b) $1.0\text{ g}$ of $\mathrm{Mg(s)}$ [Atomic mass $= 24\text{ u}$]
* (c) $1.0\text{ g}$ of $\mathrm{O_2(g)}$ [Atomic mass $= 16\text{ u}$]
* (d) $1.0\text{ g}$ of $\mathrm{Li(s)}$ [Atomic mass $= 7\text{ u}$]

#### Model Solution & Marking Scheme:
* **Mathematical Calculation:**
  

$$
\text{Number of atoms} = \frac{\text{mass}}{\text{molar mass}} \times N_A \times \text{atomicity}
$$

  * (a) $\text{Atoms in } 1.0\text{ g Ag} = \frac{1}{108} \times N_A = 0.00926\, N_A$
  * (b) $\text{Atoms in } 1.0\text{ g Mg} = \frac{1}{24} \times N_A = 0.0416\, N_A$
  * (c) $\text{Atoms in } 1.0\text{ g O_2} = \frac{1}{32} \times 2 \times N_A = \frac{1}{16} \times N_A = 0.0625\, N_A$
  * (d) $\text{Atoms in } 1.0\text{ g Li} = \frac{1}{7} \times N_A = 0.1428\, N_A$
* Since lithium has the lowest atomic mass among the monoatomic/diatomic species per gram, it yields the highest number of moles of atoms.
* **Correct Option:** **(d)**  
  *(**Marking Rubric:** $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for formula/atomicity relation, $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for selecting option (d)).*

### Question C.2: NCERT Exemplar Subjective Analysis (Isotopic Abundances & Average Atomic Mass)
**Tag:** [NCERT Exemplar Subjective, 3 Marks]  
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐ [Core]

#### Problem Statement:
Naturally occurring boron consists of two stable isotopes: $^{10}\mathrm{B}$ having an accurate isotopic mass of $10.0129\text{ u}$, and $^{11}\mathrm{B}$ having an accurate isotopic mass of $11.0093\text{ u}$. The standard atomic mass of natural boron listed on the periodic table is $10.811\text{ u}$.
1. State the principle used to calculate the average atomic mass of an element existing as multiple isotopes.
2. Determine the fractional and percentage relative abundances of $^{10}\mathrm{B}$ and $^{11}\mathrm{B}$.

#### Model Solution & Marking Scheme:
* **Step 1: Formula / Principle**
  * Average atomic mass is the weighted average of the atomic masses of its naturally occurring isotopes, weighted by their relative fractional abundances:
    

$$
\bar{A} = \sum (f_i \times A_i) = f_1 A_1 + f_2 A_2
$$

    where $f_1 + f_2 = 1$.  
    *(**Marking Rubric:** $1$ Mark for principle and algebraic statement).*

* **Step 2: Algebraic Substitution**
  * Let the fractional abundance of $^{10}\mathrm{B}$ be $x$.
  * Then the fractional abundance of $^{11}\mathrm{B}$ is $(1 - x)$.
  * Substituting known values:
    

$$
10.811 = x(10.0129) + (1 - x)(11.0093)
$$

    

$$
10.811 = 10.0129x + 11.0093 - 11.0093x
$$

    

$$
10.811 - 11.0093 = -0.9964x
$$

    

$$
-0.1983 = -0.9964x
$$

  
    *(**Marking Rubric:** $1$ Mark for correct substitution and linear reduction).*

* **Step 3: Solution of Abundance Values**
  

$$
x = \frac{0.1983}{0.9964} = 0.1990
$$

  * Fractional abundance of $^{10}\mathrm{B} = 0.1990 \implies \mathbf{19.90\%}$
  * Fractional abundance of $^{11}\mathrm{B} = 1 - 0.1990 = 0.8010 \implies \mathbf{80.10\%}$  
    *(**Marking Rubric:** $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for $^{10}\mathrm{B} = 19.90\%$, $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for $^{11}\mathrm{B} = 80.10\%$).*

### Question C.3: NCERT Exemplar Advanced Analytical Problem
**Tag:** [NCERT Exemplar HOTS / Comprehensive, 5 Marks]  
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

#### Problem Statement:
A solid mixture weighing $5.000\text{ g}$ consisting only of barium oxide ($\mathrm{BaO}$) and calcium oxide ($\mathrm{CaO}$) is dissolved completely in excess hydrochloric acid:

$$
\mathrm{BaO(s) + 2HCl(aq) \rightarrow BaCl_2(aq) + H_2O(l)}
$$

$$
\mathrm{CaO(s) + 2HCl(aq) \rightarrow CaCl_2(aq) + H_2O(l)}
$$

The resulting clear solution is subsequently treated with an excess of dilute sulfuric acid ($\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$), precipitating pure white insoluble barium sulfate ($\mathrm{BaSO_4}$), while calcium ions remain entirely in solution under the experimental dilution conditions:

$$
\mathrm{BaCl_2(aq) + H_2SO_4(aq) \rightarrow BaSO_4(s)\downarrow + 2HCl(aq)}
$$

The precipitate of $\mathrm{BaSO_4}$ is quantitatively filtered, washed thoroughly with deionized water, dried to constant weight, and found to weigh $4.668\text{ g}$.  
*(Molar masses: $\mathrm{Ba} = 137.33\text{ g mol}^{-1}$, $\mathrm{Ca} = 40.08\text{ g mol}^{-1}$, $\mathrm{S} = 32.06\text{ g mol}^{-1}$, $\mathrm{O} = 16.00\text{ g mol}^{-1}$)*.

1. Formulate the gravimetric stoichiometry connecting mass of $\mathrm{BaSO_4}$ to $\mathrm{BaO}$. **(1 Mark)**
2. Calculate the mass and percentage by mass of $\mathrm{BaO}$ in the original binary oxide mixture. **(2 Marks)**
3. Calculate the percentage by mass of $\mathrm{CaO}$ in the mixture. **(1 Mark)**
4. If the residual calcium ions in the filtrate are precipitated as calcium oxalate monohydrate ($\mathrm{CaC_2O_4 \cdot H_2O}$, Molar mass $= 146.11\text{ g mol}^{-1}$), what theoretical mass of oxalate precipitate would be recovered? **(1 Mark)**

#### Model Solution & Marking Scheme:

* **Step 1: Stoichiometric Relationship**
  * According to the reaction sequence:
    

$$
1\text{ mol }\mathrm{BaO} \rightarrow 1\text{ mol }\mathrm{BaCl_2} \rightarrow 1\text{ mol }\mathrm{BaSO_4}
$$

  * Molar mass of $\mathrm{BaO} = 137.33 + 16.00 = 153.33\text{ g mol}^{-1}$.
  * Molar mass of $\mathrm{BaSO_4} = 137.33 + 32.06 + 4(16.00) = 233.39\text{ g mol}^{-1}$.  
  *(**Marking Rubric:** $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for molar masses, $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for conservation of moles: $n(\mathrm{BaO}) = n(\mathrm{BaSO_4})$).*

* **Step 2: Mass and Percentage of $\mathrm{BaO}$**
  * Moles of $\mathrm{BaSO_4}$ isolated:
    

$$
n(\mathrm{BaSO_4}) = \frac{4.668\text{ g}}{233.39\text{ g mol}^{-1}} = 0.02000\text{ mol}
$$

  * Moles of $\mathrm{BaO}$ present in original sample:
    

$$
n(\mathrm{BaO}) = 0.02000\text{ mol}
$$

  * Mass of $\mathrm{BaO}$:
    

$$
m(\mathrm{BaO}) = 0.02000\text{ mol} \times 153.33\text{ g mol}^{-1} = 3.0666\text{ g} \approx 3.067\text{ g}
$$

  * Mass percentage of $\mathrm{BaO}$:
    

$$
\% \mathrm{BaO} = \frac{3.0666\text{ g}}{5.000\text{ g}} \times 100 = 61.33\%
$$

  
    *(**Marking Rubric:** $1$ Mark for mass of $\mathrm{BaO} = 3.067\text{ g}$, $1$ Mark for $\% \mathrm{BaO} = 61.33\%$).*

* **Step 3: Mass and Percentage of $\mathrm{CaO}$**
  * Mass of $\mathrm{CaO} = \text{Total mass} - m(\mathrm{BaO})$:
    

$$
m(\mathrm{CaO}) = 5.000\text{ g} - 3.0666\text{ g} = 1.9334\text{ g} \approx 1.933\text{ g}
$$

  * Mass percentage of $\mathrm{CaO}$:
    

$$
\% \mathrm{CaO} = \frac{1.9334\text{ g}}{5.000\text{ g}} \times 100 = 38.67\%
$$

  
    *(**Marking Rubric:** $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for mass subtraction, $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for $\% \mathrm{CaO} = 38.67\%$).*

* **Step 4: Mass of Calcium Oxalate Monohydrate Recoverable**
  * Molar mass of $\mathrm{CaO} = 40.08 + 16.00 = 56.08\text{ g mol}^{-1}$.
  * Moles of $\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}$ ions from $\mathrm{CaO}$:
    

$$
n(\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}) = \frac{1.9334\text{ g}}{56.08\text{ g mol}^{-1}} = 0.03448\text{ mol}
$$

  * Stoichiometry: $1\text{ mol }\mathrm{Ca}^{2+} \equiv 1\text{ mol }\mathrm{CaC_2O_4 \cdot H_2O}$.
  * Mass of precipitate:
    

$$
m(\mathrm{CaC_2O_4 \cdot H_2O}) = 0.03448\text{ mol} \times 146.11\text{ g mol}^{-1} = 5.038\text{ g}
$$

  
    *(**Marking Rubric:** $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for molar balance, $\frac{1}{2}$ Mark for final mass: $5.038\text{ g}$ or $5.04\text{ g}$).*
