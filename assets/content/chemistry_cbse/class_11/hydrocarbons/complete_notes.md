# SECTION 1: Fundamental Concepts, Nomenclature & Isomerism

### 1.1 Electronic Structure & Hybridization ⭐⭐⭐
Alkanes are open-chain, saturated hydrocarbons containing carbon-carbon ($\mathrm{C-C}$) and carbon-hydrogen ($\mathrm{C-H}$) single bonds ($\sigma$-bonds). Because of their inert chemical nature under normal ambient conditions (showing little affinity towards acids, bases, oxidizing, and reducing agents), they were historically termed **paraffins** (Latin: *parum* = little, *affinis* = affinity).

* **General Molecular Formula:** 
  

$$
\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n+2} \quad (n \ge 1)
$$

* **Hybridization State:** Every carbon atom in an alkane is $\mathrm{sp^3}$ hybridized.
* **Geometry:** Tetrahedral geometry around each carbon atom with ideal bond angles of:
  

$$
\theta = 109^\circ 28' \approx 109.5^\circ
$$

* **Bond Parameters:**
  * $\mathrm{C-C}$ single bond length: $154\text{ pm}\ (1.54\text{ Å})$
  * $\mathrm{C-H}$ single bond length: $112\text{ pm}\ (1.12\text{ Å})$
  * $\mathrm{C-C}$ bond dissociation enthalpy: $\approx 348\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$
  * $\mathrm{C-H}$ bond dissociation enthalpy: $\approx 414\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$

```
       H
       | (sp³ - 1s σ-bond)
   H - C - H
       |
       H
Tetrahedral Methane (CH₄)
```

***

### 1.2 IUPAC Nomenclature Rules for Alkanes ⭐⭐⭐⭐
To assign systematic names according to standard IUPAC conventions:

1. **Longest Continuous Carbon Chain (Principal Chain):** Identify the parent chain containing the maximum number of consecutively linked carbon atoms. If two chains of equal length compete, choose the chain with the greater number of substituents/side-chains.
2. **Lowest Locant Rule:** Number the principal chain from the terminal end that yields the lowest set of locants at the first point of difference.
3. **Alphabetical Ordering of Substituents:** Prefix prefixes like *ethyl-*, *methyl-*, *propyl-* in strict alphabetical order regardless of their position number. Prefixes like *di-*, *tri-*, *tetra-* are ignored when comparing alphabetical seniority, whereas structural prefixes like *iso-* and *neo-* are considered part of the fundamental alkyl group name (while *sec-* and *tert-* are hyphenated and ignored).

```
         CH₃
         |
CH₃ - CH - CH - CH₂ - CH₃
           |
           CH₂ - CH₃

Longest Chain: 6 Carbons (Hexane)
Numbering from left: Locants at 2 (methyl) and 3 (ethyl)
Correct IUPAC Name: 3-Ethyl-2-methylhexane
```

***

### 1.3 Isomerism in Alkanes ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Alkanes do not exhibit stereoisomerism involving restricted rotation (geometrical) under normal conditions, nor functional group isomerism. They display:

#### A. Chain Isomerism
Compounds possessing identical molecular formulas but differing in the carbon skeleton (straight vs. branched chains).
* Methane ($\mathrm{CH_4}$), ethane ($\mathrm{C_2H_6}$), and propane ($\mathrm{C_3H_8}$) have **no isomers**.
* Butane ($\mathrm{C_4H_{10}}$) exhibits **2 chain isomers**:
  1. $n$-Butane: $\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3}$
  2. Isobutane (2-Methylpropane): $\mathrm{CH_3-CH(CH_3)-CH_3}$
* Pentane ($\mathrm{C_5H_{12}}$) exhibits **3 chain isomers**:
  1. $n$-Pentane: $\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3}$
  2. Isopentane (2-Methylbutane): $\mathrm{CH_3-CH(CH_3)-CH_2-CH_3}$
  3. Neopentane (2,2-Dimethylpropane): $\mathrm{C(CH_3)_4}$
* Hexane ($\mathrm{C_6H_{14}}$) has **5 isomers**; Heptane ($\mathrm{C_7H_{16}}$) has **9 isomers**; Decane ($\mathrm{C_{10}H_{22}}$) has **75 isomers**.

#### B. Conformational Isomerism
Non-separable spatial arrangements produced by free rotation of carbon atoms around $\mathrm{C-C}$ single $\sigma$-bonds (covered in detail in Section 5).

# SECTION 2: Methods of Preparation

### 2.1 From Unsaturated Hydrocarbons (Sabatier-Senderens Reaction) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Hydrogenation of alkenes or alkynes over finely divided transition metal catalysts yielding corresponding alkanes.

#### Reaction Equation:

$$
\mathrm{R-CH=CH_2 + H_2 \xrightarrow{Ni / Pt / Pd} R-CH_2-CH_3}
$$

$$
\mathrm{R-C\equiv CH + 2H_2 \xrightarrow{Ni / Pt / Pd} R-CH_2-CH_3}
$$

#### Reaction Conditions:
* **Catalysts:**
  * Platinum ($\mathrm{Pt}$) or Palladium ($\mathrm{Pd}$): Operates smoothly at room temperature ($298\text{ K}$) and atmospheric pressure ($1\text{ atm}$).
  * Finely divided Nickel ($\mathrm{Raney\ Ni}$): Requires elevated temperatures ($473 - 573\text{ K}$). This high-temperature variant is specifically the **Sabatier-Senderens Reaction**.
* **Stereochemistry:** **Syn-addition** (both hydrogen atoms add across the $\pi$-bond from the same coordination face of the catalyst surface).

***

### 2.2 From Alkyl Halides

#### A. Reduction of Alkyl Halides ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Alkyl halides (except fluorides) undergo reductive dehalogenation with nascent hydrogen generated *in situ* or using metal hydride reducing agents.

$$
\mathrm{R-X + Zn + H^+ \longrightarrow R-H + Zn^{2+} + X^-}
$$

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-Cl + 2[H] \xrightarrow{Zn / \text{dil. } HCl} CH_4 + HCl}
$$

$$
\mathrm{C_2H_5-Br + 2[H] \xrightarrow{Zn / CH_3COOH} C_2H_6 + HBr}
$$

* **Alternative Modern Reducing Agents:**
  * $\mathrm{LiAlH_4}$ (Lithium Aluminium Hydride): Reduces $1^\circ$ and $2^\circ$ alkyl halides to alkanes ($3^\circ$ alkyl halides primarily undergo elimination to yield alkenes).
  * $\mathrm{NaBH_4}$ (Sodium Borohydride): Reduces $2^\circ$ and $3^\circ$ alkyl halides ($1^\circ$ halides react very slowly).
  * Triphenyltin hydride ($\mathrm{Ph_3SnH}$): Free-radical mediated reduction.

***

#### B. Wurtz Reaction ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Coupling of two molecules of alkyl halides in the presence of sodium metal in an anhydrous (dry) ethereal medium to synthesize symmetrical higher alkanes.

$$
\mathrm{2R-X + 2Na \xrightarrow{\text{Dry Ether}} R-R + 2NaX}
$$

##### Mechanistic Pathway 1: Free Radical Mechanism (Gas Phase / Non-polar media)

$$
\text{Step 1: } \mathrm{Na \longrightarrow Na^\bullet + e^-}
$$

$$
\text{Step 2: } \mathrm{R-X + e^- \longrightarrow R^\bullet + X^-}
$$

$$
\text{Step 3: } \mathrm{R^\bullet + R^\bullet \longrightarrow R-R \quad (Dimerization/Coupling)}
$$

$$
\text{Side-reaction: } \mathrm{2R-CH_2-CH_2^\bullet \longrightarrow R-CH=CH_2 + R-CH_2-CH_3 \quad (Disproportionation)}
$$

##### Mechanistic Pathway 2: Organometallic / Ionic Intermediate

$$
\text{Step 1: } \mathrm{R-X + 2Na \longrightarrow R^-Na^+ + NaX \quad (Formation\ of\ Alkylsodium)}
$$

$$
\text{Step 2: } \mathrm{R^-Na^+ + R-X \xrightarrow{S_N2} R-R + NaX}
$$

##### Scope, Characteristics & Critical Limitations:
1. **Solvent:** Dry ether (free from moisture, $\mathrm{H_2O}$, and alcohol) is mandatory. Otherwise, sodium metal violently reacts with water:
   

$$
\mathrm{2Na + 2H_2O \longrightarrow 2NaOH + H_2 \uparrow}
$$

   And the carbanionic intermediate would be protonated:
   

$$
\mathrm{R^-Na^+ + H_2O \longrightarrow R-H + NaOH}
$$

2. **Methane Cannot be Prepared:** Because the Wurtz reaction involves $\mathrm{C-C}$ bond coupling, the smallest alkane synthesizable is ethane ($n \ge 2$).
3. **Crossed Wurtz Reaction Yields Inseparable Mixtures:** If two different alkyl halides ($\mathrm{R_1-X}$ and $\mathrm{R_2-X}$) are used, three distinct alkanes are produced:
   

$$
\mathrm{R_1-X + R_2-X + 2Na \xrightarrow{\text{Dry Ether}} R_1-R_1 + R_1-R_2 + R_2-R_2 + 2NaX}
$$

   Due to close boiling points, separating this ternary alkane mixture is industrially and synthetically unfeasible.
4. **Tertiary Halides Fail:** $3^\circ$ alkyl halides undergo base-catalyzed $\mathrm{E2}$ dehydrohalogenation under Wurtz conditions, yielding alkenes as major products instead of alkanes.

***

### 2.3 From Carboxylic Acids

#### A. Decarboxylation of Carboxylic Acid Salts (Soda-Lime Decarboxylation / Dumas Method) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Heating the sodium or potassium salt of a fatty acid dry with **soda-lime** results in the loss of carbon dioxide as a carbonate, producing an alkane with **one carbon atom fewer** than the parent carboxylic acid.

$$
\mathrm{R-COONa + NaOH \xrightarrow[3:1\ ratio]{\mathrm{CaO},\ \Delta} R-H + Na_2CO_3}
$$

##### Reagent Identity:
* **Soda-lime:** A fused dry mixture of caustic soda ($\mathrm{NaOH}$) and quicklime ($\mathrm{CaO}$) in approximately a **$3:1$ weight ratio**.
* **Role of $\mathrm{CaO}$:** 
  1. $\mathrm{CaO}$ is intensely hygroscopic, keeping the reaction mass completely dry ($\mathrm{NaOH}$ is deliquescent and absorbs moisture rapidly).
  2. $\mathrm{CaO}$ prevents the glass apparatus from fusing/etching at high temperatures ($>573\text{ K}$) by mitigating caustic melt action.

##### Step-by-Step Reaction Mechanism:

$$
\text{Step 1: Carboxylate cleavage to carbanion (Rate-Determining Step):}
$$

$$
\mathrm{R-COO^- \xrightarrow{\Delta, -\mathrm{CO_2}} [R^-]}
$$

$$
\text{Step 2: Proton transfer from hydroxide to carbanion:}
$$

$$
\mathrm{[R^-] + H-O-H \ (or\ H-ONa) \longrightarrow R-H + OH^-}
$$

$$
\text{Step 3: Capture of carbon dioxide:}
$$

$$
\mathrm{2NaOH + CO_2 \longrightarrow Na_2CO_3 + H_2O}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Overall: } \mathrm{R-COONa + NaOH \xrightarrow{CaO, \Delta} R-H + Na_2CO_3}}
$$

***

#### B. Kolbe’s Electrolytic Decarboxylation ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Electrolysis of an aqueous concentrated solution of sodium or potassium salt of a carboxylic acid yields an alkane containing an **even number of carbon atoms** (possessing a symmetrical structure) at the anode.

$$
\mathrm{2R-COOK + 2H_2O \xrightarrow{\text{Electrolysis}} R-R + 2CO_2 \uparrow + 2KOH + H_2 \uparrow}
$$

##### Half-Cell Reactions & Detailed Mechanism:

$$
\text{Ionization in aqueous solution: } \mathrm{2R-COOK \rightleftharpoons 2R-COO^- + 2K^+}
$$

$$
\mathrm{2H_2O \rightleftharpoons 2H^+ + 2OH^-}
$$

* **At Anode (Oxidation):**
  

$$
\text{Loss of 2 electrons: } \mathrm{2R-COO^- \xrightarrow{-2e^-} 2[R-COO^\bullet] \quad (\text{Unstable acyloxy radicals})}
$$

  

$$
\text{Decarboxylation: } \mathrm{2[R-COO^\bullet] \longrightarrow 2R^\bullet + 2CO_2 \uparrow}
$$

  

$$
\text{Radical Coupling: } \mathrm{R^\bullet + R^\bullet \longrightarrow R-R \quad (\text{Alkane gas/liquid})}
$$

* **At Cathode (Reduction):**
  

$$
\mathrm{2H^+ + 2e^- \longrightarrow 2H^\bullet \longrightarrow H_2 \uparrow}
$$

  *(Or: $\mathrm{2H_2O + 2e^- \longrightarrow 2OH^- + H_2 \uparrow}$)*

* **Solution Phase Transformation:**
  Accumulation of residual $\mathrm{K^+}$ and $\mathrm{OH^-}$ ions causes the formation of $\mathrm{KOH}$.
  

$$
\boxed{\text{The pH of the electrolyte solution progressively increases } (\mathrm{pH} > 7)}
$$

##### Critical Constraints:
1. **Methane cannot be prepared** by Kolbe's electrolysis because radical coupling requires at least one carbon from each monomer radical, producing an ethane core ($\mathrm{CH_3^\bullet + CH_3^\bullet \to C_2H_6}$).
2. Anodic side products include small amounts of esters ($\mathrm{R-COOR}$), alkenes, and alcohols formed by disproportionation or carbocation paths if radical oxidation proceeds further.

# SECTION 3: Physical Properties of Alkanes

### 3.1 Physical States & Volatility ⭐⭐⭐
* $\mathrm{C_1}$ to $\mathrm{C_4}$ (Methane to Butane): Colorless, odorless **gases**.
* $\mathrm{C_5}$ to $\mathrm{C_{17}}$ (Pentane to Heptadecane): Colorless, volatile **liquids**.
* $\mathrm{C_{18}}$ and higher: Waxy, non-volatile **solids**.

### 3.2 Boiling Point Systematics ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Alkanes are non-polar molecules held together exclusively by weak, short-range **London dispersion forces (induced dipole-induced dipole van der Waals forces)**.

$$
\text{Magnitude of van der Waals Forces} \propto \text{Molecular Mass} \propto \text{Molecular Surface Area}
$$

1. **Dependence on Chain Length:** In a homologous series of straight-chain alkanes, each successive $-\mathrm{CH_2}-$ homologue adds more polarizable electron clouds, expanding the total molecular surface area. The boiling point increases by approximately **$20 - 30\text{ K}$** for each added $-\mathrm{CH_2}-$ unit.
2. **Dependence on Branching (Isomeric Alkanes):** For structural isomers with identical molecular formulas, **branching decreases the boiling point**.
   

$$
\boxed{\text{Boiling Point: } n\text{-isomer} > \text{iso-isomer} > \text{neo-isomer}}
$$

   * **Molecular Rationale:** As branching increases, the molecular envelope assumes a progressively spherical shape. A sphere displays the **minimum surface area** per unit volume, reducing intermolecular points of contact and weakening dispersion interactions.

$$
\text{Example: Boiling points of Pentane isomers } (\mathrm{C_5H_{12}}):
$$

$$
\text{n-Pentane } (309.1\text{ K}) > \text{Isopentane } (301.0\text{ K}) > \text{Neopentane } (282.5\text{ K})
$$

***

### 3.3 Melting Point Systematics (Alternation / Oscillatory Effect) ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Melting points of alkanes do not rise in a continuous linear curve; instead, an **alternation effect** is observed:
* Alkanes containing an **even number of carbon atoms** possess higher melting points than their immediate lower and higher homologues containing an **odd number of carbon atoms**.

```
    EVEN NUMBER OF CARBONS               ODD NUMBER OF CARBONS
End methyls on OPPOSITE sides          End methyls on SAME side
       CH₃        CH₂ - CH₃                   CH₃        CH₂
      /   \      /                           /   \      /   \
   CH₂     CH₂-CH₂                        CH₂     CH₂-CH₂    CH₃
Close, symmetrical crystal packing.     Less symmetrical packing.
Higher melting point.                   Lower melting point.
```

* **Physical Justification:** In even-carbon alkanes, the terminal $-\mathrm{CH_3}$ groups point in opposite directions across the zig-zag backbone, conferring molecular symmetry that fits tightly into the crystal lattice. Odd-carbon alkanes have both terminal groups on the same side, creating packing defects in the lattice.

***

### 3.4 Solubility & Density ⭐⭐⭐
* **Solubility:** Alkanes are strictly non-polar ($\mu \approx 0\text{ D}$) and follow the thermodynamic principle of *"like dissolves like"*. They are insoluble in water (they cannot disrupt the strong hydrogen-bonded network of liquid $\mathrm{H_2O}$) but dissolve freely in non-polar organic solvents like benzene, petroleum ether, chloroform, and carbon tetrachloride.
* **Density:** Alkanes are lighter than water. Their densities increase with molecular weight, leveling off asymptotically at approximately **$0.8\text{ g}\cdot\text{cm}^{-3}$**. Consequently, all alkanes float on water.

***

# SECTION 4: Chemical Properties & Detailed Reaction Mechanisms

Alkanes are generally unreactive towards electrophiles, nucleophiles, and common chemical reagents due to the high stability of $\mathrm{C-C}$ and $\mathrm{C-H}$ $\sigma$-bonds and the negligible electronegativity difference between Carbon ($2.5$) and Hydrogen ($2.1$). Under thermal, photochemical, or catalytic conditions, they undergo homolytic cleavage.

***

### 4.1 Free-Radical Halogenation ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
The substitution of one or more hydrogen atoms of an alkane by halogen atoms ($\mathrm{-F, -Cl, -Br, -I}$).

$$
\mathrm{R-H + X_2 \xrightarrow{h\nu\ \text{or}\ \Delta\ (520-673\text{ K})} R-X + HX}
$$

#### Order of Reactivity of Halogens:

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{F_2 \gg Cl_2 > Br_2 \gg I_2}}
$$

* **Fluorination:** Violently exothermic ($\Delta H^\circ < 0$ is extremely large), explosive, and leads to extensive $\mathrm{C-C}$ bond cleavage. Controlled fluorination is achieved by diluting with inert nitrogen or via halogen exchange (Swarts Reaction: $\mathrm{R-Cl + AgF \to R-F + AgCl}$).
* **Iodination:** Highly endothermic and reversible. It requires in situ oxidation of the formed $\mathrm{HI}$ (a strong reducing agent) using strong oxidants like iodic acid ($\mathrm{HIO_3}$) or concentrated nitric acid ($\mathrm{HNO_3}$):
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_4 + I_2 \rightleftharpoons CH_3I + HI}
$$

  

$$
\mathrm{5HI + HIO_3 \longrightarrow 3I_2 + 3H_2O}
$$

***

#### Complete Mechanistic Pathway for Chlorination of Methane

##### Phase 1: Chain Initiation
Homolytic cleavage of the chlorine-chlorine bond driven by ultraviolet light absorption ($h\nu$) or thermal activation ($\approx 520\text{ K}$).

$$
\mathrm{Cl-Cl \xrightarrow{h\nu} Cl^\bullet + Cl^\bullet \quad (\Delta H^\circ = +242\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1})}
$$

##### Phase 2: Chain Propagation
A self-sustaining catalytic cycle where radical species regenerate reactive radical centers.

$$
\text{Propagation Step A: } \mathrm{CH_4 + Cl^\bullet \longrightarrow CH_3^\bullet + HCl \quad (\Delta H^\circ = +4\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1})}
$$

$$
\text{Propagation Step B: } \mathrm{CH_3^\bullet + Cl_2 \longrightarrow CH_3Cl + Cl^\bullet \quad (\Delta H^\circ = -109\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1})}
$$

The regenerated $\mathrm{Cl^\bullet}$ in Step B triggers another cycle through Step A. Subsequent attacks on $\mathrm{CH_3Cl}$ yield polysubstituted chloromethanes:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3Cl \xrightarrow{Cl^\bullet, -\mathrm{HCl}} {}^\bullet CH_2Cl \xrightarrow{Cl_2} CH_2Cl_2 + Cl^\bullet \ (\text{Dichloromethane})}
$$

$$
\mathrm{CH_2Cl_2 \xrightarrow{Cl^\bullet, -\mathrm{HCl}} {}^\bullet CHCl_2 \xrightarrow{Cl_2} CHCl_3 + Cl^\bullet \ (\text{Trichloromethane / Chloroform})}
$$

$$
\mathrm{CHCl_3 \xrightarrow{Cl^\bullet, -\mathrm{HCl}} {}^\bullet CCl_3 \xrightarrow{Cl_2} CCl_4 + Cl^\bullet \ (\text{Tetrachloromethane / Carbon tetrachloride})}
$$

##### Phase 3: Chain Termination
Exhaustion of reactive radical intermediates through radical-radical recombination:

$$
\mathrm{Cl^\bullet + Cl^\bullet \longrightarrow Cl_2}
$$

$$
\mathrm{CH_3^\bullet + Cl^\bullet \longrightarrow CH_3Cl}
$$

$$
\mathrm{CH_3^\bullet + CH_3^\bullet \longrightarrow CH_3-CH_3 \quad (\text{Definitive mechanistic proof of free radical intermediates})}
$$

> **Key Pedagogical Note:** The detection of minute trace quantities of **ethane ($\mathrm{C_2H_6}$)** in the chlorination products of pure methane is proof that methyl free radicals ($\mathrm{CH_3^\bullet}$) form during the reaction.

***

#### Selectivity vs. Reactivity Principle (Chlorination vs. Bromination) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
The relative rates of hydrogen atom abstraction depend on the stability of the intermediate free radical formed ($3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ$ due to hyperconjugation and inductive stabilization).

$$
\text{Relative Rates of H-Abstraction at } 298\text{ K:}
$$

* **For Chlorination:**
  

$$
1^\circ\text{ H} : 2^\circ\text{ H} : 3^\circ\text{ H} = 1.0 : 3.8 : 5.0
$$

* **For Bromination:**
  

$$
1^\circ\text{ H} : 2^\circ\text{ H} : 3^\circ\text{ H} = 1 : 82 : 1600
$$

* **Pedagogical Conclusion:** Chlorination is highly **reactive and non-selective**, yielding statistical mixtures of regioisomers. Bromination is **sluggish and highly regioselective** (Hammond's Postulate), almost exclusively abstracting the tertiary ($3^\circ$) hydrogen atom.

***

### 4.2 Combustion ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Complete oxidation in an excess of dioxygen produces carbon dioxide, water vapor, and thermal energy.

$$
\mathrm{C_n H_{2n+2} + \left(\frac{3n+1}{2}\right)O_2 \longrightarrow nCO_2 + (n+1)H_2O_{(l)} \quad \Delta_c H^\circ < 0}
$$

$$
\mathrm{CH_4(g) + 2O_2(g) \longrightarrow CO_2(g) + 2H_2O(l)} \quad \Delta_c H^\circ = -890\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}
$$

$$
\mathrm{C_4H_{10}(g) + \frac{13}{2}O_2(g) \longrightarrow 4CO_2(g) + 5H_2O(l)} \quad \Delta_c H^\circ = -2875.8\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}
$$

* **Incomplete Combustion:** Under a deficient oxygen supply:
  

$$
\mathrm{2CH_4(g) + 3O_2(g) \longrightarrow 2CO(g) + 4H_2O(l)}
$$

  

$$
\mathrm{CH_4(g) + O_2(g) \longrightarrow C(s) + 2H_2O(l) \quad (\text{Carbon Black used in inks and tire manufacturing})}
$$

***

### 4.3 Controlled Catalytic Oxidation ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Under specific catalytic formulations, pressures, and temperatures, alkanes yield selective oxidation products.

1. **Methanol Synthesis:**
   

$$
\mathrm{2CH_4 + O_2 \xrightarrow[100\text{ atm},\ 523\text{ K}]{\mathrm{Cu\ tube}} 2CH_3OH}
$$

2. **Methanal (Formaldehyde) Synthesis:**
   

$$
\mathrm{CH_4 + O_2 \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\mathrm{Mo_2O_3}} HCHO + H_2O}
$$

3. **Carboxylic Acid Synthesis:**
   

$$
\mathrm{2R-CH_3 + 3O_2 \xrightarrow[(\mathrm{CH_3COO})_2\mathrm{Mn},\ \Delta]{} 2R-COOH + 2H_2O}
$$

   

$$
\mathrm{2CH_3-CH_3 + 3O_2 \xrightarrow[(\mathrm{CH_3COO})_2\mathrm{Mn},\ \Delta]{} 2CH_3COOH + 2H_2O}
$$

4. **Oxidation of Tertiary Hydrogen:** While alkanes generally resist attack by aqueous potassium permanganate ($\mathrm{KMnO_4}$), alkanes with a **$3^\circ$ tertiary hydrogen** atom are oxidized to corresponding $3^\circ$ alcohols.
   

$$
\mathrm{(CH_3)_3CH \xrightarrow{[O]\ /\ \text{alkaline } \mathrm{KMnO_4}} (CH_3)_3C-OH \quad (2\text{-Methylpropan-2-ol})}
$$

***

### 4.4 Aromatization (Reforming) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Straight-chain alkanes containing $6$ to $8$ carbon atoms, when heated under high pressure in the presence of supported oxides of transition metals, undergo concurrent cyclization and dehydrogenation to yield aromatic hydrocarbons.

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-(CH_2)_4-CH_3 \xrightarrow[773\text{ K},\ 10-20\text{ atm}]{\mathrm{Cr_2O_3} \text{ or } \mathrm{V_2O_5} \text{ or } \mathrm{Mo_2O_3} \text{ on } \mathrm{Al_2O_3}} C_6H_6 \ (\text{Benzene}) + 4H_2 \uparrow}
$$

* $n$-Heptane under identical conditions produces **Toluene** ($\mathrm{C_6H_5-CH_3}$).
* $n$-Octane produces an isomeric mixture of **$o$-, $m$-, $p$-Xylenes** and **Ethylbenzene**.

***

### 4.5 Isomerization ⭐⭐⭐
Heating $n$-alkanes with anhydrous aluminium chloride ($\mathrm{AlCl_3}$) and hydrogen chloride ($\mathrm{HCl}$) gas transforms them into branched-chain isomers.

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3 \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{Anhydrous } \mathrm{AlCl_3} / \mathrm{HCl}} CH_3-\overset{\overset{\mathrm{CH_3}}{|}}{\mathrm{CH}}-\mathrm{CH_3}}
$$

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-(CH_2)_4-CH_3 \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{Anhydrous } \mathrm{AlCl_3} / \mathrm{HCl}} CH_3-\overset{\overset{\mathrm{CH_3}}{|}}{\mathrm{CH}}-\mathrm{CH_2-CH_2-CH_3} + \mathrm{CH_3-CH_2}-\overset{\overset{\mathrm{CH_3}}{|}}{\mathrm{CH}}-\mathrm{CH_2-CH_3}}
$$

***

### 4.6 Pyrolysis / Cracking ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Thermal decomposition of higher alkanes into smaller hydrocarbons (alkanes, alkenes, and hydrogen gas) at high temperatures in the absence of air.

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_{14} \xrightarrow{773\text{ K}} C_6H_{12} + H_2 \quad (\text{Dehydrogenation})}
$$

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_{14} \xrightarrow{773\text{ K}} C_4H_8 + C_2H_6 \quad (\text{Chain Scission})}
$$

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_{14} \xrightarrow{773\text{ K}} C_3H_6 + C_2H_4 + CH_4}
$$

Mechanism proceeds via free-radical cracking along carbon-carbon $\sigma$-bonds.

***

### 4.7 Reaction with Steam ⭐⭐⭐
Methane reacts with steam at $1273\text{ K}$ in the presence of a nickel catalyst to generate industrial synthesis gas (water gas).

$$
\mathrm{CH_4(g) + H_2O(g) \xrightarrow[\Delta,\ 1273\text{ K}]{\mathrm{Ni}} CO(g) + 3H_2(g) \quad (\text{Syngas / Synthesis Gas})}
$$

# SECTION 5: Conformations of Alkanes

Because the electron distribution of a carbon-carbon single $\sigma$-bond is cylindrically symmetrical along the internuclear axis, rotation around the $\mathrm{C-C}$ bond is not completely restricted. This rotation produces different three-dimensional spatial arrangements termed **conformational isomers (conformers or rotamers)**.

However, rotation around a single bond is not entirely free; it is hindered by a small energy barrier of **$1\text{ to }20\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$**, known as the **torsional barrier**, caused by weak repulsive interactions between adjacent bonds.

***

### 5.1 Representations: Sawhorse & Newman Projections ⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### 1. Sawhorse Projections
The molecule is viewed from an oblique perspective along the $\mathrm{C-C}$ bond axis. The lower-left carbon represents the front carbon, while the upper-right represents the back carbon.

```
       SAWHORSE PROJECTIONS OF ETHANE

      H           H               H
       \         /                 \
        C-------C                   C-------C--- H
       / \     / \                 / \     /
      H   H   H   H               H   H   H
      
       Eclipsed                       Staggered
```

#### 2. Newman Projections
The molecule is viewed directly end-on along the $\mathrm{C-C}$ axis:
* The front carbon is represented by a **point (dot)** where three bonds radiate at $120^\circ$.
* The back carbon is represented by a **circle**, with its bonds radiating outwards from the circumference.

```
        NEWMAN PROJECTIONS OF ETHANE

           H                                 H
         /   \                             /   \
        C     H                           C     H
      / | \                             / | \
     H  H  H                           H  |  H
                                          H
     Eclipsed                           Staggered
(Dihedral Angle θ = 0°)            (Dihedral Angle θ = 60°)
```

***

### 5.2 Conformations of Ethane ($\mathrm{CH_3-CH_3}$) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
The rotation of one methyl group relative to the other produces an infinite number of conformations between the two extremes:

1. **Eclipsed Conformation ($\theta = 0^\circ$):** 
   Hydrogen atoms on adjacent carbons are positioned as close to each other as possible. The electron clouds of the $\mathrm{C-H}$ $\sigma$-bonds experience maximum electrostatic repulsion.
2. **Staggered Conformation ($\theta = 60^\circ, 180^\circ, 300^\circ$):** 
   Hydrogen atoms are located as far apart in space as possible, minimizing bond-pair/bond-pair interactions.
3. **Skew Conformations:** Any intermediate conformation between eclipsed and staggered ($0^\circ < \theta < 60^\circ$).

#### Types of Strain:
* **Torsional Strain:** Repulsive destabilization caused by electrostatic interaction between electron clouds of adjacent $\mathrm{C-H}$ $\sigma$-bonds in the eclipsed position.
* **Steric Strain (van der Waals Strain):** Destabilization resulting when bulky groups are forced closer than their combined van der Waals radii. In ethane, steric strain between small hydrogen atoms is negligible; instability in the eclipsed form is driven almost entirely by **torsional strain**.

#### Quantitative Energy Comparison:
* The potential energy of the eclipsed form is higher than that of the staggered form by:
  

$$
\Delta E = 12.55\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}\ (3.0\text{ kcal}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1})
$$

* Because each pair of eclipsed $\mathrm{C-H}$ bonds accounts for one-third of the total torsional strain:
  

$$
\text{Energy cost per eclipsed } \mathrm{H/H} \text{ interaction} \approx \frac{12.55}{3} \approx 4.18\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}\ (1.0\text{ kcal}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1})
$$

* At room temperature ($298\text{ K}$), the average thermal kinetic energy of collision is:
  

$$
RT \approx 2.49\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}
$$

  Because thermal energy readily overcomes this small $12.55\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$ energy barrier (millions of rotations occur per second), ethane conformers **cannot be separated or isolated** under ordinary conditions.

$$
\boxed{\text{Order of Stability: } \text{Staggered} > \text{Skew} > \text{Eclipsed}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Order of Potential Energy: } \text{Eclipsed} > \text{Skew} > \text{Staggered}}
$$

```
POTENTIAL ENERGY PROFILE OF ETHANE:

 (kJ/mol)

         |     /  \           /  \           /  \
         |    /    \         /    \         /    \
      0 -|---/------\-------/------\-------/------\-----> Dihedral Angle (θ)
            0°      60°    120°   180°   240°   300°  360°

```

### 5.3 Conformations of Butane ($\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3}$) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Rotation about the central $\mathrm{C_2-C_3}$ single bond generates four characteristic conformational points of interest:

```
                  NEWMAN PROJECTIONS ALONG C2-C3 OF BUTANE

       CH₃                                CH₃                             CH₃
     /     \                            /     \                         /     \
    H       CH₃                        H       H                       H       H
  / | \                              / | \                           / | \
 H  H  H                            H  CH₃ H                        H  H  CH₃
  I. Fully Eclipsed                II. Gauche (Skew)              III. Partially Eclipsed
     (θ = 0°)                          (θ = 60°)                       (θ = 120°)

                                           CH₃
                                         /     \
                                        H       H
                                      / | \
                                     H  CH₃ H
                                    IV. Anti (Staggered)
                                         (θ = 180°)
```

1. **Fully Eclipsed ($\theta = 0^\circ$):** Both bulky methyl groups ($-\mathrm{CH_3}$) are completely aligned with each other. Suffers maximum **torsional strain** plus maximum **steric repulsion**. Maximum potential energy state ($\approx +19\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$ relative to the anti conformation).
2. **Gauche / Skew ($\theta = 60^\circ, 300^\circ$):** Staggered arrangement where methyl groups are separated by a dihedral angle of $60^\circ$. Has zero torsional strain, but retains slight steric repulsion between methyl groups ($\approx +3.8\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$ above anti).
3. **Partially Eclipsed ($\theta = 120^\circ, 240^\circ$):** Hydrogen atoms eclipse methyl groups ($\mathrm{CH_3/H}$ interactions). Experiences substantial torsional strain ($\approx +16\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$ above anti).
4. **Anti / Trans Conformation ($\theta = 180^\circ$):** The two bulky methyl groups are located at the maximum possible spatial separation on opposite sides of the $\mathrm{C-C}$ bond. Both torsional strain and steric strain are minimized.

$$
\boxed{\text{Order of Relative Stability: } \text{Anti} > \text{Gauche} > \text{Partially Eclipsed} > \text{Fully Eclipsed}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Order of Potential Energy: } \text{Fully Eclipsed} > \text{Partially Eclipsed} > \text{Gauche} > \text{Anti}}
$$

### Problem 1: Monochlorination Product Analysis & Isomer Quantification ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
**Problem Statement:** An alkane with molecular formula $\mathrm{C_5H_{12}}$ gives:
1. Only one monochloro derivative upon photochemical chlorination.
2. Three isomeric monochloro derivatives.
3. Four isomeric monochloro derivatives.  
Identify the IUPAC name and structure of the alkane in each case.

#### Step-by-Step Solution:
The alkane has the formula $\mathrm{C_5H_{12}}$ ($n=5$), meaning it is one of three pentane chain isomers:

* **Case 1: Gives only ONE monochloro derivative.**
  * For an alkane to give only one monochloro derivative, all its hydrogen atoms must be chemically and structurally equivalent.
  * Consider **2,2-Dimethylpropane (Neopentane)**:
    

$$
\mathrm{C(CH_3)_4}
$$

    All twelve hydrogen atoms reside on four equivalent primary methyl groups attached to a central quaternary carbon.
  * Reaction:
    

$$
\mathrm{C(CH_3)_4 + Cl_2 \xrightarrow{h\nu} (CH_3)_3C-CH_2Cl + HCl \quad (1\text{-Chloro-2,2-dimethylpropane})}
$$

  * **Result:** **2,2-Dimethylpropane**

* **Case 2: Gives THREE isomeric monochloro derivatives.**
  * Consider **$n$-Pentane**:
    

$$
\mathrm{\overset{1}{C}H_3-\overset{2}{C}H_2-\overset{3}{C}H_2-\overset{2}{C}H_2-\overset{1}{C}H_3}
$$

    There are three chemically distinct sets of hydrogens:
    1. C1 and C5 hydrogens ($6\times 1^\circ\text{ H}$) $\to$ 1-Chloropentane
    2. C2 and C4 hydrogens ($4\times 2^\circ\text{ H}$) $\to$ 2-Chloropentane
    3. C3 hydrogens ($2\times 2^\circ\text{ H}$) $\to$ 3-Chloropentane
  * **Result:** **$n$-Pentane (Pentane)**

* **Case 3: Gives FOUR isomeric monochloro derivatives.**
  * Consider **2-Methylbutane (Isopentane)**:
    

$$
\mathrm{\overset{1}{C}H_3-\overset{2}{C}H(CH_3)-\overset{3}{C}H_2-\overset{4}{C}H_3}
$$

    All four carbon environments are non-equivalent:
    1. Chlorination at C1: $\mathrm{CH_2Cl-CH(CH_3)-CH_2-CH_3}$ (1-Chloro-2-methylbutane)
    2. Chlorination at C2: $\mathrm{CH_3-CCl(CH_3)-CH_2-CH_3}$ (2-Chloro-2-methylbutane)
    3. Chlorination at C3: $\mathrm{CH_3-CH(CH_3)-CHCl-CH_3}$ (2-Chloro-3-methylbutane)
    4. Chlorination at C4: $\mathrm{CH_3-CH(CH_3)-CH_2-CH_2Cl}$ (1-Chloro-3-methylbutane)
  * **Result:** **2-Methylbutane**

### Problem 2: Kolbe's Electrolysis Yield Calculation ⭐⭐⭐⭐
**Problem Statement:** A continuous current of $5.0\text{ A}$ is passed for $965\text{ s}$ through an aqueous solution of potassium propanoate ($\mathrm{CH_3CH_2COOK}$). Assuming $100\%$ current efficiency, calculate:
1. The volume of alkane gas evolved at the anode at STP ($T = 273.15\text{ K}$, $P = 1\text{ bar}$).
2. The total volume of all gases evolved at both electrodes combined at STP.

#### Step-by-Step Solution:

##### Step 1: Calculate Total Charge Passed ($Q$)

$$
Q = I \times t = 5.0\text{ A} \times 965\text{ s} = 4825\text{ C}
$$

##### Step 2: Calculate Number of Moles of Electrons Transferred ($n_e$)

$$
n_e = \frac{Q}{F} = \frac{4825\text{ C}}{96500\text{ C}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}} = 0.050\text{ mol of } e^-
$$

##### Step 3: Analyze Stoichiometry at Anode
The anodic coupling of propanoate ions yields $n$-butane:

$$
\mathrm{2CH_3CH_2COO^- \longrightarrow CH_3CH_2-CH_2CH_3 + 2CO_2 + 2e^-}
$$

From the stoichiometry:

$$
2\text{ mol of } e^- \equiv 1\text{ mol of } \mathrm{C_4H_{10}} \equiv 2\text{ mol of } \mathrm{CO_2}
$$

$$
\text{Moles of } n\text{-butane } (n_{\mathrm{butane}}) = \frac{0.050}{2} = 0.025\text{ mol}
$$

$$
\text{Moles of } \mathrm{CO_2} (n_{\mathrm{CO_2}}) = 0.050\text{ mol}
$$

Volume of $n$-butane at STP ($22.7\text{ L}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$ per IUPAC STP standard of $1\text{ bar}$):

$$
V_{\mathrm{butane}} = 0.025\text{ mol} \times 22.7\text{ L}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1} = \mathbf{0.5675\text{ L}} \quad (\text{or } 560\text{ mL at traditional } 1\text{ atm})
$$

##### Step 4: Analyze Stoichiometry at Cathode

$$
\mathrm{2H_2O + 2e^- \longrightarrow H_2\uparrow + 2OH^-}
$$

$$
\text{Moles of } \mathrm{H_2} (n_{\mathrm{H_2}}) = \frac{0.050}{2} = 0.025\text{ mol}
$$

##### Step 5: Total Volume of All Gases Evolved at Both Electrodes

$$
\text{Total moles of gas } (n_{\text{total}}) = n_{\mathrm{butane}} + n_{\mathrm{CO_2}} + n_{\mathrm{H_2}} = 0.025 + 0.050 + 0.025 = 0.100\text{ mol}
$$

$$
V_{\text{total}} = 0.100\text{ mol} \times 22.7\text{ L}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1} = \mathbf{2.27\text{ L}}
$$

### Problem 3: Quantitative Regioselectivity (Chlorination vs. Bromination) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
**Problem Statement:** Calculate the percentage composition of products obtained from the monochlorination versus monobromination of 2-methylpropane (isobutane) at $298\text{ K}$, given the relative reaction rates:
* Chlorination: $1^\circ\text{ H} = 1.0$, $3^\circ\text{ H} = 5.0$
* Bromination: $1^\circ\text{ H} = 1$, $3^\circ\text{ H} = 1600$

#### Step-by-Step Solution:
Structure of 2-methylpropane: $\mathrm{CH(CH_3)_3}$.
* Number of primary ($1^\circ$) hydrogen atoms = $9$ (three $-\mathrm{CH_3}$ groups).
* Number of tertiary ($3^\circ$) hydrogen atoms = $1$ (one $\equiv\mathrm{C-H}$ group).

##### A. Photochemical Chlorination:

$$
\text{Relative yield of } 1^\circ \text{ product (1-Chloro-2-methylpropane)} = (\text{No. of } 1^\circ\text{ H}) \times (\text{Reactivity of } 1^\circ\text{ H}) = 9 \times 1.0 = 9.0
$$

$$
\text{Relative yield of } 3^\circ \text{ product (2-Chloro-2-methylpropane)} = (\text{No. of } 3^\circ\text{ H}) \times (\text{Reactivity of } 3^\circ\text{ H}) = 1 \times 5.0 = 5.0
$$

$$
\text{Total relative yield} = 9.0 + 5.0 = 14.0
$$

$$
\%\text{ of } 1^\circ \text{ Alkyl Halide} = \left(\frac{9.0}{14.0}\right) \times 100 = \mathbf{64.3\%}
$$

$$
\%\text{ of } 3^\circ \text{ Alkyl Halide} = \left(\frac{5.0}{14.0}\right) \times 100 = \mathbf{35.7\%}
$$

*(Even though $3^\circ$ H is 5 times more reactive, the primary product dominates statistically due to the $9:1$ hydrogen ratio).*

##### B. Photochemical Bromination:

$$
\text{Relative yield of } 1^\circ \text{ product} = 9 \times 1 = 9
$$

$$
\text{Relative yield of } 3^\circ \text{ product} = 1 \times 1600 = 1600
$$

$$
\text{Total relative yield} = 9 + 1600 = 1609
$$

$$
\%\text{ of } 1^\circ \text{ Alkyl Halide} = \left(\frac{9}{1609}\right) \times 100 = \mathbf{0.56\%}
$$

$$
\%\text{ of } 3^\circ \text{ Alkyl Halide} = \left(\frac{1600}{1609}\right) \times 100 = \mathbf{99.44\%}
$$

*(Bromination is highly regioselective for the $3^\circ$ position).*

# SECTION 7: Examiner Traps & Common Student Pitfalls

1. **The Soda-Lime Carbon Count Trap:**
   * *Trap:* When asked for the alkane produced from sodium propanoate ($\mathrm{CH_3CH_2COONa}$) via soda-lime decarboxylation, students often incorrectly answer propane ($\mathrm{C_3H_8}$).
   * *Correction:* Soda-lime decarboxylation strips the carboxyl group carbon as $\mathrm{Na_2CO_3}$. An acid salt with $n$ carbons produces an alkane with **$(n-1)$ carbons**. Sodium propanoate ($3$ carbons) yields **ethane** ($2$ carbons).

2. **The "Methane from Wurtz / Kolbe" Trap:**
   * *Trap:* Questions asking for reagents to synthesize methane using Wurtz or Kolbe methods.
   * *Correction:* Neither the Wurtz reaction nor Kolbe’s electrolysis can synthesize methane ($\mathrm{CH_4}$) because both involve bimolecular radical/carbanionic coupling, yielding products with at least two carbon atoms ($n \ge 2$).

3. **Kolbe's Electrolysis Solution pH Drift:**
   * *Trap:* Assuming the electrolyte remains neutral during Kolbe's electrolysis.
   * *Correction:* Because protons ($\mathrm{H^+}$) discharge at the cathode as $\mathrm{H_2(g)}$, hydroxide ions ($\mathrm{OH^-}$) accumulate in the solution. The electrolyte solution becomes alkaline, and its **$\mathrm{pH}$ increases ($>7$)**.

4. **Torsional vs. Steric Strain Confusion in Ethane:**
   * *Trap:* Explaining the instability of eclipsed ethane as steric hindrance between hydrogen atoms.
   * *Correction:* Hydrogen atoms have small van der Waals radii ($r_{\text{vdW}} \approx 120\text{ pm}$), and the internuclear distance between eclipsed hydrogens is roughly $230\text{ pm}$, well outside the threshold of severe steric collision. The instability is almost exclusively caused by **torsional strain** (repulsion between the localized $\mathrm{C-H}$ bonding electron pairs).

5. **Wurtz Reaction Medium:**
   * *Trap:* Omitting "dry" or using wet/aqueous ether in Wurtz reaction schemes.
   * *Correction:* If trace moisture is present, sodium vigorously hydrolyzes into $\mathrm{NaOH}$ and $\mathrm{H_2}$, consuming the metal and protonating any organosodium intermediate to yield a reduced alkane rather than a coupled alkane.

# 1. Structure and Bonding in Alkenes

### Pedagogical Theory & Concepts
*Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐*

Alkenes are unsaturated hydrocarbons containing at least one carbon-carbon double bond ($C=C$). They are characterized by the general formula:

$$
\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n} \quad (n \ge 2)
$$

They are historically referred to as **olefins** (Latin: *oleum* = oil, *facere* = to make) because lower members like ethene ($\mathrm{C_2H_4}$) react with halogens like chlorine or bromine to form oily liquids (e.g., 1,2-dichloroethane).

***

### Orbital Architecture of the Carbon-Carbon Double Bond
Consider the simplest alkene, **ethene** ($\mathrm{H_2C=CH_2}$):

1. **Hybridization State:**
   Each carbon atom undergoes $\mathrm{sp^2}$ hybridization by mixing one $2\mathrm{s}$ and two $2\mathrm{p}$ orbitals ($2\mathrm{p}_x, 2\mathrm{p}_y$), leaving one unhybridized pure $2\mathrm{p}_z$ orbital perpendicular to the hybrid orbital plane.
2. **Sigma ($\sigma$) Skeleton:**
   - One $\mathrm{sp^2}$ hybrid orbital of carbon overlaps axially (head-on) with an $\mathrm{sp^2}$ hybrid orbital of the adjacent carbon, forming a localized $\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{C}$ $\sigma$-bond.
   - The remaining two $\mathrm{sp^2}$ hybrid orbitals on each carbon overlap axially with the $1\mathrm{s}$ orbitals of two hydrogen atoms to yield four $\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{H}$ $\sigma$-bonds.
   - **Geometry:** Trigonal planar around each carbon atom.
   - **Theoretical Bond Angles:** $\angle \mathrm{H}-\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{H} \approx 116.6^\circ$, $\angle \mathrm{C}-\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{H} \approx 121.7^\circ$. (Deviation from the idealized $120^\circ$ occurs due to greater repulsive interaction between the electron-dense $\mathrm{C=C}$ double bond and the $\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{H}$ single bond pair compared to $\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{H}/\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{H}$ repulsion).
3. **Pi ($\pi$) Bond:**
   - The two unhybridized $2\mathrm{p}_z$ orbitals on adjacent carbons align parallel to each other and perpendicular to the molecular plane, overlapping sideways (laterally) to form a $\pi$-bond.
   - The resulting $\pi$-electron cloud resides as two lobes: one above and one below the internuclear plane of the atoms.

```
          H         H
           \       /
            C === C      <-- Planar Framework (All 6 atoms in one plane)
           /       \
          H         H
```

***

### Key Metric Comparison: Ethane vs. Ethene

| Parameter | Ethane ($\mathrm{C_2H_6}$) | Ethene ($\mathrm{C_2H_4}$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hybridization of $\mathrm{C}$** | $\mathrm{sp^3}$ ($25\%\text{ s-character}$) | $\mathrm{sp^2}$ ($33.33\%\text{ s-character}$) |
| **$\mathrm{C-C}$ Bond Length** | $154\text{ pm}\ (1.54\ \text{Å})$ | $134\text{ pm}\ (1.34\ \text{Å})$ |
| **$\mathrm{C-H}$ Bond Length** | $110\text{ pm}$ | $108\text{ pm}$ |
| **$\mathrm{C-C}$ Bond Enthalpy** | $348\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$ | $681\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$ |
| **Calculated $\pi$-Bond Strength** | — | $681 - 348 = 333\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$ |

$$
\boxed{\text{Bond Strength: } \sigma\text{-bond } (348\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}) > \pi\text{-bond } (333\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1})}
$$

> **Key NCERT Takeaway:** The $\pi$-bond is weaker than the $\sigma$-bond because sideways atomic overlap is inherently less effective than head-on axial overlap. The loosely held $\pi$-electron cloud is exposed above and below the plane, rendering alkenes inherently vulnerable to attack by **electrophiles** (electron-seeking reagents).

***

# 2. Geometrical Isomerism (Stereoisomerism)

### Pedagogical Theory & Concepts
*Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐*

Stereoisomers have the same structural formula and connectivity but differ in the spatial arrangement of their atoms or groups. Geometrical isomerism in alkenes arises from **hindered or restricted rotation** about the carbon-carbon double bond.

***

### The Origin of Restricted Rotation
- Free rotation around a single $\mathrm{C-C}$ $\sigma$-bond is unrestricted at room temperature ($298\text{ K}$) because axial overlap is independent of rotation.
- In contrast, rotation around a $\mathrm{C=C}$ bond requires breaking the sideways overlap of the parallel $2\mathrm{p}_z$ orbitals. This requires approximately $333\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$ of energy, which is not available under ambient thermal conditions.
- Hence, the relative positions of groups attached to the double-bonded carbons remain locked.

***

### Strict Criteria for Geometrical Isomerism
For an alkene of formula $\mathrm{R_1R_2C=CR_3R_4}$ to exhibit geometrical isomerism:
1. Restricted rotation must be present (e.g., $C=C$ or a ring system).
2. **Individuality Rule:** Each individual carbon of the double bond must hold **two distinct groups**:

$$
\mathrm{R}_1 \ne \mathrm{R}_2 \quad \text{and} \quad \mathrm{R}_3 \ne \mathrm{R}_4
$$

*(Note: It is permissible for $\mathrm{R}_1$ to equal $\mathrm{R}_3$ or $\mathrm{R}_4$, provided both groups on the same carbon atom are non-identical).*

```
    a         a           a         b
     \       /             \       /
      C === C               C === C
     /       \             /       \
    b         b           b         a
      cis-isomer            trans-isomer
```

- **cis-isomer:** Identical (or priority) groups reside on the **same side** of the double bond.
- **trans-isomer:** Identical (or priority) groups reside on **opposite sides** of the double bond.

***

### Physical Property Discrepancies: cis vs. trans

Consider **cis-but-2-ene** and **trans-but-2-ene**:

```
      H3C       CH3             H3C       H
         \     /                   \     /
          C = C                     C = C
         /     \                   /     \
        H       H                 H       CH3
      cis-but-2-ene              trans-but-2-ene
      Dipole: μ = 0.33 D         Dipole: μ = 0 D
      b.p. = 277 K (4 °C)        b.p. = 274 K (1 °C)
      m.p. = 134 K (-139 °C)     m.p. = 167 K (-106 °C)
```

#### 1. Dipole Moment ($\mu$)
- Methyl groups are weakly electron-donating via inductive effects ($+I$).
- In **cis-but-2-ene**, the two individual $\mathrm{C_{sp^3}-C_{sp^2}}$ bond dipole vectors point in the same general direction; their components reinforce one another:

$$
\vec{\mu}_{\text{cis}} = \vec{\mu}_1 + \vec{\mu}_2 \ne \mathbf{0} \quad (\mu \approx 0.33\ \text{D})
$$

- In **trans-but-2-ene**, the bond vectors point in equal and opposite directions, canceling each other completely by vector addition:

$$
\vec{\mu}_{\text{trans}} = \vec{\mu}_1 + (-\vec{\mu}_1) = \mathbf{0} \quad (\mu = 0\ \text{D})
$$

#### 2. Boiling Point ($T_{\text{b}}$)
- Boiling point depends on the strength of intermolecular forces in the liquid state.
- Because $\mu_{\text{cis}} > \mu_{\text{trans}}$, the cis-isomer exhibits stronger permanent dipole-dipole attractions in addition to London dispersion forces.
- Consequently, the boiling point of the cis-isomer is slightly higher:
  

$$
T_{\text{b}}(\text{cis-but-2-ene}) = 277\ \text{K} > T_{\text{b}}(\text{trans-but-2-ene}) = 274\ \text{K}
$$

#### 3. Melting Point ($T_{\text{m}}$)
- Melting point depends on how efficiently molecules pack into a solid crystal lattice.
- The trans-isomer has a higher degree of centrosymmetry and linearity, packing more tightly into the crystal lattice with fewer void spaces.
- As a result, its lattice energy is significantly higher:
  

$$
T_{\text{m}}(\text{trans-but-2-ene}) = 167\ \text{K} > T_{\text{m}}(\text{cis-but-2-ene}) = 134\ \text{K}
$$

#### 4. Solubility in Polar Solvents
- Driven by the "like dissolves like" principle: the more polar cis-isomer dissolves more readily in polar solvents (e.g., ethanol, water).

$$
\boxed{T_{\text{b}}: \text{cis} > \text{trans} \qquad T_{\text{m}}: \text{trans} \gg \text{cis} \qquad \mu: \text{cis} > \text{trans} \qquad \text{Stability}: \text{trans} > \text{cis}}
$$

*(Stability Note: The trans-isomer is thermodynamically more stable than the cis-isomer due to minimized steric repulsion between bulky alkyl groups across the double bond).*

# 3. Preparation of Alkenes

***

## 3.1 From Alkyl Halides (Dehydrohalogenation)
*Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐*

When an alkyl halide (haloalkane) containing a $\beta$-hydrogen atom is heated with a concentrated alcoholic solution of potassium hydroxide ($\text{alc. }\mathrm{KOH}$), it undergoes an **elimination ($\beta$-elimination)** reaction: a hydrogen halide ($\mathrm{HX}$) is cleaved, generating an alkene.

### General Chemical Equation

$$
\mathrm{R}-\mathrm{CH}_2-\mathrm{CH}_2-\mathrm{X} + \mathrm{KOH}\ (\text{alc.}) \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathrm{R}-\mathrm{CH}=\mathrm{CH}_2 + \mathrm{KX} + \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}
$$

***

### Detailed E2 Mechanism
Dehydrohalogenation typically proceeds through a concerted, single-step **E2 (Elimination Bimolecular)** mechanism:

1. The strong base (ethoxide/hydroxide, $\mathrm{B}^- = \mathrm{C_2H_5O^-}$ or $\mathrm{OH^-}$) abstracts a proton from the $\beta$-carbon.
2. Simultaneously, the $\mathrm{C_\beta-H}$ bonding electron pair shifts to form the carbon-carbon $\pi$-bond.
3. Concurrently, the halide ion leaves from the $\alpha$-carbon with its bonding pair.

```
       B:⁻
       |
       H       (Anti-periplanar geometry)
       |
  --- C_β --- C_α ---
             |
             X:
```

$$
\mathrm{Rate} = k\,[\text{Alkyl Halide}][\text{Base}]
$$

- **Stereoelectronic Requirement:** The $\beta\text{-H}$ and $\alpha\text{-X}$ must adopt an **anti-periplanar** conformation (dihedral angle $\theta = 180^\circ$) to maximize orbital overlap between the breaking $\sigma$-bonds and the developing $\pi$-bond.

***

### Reactivity Trends
- **Halide Leaving Ability:** $\mathrm{R}-\mathrm{I} > \mathrm{R}-\mathrm{Br} > \mathrm{R}-\mathrm{Cl} \gg \mathrm{R}-\mathrm{F}$  
  *(Lower $\mathrm{C-X}$ bond dissociation enthalpy accelerates elimination).*
- **Alkyl Halide Structure:** $3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ$  
  *(Tertiary substrates produce more substituted, hyperconjugatively stabilized alkenes).*

***

### Regioselectivity: Zaitsev's (Saytzeff's) Rule
When an unsymmetrical alkyl halide can undergo elimination in two directions, it can yield isomeric alkenes. According to **Zaitsev's Rule**:

> *"In dehydrohalogenation reactions, the preferred product is that alkene which has the greater number of alkyl groups attached to the doubly bonded carbon atoms (i.e., the more substituted, more stable alkene)."*

#### Example: Dehydrohalogenation of 2-Bromobutane

$$
\mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}_2-\mathrm{CH}(\mathrm{Br})-\mathrm{CH}_3 \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{alc. }\mathrm{KOH}} \mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}=\mathrm{CH}-\mathrm{CH}_3\ (81\%) + \mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}_2-\mathrm{CH}=\mathrm{CH}_2\ (19\%)
$$

```
                           alc. KOH / Δ
CH3-CH2-CH(Br)-CH3 -------------------> CH3-CH=CH-CH3 (81%, Major, Saytzeff)
                                          + CH3-CH2-CH=CH2 (19%, Minor, Hofmann)
```

- **But-2-ene (Major):** Has $2$ methyl substituents on the double bond ($6\ \alpha\text{-hydrogens}$ contributing to stability via hyperconjugation).
- **But-1-ene (Minor):** Monosubstituted alkene ($2\ \alpha\text{-hydrogens}$).

***

## 3.2 From Vicinal Dihalides (Dehalogenation)
*Priority: ⭐⭐⭐*

Dihalides in which two halogen atoms are attached to adjacent carbon atoms are termed **vicinal dihalides** (*vic*-dihalides). Treating a vicinal dihalide with zinc dust in an alcoholic solvent (e.g., ethanol or methanol) under reflux yields an alkene through an elimination of $\mathrm{ZnX_2}$.

### General Chemical Equation

$$
\mathrm{R}-\mathrm{CH}(\mathrm{X})-\mathrm{CH}_2(\mathrm{X}) + \mathrm{Zn} \xrightarrow[\text{reflux}]{\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH}} \mathrm{R}-\mathrm{CH}=\mathrm{CH}_2 + \mathrm{ZnX}_2
$$

#### Example: Synthesis of Propene

$$
\mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}(\mathrm{Br})-\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{Br} + \mathrm{Zn} \xrightarrow{\Delta,\ \mathrm{CH_3OH}} \mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}=\mathrm{CH}_2 + \mathrm{ZnBr}_2
$$

***

## 3.3 From Alcohols (Acid-Catalyzed Dehydration)
*Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐*

Alcohols, when heated with concentrated protic mineral acids (such as concentrated $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ or concentrated $\mathrm{H_3PO_4}$), undergo dehydration (loss of a water molecule) to yield alkenes.

```
       H   OH
       |   |
  --- C - C ---  + H⁺ (conc. H2SO4, Δ) ---> --- C = C --- + H2O
       β   α
```

### Reaction Conditions and Regimes

$$
\text{Ethanol: } \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{OH} \xrightarrow[443\ \text{K}\ (170\ ^\circ\text{C})]{\text{conc. }\mathrm{H_2SO_4}} \mathrm{CH}_2=\mathrm{CH}_2 + \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}
$$

$$
\text{Propan-2-ol: } \mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}(\mathrm{OH})-\mathrm{CH}_3 \xrightarrow[358-363\ \text{K}]{\approx 85\%\ \mathrm{H_3PO_4}} \mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}=\mathrm{CH}_2 + \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}
$$

$$
\text{2-Methylpropan-2-ol: } (\mathrm{CH}_3)_3\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{OH} \xrightarrow[358\ \text{K}]{\approx 20\%\ \mathrm{H_3PO_4}} (\mathrm{CH}_3)_2\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CH}_2 + \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}
$$

### Relative Ease of Dehydration

$$
\boxed{3^\circ\text{ Alcohol} > 2^\circ\text{ Alcohol} > 1^\circ\text{ Alcohol}}
$$

***

### Step-by-Step E1 Mechanism (Illustrated with Propan-2-ol)

#### Step 1: Rapid, Reversible Protonation of the Hydroxyl Group
The lone pair on the oxygen of the alcohol attacks the proton ($\mathrm{H^+}$) provided by the acid catalyst, converting the poor leaving group ($-\mathrm{OH}$) into an excellent leaving group ($-\mathrm{OH_2^+}$):

$$
\mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}(\mathrm{OH})-\mathrm{CH}_3 + \mathrm{H}^+ \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}\left(\overset{+}{\mathrm{O}}\mathrm{H}_2\right)-\mathrm{CH}_3
$$

#### Step 2: Cleavage of the $\mathrm{C-O}$ Bond to Form a Carbocation (Rate-Determining Step)
Heterolytic cleavage of the $\mathrm{C-O}$ bond eliminates a neutral water molecule, forming a planar, $\mathrm{sp^2}$-hybridized carbocation:

$$
\mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}\left(\overset{+}{\mathrm{O}}\mathrm{H}_2\right)-\mathrm{CH}_3 \xrightarrow[\text{slow}]{\text{r.d.s.}} \mathrm{CH}_3-\overset{+}{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{H}-\mathrm{CH}_3 + \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}
$$

*(Because this carbocation is formed in the rate-determining step, more stable carbocations lower the activation energy, explaining the $3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ$ reactivity order. Carbocation rearrangements such as 1,2-hydride or 1,2-methyl shifts can also occur here).*

#### Step 3: Deprotonation by Base to Yield the Alkene
A weak conjugate base ($\mathrm{H_2O}$ or $\mathrm{HSO_4^-}$) abstracts a $\beta$-proton from the carbocation, shifting the $\sigma$-electrons to form the carbon-carbon $\pi$-bond and regenerating the acid catalyst:

$$
\mathrm{CH}_3-\overset{+}{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{H}-\mathrm{CH}_2-\mathrm{H} + \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} \xrightarrow{\text{fast}} \mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}=\mathrm{CH}_2 + \mathrm{H}_3\mathrm{O}^+
$$

***

## 3.4 From Alkynes (Controlled Stereoselective Hydrogenation)
*Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐*

Alkynes can be selectively reduced to alkenes by adding one molar equivalent of dihydrogen under controlled catalytic conditions. The reaction can be steered to produce either the **cis** or **trans** isomer based on the chosen reagent.

```
                   H2 / Lindlar's Catalyst
                      (Syn-addition)
                     ------------------>  cis-alkene
                    /
R - C ≡ C - R' ----
                    \
                     ------------------>  trans-alkene
                   Na / liq. NH3 (Birch)
                     (Anti-addition)
```

***

### (a) Synthesis of cis-Alkenes: Lindlar's Catalyst
Hydrogenation of an alkyne over **palladised charcoal partially deactivated (poisoned)** with quinoline or lead acetate/sulfur:

$$
\mathrm{R}-\mathrm{C}\equiv\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{R} + \mathrm{H}_2 \xrightarrow[\mathrm{Pd/CaCO_3\text{ or }Pd/BaSO_4}]{\text{Quinoline (Lindlar's)}} \begin{matrix} \mathrm{R} & & \mathrm{R} \\ & \mathrm{C}=\mathrm{C} & \\ \mathrm{H} & & \mathrm{H} \end{matrix} \quad (\text{cis-alkene})
$$

- **Mechanism:** Heterogeneous surface catalysis. Both hydrogen atoms are delivered from the solid metal surface to the **same face** of the alkyne triple bond (**syn-addition**).
- **Function of Poison:** Prevents over-hydrogenation to the alkane stage by moderating catalytic activity.

***

### (b) Synthesis of trans-Alkenes: Birch Reduction
Reduction using sodium or lithium metal dissolved in liquid ammonia at low temperature ($\approx 240\ \text{K}$):

$$
\mathrm{R}-\mathrm{C}\equiv\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{R} + 2\mathrm{Na} + 2\mathrm{NH}_3\ (\text{liq.}) \xrightarrow{240\ \text{K}} \begin{matrix} \mathrm{R} & & \mathrm{H} \\ & \mathrm{C}=\mathrm{C} & \\ \mathrm{H} & & \mathrm{R} \end{matrix} \quad (\text{trans-alkene}) + 2\mathrm{NaNH}_2
$$

- **Mechanism:** Involves single-electron transfer (SET) steps:
  1. $\mathrm{Na} \to \mathrm{Na}^+ + \mathrm{e}^-_{\text{ammoniated}}$
  2. Electron transfer to the alkyne produces a radical anion.
  3. The radical anion adopts a *trans* configuration to minimize electrostatic and steric repulsion between lone pairs and bulky alkyl groups.
  4. Subsequent protonation by $\mathrm{NH_3}$ produces the *trans*-alkene via **anti-addition**.

***

# 4. Addition Reactions of Alkenes

Because of their loosely held, nucleophilic $\pi$-electron clouds, alkenes predominantly undergo **Electrophilic Addition Reactions ($\mathrm{Ad_E}$)**.

***

## 4.1 Addition of Halogens ($\mathrm{X_2}$)
*Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐*

Alkenes react smoothly with chlorine ($\mathrm{Cl_2}$) or bromine ($\mathrm{Br_2}$) dissolved in an inert, non-polar solvent like carbon tetrachloride ($\mathrm{CCl_4}$) to yield **vicinal dihalides**.

### General Reaction

$$
\mathrm{R}-\mathrm{CH}=\mathrm{CH}_2 + \mathrm{Br}_2 \xrightarrow{\mathrm{CCl_4}} \mathrm{R}-\mathrm{CH}(\mathrm{Br})-\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{Br}
$$

***

### The Bromine Water Test (Test for Unsaturation)
When red-orange bromine solution in $\mathrm{CCl_4}$ is added dropwise to an alkene, the solution turns **colorless** as dibromoalkanes are formed.

$$
\mathrm{CH}_2=\mathrm{CH}_2\ (\text{colorless}) + \mathrm{Br}_2\ (\text{reddish-orange}) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{CCl_4}} \mathrm{Br}-\mathrm{CH}_2-\mathrm{CH}_2-\mathrm{Br}\ (\text{colorless})
$$

***

### Mechanism: Cyclic Halonium Ion & Anti-Stereospecific Addition
The addition of halogens proceeds through a cyclic intermediate rather than an open carbocation:

```
  C = C  +  Br-Br  --->  [   C --- C   ]⁺  +  Br⁻  --->  Br-C --- C-Br
                              \   /                          (Anti)
                                Br
                         (Cyclic Bromonium)
```

1. **Electrophilic Polarization:** As the non-polar $\mathrm{Br-Br}$ molecule nears the $\pi$-cloud, it is polar-induced into an electrophilic center ($\overset{\delta+}{\mathrm{Br}}-\overset{\delta-}{\mathrm{Br}}$).
2. **Formation of Cyclic Bromonium Ion:** The $\pi$-electrons attack the electrophilic bromine atom, displacing bromide ion ($\mathrm{Br^-}$). The attacking bromine uses its lone pairs to back-bond to both carbon atoms, forming a three-membered **cyclic bromonium ion**.
3. **Backside Nucleophilic Attack:** The bromide ion ($\mathrm{Br^-}$) attacks from the **opposite face** (anti-attack) to open the strained three-membered ring. This results in exclusive **anti-addition**.

***

## 4.2 Addition of Hydrogen Halides ($\mathrm{HX}$)
*Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐*

Alkenes react with hydrogen halides ($\mathrm{HI} > \mathrm{HBr} > \mathrm{HCl} \gg \mathrm{HF}$) to yield alkyl halides (haloalkanes).

### Symmetrical Alkenes
Both carbons of the double bond are equivalent; only a single product is possible:

$$
\mathrm{CH}_2=\mathrm{CH}_2 + \mathrm{HBr} \longrightarrow \mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{Br}
$$

$$
\mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}=\mathrm{CH}-\mathrm{CH}_3 + \mathrm{HBr} \longrightarrow \mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}_2-\mathrm{CH}(\mathrm{Br})-\mathrm{CH}_3
$$

***

### Unsymmetrical Alkenes and Markovnikov's Rule
When an unsymmetrical reagent ($\mathrm{HX}$) adds across an unsymmetrical alkene (e.g., propene), two constitutional isomers can form:

```
                         /---> CH3-CH(Br)-CH3  (2-Bromopropane, Major)
CH3-CH=CH2  +  HBr  ---
                         \---> CH3-CH2-CH2Br   (1-Bromopropane, Minor)
```

In 1869, Russian chemist Vladimir Markovnikov formulated an empirical rule:

> **Markovnikov's Rule (NCERT Definition):**  
> *"When an unsymmetrical reagent adds to an unsymmetrical alkene, the negative part of the addendum (reagent) gets attached to that carbon atom of the double bond which carries the lesser number of hydrogen atoms."*

***

### Modern Mechanistic Basis for Markovnikov's Rule

Electrophilic addition occurs in two distinct steps:

```
                                  H⁺ (slow)
   CH3-CH=CH2 ----------------------------------------------------->
                |                                                 |
                v                                                 v
   CH3-CH(+)-CH3 (Secondary, 2° C+)                 CH3-CH2-CH2(+) (Primary, 1° C+)
   [6 α-H via hyperconjugation: MORE STABLE]        [2 α-H via hyperconjugation: LESS STABLE]
                |                                                 |
                | Br⁻ (fast)                                      | Br⁻ (fast)
                v                                                 v
   CH3-CH(Br)-CH3 [MAJOR PRODUCT]                   CH3-CH2-CH2Br [MINOR PRODUCT]
```

#### Step 1: Electrophilic Attack by a Proton ($\mathrm{H^+}$) — Rate-Determining Step
The alkene $\pi$-system protonates to yield a carbocation intermediate:
- **Path A:** Protonation at $\mathrm{C-1}$ yields a secondary ($2^\circ$) carbocation:
  

$$
\mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}=\mathrm{CH}_2 + \mathrm{H}^+ \xrightarrow{\text{slow}} \mathrm{CH}_3-\overset{+}{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{H}-\mathrm{CH}_3 \quad (2^\circ\text{ carbocation})
$$

- **Path B:** Protonation at $\mathrm{C-2}$ yields a primary ($1^\circ$) carbocation:
  

$$
\mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}=\mathrm{CH}_2 + \mathrm{H}^+ \xrightarrow{\text{slow}} \mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}_2-\overset{+}{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{H}_2 \quad (1^\circ\text{ carbocation})
$$

**Hyperconjugation and Inductive Stabilization:**
- The $2^\circ$ carbocation has two flanking methyl groups offering $6\ \alpha\text{-hydrogens}$, allowing stabilization through six hyperconjugative canonical structures plus $+I$ inductive effects.
- The $1^\circ$ carbocation has only one ethyl group with $2\ \alpha\text{-hydrogens}$.
- Because the $2^\circ$ carbocation has lower activation energy ($\Delta G^\ddagger$), it forms at a substantially faster rate:

$$
\text{Carbocation Stability: } 3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ > \overset{+}{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{H}_3
$$

#### Step 2: Nucleophilic Attack by Halide ($\mathrm{X^-}$)
The halide ion rapidly attacks the carbocation center:

$$
\mathrm{CH}_3-\overset{+}{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{H}-\mathrm{CH}_3 + \mathrm{Br}^- \xrightarrow{\text{fast}} \mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}(\mathrm{Br})-\mathrm{CH}_3 \quad (\text{Major})
$$

***

## 4.3 Anti-Markovnikov Addition (Peroxide Effect / Kharasch Effect)
*Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐*

In 1933, M. S. Kharasch and F. R. Mayo observed that adding hydrogen bromide ($\mathrm{HBr}$) to an unsymmetrical alkene in the presence of organic peroxides ($\mathrm{R-O-O-R}$, such as benzoyl peroxide) gives an orientation opposite to Markovnikov's rule:

$$
\mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}=\mathrm{CH}_2 + \mathrm{HBr} \xrightarrow{(\mathrm{C_6H_5CO})_2\mathrm{O}_2} \mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}_2-\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{Br} \quad (\text{1-Bromopropane, Major})
$$

> **Important Limitation:** The peroxide effect is observed **strictly with $\mathrm{HBr}$**. It does not occur with $\mathrm{HF}$, $\mathrm{HCl}$, or $\mathrm{HI}$, which yield Markovnikov products even when peroxides are present.

***

### Step-by-Step Free-Radical Mechanism

#### 1. Chain Initiation
- **Homolysis of Benzoyl Peroxide:**
  

$$
(\mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_5\mathrm{COO})_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta \text{ or } h\nu} 2\,\mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_5\mathrm{COO}^\bullet \xrightarrow{-\,2\,\mathrm{CO}_2} 2\,\mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_5^\bullet \quad (\text{Phenyl radical})
$$

- **Generation of the Bromine Free Radical:**
  

$$
\mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_5^\bullet + \mathrm{H}-\mathrm{Br} \longrightarrow \mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_6 + \mathrm{Br}^\bullet
$$

#### 2. Chain Propagation

```
                            /---> CH3-C(•)H-CH2Br  (2° Radical, MORE STABLE)
CH3-CH=CH2  +  Br•  -------
                            \---> CH3-CH(Br)-CH2•  (1° Radical, LESS STABLE)
```

- **Attack by $\mathrm{Br}^\bullet$ on Propene:**  
  The electrophilic $\mathrm{Br}^\bullet$ radical attacks the terminal carbon ($\mathrm{C-1}$) to generate a secondary ($2^\circ$) free radical:

$$
\mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}=\mathrm{CH}_2 + \mathrm{Br}^\bullet \longrightarrow \mathrm{CH}_3-\dot{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{H}-\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{Br} \quad (2^\circ\text{ radical, more stable})
$$

  *(If it attacked $\mathrm{C-2}$, it would yield a primary radical, $\mathrm{CH_3-CH(Br)-\dot{C}H_2}$, which is less stable due to fewer hyperconjugative structures).*

- **Radical Abstraction from $\mathrm{HBr}$:**  
  The stable $2^\circ$ radical abstracts a hydrogen atom from an intact $\mathrm{HBr}$ molecule, forming the anti-Markovnikov product and regenerating a $\mathrm{Br}^\bullet$ radical to sustain the chain:

$$
\mathrm{CH}_3-\dot{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{H}-\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{Br} + \mathrm{H}-\mathrm{Br} \longrightarrow \mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}_2-\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{Br} + \mathrm{Br}^\bullet
$$

#### 3. Chain Termination
Radical pairs combine to terminate the reaction:

$$
\mathrm{Br}^\bullet + \mathrm{Br}^\bullet \longrightarrow \mathrm{Br}_2
$$

$$
\mathrm{R}^\bullet + \mathrm{Br}^\bullet \longrightarrow \mathrm{R}-\mathrm{Br}
$$

$$
\mathrm{R}^\bullet + \mathrm{R}^\bullet \longrightarrow \mathrm{R}-\mathrm{R}
$$

***

### Thermodynamic Explanation: Why Only $\mathrm{HBr}$?

A free-radical chain reaction is thermodynamically viable only when **both** propagation steps are exothermic ($\Delta H^\circ < 0$):

$$
\text{Step 1: } >\mathrm{C=C}< + \mathrm{X}^\bullet \longrightarrow >\dot{\mathrm{C}}-\mathrm{C}(\mathrm{X})<
$$

$$
\text{Step 2: } >\dot{\mathrm{C}}-\mathrm{C}(\mathrm{X})< + \mathrm{HX} \longrightarrow >\mathrm{CH}-\mathrm{C}(\mathrm{X})< + \mathrm{X}^\bullet
$$

| Hydrogen Halide ($\mathrm{HX}$) | Step 1 Enthalpy ($\Delta H^\circ_1$) | Step 2 Enthalpy ($\Delta H^\circ_2$) | Overall Outcome |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\mathrm{H-F}$ | Endothermic ($\mathrm{C-F}$ formed, but $\dots$) | Highly Endothermic ($+159\text{ kJ/mol}$) | **No chain:** $\mathrm{H-F}$ bond is too strong ($568\text{ kJ/mol}$) to break homolytically in Step 2. |
| $\mathrm{H-Cl}$ | Exothermic ($-101\text{ kJ/mol}$) | Endothermic ($+27\text{ kJ/mol}$) | **No chain:** $\mathrm{H-Cl}$ bond is too strong ($431\text{ kJ/mol}$); Step 2 is unfavorable. |
| $\mathrm{H-Br}$ | **Exothermic ($-42\text{ kJ/mol}$)** | **Exothermic ($-38\text{ kJ/mol}$)** | **Fast chain reaction:** Both steps are exothermic. |
| $\mathrm{H-I}$ | Endothermic ($+50\text{ kJ/mol}$) | Exothermic ($-63\text{ kJ/mol}$) | **No chain:** Step 1 is endothermic; $\mathrm{I}^\bullet$ radicals prefer dimerizing into $\mathrm{I}_2$ ($2\mathrm{I}^\bullet \to \mathrm{I}_2$). |

$$
\boxed{\text{Only in the case of }\mathrm{HBr}\text{ are both propagation steps exothermic!}}
$$

***

## 4.4 Addition of Sulfuric Acid ($\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$)
*Priority: ⭐⭐⭐*

Alkenes absorb cold, concentrated sulfuric acid via an electrophilic mechanism conforming strictly to **Markovnikov's Rule**, producing alkyl hydrogen sulfates:

$$
\mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}=\mathrm{CH}_2 + \mathrm{H}-\mathrm{OSO}_2\mathrm{OH} \xrightarrow{\text{Cold, conc.}} \mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}(\mathrm{OSO}_3\mathrm{H})-\mathrm{CH}_3 \quad (\text{Isopropyl hydrogen sulfate})
$$

Boiling with water hydrolyzes the alkyl hydrogen sulfate to an alcohol:

$$
\mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}(\mathrm{OSO}_3\mathrm{H})-\mathrm{CH}_3 + \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}(\mathrm{OH})-\mathrm{CH}_3 + \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{SO}_4
$$

This sequence provides an indirect route for the **Markovnikov hydration** of alkenes.

***

## 4.5 Addition of Water (Direct Acid-Catalyzed Hydration)
*Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐*

Treating an alkene with steam in the presence of an acid catalyst ($\text{dilute }\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ or $\mathrm{H_3PO_4}$) hydrates the double bond, yielding an alcohol in accordance with **Markovnikov's Rule**:

$$
\mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{C}(\mathrm{CH}_3)=\mathrm{CH}_2 + \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{H}^+} \mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{C}(\mathrm{OH})(\mathrm{CH}_3)-\mathrm{CH}_3 \quad (\text{2-Methylpropan-2-ol})
$$

***

## 4.6 Oxidation Reactions

### (a) Hydroxylation with Baeyer's Reagent (Test for Unsaturation)
*Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐*

Dilute, cold ($273-283\ \text{K}$), alkaline potassium permanganate ($\mathrm{KMnO_4}$) solution is known as **Baeyer's Reagent**:

$$
\mathrm{CH}_2=\mathrm{CH}_2 + \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} + [\mathrm{O}] \xrightarrow[\text{Cold, alkaline, } 273\ \text{K}]{\mathrm{KMnO_4}} \underset{\text{Ethane-1,2-diol (Glycol)}}{\mathrm{HO}-\mathrm{CH}_2-\mathrm{CH}_2-\mathrm{OH}} + \mathrm{MnO}_2\downarrow
$$

- **Visual Observation:** The deep purple color of $\mathrm{MnO_4^-}$ is discharged, yielding a dark brown precipitate of manganese dioxide ($\mathrm{MnO_2}$).
- **Stereochemical Course:** The reaction proceeds via a cyclic hypomanganate ester, giving exclusive **syn-dihydroxylation** (both $-\mathrm{OH}$ groups add to the same face).

***

### (b) Vigorous Oxidative Cleavage
*Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐*

Acidic or hot alkaline $\mathrm{KMnO_4}$ cleaves the carbon-carbon double bond completely:

```
  R-CH = CH-R'  +  [O]  ---(hot KMnO4/H⁺)--->  R-COOH  +  R'-COOH  (Acids)
  R(R')C = CH2   +  [O]  ---(hot KMnO4/H⁺)--->  R-CO-R' +  CO2  +  H2O
```

- $=\mathrm{CH}_2$ groups oxidize to $\mathrm{CO}_2$ and $\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}$.
- $=\mathrm{CH}-\mathrm{R}$ groups oxidize to carboxylic acid salts ($\mathrm{R}-\mathrm{COOH}$).
- $=\mathrm{C}(\mathrm{R}_1)(\mathrm{R}_2)$ groups oxidize to ketones ($\mathrm{R}_1-\mathrm{CO}-\mathrm{R}_2$).

#### Example: Oxidation of 2-Methylbut-2-ene

$$
\mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{C}(\mathrm{CH}_3)=\mathrm{CH}-\mathrm{CH}_3 + 3\,[\mathrm{O}] \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\mathrm{KMnO_4}/\mathrm{H}^+} \underset{\text{Propan-2-one}}{(\mathrm{CH}_3)_2\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{O}} + \underset{\text{Ethanoic acid}}{\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{COOH}}
$$

***

## 4.7 Ozonolysis
*Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐*

Ozonolysis involves treating an alkene with ozone ($\mathrm{O_3}$) to form an ozonide, followed by cleavage with zinc dust and water. It is a key analytical tool for locating carbon-carbon double bonds in unknown alkenes.

```
                  O3 / CCl4                     Zn / H2O
  >C = C<  --------------------> [ Ozonide ] ------------->  >C=O  +  O=C<  +  ZnO
                                (Cleavage)
```

### General Reaction Mechanism

```
      \   /                  \  O  /                     \       /
       C=C   +  O3  --->      C   C     ---(Zn/H2O)--->   C=O + O=C   + H2O2 (quenched)
      /   \                  / \ / \                     /       \
                            O---O---O
                            (Ozonide)
```

1. **Formation of Molozonide:** 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition of ozone across the alkene yields an unstable primary ozonide (molozonide).
2. **Rearrangement to Ozonide:** The molozonide rearranges into a stable bicyclic ozonide.
3. **Reductive Cleavage with $\mathrm{Zn / H_2O}$:**  
   The ozonide is cleaved by treatment with zinc dust and water. Zinc reduces the byproduct hydrogen peroxide ($\mathrm{H_2O_2}$) to water, preventing further oxidation of aldehydes to carboxylic acids:

$$
\mathrm{Zn} + \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2 \longrightarrow \mathrm{ZnO} + \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}
$$

#### Concrete Example: Ozonolysis of Propene

$$
\mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}=\mathrm{CH}_2 + \mathrm{O}_3 \xrightarrow{\mathrm{CH_2Cl_2}} \text{Propene Ozonide} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Zn}/\mathrm{H_2O}} \underset{\text{Ethanal}}{\mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CHO}} + \underset{\text{Methanal}}{\mathrm{HCHO}} + \mathrm{ZnO}
$$

# 5. High-Yield Application Problems & Exemplar Archetypes

### Problem 1: Alkene Identification via Ozonolysis
**Question:** An alkene '$\mathbf{A}$' on reductive ozonolysis with $\mathrm{O_3}$ followed by $\mathrm{Zn/H_2O}$ produces an equimolar mixture of **propan-2-one** and **ethanal**.  
(a) Deduce the structural formula and IUPAC name of alkene '$\mathbf{A}$'.  
(b) Does '$\mathbf{A}$' exhibit geometrical isomerism? Justify your answer.

**Solution:**  
**(a)** Write the structures of the carbonyl products with their oxygen atoms facing each other:
```
        CH3                 H
           \               /
            C = O  +  O = C
           /               \
        CH3                 CH3
     Propan-2-one          Ethanal
```
Remove both oxygen atoms and join the remaining fragments with a double bond:

$$
\boxed{(\mathrm{CH}_3)_2\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CH}-\mathrm{CH}_3} \quad \text{2-Methylbut-2-ene}
$$

**(b)** Analyze the groups attached to each double-bonded carbon atom:
- At $\mathrm{C-2}$: Both substituents are methyl groups ($-\mathrm{CH_3}$).
- According to the criteria for stereoisomerism, the two groups on each individual double-bonded carbon must be distinct:

$$
\mathrm{R}_1 \ne \mathrm{R}_2
$$

Because $\mathrm{C-2}$ holds two identical methyl groups, **2-methylbut-2-ene cannot exhibit geometrical (cis-trans) isomerism**.

***

### Problem 2: Carbocation Rearrangement in Addition
**Question:** Predict the major organic product obtained when **3,3-dimethylbut-1-ene** reacts with dry gaseous hydrogen chloride ($\mathrm{HCl}$).

**Solution:**  
This reaction proceeds via an electrophilic addition mechanism involving carbocation intermediates.

#### Step 1: Protonation by $\mathrm{H^+}$
Following Markovnikov's rule, the $\pi$-bond attacks $\mathrm{H^+}$ to generate the more stable secondary carbocation:

$$
\mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{C}(\mathrm{CH}_3)_2-\mathrm{CH}=\mathrm{CH}_2 + \mathrm{H}^+ \xrightarrow{\text{slow}} \mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{C}(\mathrm{CH}_3)_2-\overset{+}{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{H}-\mathrm{CH}_3 \quad (\text{Secondary, } 2^\circ\text{ carbocation})
$$

This $2^\circ$ carbocation has only **$1\ \alpha\text{-hydrogen}$** available for hyperconjugation from the adjacent methine position.

#### Step 2: 1,2-Methyl Shift (Wagner-Meerwein Rearrangement)
The adjacent quaternary carbon has two methyl groups. A 1,2-methyl shift generates a significantly more stable tertiary ($3^\circ$) carbocation:

```
      CH3                   CH3
      |                     |
  CH3-C — CH(+) - CH3 ---> CH3-C(+) — CH - CH3
      |                     |         |
      CH3                   CH3       CH3
   (2° Carbocation)       (3° Carbocation, 7 α-H atoms: Highly Stable)
```

#### Step 3: Nucleophilic Attack by $\mathrm{Cl^-}$
Chloride ion attacks the tertiary carbocation to yield the major product:

$$
\mathrm{CH}_3-\overset{+}{\mathrm{C}}(\mathrm{CH}_3)-\mathrm{CH}(\mathrm{CH}_3)_2 + \mathrm{Cl}^- \xrightarrow{\text{fast}} \boxed{\mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CCl}(\mathrm{CH}_3)-\mathrm{CH}(\mathrm{CH}_3)_2} \quad (\text{2-Chloro-2,3-dimethylbutane})
$$

*(Note: Direct capture of the un-rearranged $2^\circ$ carbocation gives 3-chloro-2,2-dimethylbutane as a minor product).*

***

### Problem 3: Contrasting Regiochemistry with $\mathrm{HBr}$
**Question:** An unsymmetrical alkene, **hex-1-ene**, is treated separately with:
1. $\mathrm{HBr}$ in the absence of peroxides.
2. $\mathrm{HBr}$ in the presence of benzoyl peroxide.
3. $\mathrm{HCl}$ in the presence of benzoyl peroxide.  
Write the structural formula and IUPAC name of the major product in each case.

**Solution:**
1. **$\mathrm{HBr}$ without peroxides:**  
   Proceeds through an electrophilic addition mechanism. Protonation forms the more stable secondary carbocation ($\mathrm{CH_3(CH_2)_3-\overset{+}{C}H-CH_3}$). Halide attack yields the Markovnikov product:
   

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{CH}_3(\mathrm{CH}_2)_3-\mathrm{CH}(\mathrm{Br})-\mathrm{CH}_3} \quad \text{2-Bromohexane}
$$

2. **$\mathrm{HBr}$ with benzoyl peroxide:**  
   Proceeds through a free-radical chain mechanism. The $\mathrm{Br}^\bullet$ radical attacks the terminal carbon to form the more stable secondary radical ($\mathrm{CH_3(CH_2)_3-\dot{C}H-CH_2Br}$). Hydrogen abstraction yields the anti-Markovnikov product:
   

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{CH}_3(\mathrm{CH}_2)_3-\mathrm{CH}_2-\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{Br}} \quad \text{1-Bromohexane}
$$

3. **$\mathrm{HCl}$ with benzoyl peroxide:**  
   Because the $\mathrm{H-Cl}$ bond dissociation enthalpy is high ($431\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$), Step 2 of the free-radical pathway is endothermic and does not occur. The reaction reverts to the ionic electrophilic addition pathway, yielding the Markovnikov product:
   

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{CH}_3(\mathrm{CH}_2)_3-\mathrm{CH}(\mathrm{Cl})-\mathrm{CH}_3} \quad \text{2-Chlorohexane}
$$

# 6. Examiner Traps & Common Mistakes

***

### 1. Reagent Confusion: $\text{alc. }\mathrm{KOH}$ vs. $\text{aq. }\mathrm{KOH}$
- **Trap:** Treating an alkyl halide with $\text{aq. }\mathrm{KOH}$ instead of $\text{alc. }\mathrm{KOH}$.
- **Correction:**
  - $\mathrm{KOH}\ (\text{aq.})$ contains hydrated hydroxide ions ($\mathrm{OH^-}$), which are weak bases but effective nucleophiles; they favor **nucleophilic substitution ($\mathrm{S_N2/S_N1}$)** to yield **alcohols**.
  - $\mathrm{KOH}\ (\text{alc.})$ in ethanol forms ethoxide ions ($\mathrm{C_2H_5O^-}$), which act as strong, bulky Brønsted-Lowry bases; they favor **$\beta$-elimination ($\mathrm{E2}$)** to yield **alkenes**.

***

### 2. Misapplying the Peroxide Effect to $\mathrm{HCl}$ or $\mathrm{HI}$
- **Trap:** Writing 1-chloropropane when propene reacts with $\mathrm{HCl}$ in the presence of peroxides.
- **Correction:** **The peroxide effect operates strictly for $\mathrm{HBr}$.** Both $\mathrm{HCl}$ and $\mathrm{HI}$ undergo ionic addition following Markovnikov's rule, even in the presence of peroxides.

***

### 3. Missing Carbocation Rearrangements
- **Trap:** Predicting 2-bromo-3-methylbutane as the exclusive product from 3-methylbut-1-ene and $\mathrm{HBr}$ by directly applying Markovnikov's rule.
- **Correction:** Whenever an ionic addition forms a carbocation, check whether an adjacent carbon can provide a more stable carbocation via a **1,2-hydride** or **1,2-methyl** shift:
  

$$
\mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}(\mathrm{CH}_3)-\mathrm{CH}=\mathrm{CH}_2 \xrightarrow{\mathrm{H}^+} \mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{CH}(\mathrm{CH}_3)-\overset{+}{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{H}-\mathrm{CH}_3 \xrightarrow{1,2\text{-H shift}} \mathrm{CH}_3-\overset{+}{\mathrm{C}}(\mathrm{CH}_3)-\mathrm{CH}_2-\mathrm{CH}_3
$$

  The major product is the tertiary halide: **2-bromo-2-methylbutane**.

***

### 4. Overlooking the Role of Zinc in Ozonolysis
- **Trap:** Writing carboxylic acids instead of aldehydes when an alkene is cleaved with $\mathrm{O_3}$ and $\mathrm{H_2O}$ without zinc.
- **Correction:**
  - **Reductive Ozonolysis ($\mathrm{Zn / H_2O}$ or $(\mathrm{CH_3)_2S}$):** Cleavage stops at aldehydes ($\mathrm{-CHO}$) and ketones ($>\mathrm{C=O}$) because zinc reduces the byproduct $\mathrm{H_2O_2}$.
  - **Oxidative Ozonolysis ($\mathrm{O_3 / H_2O}$ without zinc):** The byproduct $\mathrm{H_2O_2}$ oxidizes aldehydes to carboxylic acids ($\mathrm{-COOH}$).

***

### 5. Inverting Melting and Boiling Point Trends for Geometrical Isomers
- **Trap:** Stating that the cis-isomer has both a higher boiling point and a higher melting point than the trans-isomer.
- **Correction:**
  - **Boiling Point:** $\text{cis} > \text{trans}$ (governed by molecular dipole moments and dipole-dipole attractions).
  - **Melting Point:** $\text{trans} \gg \text{cis}$ (governed by molecular symmetry and crystal lattice packing efficiency).

### Section 1: Introduction, Nomenclature & Isomerism

#### 1.1 Fundamental Definition & General Formula ⭐⭐⭐
Alkynes are acyclic unsaturated hydrocarbons containing at least one carbon–carbon triple bond ($-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-$). They possess four hydrogen atoms fewer than the corresponding alkanes.

- **General Molecular Formula:** 
  

$$
\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n-2} \quad (n \ge 2)
$$

- **Homologous Series:** 
  - $n = 2: \text{C}_2\text{H}_2$ (Ethyne / Acetylene)
  - $n = 3: \text{C}_3\text{H}_4$ (Propyne / Methylacetylene)
  - $n = 4: \text{C}_4\text{H}_6$ (Butyne)

#### 1.2 IUPAC Nomenclature Rules ⭐⭐⭐⭐
1. **Principal Chain:** Select the longest continuous carbon chain containing the maximum number of $-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-$ bonds, regardless of whether it gives a shorter chain overall.
2. **Numbering:** Number from the end nearest to the triple bond:
   - If both a double bond ($=\text{ene}$) and a triple bond ($=\text{yne}$) are equidistant from chain ends, **double bond receives lower locant priority** (alphabetical precedence: *ene* before *yne*).
   - Suffix construction: Drop the terminal `e` of `ene` before `yne` $\rightarrow$ `-en-yne` (never `-eneyne`).

##### Exemplar Nomenclature Table:

| Structural Formula | Common Name | IUPAC Name |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\text{HC}\equiv\text{CH}$ | Acetylene | Ethyne |
| $\text{CH}_3-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH}$ | Methylacetylene | Prop-1-yne |
| $\text{CH}_3-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-\text{CH}_3$ | Dimethylacetylene | But-2-yne |
| $\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH}$ | Vinylacetylene | But-1-en-3-yne |
| $\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}=\text{CH}-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH}$ | — | Pent-3-en-1-yne |
| $\text{HC}\equiv\text{C}-\text{CH}_2-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH}$ | — | Penta-1,4-diyne |

#### 1.3 Structural Isomerism in Alkynes ⭐⭐⭐
Alkynes exhibit three major structural isomerism classes:
1. **Chain Isomerism:** Branching within the carbon skeleton ($n \ge 5$).
   - Pent-1-yne $\left(\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH}\right)$ and 3-Methylbut-1-yne $\left((\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CH}-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH}\right)$.
2. **Position Isomerism:** Variation in the position of the $-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-$ group ($n \ge 4$).
   - But-1-yne $\left(\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH}\right)$ and But-2-yne $\left(\text{CH}_3-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-\text{CH}_3\right)$.
3. **Functional Isomerism (Ring-Chain & Dienes):**
   - Alkynes ($\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n-2}$) are functional isomers of alkadienes and cycloalkenes.
   - Example: $\text{C}_3\text{H}_4 \implies \text{CH}_3-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH}$ (Propyne), $\text{CH}_2=\text{C}=\text{CH}_2$ (Allene/Propa-1,2-diene), and Cyclopropene.

### Section 2: Electronic Structure & Bonding of Ethyne ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### 2.1 Orbital Hybridization & Geometry
In ethyne ($\text{H}-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-\text{H}$):
- Each carbon undergoes **$sp$-hybridization** by mixing one $2s$ and one $2p$ orbital ($2p_x$), leaving two unhybridized pure $p$-orbitals ($2p_y$ and $2p_z$) perpendicular to each other and to the internuclear axis.
- **$\sigma$-Bond Framework:**
  - One $\text{C}_{sp}-\text{C}_{sp}$ sigma bond formed by axial end-to-end overlap.
  - Two $\text{C}_{sp}-\text{H}_{1s}$ sigma bonds formed by axial overlap.
- **$\pi$-Bond Framework:**
  - Lateral (sideways) overlap of mutually parallel $2p_y-2p_y$ orbitals forms $\pi_1$.
  - Lateral overlap of mutually parallel $2p_z-2p_z$ orbitals forms $\pi_2$.
- The combination of the two mutually perpendicular $\pi$-electron clouds generates a **cylindrically symmetrical electron sheath** surrounding the internuclear $\text{C}-\text{C}$ axis.

#### 2.2 Bond Metrics & Physical Parameters
```
        H ────(106 pm)──── C ≡≡≡≡(120 pm)≡≡≡≡ C ────(106 pm)──── H
        └─────── 180° ─────┴────── 180° ──────┘
```
- **Geometry:** Linear
- **Bond Angle:** $\angle\text{H}-\text{C}-\text{C} = 180^\circ$
- **$\text{C}\equiv\text{C}$ Bond Length:** $120\text{ pm}$ (vs $134\text{ pm}$ in ethene, $154\text{ pm}$ in ethane)
- **$\text{C}-\text{H}$ Bond Length:** $106\text{ pm}$ (vs $108.7\text{ pm}$ in ethene, $111\text{ pm}$ in ethane)
- **Bond Enthalpy:** $\text{C}\equiv\text{C} = 823\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$ (vs $\text{C}=\text{C} = 681\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$, $\text{C}-\text{C} = 348\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$)

### Section 3: Industrial & Laboratory Methods of Preparation

#### 3.1 From Calcium Carbide (Industrial Preparation) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Calcium carbide is prepared by endothermic reduction of quicklime ($\text{CaO}$) with coke in an electric arc furnace at $2273\text{ K}$:

$$
\text{CaCO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{CaO} + \text{CO}_2 \uparrow
$$

$$
\text{CaO} + 3\text{C} \xrightarrow{2273\text{ K}} \text{CaC}_2 + \text{CO} \uparrow
$$

Subsequent hydrolysis of calcium carbide produces ethyne gas:

$$
\boxed{\text{CaC}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{Ca(OH)}_2 + \text{HC}\equiv\text{CH} \uparrow}
$$

> **Mechanism & Charge Separation:**
> Calcium carbide is predominantly ionic: $\text{Ca}^{2+} [:\text{C}\equiv\text{C}:]^{2-}$. The acetylide dicarbide dianion abstracts protons sequentially from water:
> 

$$
[:\text{C}\equiv\text{C}:]^{2-} + \text{H}-\text{OH} \longrightarrow [:\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-\text{H}]^- + \text{OH}^-
$$

> 

$$
[:\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-\text{H}]^- + \text{H}-\text{OH} \longrightarrow \text{H}-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-\text{H} + \text{OH}^-
$$

#### 3.2 Dehydrohalogenation of Vicinal & Geminal Dihalides ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
The synthesis requires a sequential two-step elimination:

##### Step 1: Using Alcoholic Potassium Hydroxide ($\text{alc. KOH}$)
First dehydrohalogenation proceeds smoothly via an $\text{E}2$ mechanism to yield a haloalkene (vinyl halide):

$$
\text{R}-\text{CHX}-\text{CH}_2\text{X} + \text{KOH}(\text{alc.}) \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{R}-\text{CH}=\text{CHX} + \text{KX} + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

##### Step 2: Using Sodiated Base ($\text{NaNH}_2$) in Liquid Ammonia
The halogen in the vinyl halide ($\text{R}-\text{CH}=\text{CHX}$) is stabilized by $p-\pi$ resonance with the double bond ($\text{C}-\text{X}$ bond gains partial double-bond character), rendering it unreactive toward $\text{alc. KOH}$. A far stronger base, **sodium amide ($\text{NaNH}_2$)**, is required:

$$
\text{R}-\text{CH}=\text{CHX} + \text{NaNH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{liq. }\text{NH}_3, \, 196\text{ K}} \boxed{\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH} + \text{NaX} + \text{NH}_3}
$$

##### Complete Representative Equation:

$$
\text{Br}-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\text{Br} \xrightarrow{\text{alc. KOH}, \, \Delta} \text{CH}_2=\text{CHBr} \xrightarrow{\text{NaNH}_2, \, \text{liq. }\text{NH}_3} \text{HC}\equiv\text{CH}
$$

#### 3.3 Dehalogenation of Tetrahaloalkanes ⭐⭐⭐
Heating 1,1,2,2-tetrahaloalkanes with zinc dust suspended in ethanol causes dual $\beta$-elimination:

$$
\text{CHBr}_2-\text{CHBr}_2 + 2\text{Zn} \xrightarrow{\text{Ethanol}, \, \Delta} \boxed{\text{HC}\equiv\text{CH} + 2\text{ZnBr}_2}
$$

#### 3.4 Kolbe’s Electrolytic Decarboxylation ⭐⭐⭐
Electrolysis of an aqueous solution of potassium maleate or potassium fumarate yields ethyne at the anode:
- **At Anode:**
  

$$
\begin{aligned}
  \begin{matrix} \text{CH}-\text{COO}^- \\ \parallel \qquad\quad \\ \text{CH}-\text{COO}^- \end{matrix} 
  &\xrightarrow{-2e^-} \begin{matrix} \text{CH}-\text{COO}^\bullet \\ \parallel \qquad\quad \\ \text{CH}-\text{COO}^\bullet \end{matrix} \xrightarrow{-2\text{CO}_2} \boxed{\text{HC}\equiv\text{CH} \uparrow}
  \end{aligned}
$$

- **At Cathode:**
  

$$
2\text{H}_2\text{O} + 2e^- \longrightarrow 2\text{OH}^- + \text{H}_2 \uparrow
$$

### Section 4: Acidic Nature of Alkynes & Chemical Distinctions

#### 4.1 Hybridization and Electronegativity Rationale ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
The acidity of terminal hydrogens in hydrocarbons follows the order:

$$
\boxed{\text{HC}\equiv\text{CH} > \text{CH}_2=\text{CH}_2 > \text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_3}
$$

| Parameter | Ethane ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_6$) | Ethene ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_4$) | Ethyne ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_2$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hybridization** | $sp^3$ | $sp^2$ | $sp$ |
| **$s$-Character** | $25\%$ ($1/4$) | $33.3\%$ ($1/3$) | $\mathbf{50\%}$ ($1/2$) |
| **Carbon Electronegativity** | $2.5$ | $2.75$ | $\mathbf{3.25}$ |
| **$\text{p}K_a$** | $\approx 50$ | $\approx 44$ | $\mathbf{\approx 25}$ |

**Physical Origin:**
Because an $s$-orbital is spherical, closer to the nucleus, and lower in energy than a $p$-orbital, electrons in $sp$-hybrid orbitals experience greater nuclear attraction. This high electronegativity strongly polarizes the $\text{C}_{sp}-\text{H}$ bond toward carbon, allowing the proton to be extracted by strong conjugate bases.

> **CRUCIAL DISTINCTION:** Alkynes are weaker acids than water ($\text{p}K_a = 15.7$) and alcohols ($\text{p}K_a \approx 16-18$), but are stronger acids than ammonia ($\text{p}K_a \approx 38$) and alkenes/alkanes.

#### 4.2 Reactions Establishing Acidic Character ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

##### 1. Reaction with Active Alkali Metals (Sodium Metal)

$$
\text{HC}\equiv\text{CH} + \text{Na} \xrightarrow{473\text{ K}} \text{HC}\equiv\text{C}^-\text{Na}^+ + \frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2 \uparrow \quad (\text{Monosodium acetylide})
$$

$$
\text{HC}\equiv\text{C}^-\text{Na}^+ + \text{Na} \xrightarrow{473\text{ K}} \text{Na}^+ -\!:\!\text{C}\equiv\text{C}:^-\! \text{Na}^+ + \frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2 \uparrow \quad (\text{Disodium acetylide})
$$

##### 2. Reaction with Sodium Amide ($\text{NaNH}_2$)

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH} + \text{NaNH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{liq. }\text{NH}_3} \text{CH}_3-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}^-\text{Na}^+ + \text{NH}_3 \uparrow
$$

*(Internal alkynes such as $\text{CH}_3-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-\text{CH}_3$ lack an $sp\text{ C}-\text{H}$ bond and show **no reaction**).*

#### 4.3 Qualitative Distinctions: Terminal vs. Non-Terminal Alkynes ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

##### 1. Ammoniacal Cuprous Chloride Test:
Passing a terminal alkyne into an ammoniacal solution of copper(I) chloride ($\text{Cu}_2\text{Cl}_2 + \text{NH}_4\text{OH}$) produces a **red precipitate** of dicopper acetylide:

$$
\text{HC}\equiv\text{CH} + 2[\text{Cu}(\text{NH}_3)_2]\text{Cl} \longrightarrow \boxed{\text{Cu}-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-\text{Cu} \downarrow \, (\text{Red})} + 2\text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + 2\text{NH}_3
$$

##### 2. Ammoniacal Silver Nitrate Test (Tollens' Reagent):
Terminal alkynes react with Tollens' reagent ($[\text{Ag}(\text{NH}_3)_2]\text{NO}_3$) to form a **white/grey precipitate** of silver acetylide:

$$
\text{HC}\equiv\text{CH} + 2[\text{Ag}(\text{NH}_3)_2]\text{NO}_3 \longrightarrow \boxed{\text{Ag}-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-\text{Ag} \downarrow \, (\text{White})} + 2\text{NH}_4\text{NO}_3 + 2\text{NH}_3
$$

$$
\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH} + [\text{Ag}(\text{NH}_3)_2]\text{OH} \longrightarrow \text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-\text{Ag} \downarrow + 2\text{NH}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

***

### Section 5: Addition Reactions & Detailed Mechanisms

#### 5.1 Electrophilic Addition Overview
The cylindrical $\pi$-cloud of alkynes holds electrons tightly due to the high electronegativity of $sp$-carbon. Consequently, **alkynes are generally less reactive than alkenes toward electrophiles**, despite containing two $\pi$-bonds.

#### 5.2 Addition of Dihydrogen ($\text{H}_2$) & Stereoselective Hydrogenation ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

##### Path A: Complete Saturation

$$
\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-\text{R}' + 2\text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Ni/Pt/Pd}, \, \Delta} \text{R}-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\text{R}'
$$

##### Path B: Controlled Stereoselective Hydrogenation
1. **Lindlar's Catalyst ($cis\text{-alkene}$):**
   - **Reagent:** Palladium supported on calcium carbonate, partially deactivated (poisoned) with lead acetate, quinoline, or sulfur ($\text{Pd}/\text{CaCO}_3/\text{quinoline}$).
   - **Mechanism:** Heterogeneous surface delivery of both hydrogen atoms from the same face (**syn-addition**).
   

$$
\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-\text{R} + \text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Pd/CaCO}_3, \, \text{quinoline}} \begin{matrix} \text{R} \qquad\quad \text{R} \\ \ \backslash \quad\quad / \ \\ \text{C}=\text{C} \\ \ / \quad\quad \backslash \ \\ \text{H} \qquad\quad \text{H} \end{matrix} \quad \mathbf{(cis\text{-alkene})}
$$

2. **Birch Reduction ($trans\text{-alkene}$):**
   - **Reagent:** Sodium or Lithium metal dissolved in liquid ammonia at $240\text{ K}$ ($\text{Na} / \text{liq. NH}_3$).
   - **Mechanism:** Dissolving metal reduction via sequential single-electron transfer (SET) generating a trans-radical anion intermediate (**anti-addition**).
   

$$
\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-\text{R} + 2\text{Na} + 2\text{NH}_3 \xrightarrow{240\text{ K}} \begin{matrix} \text{R} \qquad\quad \text{H} \\ \ \backslash \quad\quad / \ \\ \text{C}=\text{C} \\ \ / \quad\quad \backslash \ \\ \text{H} \qquad\quad \text{R} \end{matrix} \quad \mathbf{(trans\text{-alkene})} + 2\text{NaNH}_2
$$

#### 5.3 Addition of Halogens ($\text{X}_2 = \text{Br}_2, \text{Cl}_2$) ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Addition occurs via a cyclic halonium ion intermediate giving *anti*-stereochemistry:

$$
\text{HC}\equiv\text{CH} + \text{Br}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{CCl}_4} \begin{matrix} \text{Br} \qquad\quad \text{H} \\ \ \backslash \quad\quad / \ \\ \text{C}=\text{C} \\ \ / \quad\quad \backslash \ \\ \text{H} \qquad\quad \text{Br} \end{matrix} \xrightarrow{\text{Br}_2, \, \text{CCl}_4} \text{CHBr}_2-\text{CHBr}_2
$$

*(1,2-Dibromoethene $\rightarrow$ 1,1,2,2-Tetrabromoethane)*

> **Diagnostic Test:** Discharge of the reddish-brown color of bromine in $\text{CCl}_4$ is an analytical test for unsaturation.

#### 5.4 Addition of Hydrogen Halides ($\text{HX}$) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Addition follows **Markovnikov’s Rule** at both steps, resulting in a **geminal dihalide** ($gem$-dihalide):

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH} + \text{HBr} \longrightarrow \left[\text{CH}_3-\text{C(Br)}=\text{CH}_2\right] \xrightarrow{\text{HBr}} \boxed{\text{CH}_3-\text{C(Br)}_2-\text{CH}_3}
$$

*(2-Bromopropene $\rightarrow$ 2,2-Dibromopropane)*

##### Reaction Mechanism:
1. **First Protonation:**
   

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH} + \text{H}^+ \longrightarrow \text{CH}_3-\overset{+}{\text{C}}=\text{CH}_2 \quad (\text{More stable secondary vinylic carbocation})
$$

2. **First Nucleophilic Attack:**
   

$$
\text{CH}_3-\overset{+}{\text{C}}=\text{CH}_2 + \text{Br}^- \longrightarrow \text{CH}_3-\text{C(Br)}=\text{CH}_2
$$

3. **Second Protonation & Resonance Stabilization:**
   

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{C(Br)}=\text{CH}_2 + \text{H}^+ \longrightarrow \left[ \text{CH}_3-\overset{+}{\text{C}}(\text{Br})-\text{CH}_3 \longleftrightarrow \text{CH}_3-\overset{+..}{\text{C}}(\overset{..}{\text{Br}}\!^+)-\text{CH}_3 \right]
$$

   Halogen lone-pair back-donation strongly stabilizes the carbocation on the carbon already bearing the bromine atom.
4. **Second Nucleophilic Attack:**
   

$$
\text{CH}_3-\overset{+}{\text{C}}(\text{Br})-\text{CH}_3 + \text{Br}^- \longrightarrow \text{CH}_3-\text{CBr}_2-\text{CH}_3
$$

#### 5.5 Hydration (Kucherov Reaction) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Alkynes add one molecule of water in the presence of warm dilute sulfuric acid ($\approx 333\text{ K}$) and mercuric sulfate catalyst:

$$
\text{Conditions:} \quad 40\% \, \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 + 1\% \, \text{HgSO}_4 \quad \text{at } 333\text{ K}
$$

##### 1. Hydration of Ethyne $\longrightarrow$ Ethanal (Acetaldehyde):

$$
\text{HC}\equiv\text{CH} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow[\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4, \, 333\text{ K}]{\text{Hg}^{2+}} \left[\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}-\text{OH}\right] \underset{\text{Tautomerism}}{\overset{\text{Keto-Enol}}{\rightleftharpoons}} \boxed{\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}=\text{O}}
$$

##### 2. Hydration of Propyne $\longrightarrow$ Propan-2-one (Acetone):
Addition of water proceeds in accordance with Markovnikov’s rule:

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow[\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4, \, 333\text{ K}]{\text{Hg}^{2+}} \left[\text{CH}_3-\underset{\text{OH}}{\underset{\mid}{\text{C}}}=\text{CH}_2\right] \underset{\text{Tautomerism}}{\overset{\text{Keto-Enol}}{\rightleftharpoons}} \boxed{\text{CH}_3-\overset{\text{O}}{\overset{\parallel}{\text{C}}}-\text{CH}_3}
$$

> **Key Rule:** **Ethyne is the ONLY alkyne that yields an aldehyde upon hydration.** All other terminal and unsymmetrical alkynes yield ketones.

#### 5.6 Cyclic Polymerization ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Passing ethyne through a red-hot iron tube at $873\text{ K}$ causes cyclic trimerization to yield **benzene**:

$$
3\,\text{C}_2\text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Red-hot Fe tube}, \, 873\text{ K}} \boxed{\text{C}_6\text{H}_6}
$$

```
                CH ═══ CH
              //         \
            HC            CH
             \           //
              CH ═══ CH
                   ↓ (Red-hot Fe, 873 K)
                Benzene
```

Similarly, propyne trimerizes to form **1,3,5-trimethylbenzene (Mesitylene)**:

$$
3\,\text{CH}_3-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH} \xrightarrow{\text{Red-hot Fe tube}, \, 873\text{ K}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_3(\text{CH}_3)_3 \quad \text{(Mesitylene)}
$$

**Question:** An alkyne 'A' ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_8$) on hydrogenation with $\text{H}_2/\text{Pd-BaSO}_4$ gives 'B' ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}$), whereas with $\text{Na}/\text{liq. NH}_3$ it gives 'C' ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}$). Compound 'A' does not react with Tollens' reagent. Identify A, B, and C.

### Section 7: Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

1. **The Double-Dehydrohalogenation Base Trap:**
   - *Mistake:* Using excess $\text{alc. KOH}$ at room temperature to transform 1,2-dibromoethane into ethyne.
   - *Fact:* $\text{alc. KOH}$ removes the first $\text{HBr}$ mole readily, but vinyl bromide ($\text{CH}_2=\text{CHBr}$) has resonance-stabilized $\text{C}-\text{Br}$ bonds. High temperature and a stronger base ($\mathbf{NaNH}_2$) are mandatory for the second step.

2. **The Birch Reduction with Terminal Alkynes:**
   - *Mistake:* Attempting to reduce Prop-1-yne or But-1-yne to a $trans$-alkene using $\text{Na}/\text{liq. NH}_3$.
   - *Fact:* Terminal alkynes are acidic! Rather than undergoing reduction, they undergo an **acid-base reaction**, releasing hydrogen gas and forming sodium alkynide:
     

$$
\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH} + \text{Na} \xrightarrow{\text{liq. NH}_3} \text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}^-\text{Na}^+ + \frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\uparrow
$$

3. **Vicinal vs. Geminal Halide Confusion upon $\text{HX}$ Addition:**
   - *Mistake:* Writing 1,2-dibromopropane as the product of $\text{propyne} + 2\text{HBr}$.
   - *Fact:* Both bromide ions attach to the **same** central carbon (Markovnikov orientation), forming **2,2-dibromopropane** ($gem$-dihalide), not a vicinal dihalide.

4. **Keto-Enol Tautomerism Arrow:**
   - *Mistake:* Indicating that enol and keto forms are resonance structures with a double-headed resonance arrow ($\longleftrightarrow$).
   - *Fact:* Tautomerism involves nuclear motion (proton shift); it is an equilibrium denoted by reversible arrows ($\rightleftharpoons$), favoring the keto tautomer due to the thermodynamic strength of the $\text{C}=\text{O}$ bond ($\approx 745\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$ vs $\text{C}=\text{C} \approx 610\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$).

***

### Section 8: Speed Hacks & Golden Revision Points

1. **Degree of Unsaturation Shortcut:**
   

$$
\text{Index of Hydrogen Deficiency (IHD)} = C + 1 - \frac{H - N + X}{2}
$$

   - Any acyclic mono-alkyne must have $\text{IHD} = 2$.

2. **Stereocontrol Mnemonic:**
   - **L**indlar $\rightarrow$ **C**is $\implies$ **"LC"** (Low Calorie / Liquid Crystal)
   - **B**irch $\rightarrow$ **T**rans $\implies$ **"BT"** (Bluetooth)

3. **Hydration Carbon Count:**
   - $2\text{ Carbons } (\text{Ethyne}) \implies \text{Aldehyde (Ethanal)}$
   - $\ge 3\text{ Carbons } (\text{All Other Alkynes}) \implies \text{Ketones}$

4. **Acidic Proton Quick Test:**
   - Look for the pattern: $-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-\mathbf{H}$ at carbon-1.
   - If the name starts with `1-` (e.g., But-1-yne), it reacts with Tollens' and Fehling's reagents / active metals.
   - If it has any other locant (e.g., But-2-yne), it is completely inert to ammoniacal metal salts.

# Module 1: Introduction, Classification, and IUPAC Nomenclature

### 1.1 Fundamental Terminology and Classification ⭐⭐⭐
Aromatic hydrocarbons are commonly known as **arenes**. Most arenes possess distinctive aromas (Greek: *aroma* = pleasant-smelling fragrance), though present-day classification depends entirely on electronic structure, cyclic conjugation, and thermodynamic stability rather than odor.

Arenes are broadly classified into two categories:
1. **Benzenoid Aromatic Compounds:** Contain at least one benzene ring (e.g., Benzene, Toluene, Naphthalene, Anthracene, Phenanthrene).
2. **Non-Benzenoid Aromatic Compounds:** Possess aromatic stability but lack an isolated or fused benzene nucleus (e.g., Cyclopropenyl cation, Tropylium cation, Azulene, Cyclopentadienyl anion).

***

### 1.2 IUPAC Nomenclature and Isomerism of Arene Derivatives ⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### (A) Monosubstituted Benzene Derivatives
Monosubstituted benzenes are systematically named by prefixing the substituent name to the word **"benzene"**. However, IUPAC explicitly retains several traditional common names:

| Substituent Structure | Common Name Accepted by IUPAC | Systematic IUPAC Name |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{CH}_3$ | Toluene | Methylbenzene |
| $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{OCH}_3$ | Anisole | Methoxybenzene |
| $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{NH}_2$ | Aniline | Benzenamine / Aminobenzene |
| $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{OH}$ | Phenol | Benzenol / Hydroxybenzene |
| $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{COOH}$ | Benzoic acid | Benzenecarboxylic acid |
| $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{CHO}$ | Benzaldehyde | Benzenecarbaldehyde |
| $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{NO}_2$ | Nitrobenzene | Nitrobenzene |
| $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2$ | Styrene | Ethenylbenzene |

#### (B) Disubstituted Benzene Derivatives: Positional Descriptors
When two substituents are present on the benzene ring, their relative positions are indicated either by positional locants $(1,2;\; 1,3;\; 1,4)$ in IUPAC nomenclature or by classical classical prefixes in common nomenclature:
* **$1,2$-position:** *ortho-* ($o$-)
* **$1,3$-position:** *meta-* ($m$-)
* **$1,4$-position:** *para-* ($p$-)

##### Positional Isomers of Dimethylbenzene (Xylene):
1. **$1,2$-Dimethylbenzene** $\rightarrow$ *o*-Xylene
2. **$1,3$-Dimethylbenzene** $\rightarrow$ *m*-Xylene
3. **$1,4$-Dimethylbenzene** $\rightarrow$ *p*-Xylene

#### (C) Priority Rules for Polysubstituted Arenes
1. If a principal functional group is present (e.g., $-\text{COOH}, -\text{CHO}, -\text{OH}, -\text{NH}_2$), the carbon carrying this group is designated as $\text{C-1}$. Numbering proceeds in the direction that gives the lowest set of locants to the incoming substituents.
2. If substituents belong to the same class (e.g., halogens or alkyl groups), numbering is assigned according to the **lowest locant rule**, and substituents are cited alphabetically.
3. If an alkyl chain contains greater than 6 carbon atoms, or contains a principal functional group/unsaturation, the benzene ring is treated as a substituent called the **phenyl group** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-$ or $\text{Ph}-$).

# Module 2: Structure of Benzene — Historical Evolution to Modern Orbital Theory

### 2.1 Kekulé Structure and Its Inherent Anomalies ⭐⭐⭐⭐
In 1865, Friedrich August Kekulé proposed that benzene consists of a regular planar hexagonal ring of six carbon atoms with alternating single and double bonds, where each carbon is bonded to one hydrogen atom:

$$
\text{Cyclohexa-1,3,5-triene model}
$$

Kekulé envisioned a dynamic equilibrium between two valence isomers to explain why only one $1,2$-disubstituted product was isolated:

$$
\text{Structure (I)} \rightleftharpoons \text{Structure (II)}
$$

#### Fundamental Failures of the Kekulé Model:
1. **Unusual Chemical Inertia toward Addition:** If benzene were simply cyclohexa-1,3,5-triene, it should rapidly decolorize bromine water ($\text{Br}_2/\text{CCl}_4$) and cold alkaline potassium permanganate ($\text{KMnO}_4$, Baeyer's Reagent). Benzene **fails** to give these electrophilic additions under ambient conditions; it undergoes electrophilic **substitution** ($\text{S}_\text{E}\text{Ar}$) instead.
2. **Identical Carbon-Carbon Bond Lengths:** X-ray diffraction measurements confirm that **all six $\text{C}-\text{C}$ bonds in benzene are strictly identical** at $139\text{ pm}$ ($1.39\text{ Å}$). In a classical polyene:
   * Isolated single bond: $d(\text{C}-\text{C}) = 154\text{ pm}$ ($1.54\text{ Å}$)
   * Isolated double bond: $d(\text{C}=\text{C}) = 134\text{ pm}$ ($1.34\text{ Å}$)
   The observed value of $139\text{ pm}$ is intermediate, ruling out alternating localized bonds.

```
       H                        H
       |                        |
       C                        C
     // \                     /   \\
H - C     C - H   <=====> H - C     C - H
    |     ||                  ||    |
H - C     C - H           H - C     C - H
     \\ /                     \   //
       C                        C
       |                        |
       H                        H
   Kekulé (I)               Kekulé (II)
```

***

### 2.2 Modern Orbital Picture of Benzene ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Spectroscopic and diffraction studies establish the exact geometry of benzene:
1. **Hybridization:** Each carbon atom is $\text{sp}^2$ hybridized.
2. **$\sigma$-Bond Framework:**
   * Each carbon forms two $\text{C}_{(\text{sp}^2)}-\text{C}_{(\text{sp}^2)}$ $\sigma$-bonds with neighboring ring carbons.
   * Each carbon forms one $\text{C}_{(\text{sp}^2)}-\text{H}_{(\text{1s})}$ $\sigma$-bond.
   * Total $\sigma$-bonds in benzene $= 6\;(\text{C}-\text{C}) + 6\;(\text{C}-\text{H}) = 12\ \sigma\text{-bonds}$.
3. **Planar Geometry:** Bond angles are strictly $120^\circ$. The molecule exhibits regular planar hexagonal geometry ($D_{6h}$ point group symmetry).
4. **Delocalized $\pi$-Electron Cloud:**
   * Each carbon atom retains an unhybridized $2\text{p}_z$ atomic orbital oriented perpendicular to the hexagonal molecular plane.
   * The six $2\text{p}_z$ orbitals undergo continuous lateral (sideways) overlap around the entire ring.
   * This forms a continuous, cyclic, delocalized $\pi$-electron system comprising two doughnut-shaped electron clouds: one positioned directly **above** and one **below** the plane of the carbon ring.

$$
\boxed{\text{Total Valence Electrons} = 30 \implies 12\ \sigma\text{-pairs (24 } e^-\text{)} + 3\ \pi\text{-pairs (6 } e^-\text{)}}
$$

***

# Module 3: Resonance Theory, Heat of Hydrogenation, and Resonance Energy

### 3.1 Valence Bond (Resonance) Description ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
According to Resonance Theory, benzene cannot be adequately represented by any single Lewis structure. It exists as a **resonance hybrid** of two principal Kekulé contributing structures and three minor Dewar structures:

$$
\Psi_{\text{hybrid}} = c_1 \Psi_{\text{Kekul\'e A}} + c_2 \Psi_{\text{Kekul\'e B}} + \sum_{i=1}^3 c_{3,i} \Psi_{\text{Dewar } i}
$$

* The two Kekulé structures are identical in energy and contribute equally ($\approx 80\%$ total contribution).
* The three Dewar structures (possessing a long, formal cross-ring para bond) are substantially higher in potential energy and contribute minimally ($\approx 20\%$).

***

### 3.2 Quantitative Derivation of Resonance Energy from Heats of Hydrogenation ($\Delta H^\circ_{\text{hydro}}$) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### Step 1: Definition of Experimental Reference
Resonance energy is defined as the difference in enthalpy between the hypothetical localized structure (the most stable canonical form, cyclohexa-1,3,5-triene) and the real, experimentally observed resonance hybrid (benzene).

$$
\text{Resonance Energy} = \Delta H^\circ_{\text{hydro (theoretical, cyclohexatriene)}} - \Delta H^\circ_{\text{hydro (experimental, benzene)}}
$$

#### Step 2: Experimental Thermodynamic Data
* Hydrogenation of Cyclohexene to Cyclohexane:

$$
\text{Cyclohexene}_{(l)} + \text{H}_{2(g)} \xrightarrow{\text{Pt/Pd/Ni}} \text{Cyclohexane}_{(l)} \quad \Delta H^\circ_1 = -119.5\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}
$$

#### Step 3: Theoretical Calculation for Hypothetical Cyclohexa-1,3,5-triene
If the three double bonds in cyclohexa-1,3,5-triene behaved as isolated, non-interacting double bonds:

$$
\Delta H^\circ_{\text{hydro (calc)}} = 3 \times \Delta H^\circ_1 = 3 \times (-119.5\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}) = -358.5\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}
$$

#### Step 4: Experimental Hydrogenation of Benzene
Complete reduction of benzene to cyclohexane:

$$
\text{Benzene}_{(l)} + 3\text{H}_{2(g)} \xrightarrow[\Delta, P]{\text{Ni}} \text{Cyclohexane}_{(l)} \quad \Delta H^\circ_{\text{hydro (obs)}} = -208.2\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}
$$

#### Step 5: Final Evaluation of Resonance Energy

$$
\text{Resonance Energy of Benzene} = |-358.5 - (-208.2)|\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Resonance Energy of Benzene} = 150.3\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1} \quad (\approx 36.0\text{ kcal}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1})}
$$

**Physical Implication:** Benzene is thermodynamically more stable than the hypothetical unconjugated cyclohexatriene by $150.3\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$. This substantial stabilization energy explains its reluctance to undergo addition reactions that would disrupt the delocalized $\pi$-sextet.

***

# Module 4: Hückel’s $(4n+2)\;\pi$-Electron Rule and Criteria for Aromaticity

### 4.1 The Four Rigorous Criteria for Aromaticity ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
For a chemical species to be categorized as **Aromatic**, it must satisfy all four of the following conditions without exception:
1. **Cyclic Structure:** The molecule or ion must possess a closed ring of atoms.
2. **Complete Planarity:** All atoms constituting the ring framework must lie in a single plane (or deviate only negligibly) to allow parallel alignment of p-orbitals. Every ring atom must be $\text{sp}^2$ (or occasionally $\text{sp}$) hybridized.
3. **Continuous Cyclic Conjugation:** There must be an uninterrupted ring of p-orbitals. The presence of even a single saturated, tetrahedral $\text{sp}^3$ hybridized ring atom breaks the conjugation, rendering the system non-aromatic.
4. **Hückel’s Number of Delocalized $\pi$-Electrons:** The conjugated cyclic $\pi$-system must contain exactly:

$$
N_\pi = 4n + 2 \quad \text{where } n \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, \dots\}
$$

* For $n=0$: $N_\pi = 2$
* For $n=1$: $N_\pi = 6$ (Aromatic Sextet)
* For $n=2$: $N_\pi = 10$
* For $n=3$: $N_\pi = 14$
* For $n=4$: $N_\pi = 18$

***

### 4.2 Comparison: Aromatic vs. Anti-Aromatic vs. Non-Aromatic Systems ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

| Parameter | Aromatic System | Anti-Aromatic System | Non-Aromatic System |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ring Topology** | Cyclic | Cyclic | Cyclic or Acyclic |
| **Hybridization of Ring Atoms**| Exclusively $\text{sp}^2$ / $\text{sp}$ | Exclusively $\text{sp}^2$ / $\text{sp}$ | At least one atom is $\text{sp}^3$ (or non-planar) |
| **Conjugation** | Unbroken cyclic conjugation | Unbroken cyclic conjugation | Broken conjugation / discontinuous |
| **Geometry** | Strictly Planar | Strictly Planar | Non-planar (puckered, boat, tub, etc.) |
| **$\pi$-Electron Count** | **$4n + 2$** ($2, 6, 10, 14, \dots$) | **$4n$** ($4, 8, 12, 16, \dots$ with $n \ge 1$) | Any count; not governed by Hückel's Rule |
| **Thermodynamic Stability** | $\Delta H_f$ exceptionally low; **Extremely Stable** | $\Delta H_f$ exceptionally high; **Highly Unstable** | Comparable to corresponding open-chain analog |
| **Magnetic Behavior** | Diatropic (Diamagnetic ring current) | Paratropic (Paramagnetic ring current) | No sustained ring current |

$$
\boxed{\text{Thermodynamic Stability Order: } Aromatic \gg Non-Aromatic > Anti-Aromatic}
$$

***

### 4.3 Classification of Key Systems

#### Category A: Carbocyclic Cations and Anions
1. **Cyclopropenyl Cation ($\text{C}_3\text{H}_3^+$):**
   * Structure: 3-membered ring with two double-bond carbons ($\text{sp}^2$) and one carbocation carbon ($\text{sp}^2$).
   * $N_\pi = 2$ electrons ($n = 0$).
   * Result: **Aromatic**.

2. **Cyclopropenyl Anion ($\text{C}_3\text{H}_3^-$):**
   * Structure: 3-membered ring; carbanion lone pair resides in an unhybridized p-orbital.
   * $N_\pi = 4$ electrons ($n = 1$ in $4n$).
   * Result: **Anti-Aromatic** (highly unstable at room temperature).

3. **Cyclopentadienyl Anion ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_5^-$):**
   * Formed by deprotonation of cyclopentadiene ($\text{p}K_a \approx 16$, remarkably acidic for a hydrocarbon).
   * Carbanion carbon rehybridizes from $\text{sp}^3 \rightarrow \text{sp}^2$.
   * $N_\pi = 4\ (\text{from double bonds}) + 2\ (\text{from carbanion lone pair}) = 6\ \pi\text{-electrons}$ ($n = 1$).
   * Result: **Aromatic**.

4. **Cycloheptatrienyl Cation ($\text{C}_7\text{H}_7^+$, Tropylium Cation):**
   * Formed by abstraction of hydride from cycloheptatriene.
   * All 7 carbons are $\text{sp}^2$ hybridized.
   * $N_\pi = 6\ \pi\text{-electrons}$ ($n=1$).
   * Result: **Aromatic** (forms crystalline ionic salts, e.g., $\text{C}_7\text{H}_7^+\text{Br}^-$).

***

#### Category B: Annulenes ($[N]\text{-Annulenes}$)
Annulenes are completely conjugated monocyclic hydrocarbons containing alternating single and double bonds, designated as $[N]\text{-annulene}$, where $N$ represents the total number of ring carbons.

1. **$[4]\text{-Annulene (Cyclobutadiene):}$**
   * Planar, cyclic, fully conjugated, $N_\pi = 4$ ($4n, n=1$).
   * Result: **Anti-Aromatic**. Highly reactive; dimerizes instantly at temperatures above $35\text{ K}$ via Diels-Alder cycloaddition.

2. **$[6]\text{-Annulene (Benzene):}$**
   * Planar, cyclic, fully conjugated, $N_\pi = 6$ ($4n+2, n=1$).
   * Result: **Aromatic**.

3. **$[8]\text{-Annulene (Cyclooctatetraene / COT):}$**
   * Contains $8\ \pi\text{-electrons}$. If planar, it would be forced to adopt an anti-aromatic configuration ($4n, n=2$).
   * To relieve anti-aromatic destabilization and angle strain, COT twists out of planarity to adopt a non-planar **"tub" conformation** ($D_{2d}$ symmetry).
   * Because it is non-planar, p-orbital overlap is broken $\implies$ **Non-Aromatic**. Readily undergoes electrophilic additions (e.g., adds $\text{Br}_2$).
   * *Reduction to Dianion:* Reaction with potassium metal adds two electrons:
     

$$
\text{COT} + 2\text{K} \longrightarrow \text{COT}^{2-} + 2\text{K}^+
$$

     $\text{COT}^{2-}$ has $10\ \pi\text{-electrons}$ ($4n+2, n=2$). It becomes completely planar and **Aromatic**!

```
       CH = CH
     /         \
    CH          CH         K-metal reduction         [   COT^2-   ] 2K+
    ||          ||       --------------------->      Planar Hexagonal/Octagonal
    CH          CH        + 2e- (Forms 10 pi e-)     4n + 2 = 10 (n = 2)
     \         /                                     AROMATIC
       CH = CH
   Tub-shaped COT
    (Non-planar)
    NON-AROMATIC
```

4. **$[10]\text{-Annulene:}$**
   * $10\ \pi$-electrons suggests aromaticity under $4n+2$ ($n=2$).
   * However, in the *(E,Z,Z,Z,E)* or *(1E,3Z,5E,7Z,9Z)* isomer, severe steric hindrance occurs between the internal transannular hydrogen atoms:

```
           H       H
            \     /
         C = C - C = C
        /             \
       C               C
        \             /
         C = C - C = C
```

   * The non-bonded steric clash between these internal hydrogens forces the ring out of planarity.
   * Result: **Non-Aromatic**.
   * Bridged $[10]$-annulene (1,6-methano$[10]$annulene), where the internal hydrogens are replaced by a $-\text{CH}_2-$ bridge holding the perimeter planar, is **Aromatic**.

***

#### Category C: Heterocyclic Aromatic Compounds
When heteroatoms (N, O, S) are incorporated into conjugated rings:
* **Rule:** If the heteroatom has a lone pair and the ring needs electrons to complete a $(4n+2)$ aromatic sextet, the lone pair occupies an unhybridized p-orbital and joins the aromatic $\pi$-system.
* If the heteroatom is already double-bonded in the ring, its lone pair resides in an $\text{sp}^2$ hybrid orbital coplanar with the ring and **does not** contribute to the $\pi$-count.

1. **Pyridine ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_5\text{N}$):**
   * The nitrogen atom is $\text{sp}^2$ hybridized and forms one $\sigma$-bond to each adjacent carbon using two $\text{sp}^2$ orbitals.
   * The remaining $\text{sp}^2$ orbital contains the nitrogen lone pair, pointing **radially outward** in the molecular plane.
   * The unhybridized $2\text{p}_z$ orbital contains one electron, contributing to the $6\ \pi$-electron ring.
   * Total $\pi$-electrons $= 5\ (\text{from carbons}) + 1\ (\text{from N}) = 6\ \pi\text{-electrons}$.
   * Result: **Aromatic**. (Lone pair is basic and nucleophilic).

2. **Pyrrole ($\text{C}_4\text{H}_5\text{N}$):**
   * Nitrogen forms three $\sigma$-bonds (two to carbons, one to hydrogen) using three $\text{sp}^2$ hybrid orbitals.
   * The nitrogen lone pair occupies the unhybridized $2\text{p}_z$ orbital perpendicular to the ring.
   * Total $\pi$-electrons $= 4\ (\text{from carbon double bonds}) + 2\ (\text{from N lone pair}) = 6\ \pi\text{-electrons}$.
   * Result: **Aromatic**. (Lone pair is tied up in resonance; pyrrole is a very weak base, $\text{p}K_b \approx 13.6$).

3. **Furan ($\text{C}_4\text{H}_4\text{O}$) and Thiophene ($\text{C}_4\text{H}_4\text{S}$):**
   * Oxygen/sulfur possesses two lone pairs.
   * One lone pair resides in an unhybridized p-orbital and participates in the aromatic $\pi$-sextet ($2\pi$ contribution).
   * The second lone pair occupies an $\text{sp}^2$ hybrid orbital in the molecular plane and does not count toward aromaticity.
   * Total $\pi$-electrons $= 4 + 2 = 6\ \pi\text{-electrons}$.
   * Result: **Aromatic**.

***

# Module 5: Step-by-Step Chemical Reactions & Mechanisms Related to Aromaticity

### 5.1 Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution ($\text{S}_\text{E}\text{Ar}$) Mechanism ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
The hallmark reaction of aromatic hydrocarbons is substitution rather than addition, driven by the preservation of aromatic resonance energy.

```
       H                            H   E
       |                            |  /
    +--+--+                      +--+-+-+
   /       \   + E(+)           /       \ (+)
  |    O    | -------->        |         |       -------->
   \       /  (Slow, r.d.s)     \       /       (- H+)
    +--+--+                      +--+--+         (Fast)
                               Arenium Ion
                             (sigma-complex)
                               NON-AROMATIC
                                                              E
                                                              |
                                                           +--+--+
                                                          /       \
                                                         |    O    |
                                                          \       /
                                                           +--+--+
                                                          AROMATIC
```

#### Step 1: Generation of the Electrophile ($\text{E}^+$)
Taking nitration as an exemplar:

$$
\text{HNO}_3 + 2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightleftharpoons \text{NO}_2^+ + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+ + 2\text{HSO}_4^-
$$

#### Step 2: Formation of the Arenium Ion ($\sigma$-Complex / Wheland Intermediate) — Rate Determining Step
Electrophilic attack by $\text{E}^+$ disrupts the aromatic $\pi$-system. The transition state collapses into a non-aromatic cyclohexadienyl cation:

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{E}^+ \xrightarrow{\text{slow, r.d.s.}} [\text{C}_6\text{H}_6\text{E}]^+
$$

The carbocation intermediate is resonance-stabilized across the ortho and para positions:

$$
[\text{C}_6\text{H}_6\text{E}]^+ \longleftrightarrow \left[ \text{Structure}_{\text{ortho}} \leftrightarrow \text{Structure}_{\text{para}} \leftrightarrow \text{Structure}_{\text{ortho}'} \right]
$$

* *Note:* The $\text{sp}^3$ hybridized carbon carrying $-\text{H}$ and $-\text{E}$ disrupts cyclic conjugation. The Wheland intermediate is **Non-Aromatic**.

#### Step 3: Rapid Proton Deprotonation (Aromatization)
A weak base (e.g., $\text{HSO}_4^-$) removes the proton from the $\text{sp}^3$ center. The electron pair of the $\text{C}-\text{H}$ $\sigma$-bond collapses back into the ring, restoring the $(4n+2)\;\pi$-electron aromatic sextet ($150.3\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$ resonance energy regained):

$$
[\text{C}_6\text{H}_6\text{E}]^+ + \text{HSO}_4^- \xrightarrow{\text{fast}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{E} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Driving Force: Restoration of Aromatic Resonance Energy}}
$$

***

### 5.2 Birch Reduction of Benzene ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Demonstrates controlled loss of complete conjugation under dissolving-metal conditions.

#### Reaction Scheme:

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + 2\text{Na} + 2\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \xrightarrow{\text{liq. }\text{NH}_3,\ -33^\circ\text{C}} \text{Cyclohexa-1,4-diene} + 2\text{NaOC}_2\text{H}_5
$$

#### Crucial Mechanistic Feature:
The product is **cyclohexa-1,4-diene** (unconjugated diene) rather than cyclohexa-1,3-diene (conjugated diene), due to the principle of least motion and kinetic protonation at positions of maximum electron density in the radical anion intermediate.

# Module 6: High-Yield Solved Examples & NCERT Exemplar Problems

### Example 1: Thermodynamic Calculation of Resonance Energy
**Question:** The experimental standard enthalpy of combustion ($\Delta_c H^\circ$) of benzene is $-3268\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$. The enthalpy of combustion of gaseous carbon to $\text{CO}_2$ is $-393.5\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$ and that of $\text{H}_{2(g)}$ to form $\text{H}_2\text{O}_{(l)}$ is $-285.8\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$. The theoretical enthalpy of formation ($\Delta_f H^\circ$) of cyclohexa-1,3,5-triene calculated from bond enthalpy data is $+232\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$. Calculate the resonance energy of benzene.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. Write the balanced equation for the combustion of benzene:

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_{6(l)} + \frac{15}{2}\text{O}_{2(g)} \longrightarrow 6\text{CO}_{2(g)} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}_{(l)} \quad \Delta_c H^\circ = -3268\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}
$$

2. Express $\Delta_c H^\circ$ in terms of standard enthalpies of formation:

$$
\Delta_c H^\circ = \left[6 \times \Delta_f H^\circ(\text{CO}_2) + 3 \times \Delta_f H^\circ(\text{H}_2\text{O})\right] - \Delta_f H^\circ(\text{C}_6\text{H}_{6,\text{ exp}})
$$

3. Substitute the values:

$$
-3268 = [6(-393.5) + 3(-285.8)] - \Delta_f H^\circ(\text{C}_6\text{H}_{6,\text{ exp}})
$$

$$
-3268 = [-2361.0 - 857.4] - \Delta_f H^\circ(\text{C}_6\text{H}_{6,\text{ exp}})
$$

$$
-3268 = -3218.4 - \Delta_f H^\circ(\text{C}_6\text{H}_{6,\text{ exp}})
$$

$$
\Delta_f H^\circ(\text{C}_6\text{H}_{6,\text{ exp}}) = -3218.4 + 3268 = +49.6\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}
$$

4. Determine Resonance Energy:

$$
\text{Resonance Energy} = \Delta_f H^\circ(\text{theoretical}) - \Delta_f H^\circ(\text{experimental})
$$

$$
\text{Resonance Energy} = (+232\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}) - (+49.6\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}) = \mathbf{182.4\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}}
$$

### Example 2: Systematic Evaluation of Aromatic Character (NCERT Exemplar Archetype)
**Question:** Classify the following species as Aromatic, Anti-Aromatic, or Non-Aromatic with theoretical justification:
1. Cyclopentadienyl cation ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_5^+$)
2. Azulene ($\text{C}_{10}\text{H}_8$)
3. Cyclooctatetraenyl dianion ($\text{C}_8\text{H}_8^{2-}$)
4. Pyran-4-one ring system

**Step-by-Step Analysis:**
1. **Cyclopentadienyl Cation ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_5^+$):**
   * *Cyclic?* Yes.
   * *Conjugated?* Yes, continuous loop with carbocation p-orbital.
   * *Planar?* Yes.
   * *$\pi$-electron count:* Two double bonds $= 4\ \pi$-electrons.
   * Matches $4n$ with $n=1$.
   * **Verdict: Anti-Aromatic.**

2. **Azulene ($\text{C}_{10}\text{H}_8$):**
   * Fused 7-membered and 5-membered rings.
   * Entire perimeter is planar and continuously conjugated.
   * $N_\pi = 5$ double bonds $= 10\ \pi$-electrons.
   * Matches $4n+2$ with $n=2$. It possesses a significant dipole moment ($1.08\text{ D}$) due to contribution of the aromatic charge-separated resonance form: $\text{[Cycloheptatrienyl]}^+ - \text{[Cyclopentadienyl]}^-$.
   * **Verdict: Aromatic.**

3. **Cyclooctatetraenyl Dianion ($\text{C}_8\text{H}_8^{2-}$):**
   * Adding two electrons to non-planar neutral COT gives $8 + 2 = 10\ \pi$-electrons.
   * To achieve aromatic stabilization, the ring flattens into a planar regular octagon.
   * Matches $4n+2$ with $n=2$.
   * **Verdict: Aromatic.**

4. **Pyran-4-one:**
   * Contains a carbonyl ($\text{C}=\text{O}$) group at position 4.
   * Polarization of the carbonyl bond: $>\text{C}=\text{O} \longleftrightarrow\ >\text{C}^+ - \text{O}^-$.
   * The ring carbocation provides an empty p-orbital, yielding a 6-membered ring with two endocyclic $\text{C}=\text{C}$ bonds ($4\pi$), a carbonyl carbocation carbon (empty p), and the ring oxygen donating its unhybridized lone pair ($2\pi$).
   * $N_\pi = 4 + 2 = 6\ \pi$-electrons in an unbroken cyclic system.
   * **Verdict: Aromatic.**

### Example 3: Acidity Influenced by Aromaticity
**Question:** Explain why cyclopentadiene exhibits an abnormally high acidity ($\text{p}K_a \approx 16$) compared to cycloheptatriene ($\text{p}K_a \approx 36$), despite both being cyclic polyenes.

**Step-by-Step Explanation:**
1. **Deprotonation of Cyclopentadiene:**

$$
\text{C}_5\text{H}_6 + \text{B}^- \rightleftharpoons \text{C}_5\text{H}_5^- (\text{Cyclopentadienyl anion}) + \text{BH}
$$

   * The conjugate base ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_5^-$) has $6\ \pi$-electrons in a planar, uninterrupted cyclic system ($4n+2, n=1$).
   * Deprotonation converts a non-aromatic hydrocarbon into an **extremely stable Aromatic anion**. The gain of aromatic resonance energy shifts the equilibrium to the right.

2. **Deprotonation of Cycloheptatriene:**

$$
\text{C}_7\text{H}_8 + \text{B}^- \rightleftharpoons \text{C}_7\text{H}_7^- (\text{Cycloheptatrienyl anion}) + \text{BH}
$$

   * Conjugate base ($\text{C}_7\text{H}_7^-$) contains $6\ \pi$-electrons from the three double bonds plus $2\ \pi$-electrons from the carbanion lone pair $= 8\ \pi$-electrons.
   * Matches $4n$ rule ($n=2$). If planar, this conjugate base is **Anti-Aromatic** and destabilized.
   * Consequently, loss of a proton is thermodynamically unfavorable, leading to a much higher $\text{p}K_a \approx 36$.

# Module 7: Examiner Traps & Common Student Pitfalls

> [!CAUTION]
> **Trap 1: Confusing "Total Electrons" with "$\pi$-Electrons" in Hückel Calculations**
> * *Error:* In pyridine ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_5\text{N}$), counting the nitrogen lone pair alongside the ring $\pi$-electrons, arriving at $8\ \pi$-electrons and concluding it is anti-aromatic.
> * *NCERT Correction:* The lone pair of pyridine resides in an $\text{sp}^2$ hybrid orbital that is in the molecular plane, strictly perpendicular to the vertical $2\text{p}_z$ $\pi$-system. It has zero overlap with the $\pi$-cloud. Pyridine has **only $6\ \pi$-electrons** and is aromatic.

> [!WARNING]
> **Trap 2: Ignoring Molecular Non-Planarity (The Cyclooctatetraene Trap)**
> * *Error:* Concluding that cyclooctatetraene (COT, $\text{C}_8\text{H}_8$) is *anti-aromatic* based solely on its $8\ \pi$-electron count ($4n$).
> * *NCERT Correction:* Anti-aromaticity is thermodynamically unfavorable. Molecules with sufficient ring flexibility undergo conformational distortion to escape it. COT adopts a non-planar **tub conformation**, making it **Non-Aromatic**, not anti-aromatic.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Trap 3: Overlooking $\text{sp}^3$ Interruption in the Ring**
> * *Error:* Classifying cyclohexa-1,3-diene or cyclopentadiene as anti-aromatic ($4\ \pi$-electrons).
> * *NCERT Correction:* Both molecules contain saturated $-\text{CH}_2-$ carbon atoms ($\text{sp}^3$ hybridized). The p-orbital conjugation path is broken. Any monocyclic system with an $\text{sp}^3$ ring carbon is **Non-Aromatic**, regardless of the $\pi$-electron count.

> [!WARNING]
> **Trap 4: Identifying Aromaticity Solely in Neutral Compounds**
> * *Error:* Searching only for alternating double bonds and missing ionic aromatic species like $\text{C}_3\text{H}_3^+$ or $\text{C}_7\text{H}_7^+$.
> * *NCERT Correction:* Hückel’s rule applies equally to neutral molecules, carbocations, carbanions, and heterocyclic compounds, provided the ring is planar and cyclic conjugation is maintained.

# Module 8: Speed Hacks, Mnemonics, and Golden Key Points

### 1. Frost-Musulin Circle Inscription Method (Molecular Orbital Mnemonics)
To rapidly find the $\pi$-molecular orbital energy levels and determine aromaticity/anti-aromaticity for any regular monocyclic conjugated ring:
1. Draw a circle of radius $2\beta$.
2. Inscribe the regular polygon with $N$ vertices inside the circle, **placing one vertex pointing directly downward at the bottom**.
3. Every vertex corresponds to a $\pi$-molecular orbital energy level.
4. Draw a horizontal center line through the center of the circle:
   * Orbitals **below** the line are **Bonding MOs (BMOs)**.
   * Orbitals **on** the line are **Non-Bonding MOs (NBMOs)**.
   * Orbitals **above** the line are **Anti-Bonding MOs (ABMOs)**.
5. Fill the orbitals with the available $\pi$-electrons using the Aufbau principle and Hund's rule:
   * **Aromatic:** Completely filled closed-shell electron configuration (all BMOs paired, no unpaired electrons in NBMO/ABMO).
   * **Anti-Aromatic:** Open-shell configuration with degenerate, half-filled orbitals (diradical character in NBMOs).

```
Frost Circle for Benzene (6-membered):
           _             <-- 1 Anti-bonding MO (alpha - 2beta)
        _     _          <-- 2 Anti-bonding MOs (alpha - beta)
=======================  <-- Non-bonding reference (alpha)
       [#]   [#]         <-- 2 Bonding MOs (alpha + beta) [Fully Paired]
           [#]           <-- 1 Bonding MO (alpha + 2beta) [Fully Paired]
        (Vertex Down)
Total: 6 pi-electrons completely fill all Bonding MOs -> AROMATIC STABILITY
```

# SECTION 1: Pedagogical Theory, Molecular Concepts & Fundamental Principles

### 1.1 Aromaticity & The $\pi$-Electron Cloud of Benzene (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
According to **Hückel's Rule of Aromaticity**, a planar, cyclic, completely conjugated system containing $(4n + 2)\;\pi\text{-electrons}$ (where $n = 0, 1, 2, 3 \dots$ is an integer) possesses exceptional thermodynamic stability known as **resonance energy** (or aromatic stabilization energy).
* Benzene ($C_6H_6$) possesses $n = 1$, giving $4(1) + 2 = 6\;\pi\text{-electrons}$.
* Each of the six $sp^2$-hybridized carbon atoms contributes one unhybridized $2p_z$ orbital perpendicular to the molecular plane.
* Lateral overlap of these six $2p_z$ orbitals generates a delocalized cyclic $\pi$-electron cloud distributed equally above and below the nodal plane of the ring carbon nuclei.
* **Resonance Stabilization Energy:** The experimental heat of hydrogenation of cyclohexene to cyclohexane is $\Delta H^\circ = -119.5\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$. A hypothetical cyclohexatriene with three isolated double bonds would have:
  

$$
\Delta H^\circ_{\text{theoretical}} = 3 \times (-119.5\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}) = -358.5\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}
$$

  The experimentally measured heat of hydrogenation for benzene is:
  

$$
\Delta H^\circ_{\text{experimental}} = -208.5\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}
$$

  

$$
\text{Resonance Stabilization Energy} = |\Delta H^\circ_{\text{theoretical}} - \Delta H^\circ_{\text{experimental}}| = 358.5 - 208.5 = 150.0\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}
$$

> **NCERT Key Law / Definition:**  
> Because of the high electron density of the delocalized $\pi$-cloud ($6\;\pi\text{-electrons}$ spread over 6 carbons), benzene behaves as an **electron donor (nucleophilic substrate)**. It readily attracts electron-deficient species called **electrophiles** ($E^+$). Benzene undergoes **substitution rather than addition** reactions because addition would disrupt the cyclic delocalization and destroy the $150.0\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ resonance stabilization energy. Substitution retains the stable $(4n + 2)\;\pi$-electron aromatic sextet.

***

### 1.2 Generalized Mechanism of Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution ($S_E\text{Ar}$) (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

The general $S_E\text{Ar}$ reaction involves three distinct elementary stages:

```
Step 1: Generation of the Electrophile (E⁺) via Lewis acid-base or Bronsted acid-base interaction.
Step 2: Formation of the Arenium Ion / σ-Complex (Wheland Intermediate) — Rate Determining Step (RDS).
Step 3: Loss of Proton (H⁺) from the sp³ carbon to restore aromaticity — Fast Step.
```

#### Step 1: Generation of the Electrophile ($E^+$)
The attacking electrophile is generated by heterolytic cleavage of a covalent bond, accelerated by a catalyst (e.g., anhydrous Lewis acids like $\text{AlCl}_3$, $\text{FeBr}_3$, or proton acids like concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$):

$$
E\text{---}Y + \text{Lewis Acid} \rightleftharpoons E^+ + [Y\text{---}\text{Lewis Acid}]^-
$$

#### Step 2: Formation of the Carbocation Intermediate (Arenium Ion / $\sigma$-Complex)
The delocalized $\pi$-electron pair attacks the electrophile $E^+$. One ring carbon transitions from $sp^2$ to $sp^3$ hybridization. This disrupts cyclic conjugation, leaving five $sp^2$ carbons sharing a single positive charge over the remaining four $\pi$-electrons:

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + E^+ \xrightarrow{\text{slow (RDS)}} [\text{C}_6\text{H}_6E]^+ \quad (\sigma\text{-complex / Arenium ion})
$$

Resonance structures of the Arenium Ion:
* **Canonical Structure I:** Positive charge localized at position 2 (*ortho* to $sp^3$ carbon).
* **Canonical Structure II:** Delocalization of $\pi$-bond moves positive charge to position 4 (*para*).
* **Canonical Structure III:** Delocalization moves positive charge to position 6 (*ortho*).

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Hybrid: } & \text{Positive charge is delocalized strictly over the } ortho \text{ and } para \text{ positions relative to the } sp^3 \text{ carbon;} \\
& \text{the } meta \text{ positions carry negligible formal positive charge.}
\end{aligned}
$$

Because cyclic aromatic delocalization is broken, this step possesses a high activation energy ($\Delta G^\ddagger_1$) and is the **Rate-Determining Step (RDS)**.

#### Step 3: Removal of Proton to Restore Aromaticity
A weak base present in the reaction medium (e.g., $[\text{AlCl}_4]^-$, $\text{HSO}_4^-$, or $\text{H}_2\text{O}$) abstracts the proton ($H^+$) from the $sp^3$ ring carbon. The electron pair of the $\text{C}\text{---}\text{H}$ $\sigma$-bond re-enters the ring $\pi$-system:

$$
[\text{C}_6\text{H}_6E]^+ + B^- \xrightarrow{\text{fast}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5E + H\text{---}B
$$

The carbon reverts to $sp^2$ hybridization, completely regenerating the planar $6\;\pi$-electron aromatic sextet.

***

### 1.3 Kinetic Isotope Effect (KIE) in $S_E\text{Ar}$ (⭐⭐⭐⭐)
The Primary Kinetic Isotope Effect is defined as the ratio of rate constants when hydrogen is replaced by deuterium:

$$
\text{KIE} = \frac{k_\text{H}}{k_\text{D}}
$$

* For **Nitration, Halogenation, and Friedel-Crafts reactions**:
  

$$
\frac{k_\text{H}}{k_\text{D}} \approx 1.0
$$

  *Interpretation:* Breaking of the $\text{C}\text{---}\text{H}$ bond occurs in Step 3 (the fast step), which occurs after the rate-determining step ($\text{RDS} = \text{Step 2}$). Thus, isotopic substitution of $\text{H}$ with $\text{D}$ or $\text{T}$ causes no observable change in the macroscopic reaction rate.
* **Exceptions (Reversible Substitutions):**
  * **Sulphonation** and **Iodination**: Step 3 (loss of proton) or the reverse of Step 2 has an activation energy comparable to Step 2 ($k_{-1} \approx k_2$). Hence, $\text{C}\text{---}\text{H}$ bond cleavage participates in the rate expression:
    

$$
\frac{k_\text{H}}{k_\text{D}} \approx 1.4 - 2.0 \quad (\text{Primary Isotope Effect is observed})
$$

### 1.4 Directive and Activating/Deactivating Influence of Monosubstituted Groups (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

When a functional group $-G$ is already bonded to the benzene ring:

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{---}G + E^+ \longrightarrow [o\text{-}, m\text{-}, \text{or } p\text{- isomer}]
$$

The group $-G$ dictates two distinct characteristics:
1. **Reactivity (Kinetic Control):** Whether the substituted ring reacts faster (Activated) or slower (Deactivated) than unsubstituted benzene.
2. **Orientation / Regiochemistry:** Which ring position (*ortho*, *meta*, or *para*) experiences the fastest electrophilic attack.

#### Electronic Effects Governing Directive Influence:
1. **Resonance Effect ($\pm R$ or $\pm M$):** Delocalization of lone pairs or $\pi$-electrons between the ring and $-G$.
2. **Inductive Effect ($\pm I$):** Polarization of $\sigma$-bonds due to electronegativity differences.

### 2.1 Halogenation (Chlorination & Bromination) (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

* **Overall Stoichiometric Equation:**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{Cl}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{anhyd. }\text{AlCl}_3,\; 310-320\text{ K},\; \text{dark}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} + \text{HCl}
$$

* **Step 1: Generation of the Chloronium Complex / Ion ($E^+$)**
  Anhydrous aluminum chloride has an incomplete octet on aluminum ($6$ valence electrons) and acts as a strong Lewis acid:
  

$$
\text{Cl}\text{---}\text{Cl} + \text{AlCl}_3 \rightleftharpoons \overset{\delta+}{\text{Cl}}\text{---}\overset{\delta-}{\text{Cl}}\text{---}\text{AlCl}_3 \rightleftharpoons \text{Cl}^+ + [\text{AlCl}_4]^-
$$

* **Step 2: Electrophilic Attack & $\sigma$-Complex Formation (RDS)**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{Cl}^+ \xrightarrow[\text{RDS}]{k_1} [\text{C}_6\text{H}_6\text{Cl}]^+
$$

  

$$
\begin{aligned}
  \text{Resonance structures: } & [\text{C}_6\text{H}_6\text{Cl}]^+ \leftrightarrow \text{ortho-carbocation} \leftrightarrow \text{para-carbocation} \leftrightarrow \text{ortho-carbocation}
  \end{aligned}
$$

* **Step 3: Aromatization via Proton Transfer (Fast)**
  

$$
[\text{C}_6\text{H}_6\text{Cl}]^+ + [\text{AlCl}_4]^- \xrightarrow[\text{fast}]{k_2} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} + \text{HCl} + \text{AlCl}_3
$$

  *(Catalyst $\text{AlCl}_3$ is regenerated).*

* **Fluorination & Iodination Peculiarities:**
  * **Fluorination** is violently exothermic ($\Delta H \ll 0$) and causes rupture of $\text{C}\text{---}\text{C}$ ring bonds. It is conducted via indirect methods (e.g., Balz-Schiemann reaction).
  * **Iodination** is reversible ($\Delta G^\circ \approx 0$) because $\text{HI}$ is a powerful reducing agent that reduces iodobenzene back to benzene:
    

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{I}_2 \rightleftharpoons \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{I} + \text{HI}
$$

    To drive the equilibrium forward by Le Chatelier's Principle, oxidizing agents like concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$ or $\text{HIO}_3$ are added to destroy $\text{HI}$:
    

$$
5\text{HI} + \text{HIO}_3 \longrightarrow 3\text{I}_2 + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

    

$$
2\text{HI} + 2\text{HNO}_3 \longrightarrow \text{I}_2 + 2\text{NO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

### 2.2 Nitration of Benzene (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

* **Overall Reaction:**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{HNO}_3(\text{conc.}) \xrightarrow[\text{323 - 333 K (50-60 }^\circ\text{C})]{\text{conc. }\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

* **Step 1: Generation of Nitronium Ion ($\text{NO}_2^+$)**
  Sulfuric acid is a stronger Brønsted-Lowry acid than nitric acid ($pK_a(\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4) \approx -3.0$, $pK_a(\text{HNO}_3) \approx -1.4$). Nitric acid acts as a **Brønsted base**:
  

$$
\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightleftharpoons \text{H}^+ + \text{HSO}_4^-
$$

  

$$
\text{H}\text{---}\text{O}\text{---}\text{NO}_2 + \text{H}^+ \rightleftharpoons \text{H}_2\overset{+}{\text{O}}\text{---}\text{NO}_2 \quad (\text{Protonated Nitric Acid})
$$

  

$$
\text{H}_2\overset{+}{\text{O}}\text{---}\text{NO}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{slow}} \text{H}_2\text{O} + \overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{O}_2 \quad (\text{Nitronium Ion, linear: } [\text{O}=\overset{+}{\text{N}}=\text{O}])
$$

  

$$
\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightleftharpoons \text{H}_3\text{O}^+ + \text{HSO}_4^-
$$

  **Net Ionic Activation Equation:**
  

$$
\text{HNO}_3 + 2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightleftharpoons \text{NO}_2^+ + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+ + 2\text{HSO}_4^-
$$

  *(Van 't Hoff factor $i = 4$ in pure $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ cryoscopy).*

* **Step 2: Formation of the Arenium Ion (RDS)**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{NO}_2^+ \xrightarrow{\text{slow (RDS)}} [\text{C}_6\text{H}_6\text{NO}_2]^+
$$

* **Step 3: Deprotonation (Fast)**
  

$$
[\text{C}_6\text{H}_6\text{NO}_2]^+ + \text{HSO}_4^- \xrightarrow{\text{fast}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4
$$

### 2.3 Sulphonation of Benzene (⭐⭐⭐⭐)

* **Overall Reaction:**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4(\text{fuming / oleum, }\text{H}_2\text{S}_2\text{O}_7) \xrightarrow{353\text{ K}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_3\text{H} + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

* **Step 1: Generation of the Electrophile (Neutral Sulfur Trioxide, $\text{SO}_3$)**
  

$$
2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightleftharpoons \text{SO}_3 + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+ + \text{HSO}_4^-
$$

  Although electrically neutral, the sulfur atom in $\text{SO}_3$ is intensely electrophilic due to formal double bonds with three highly electronegative oxygen atoms:
  

$$
\overset{\delta+}{\text{S}}(=\text{O})_3
$$

* **Step 2: Attack on Benzene Ring (RDS)**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{SO}_3 \rightleftharpoons [\text{C}_6\text{H}_6\text{---}\text{SO}_3^-]^+ \quad (\text{Zwitterionic Arenium Intermediate})
$$

* **Step 3: Deprotonation and Proton Re-shuffling**
  

$$
[\text{C}_6\text{H}_6\text{---}\text{SO}_3^-]^+ + \text{HSO}_4^- \rightleftharpoons \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_3^- + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4
$$

  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_3^- + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+ \rightleftharpoons \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_3\text{H} + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

> **Reversibility & Desulphonation:**  
> Sulphonation is readily reversible. Heating benzenesulphonic acid with superheated steam (dilute aqueous $\text{HCl}$ or $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ at $373-423\text{ K}$) causes desulphonation:
> 

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_3\text{H} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{dil. }\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4} \text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4
$$

> *Application:* $-\text{SO}_3\text{H}$ acts as a transient **blocking group** in organic syntheses.

### 2.4 Friedel-Crafts Alkylation & Acylation (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

#### A. Friedel-Crafts Alkylation
* **Reaction:**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{CH}_3\text{Cl} \xrightarrow{\text{anhyd. }\text{AlCl}_3} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_3 + \text{HCl}
$$

* **Mechanism:**
  

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{Cl} + \text{AlCl}_3 \rightleftharpoons [\text{CH}_3^{\delta+}\cdots\text{Cl}\cdots\text{AlCl}_3^{\delta-}] \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3^+ + [\text{AlCl}_4]^-
$$

  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{CH}_3^+ \xrightarrow{\text{slow}} [\text{C}_6\text{H}_6\text{CH}_3]^+ \xrightarrow[\text{fast}]{[\text{AlCl}_4]^-} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_3 + \text{HCl} + \text{AlCl}_3
$$

* **Critical Inherent Limitations of Alkylation:**
  1. **Carbocation Rearrangement:** Alkyl groups with $\ge 3$ carbons undergo $1,2\text{-hydride}$ or $1,2\text{-methyl}$ shifts to form a more stable carbocation before attacking the ring.
     

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{Cl} + \text{AlCl}_3 \longrightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{1,2\text{-H shift}} \text{CH}_3\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}\text{CH}_3
$$

     

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{CH}_3\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}\text{CH}_3 \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Cumene (Major, 65-70%)}}{\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}(\text{CH}_3)_2} + \underset{n\text{-propylbenzene (Minor, 30-35%)}}{\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3}
$$

  2. **Polyalkylation:** The alkyl product (e.g., toluene) is more activated than the starting benzene ring due to the $+I$ and hyperconjugative ($+H$) effect of $-\text{CH}_3$. Hence, toluene outcompetes benzene for remaining electrophile, yielding di- and tri-alkylated byproducts (*ortho* and *para*-xylenes).

#### B. Friedel-Crafts Acylation (Remedy for Rearrangement & Polyalkylation)
* **Reaction:**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{CH}_3\text{COCl} \xrightarrow{\text{anhyd. }\text{AlCl}_3} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COCH}_3 + \text{HCl}
$$

* **Mechanism:**
  

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{---}\overset{\text{O}}{\overset{\parallel}{\text{C}}}\text{---}\text{Cl} + \text{AlCl}_3 \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3\text{---}\overset{+}{\text{C}}=\text{O} \longleftrightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{---}\text{C}\equiv\overset{+}{\text{O}} \quad (\text{Acylium Ion, resonance stabilized octet}) + [\text{AlCl}_4]^-
$$

  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{CH}_3\text{CO}^+ \xrightarrow{\text{slow}} [\text{C}_6\text{H}_6\text{COCH}_3]^+ \xrightarrow[\text{fast}]{[\text{AlCl}_4]^-} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COCH}_3 + \text{HCl} + \text{AlCl}_3
$$

* **Why Acylation Avoids Alkylation Pitfalls:**
  * **No Rearrangement:** The acylium ion is resonance stabilized (every atom has an octet in $\text{R}\text{---}\text{C}\equiv\overset{+}{\text{O}}$) and does not undergo skeletal rearrangement.
  * **No Polyacylation:** The introduced acyl group ($-\text{COR}$) is strongly electron-withdrawing ($-R, -I$), which strongly deactivates the ring against further electrophilic attack.
* **Conversion to Linear Alkylbenzenes:**
  To synthesize pure, un-rearranged $n$-propylbenzene, carry out acylation followed by **Clemmensen Reduction** or **Wolff-Kishner Reduction**:
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{COCl} \xrightarrow{\text{anhyd. }\text{AlCl}_3} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COCH}_2\text{CH}_3 \xrightarrow[\text{conc. }\text{HCl}]{\text{Zn-Hg}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3
$$

# SECTION 3: Deep Dive into Directive Influence Mechanisms

### 3.1 Ortho/Para-Directing and Activating Groups (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

Consider $-G = -\text{OH}, -\text{NH}_2, -\text{OCH}_3, -\text{CH}_3$:
These groups possess either a lone pair on the atom directly attached to the ring ($+R$) or participate in hyperconjugation ($+H$).

#### Resonance Proof of *o/p*-Directing Ability ($+R$ Groups):
When $-G$ releases electrons into the ring via resonance:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Canonical Structures: } & \text{Neutral ground state shows negative formal charges accumulating} \\
& \text{exclusively at the } \mathbf{ortho} \text{ and } \mathbf{para} \text{ positions.}
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, an attacking electrophile ($E^+$) experiences maximum electrostatic attraction at the *ortho* and *para* positions.

#### Carbocation Intermediate ($\sigma$-Complex) Stability Analysis:
* **Attack at *ortho*-position (Attack at C-2):**
  Four canonical resonance forms are generated:
  1. Positive charge at C-3
  2. Positive charge at C-5
  3. Positive charge at C-1 directly below the substituent:
     

$$
\mathbf{C}_1^+ \text{---}\ddot{\text{O}}\text{H} \longleftrightarrow \mathbf{C}_1=\overset{+}{\text{O}}\text{H} \quad (\text{Extra stable form: All octets complete!})
$$

* **Attack at *para*-position (Attack at C-4):**
  Four canonical resonance forms are generated, including the uniquely stable structure where the positive charge is directly on C-1:
  

$$
\mathbf{C}_1^+ \text{---}\ddot{\text{O}}\text{H} \longleftrightarrow \mathbf{C}_1=\overset{+}{\text{O}}\text{H} \quad (\text{Extra stable form})
$$

* **Attack at *meta*-position (Attack at C-3):**
  Only three resonance forms can be drawn. The positive charge resides on C-2, C-4, and C-6:
  

$$
\text{The positive charge never resides on } \mathbf{C}_1\text{; hence, the lone pair cannot be donated into the ring.}
$$

  No complete-octet structure exists.

$$
\text{Activation Energy: } \Delta G^\ddagger(ortho), \Delta G^\ddagger(para) \ll \Delta G^\ddagger(meta)
$$

Hence, $+R$ groups are strictly **ortho/para-directing and activating**.

### 3.2 Meta-Directing and Deactivating Groups (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

Consider $-G = -\text{NO}_2, -\text{CN}, -\text{CHO}, -\text{COOH}, -\text{CF}_3, -\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{R}_3$:
These groups are bonded to the ring through an atom with a partial positive or formal positive charge, causing electron withdrawal via $-R$ and/or $-I$ effects.

#### Resonance Proof of *meta*-Directing Ability:
Electron withdrawal shifts electron density away from the ring:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Canonical Structures: } & \text{Formal positive charges are deposited directly at the } \\
& \mathbf{ortho} \text{ and } \mathbf{para} \text{ positions of the ring.}
\end{aligned}
$$

* The entire ring is deactivated relative to benzene, but the *ortho* and *para* positions are most strongly depleted of electron density.
* The *meta* positions are the *least deactivated* positions.

#### Carbocation Intermediate ($\sigma$-Complex) Stability Analysis for Nitrobenzene:
* **Attack at *ortho*-position:** Generates a structure with the positive charge on C-1, immediately adjacent to the positively charged nitrogen of $-\text{NO}_2$:
  

$$
\overset{+}{\text{C}}_1\text{---}\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{O}_2^- \quad (\mathbf{Extremely\; Unstable:}\; \text{adjacent like charges, high electrostatic repulsion})
$$

* **Attack at *para*-position:** Similarly generates an intermediate with the positive charge on C-1:
  

$$
\overset{+}{\text{C}}_1\text{---}\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{O}_2^- \quad (\mathbf{Extremely\; Unstable})
$$

* **Attack at *meta*-position:** The positive charge delocalizes over C-2, C-4, and C-6. The carbocation charge **never** lands on C-1.
  

$$
\text{Thus, the catastrophic repulsion } (\overset{+}{\text{C}}_1\text{---}\overset{+}{\text{N}}) \text{ is completely avoided!}
$$

$$
\Delta G^\ddagger(meta) < \Delta G^\ddagger(ortho), \Delta G^\ddagger(para)
$$

Because all positions are deactivated compared to benzene, attack occurs preferentially at the *meta* position at a much slower rate.

### 3.3 The Halogen Anomaly: Deactivating yet *o/p*-Directing (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

Halobenzenes ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl}, \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Br}$) present a unique dual-effect scenario:
* **Inductive Effect ($-I$):** Halogens are strongly electronegative ($\chi_\text{F} > \chi_\text{Cl} > \chi_\text{Br} > \chi_\text{I} > \chi_\text{C}$). They withdraw $\sigma$-electron density from the ring, destabilizing the arenium ion overall. Therefore, halogens are **deactivating** ($\text{Rate}_{\text{PhCl}} < \text{Rate}_{\text{PhH}}$).
* **Resonance Effect ($+R$):** The halogen possesses non-bonding lone pairs ($:!\ddot{\text{X}}:$) that can conjugate into the $\pi$-system.
  * For attack at *ortho* or *para* positions: A fourth resonance structure forms where halogen donates a lone pair to satisfy the octet of all atoms:
    

$$
\mathbf{C}_1^+ \text{---}\ddot{\text{Cl}}: \longleftrightarrow \mathbf{C}_1=\overset{+}{\text{Cl}}: \quad (\text{Complete octet structure})
$$

  * For attack at *meta* positions: Halogen lone pair participation is topologically impossible.

> **NCERT Synthesis of the Halogen Anomaly:**  
> The inductive effect ($-I$) controls the **overall net rate** (reactivity), making the ring deactivated compared to benzene.  
> The resonance effect ($+R$) controls the **regiochemistry** (orientation) by selectively stabilizing the *ortho* and *para* arenium intermediates relative to the *meta* intermediate.  
> **Result:** Halogens are **deactivating**, yet strictly **ortho/para-directing**.

# SECTION 4: High-Yield Worked Examples & Exemplar Archetypes

### Example 4.1: Predicting the Major Product in Multiple Alkylation vs Acylation (⭐⭐⭐⭐)
**Problem:** Predict the organic products $A$ and $B$ in the following transformations:
1. $\text{Benzene} + \text{1-chloropropane} \xrightarrow{\text{anhyd. }\text{AlCl}_3} A$
2. $\text{Benzene} + \text{propanoyl chloride} \xrightarrow{\text{anhyd. }\text{AlCl}_3} \text{Intermediate } \xrightarrow{\text{Zn-Hg / conc. HCl}} B$

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. **Formation of Product $A$:**
   

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{Cl} + \text{AlCl}_3 \longrightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2 + [\text{AlCl}_4]^- \quad (1^\circ\text{ carbocation})
$$

   A $1,2\text{-hydride}$ shift occurs rapidly:
   

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{1,2\text{-H shift}} \text{CH}_3\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}\text{CH}_3 \quad (2^\circ\text{ carbocation, more stable by hyperconjugation})
$$

   Attack of isopropyl cation on benzene ring yields isopropylbenzene (cumene) as the major product:
   

$$
\text{Product } A = \mathbf{C_6H_5CH(CH_3)_2 \quad (\text{Isopropylbenzene / Cumene})}
$$

2. **Formation of Product $B$:**
   

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{COCl} + \text{AlCl}_3 \longrightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\overset{+}{\text{C}}=\text{O} \longleftrightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{C}\equiv\overset{+}{\text{O}} + [\text{AlCl}_4]^-
$$

   The acylium ion cannot rearrange. Electrophilic attack on benzene yields propiophenone:
   

$$
\text{Intermediate} = \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COCH}_2\text{CH}_3
$$

   Clemmensen reduction converts the ketone carbonyl ($\text{C}=\text{O}$) into a methylene group ($-\text{CH}_2-$):
   

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COCH}_2\text{CH}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{Zn-Hg / conc. HCl}} \mathbf{C_6H_5CH_2CH_2CH_3 \quad (n\text{-Propylbenzene})}
$$

   

$$
\text{Product } B = \mathbf{C_6H_5CH_2CH_2CH_3 \quad (n\text{-Propylbenzene})}
$$

### Example 4.2: Regioselectivity with Multiple Substituents (NCERT Exemplar Archetype) (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
**Problem:** Predict the major mononitration product of $m$-nitrotoluene treated with $\text{conc. }\text{HNO}_3 / \text{conc. }\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. Draw the structural layout of $m$-nitrotoluene:
   * Position 1: $-\text{CH}_3$ (electron-donating group by $+I$ and $+H$; activating; *o/p*-directing).
   * Position 3: $-\text{NO}_2$ (electron-withdrawing group by $-I$ and $-R$; deactivating; *meta*-directing).
2. **Governing Rule:** When an activating group and a deactivating group compete, the **strongest activating (electron-donating) group directs orientation**. Here, $-\text{CH}_3$ controls regiochemistry:
   * *ortho* to $-\text{CH}_3$: Positions C-2, C-6
   * *para* to $-\text{CH}_3$: Position C-4
3. **Steric Evaluation:**
   * **C-2:** Position flanked between $-\text{CH}_3$ and $-\text{NO}_2$. This position experiences strong steric hindrance. Nitration at C-2 yields $< 1\%$.
   * **C-4:** *para* to $-\text{CH}_3$ and *ortho* to $-\text{NO}_2$. Directing effects reinforce here ($-\text{CH}_3$ directs *para*; $-\text{NO}_2$ directs *meta* to itself at C-5, not C-4).
   * **C-6:** *ortho* to $-\text{CH}_3$ and *para* to $-\text{NO}_2$. Sterically accessible, and $-\text{NO}_2$ directs *meta* to C-5.
   * Evaluating overall resonance stabilization: Attack at C-4 and C-6 provides arenium ions stabilized by $-\text{CH}_3$ hyperconjugation. C-4 product is strongly favored:
     

$$
\mathbf{\text{Major Product: 4-methyl-1,2-dinitrobenzene (2-nitro-4-methylnitrobenzene)}}
$$

### Example 4.3: Absolute Rate Comparison in EAS (⭐⭐⭐⭐)
**Problem:** Arrange the following in decreasing order of their rate of electrophilic chlorination:

$$
\text{Benzene (I), Toluene (II), Nitrobenzene (III), Chlorobenzene (IV), Anisol (V)}
$$

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. Evaluate substituent electronic effects:
   * Anisole ($\text{V}$): $-\text{OCH}_3$ ($+R > -I$, strong electron-donating activator).
   * Toluene ($\text{II}$): $-\text{CH}_3$ ($+H, +I$, moderate activator).
   * Benzene ($\text{I}$): Substrate reference standard ($H$ has no net inductive/resonance effect).
   * Chlorobenzene ($\text{IV}$): $-\text{Cl}$ ($-I > +R$, weakly deactivating).
   * Nitrobenzene ($\text{III}$): $-\text{NO}_2$ ($-R, -I$, strongly deactivating).
2. Establish ranking based on arenium ion activation energy ($\Delta G^\ddagger$):
   

$$
\mathbf{V > II > I > IV > III}
$$

# SECTION 5: Examiner Traps & Common Student Mistakes

### Trap 1: Aniline in Friedel-Crafts Reactions
* **The Mistake:** Writing that aniline ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2$) undergoes Friedel-Crafts alkylation/acylation to yield $o$- and $p$-alkylanilines.
* **The Reality:** Aniline does **not** undergo Friedel-Crafts reactions. The Lewis acid catalyst ($\text{AlCl}_3$) coordinates to the lone pair of the basic nitrogen:
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\ddot{\text{N}}\text{H}_2 + \text{AlCl}_3 \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}_2\text{---}\overset{-}{\text{Al}}\text{Cl}_3
$$

  This creates a strong $-I$ group ($-\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}_2\text{AlCl}_3^-$), which heavily deactivates the ring against electrophilic attack.

***

### Trap 2: Direct Nitration of Aniline
* **The Mistake:** Expecting only $o$- and $p$-nitroaniline from direct nitration of aniline using nitrating mixture ($\text{HNO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$).
* **The Reality:** In strongly acidic media, aniline undergoes protonation to the anilinium ion:
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{H}^+ \rightleftharpoons \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}_3
$$

  The $-\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}_3$ group is strongly deactivating and *meta*-directing. Consequently, a substantial yield of $m$-nitroaniline forms:
  * $p$-nitroaniline: $51\%$
  * $m$-nitroaniline: $47\%$
  * $o$-nitroaniline: $2\%$
* **Synthetic Remedy:** Protect the amino group via acetylation with acetic anhydride ($\text{Ac}_2\text{O}$) to form acetanilide ($-\text{NHCOCH}_3$), run nitration to obtain $p$-nitroacetanilide, and hydrolyze back to pure $p$-nitroaniline.

***

### Trap 3: Nitrobenzene as a Solvent in Friedel-Crafts Alkylation
* **The Trap:** Asking why nitrobenzene is frequently used as a solvent in Friedel-Crafts reactions of other substrates.
* **The Explanation:** The $-\text{NO}_2$ group is so strongly deactivating that nitrobenzene itself does not undergo Friedel-Crafts alkylation or acylation, allowing it to act as an inert polar solvent.

***

### Trap 4: Kinetic Isotope Effect Misconceptions
* **The Mistake:** Assuming deutero-benzene ($\text{C}_6\text{D}_6$) nitrates slower than benzene ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_6$).
* **The Reality:** In nitration, Step 2 ($\sigma$-complex formation) is rate-determining, while $\text{C}\text{---}\text{H}$ or $\text{C}\text{---}\text{D}$ bond breakage occurs during Step 3 (fast). Therefore:
  

$$
\frac{k_\text{H}}{k_\text{D}} \approx 1.0
$$

  Deuterated benzene nitrates at essentially the same rate.

***

# SECTION 6: Speed Hacks, Memorization Mnemonics & Golden Points

### 6.1 The "Atom Connected to Ring" Shortcut
To determine directive influence in under 5 seconds:
* **Rule A (Lone Pair / Saturated):** If the atom directly attached to the benzene ring has a **lone pair** (e.g., $-\ddot{\text{O}}\text{H}, -\ddot{\text{N}}\text{H}_2, -\ddot{\text{C}}\text{l}:$) OR is part of an **alkyl chain** ($-\text{CH}_3$), it is **ortho/para-directing**.
* **Rule B (Multiple Bonds to Electronegative Atoms):** If the atom directly attached to the ring is connected by a **double or triple bond** to a more electronegative atom (e.g., $-\text{C}=\text{O}, -\text{N}=\text{O}, -\text{C}\equiv\text{N}, -\text{S}=\text{O}$), or carries a **formal positive charge** ($-\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{R}_3$), it is **meta-directing**.

***

### 6.2 The Ortho/Para Ratio Steric Rule
* Electrophilic attack on a monosubstituted ring statistically has two *ortho* positions and one *para* position:
  

$$
\text{Statistical Ratio: } \frac{\text{ortho}}{\text{para}} = \frac{2}{1} = 2.0
$$

* **Actual Experimental Ratio:** In practice, the *para* isomer is typically the **major product** due to:
  1. Steric hindrance between the substituent $-G$ and the incoming electrophile at the *ortho* position.
  2. Greater symmetry of the *para* isomer, which often leads to a higher melting point and easier crystallization.
* **Exception:** When intramolecular hydrogen bonding stabilizes the *ortho* transition state or product (e.g., nitration of phenol yields $o$-nitrophenol as a substantial component separable by steam distillation).

***

### 6.3 Golden Points for Speed Solving
* **$E^+$ Summary Table:**
  

$$
\begin{aligned}
  \text{Nitration:} & \quad E^+ = \text{NO}_2^+ \quad (\text{Nitronium ion}) \\
  \text{Chlorination:} & \quad E^+ = \text{Cl}^+ \quad (\text{Chloronium ion}) \\
  \text{Sulphonation:} & \quad E^+ = \text{SO}_3 \quad (\text{Neutral Sulfur Trioxide}) \\
  \text{Alkylation:} & \quad E^+ = \text{R}^+ \quad (\text{Carbocation, can rearrange}) \\
  \text{Acylation:} & \quad E^+ = \text{R---}\overset{+}{\text{C}}=\text{O} \quad (\text{Acylium ion, does NOT rearrange})
  \end{aligned}
$$

* **Deactivating groups that cannot undergo Friedel-Crafts reactions at all:**
  Ring systems containing groups moderately or strongly more deactivating than halogens (e.g., $-\text{NO}_2, -\text{CF}_3, -\text{SO}_3\text{H}, -\text{CHO}, -\text{COOH}$) fail to react in Friedel-Crafts alkylation or acylation reactions.

### Question 1: Case-Based / Data-Driven Quantitative Scenario
**Tag:** [CBSE Competency Pattern, Case-Based, 4 Marks]  
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

#### Context & Scenario:
In an industrial petrochemical laboratory, chemical engineers are optimizing the free-radical halogenation and catalytic cracking of petroleum fractions to synthesize valuable feedstocks for polymer and fuel applications. 

During an experiment, a technician subjects $2\text{-methylbutane}$ to photochemical monobromination at $400\text{ K}$ and monochlorination at $298\text{ K}$. The analytical team evaluates the kinetic product distributions alongside thermodynamic stability:
- Relative reactivity ratios of hydrogen abstraction by chlorine radicals ($\text{Cl}^\bullet$) at $298\text{ K}$:  
  $3^\circ : 2^\circ : 1^\circ = 5.0 : 3.8 : 1.0$
- Relative reactivity ratios of hydrogen abstraction by bromine radicals ($\text{Br}^\bullet$) at $400\text{ K}$:  
  $3^\circ : 2^\circ : 1^\circ = 1600 : 82 : 1$

Simultaneously, the cracking furnace converts $n\text{-hexane}$ into lower alkenes and alkanes over a zeolite catalyst ($\text{Pt/Al}_2\text{O}_3/\text{SiO}_2$) at $773\text{ K}$.

#### Sub-Questions:
1. **(1 Mark)** Calculate the theoretical percentage yield of the major monobromo derivative obtained from $2\text{-methylbutane}$.
2. **(1 Mark)** Explain why bromination exhibits near absolute regioselectivity compared to chlorination in terms of transition state Hammond Postulate and reaction enthalpy ($\Delta_r H^\circ$).
3. **(2 Marks)** Give the balanced chemical equations for:
   - (a) The aromatization (catalytic reforming) of $n\text{-hexane}$ under $\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_3/\text{V}_2\text{O}_5$ at $773\text{ K}$, $10\text{--}20\text{ atm}$.
   - (b) The initiation and radical propagation steps of the photochemical monobromination at the tertiary carbon of $2\text{-methylbutane}$.

***

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Scheme:

#### 1. Calculation of Percentage Yield:
- **Identification of hydrogens in $2\text{-methylbutane}$:**  
  

$$
\text{Structure: }\overset{1}{\text{C}}\text{H}_3\text{--}\overset{2}{\text{C}}\text{H}(\text{CH}_3)\text{--}\overset{3}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2\text{--}\overset{4}{\text{C}}\text{H}_3
$$

  - $1^\circ\text{ H}$ atoms: $6\text{ (at C1 & branch)} + 3\text{ (at C4)} = 9\text{ H}$
  - $2^\circ\text{ H}$ atoms: $2\text{ H (at C3)}$
  - $3^\circ\text{ H}$ atoms: $1\text{ H (at C2)}$  
  *(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{\frac{1}{2}\text{ Mark}}$ for correct hydrogen classification)*

- **Relative Reaction Rates:**
  

$$
\text{Rate}(1^\circ) = 9 \times 1 = 9
$$

  

$$
\text{Rate}(2^\circ) = 2 \times 82 = 164
$$

  

$$
\text{Rate}(3^\circ) = 1 \times 1600 = 1600
$$

  

$$
\text{Total Relative Reactivity} = 9 + 164 + 1600 = 1773
$$

- **Percentage of Major Product ($2\text{-bromo-2-methylbutane}$):**
  

$$
\% \text{ Yield} = \frac{1600}{1773} \times 100 = 90.24\% \approx 90.2\%
$$

  
  *(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{\frac{1}{2}\text{ Mark}}$ for relative rate calculation and final percentage)*

***

#### 2. Regioselectivity, Enthalpy & Hammond's Postulate:
- The abstraction of hydrogen by $\text{Cl}^\bullet$ is exothermic ($\Delta H^\circ < 0$), whereas abstraction by $\text{Br}^\bullet$ is endothermic ($\Delta H^\circ > 0$).
- By **Hammond's Postulate**, for the endothermic bromination, the transition state is late (product-like), resembling the free radical intermediate. Consequently, stability differences of the forming alkyl radicals ($3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ$) are strongly reflected in the activation energy barriers ($E_a$), conferring high regioselectivity.
- For chlorination, the transition state is early (reactant-like); structural differences in radicals have less influence on $E_a$, leading to low selectivity.  
*(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{\frac{1}{2}\text{ Mark}}$ for identifying endothermic vs. exothermic nature; $\mathbf{\frac{1}{2}\text{ Mark}}$ for applying Hammond's postulate / transition state resemblance)*

***

#### 3. Balanced Chemical Equations & Mechanism:
- **(a) Aromatization of $n\text{-hexane}$:**
  

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{(CH}_2)_4\text{CH}_3 \xrightarrow[\text{773 K, 10--20 atm}]{\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_3/\text{V}_2\text{O}_5/\text{Mo}_2\text{O}_3} \text{C}_6\text{H}_6\text{ (Benzene)} + 4\text{H}_2\uparrow
$$

  
  *(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{1\text{ Mark}}$ for balanced reaction and reagents)*

- **(b) Radical mechanism steps:**
  

$$
\text{Initiation: } \text{Br}_2 \xrightarrow{h\nu} 2\text{Br}^\bullet
$$

  

$$
\text{Propagation Step 1: } (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CHCH}_2\text{CH}_3 + \text{Br}^\bullet \longrightarrow (\text{CH}_3)_2\dot{\text{C}}\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3 + \text{HBr}
$$

  

$$
\text{Propagation Step 2: } (\text{CH}_3)_2\dot{\text{C}}\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3 + \text{Br}_2 \longrightarrow (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{C(Br)CH}_2\text{CH}_3 + \text{Br}^\bullet
$$

  
  *(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{1\text{ Mark}}$ for both propagation steps showing radical species clearly)*

### Question 2: Environmental & Petrochemical Scenario

#### Context & Scenario:
In urban traffic zones, incomplete combustion of gasoline generates severe atmospheric pollutants. To assess engine knock, refiners employ the "Octane Rating" scale where $2,2,4\text{-trimethylpentane}$ is assigned an octane number of $100$ and $n\text{-heptane}$ is assigned $0$.

An environmental monitoring station records high levels of ground-level tropospheric ozone ($\text{O}_3$) and photochemical smog during summer afternoons. The analytical data reveals high concentrations of peroxyacetyl nitrate (PAN) and formaldehyde ($\text{HCHO}$). The source is tracked to unburned volatile terminal alkenes escaping automotive exhausts.

```
Automotive Exhaust (Alkenes) + NO_x + Sunlight
```

1. **(1 Mark)** A volatile alkene $\mathbf{A}$ ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}$) undergoes reductive ozonolysis ($\text{O}_3/\text{Zn-H}_2\text{O}$) to yield methanal ($\text{HCHO}$) and butan-$2$-one. Deduce the IUPAC name and structure of alkene $\mathbf{A}$.

3. **(2 Marks)** Explain:
   - (a) Why does catalytic converter installation ($\text{Pt/Rh/Pd}$) in automobiles prevent photochemical smog formation?

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Scheme:

#### 1. Identification of Alkene A:
- Reductive ozonolysis cleaves the double bond into carbonyl groups:
  

$$
\mathbf{A} + \text{O}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{Zn/H}_2\text{O}} \text{HCHO} + \text{CH}_3\text{--C}(=\text{O})\text{--CH}_2\text{CH}_3
$$

- Joining the two carbonyl carbons via a double bond:
  

$$
\text{H}_2\text{C}=\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)\text{--CH}_2\text{CH}_3
$$

- **IUPAC Name:** $2\text{-methylbut-1-ene}$  
*(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{\frac{1}{2}\text{ Mark}}$ for deduction; $\mathbf{\frac{1}{2}\text{ Mark}}$ for IUPAC name)*

#### 2. Anti-Markovnikov Addition & Peroxide Effect:
- **Reaction:**
  

$$
\text{CH}_2=\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3 + \text{HBr} \xrightarrow{(\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CO})_2\text{O}_2} \text{BrCH}_2\text{--CH}(\text{CH}_3)\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3
$$

- **Major Product:** $1\text{-bromo-2-methylbutane}$.
- **Mechanism:**
  - Peroxide dissociates into alkoxy/carboxylate radicals which react with $\text{HBr}$ to generate a bromine free radical ($\text{Br}^\bullet$).
  - Electrophilic addition of $\text{Br}^\bullet$ to the terminal double bond produces a stable tertiary free radical:
    

$$
\text{CH}_2=\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3 + \text{Br}^\bullet \longrightarrow \text{BrCH}_2\text{--}\dot{\text{C}}(\text{CH}_3)\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3 \quad (3^\circ \text{ radical, more stable})

  - The $3^\circ$ radical abstracts $\text{H}^\bullet$ from $\text{HBr}$ to regenerate $\text{Br}^\bullet$.

#### 3. Catalytic Converters & Newman Projections:

#### Q3.1

- **Assertion (A):** The boiling point of $n\text{-pentane}$ ($309.1\text{ K}$) is significantly higher than that of $2,2\text{-dimethylpropane}$ (neopentane, $282.5\text{ K}$).

#### Q3.2
**Tag:** [CBSE Competency Pattern, Assertion-Reason, 1 Mark]  
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

- **Assertion (A):** Propene reacts with $\text{HBr}$ in the presence of benzoyl peroxide to yield $1\text{-bromopropane}$, but propene reacts with $\text{HCl}$ under identical conditions to yield exclusively $2\text{-chloropropane}$.
- **Reason (R):** The $\text{H--Cl}$ bond dissociation enthalpy ($430.5\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$) is significantly higher than the $\text{H--Br}$ bond dissociation enthalpy ($363.7\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$), making the homolytic cleavage of $\text{HCl}$ by free radicals endothermic and kinetically non-viable.

**Correct Option:** **(a)**  
**Step-Marking / Justification:**  
- The peroxide effect (Kharasch effect) operates only with $\text{HBr}$. For $\text{HCl}$, step 1 of propagation (abstraction of $\text{H}$ from $\text{H--Cl}$ by alkyl/peroxy radical) is endothermic due to the high bond enthalpy of $\text{H--Cl}$. For $\text{HI}$, the $\text{H--I}$ bond is weak, but iodine radicals readily dimerize into $\text{I}_2$ rather than adding to the double bond.  
- Thus, $\text{HCl}$ undergoes normal heterolytic electrophilic addition via Markovnikov's rule.  
*(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{1\text{ Mark}}$ for recognizing thermodynamic unfavourability of the propagation step)*

#### Q3.3
**Tag:** [CBSE Competency Pattern, Assertion-Reason, 1 Mark]  
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ [High-Yield]

- **Assertion (A):** Cyclooctatetraene ($\text{C}_8\text{H}_8$) is non-aromatic and rapidly decolourizes bromine water, despite having a completely conjugated cyclic system of alternating single and double bonds.
- **Reason (R):** A planar cyclic conjugated system with $8\pi$ electrons would be anti-aromatic ($4n\pi$ where $n=2$); hence the molecule puckers into a non-planar "tub-shaped" conformation to relieve anti-aromatic destabilization.

**Correct Option:** **(a)**  
**Step-Marking / Justification:**  
- According to Hückel's rule, planar rings with $4n\pi$ electrons are anti-aromatic and unstable. Cyclooctatetraene escapes this destabilization by distorting out of planarity into a tub conformation.  
- Being non-planar, the p-orbitals cannot achieve cyclic overlap; it behaves as an open-chain polyene, readily undergoing addition reactions.  
*(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{1\text{ Mark}}$ for Hückel rule and conformation analysis)*

- **Assertion (A):** Ethyne is significantly more acidic than ethene and ethane.
- **Reason (R):** The carbon atoms in ethyne are $sp$ hybridized with $50\%$ s-character, which pulls the electron density of the $\text{C--H}$ $\sigma$-bond closer to the carbon nucleus, facilitating the release of the proton.

### Question 4: Higher Order Thinking Skills (HOTS) Multi-Concept Reaction Cascade
**Tag:** [CBSE Competency Pattern, HOTS, 5 Marks]  
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

#### Problem Statement:
An aliphatic alkyne $\mathbf{A}$ ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_8$) does not form any precipitate when treated with ammoniacal silver nitrate solution ($\text{Tollens' reagent}$). 

1. When alkyne $\mathbf{A}$ is hydrogenated with $\text{H}_2$ over Lindlar's catalyst ($\text{Pd/CaCO}_3$, lead acetate poisoned), it yields compound $\mathbf{B}$ ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}$).
2. When alkyne $\mathbf{A}$ is treated with sodium in liquid ammonia ($\text{Na/liq. NH}_3$) at $240\text{ K}$, it yields an isomeric compound $\mathbf{C}$ ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}$).
3. When compound $\mathbf{B}$ is reacted with dilute, alkaline $\text{KMnO}_4$ ($273\text{ K}$, Baeyer’s reagent), it forms a stereoisomer $\mathbf{D}$ ($2,3\text{-pentanediol}$).
4. When alkyne $\mathbf{A}$ is subjected to hydration with $\text{HgSO}_4/\text{dil. H}_2\text{SO}_4$ at $333\text{ K}$, it yields a single ketone $\mathbf{E}$ ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}\text{O}$) and a structural isomer $\mathbf{F}$ in equal amounts.

#### Tasks:
- Deduce the structural formulas of compounds $\mathbf{A}$, $\mathbf{B}$, $\mathbf{C}$, $\mathbf{D}$, $\mathbf{E}$, and $\mathbf{F}$.
- Identify the stereochemical relationship between $\mathbf{B}$ and $\mathbf{C}$.
- Explain using 3D wedge-dash or Fischer projection why $\mathbf{D}$ is an optically active pair or meso compound.
- Write balanced chemical equations for the conversions $\mathbf{A} \to \mathbf{B}$ and $\mathbf{A} \to \mathbf{C}$.

***

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Scheme:

#### Step 1: Structural Deduction of Alkyne A
- The molecular formula $\text{C}_5\text{H}_8$ indicates two degrees of unsaturation:
  

$$
\text{Index of Hydrogen Deficiency (IHD)} = 5 - \frac{8}{2} + 1 = 2
$$

- Because it gives no precipitate with ammoniacal $\text{AgNO}_3$, it lacks an acidic terminal acetylenic hydrogen ($\text{--C}\equiv\text{C--H}$).
- Therefore, the triple bond must be internal:
  

$$
\text{Structure of A: } \text{CH}_3\text{--C}\equiv\text{C--CH}_2\text{CH}_3 \quad (\text{Pent-2-yne})
$$

  
*(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{1\text{ Mark}}$ for deducing $\text{Pent-2-yne}$ with chemical justification)*

***

#### Step 2: Stereochemical Analysis of Reduction Products (B & C)
- **Catalytic Hydrogenation over Lindlar's catalyst:**  
  Syn-addition of hydrogen across the alkyne yields the **cis-alkene**:
  

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{--C}\equiv\text{C--CH}_2\text{CH}_3 + \text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Pd/CaCO}_3\text{, quinoline}} \text{\textit{cis}-pent-2-ene (B)}
$$

  ```
       H       H
        \     /
         C = C
        /     \
      CH3     CH2CH3
  ```

- **Birch Reduction ($\text{Na/liq. NH}_3$):**  
  Anti-addition via dissolving metal reduction yields the **trans-alkene**:
  

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{--C}\equiv\text{C--CH}_2\text{CH}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{Na/liq. NH}_3} \text{\textit{trans}-pent-2-ene (C)}
$$

  ```
      CH3      H
        \     /
         C = C
        /     \
       H      CH2CH3
  ```
- **Relationship:** $\mathbf{B}$ and $\mathbf{C}$ are **geometrical isomers (diastereomers)**.  
*(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{1\text{ Mark}}$ for correct structures of B and C with stereochemical descriptors)*

***

#### Step 3: Stereochemistry of Compound D (Baeyer's Oxidation)
- Alkaline $\text{KMnO}_4$ carries out **syn-dihydroxylation**.
- Syn-addition of two $-\text{OH}$ groups to a *cis*-alkene with two non-identical ends ($-\text{CH}_3$ and $-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3$) gives an equimolar mixture of enantiomers (threo/erythro pair, specifically a racemic erythro pair). It is **not** a meso compound because the two terminal groups are unequal ($-\text{CH}_3 \neq -\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3$).

```
        CH3
         |
      H--+--OH
         |
      H--+--OH
         |
       CH2CH3
  (2R, 3S and 2S, 3R pair - Chiral, Optically Active)
```
*(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{1\text{ Mark}}$ for explaining syn-addition and absence of a plane of symmetry)*

***

#### Step 4: Hydration Products (E & F)
- Hydration of $\text{Pent-2-yne}$ with $\text{Hg}^{2+}/\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ involves electrophilic addition of water across the unsymmetrical internal triple bond:
  

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{--C}\equiv\text{C--CH}_2\text{CH}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow[\text{333 K}]{\text{HgSO}_4, \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4} [\text{Enol intermediates}] \rightleftharpoons
$$

  

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{--C}(=\text{O})\text{--CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3\text{ (Pentan-2-one, E)} + \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{--C}(=\text{O})\text{--CH}_2\text{CH}_3\text{ (Pentan-3-one, F)}
$$

  
*(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{1\text{ Mark}}$ for enol-keto tautomerism and identifying pentan-2-one and pentan-3-one)*

***

#### Step 5: Chemical Equations Summary

$$
\text{Conversion A} \to \text{B: } \text{CH}_3\text{C}\equiv\text{CCH}_2\text{CH}_3 + \text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Lindlar's Cat.}} \text{\textit{cis}-CH}_3\text{CH}=\text{CHCH}_2\text{CH}_3
$$

$$
\text{Conversion A} \to \text{C: } \text{CH}_3\text{C}\equiv\text{CCH}_2\text{CH}_3 \xrightarrow{2\text{Na} + 2\text{NH}_3} \text{\textit{trans}-CH}_3\text{CH}=\text{CHCH}_2\text{CH}_3 + 2\text{NaNH}_2
$$

  
*(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{1\text{ Mark}}$ for both balanced chemical equations)*

### Question 5: NCERT Exercise 13.8 — Friedel-Crafts Alkylation Anomaly
**Tag:** [NCERT Exercise Q13.8, 3 Marks]  
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

#### Problem Statement:
Why is benzene extraordinarily stable, and why does the reaction of benzene with $1\text{-chloropropane}$ in the presence of anhydrous $\text{AlCl}_3$ produce isopropylbenzene (cumene) as the major product instead of $n\text{-propylbenzene}$? Formulate the complete reaction mechanism with carbocation rearrangement.

***

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Scheme:

#### Step 1: Stability of Benzene
Benzene has a planar cyclic hexagonal ring of $sp^2$ hybridized carbons with six delocalized $\pi$ electrons satisfying Hückel's Rule ($4n+2$, where $n=1$). The resonance stabilization energy of benzene is approximately $150.6\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ ($36\text{ kcal mol}^{-1}$), rendering it thermodynamically resistant to addition reactions that would disrupt its aromatic sextet.  
*(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{\frac{1}{2}\text{ Mark}}$ for Hückel rule, delocalization, and resonance energy)*

***

#### Step 2: Generation and Rearrangement of Electrophile
1. Complexation of $1\text{-chloropropane}$ with anhydrous $\text{AlCl}_3$:
   

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{Cl} + \text{AlCl}_3 \rightleftharpoons [\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2^{\delta+}\cdots\text{Cl}\cdots\text{AlCl}_3^{\delta-}] \longrightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2 + [\text{AlCl}_4]^-
$$

   *(Primary carbocation, $1^\circ$ - less stable)*

2. $1,2\text{-Hydride Shift:}$
   

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{--CH}_2\text{--}\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{1,2\text{-hydride shift}} \text{CH}_3\text{--}\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H--CH}_3
$$

   *(Secondary carbocation, $2^\circ$ - stabilized by 6 hyperconjugative $\alpha$-hydrogens)*  
*(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{1\text{ Mark}}$ for Lewis acid interaction and $1,2$-hydride shift)*

***

#### Step 3: Electrophilic Attack & Arenium Ion (Wheland Intermediate) Formation
Electrophilic attack of the secondary carbocation on the benzene $\pi$-electron cloud forms the resonance-stabilized $\sigma$-complex (arenium ion):

```
        H       +
         \     / CH(CH3)2
          \   /
         /-----\
        |   +   |  <---> (Resonance structures delocalizing positive charge 
         \_____/          over ortho and para positions)
```

$$
\text{Arenium Ion} + [\text{AlCl}_4]^- \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{--CH}(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{ (Cumene)} + \text{HCl} + \text{AlCl}_3
$$

- **Product Ratio:** Cumene ($\approx 65\text{--}70\%$, major) vs $n\text{-propylbenzene}$ ($\approx 30\text{--}35\%$, minor).  
*(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{1\frac{1}{2}\text{ Marks}}$ for arenium ion resonance structures and deprotonation regenerating $\text{AlCl}_3$)*

### Question 6: NCERT Exercise 13.16 — Directing Influence & Activating Mechanisms
**Tag:** [NCERT Exercise Q13.16, 4 Marks]

#### Problem Statement:
How will you convert benzene into:
1. $p\text{-Nitrotoluene}$
2. $m\text{-Nitrochlorobenzene}$
3. Acetophenone
4. $p\text{-Chlorotoluene}$

Clearly explain the sequence of each synthetic pathway, stating why the order of synthetic steps cannot be reversed based on the directing and activating/deactivating properties of substituents.

***

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Scheme:

#### 1. Conversion of Benzene into $p\text{-Nitrotoluene}$
- **Step 1: Friedel-Crafts Methylation:**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{CH}_3\text{Cl} \xrightarrow{\text{Anhyd. AlCl}_3} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_3\text{ (Toluene)} + \text{HCl}
$$

- **Step 2: Nitration:**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_3 + \text{conc. HNO}_3 \xrightarrow[\text{313--323 K}]{\text{conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4} p\text{-Nitrotoluene (Major)} + o\text{-Nitrotoluene (Minor)}
$$

- *Why order matters:* If nitration were done first, nitrobenzene would form. The $-\text{NO}_2$ group is strongly deactivating; hence nitrobenzene does not undergo Friedel-Crafts alkylation.  
*(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{1\text{ Mark}}$ for correct sequence with justification)*

***

#### 2. Conversion of Benzene into $m\text{-Nitrochlorobenzene}$
- **Step 1: Nitration:**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{conc. HNO}_3 \xrightarrow[\text{323--333 K}]{\text{conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NO}_2\text{ (Nitrobenzene)} + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

- **Step 2: Chlorination:**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NO}_2 + \text{Cl}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Anhyd. FeCl}_3\text{, heat}} m\text{-Chloronitrobenzene} + \text{HCl}
$$

- *Why order matters:* $-\text{NO}_2$ is a meta-director. If chlorination were performed first, $-\text{Cl}$ is an ortho/para director, yielding $o$- and $p$-chloronitrobenzene instead of the desired meta-isomer.  
*(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{1\text{ Mark}}$ for nitration first followed by chlorination)*

***

#### 3. Conversion of Benzene into Acetophenone
- **Friedel-Crafts Acylation:**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{CH}_3\text{COCl} \xrightarrow{\text{Anhyd. AlCl}_3} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COCH}_3\text{ (Acetophenone)} + \text{HCl}
$$

  *(Alternatively using acetic anhydride, $(\text{CH}_3\text{CO})_2\text{O}$)*  
*(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{1\text{ Mark}}$ for balanced reaction and Lewis acid catalyst)*

***

#### 4. Conversion of Benzene into $p\text{-Chlorotoluene}$
- **Step 1: Methylation:**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{CH}_3\text{Cl} \xrightarrow{\text{Anhyd. AlCl}_3} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_3 + \text{HCl}
$$

- **Step 2: Chlorination in dark with Lewis acid:**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_3 + \text{Cl}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{FeCl}_3\text{, dark, 298 K}} p\text{-Chlorotoluene (Major)} + o\text{-Chlorotoluene (Minor)}
$$

  *(The para isomer is easily separated from ortho due to higher melting point and symmetry)*  
*(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{1\text{ Mark}}$ for methylation followed by halogenation under electrophilic conditions)*

### Question 7: NCERT Exercise 13.22 — Acidic Character of Alkynes vs Alkenes/Alkanes
**Tag:** [NCERT Exercise Q13.22, 3 Marks]  
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

#### Problem Statement:
An unknown gas $\mathbf{X}$ decolourizes bromine in carbon tetrachloride and reacts with sodium metal with the evolution of a combustible, colourless gas. Another hydrocarbon gas $\mathbf{Y}$ decolourizes bromine water but does not react with sodium metal. 

1. Identify the homologous families of $\mathbf{X}$ and $\mathbf{Y}$.
2. Write chemical equations showing the reaction of $\mathbf{X}$ with:
   - (a) Sodium amide ($\text{NaNH}_2$) in liquid ammonia.
   - (b) Heavy water ($\text{D}_2\text{O}$).
3. Justify using hybridization why $\text{C--H}$ bonds in alkanes show no acidic character under the same conditions.

***

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Scheme:

#### 1. Identification:
- Gas $\mathbf{X}$ is a **terminal alkyne** (e.g., Ethyne, $\text{HC}\equiv\text{CH}$). It has unsaturation (decolourizes $\text{Br}_2/\text{CCl}_4$) and acidic terminal hydrogen (reacts with $\text{Na}$ metal releasing $\text{H}_2$).
- Gas $\mathbf{Y}$ is an **alkene** (e.g., Ethene, $\text{H}_2\text{C}=\text{CH}_2$) or an **internal alkyne** (e.g., $\text{CH}_3\text{C}\equiv\text{CCH}_3$). It has unsaturation but lacks acidic hydrogens.  
*(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{1\text{ Mark}}$ for correct functional class identification)*

***

#### 2. Chemical Equations:
- **(a) Reaction with Sodium Amide:**
  

$$
\text{HC}\equiv\text{CH} + \text{NaNH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{liq. NH}_3} \text{HC}\equiv\text{C}^-\text{Na}^+ + \text{NH}_3\uparrow
$$

  

$$
\text{HC}\equiv\text{C}^-\text{Na}^+ + \text{NaNH}_2 \longrightarrow \text{Na}^+ {}^-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}^-\text{Na}^+ + \text{NH}_3\uparrow
$$

  *(Monosodium and disodium ethynide formation)*

- **(b) Reaction with Heavy Water ($\text{D}_2\text{O}$):**
  

$$
\text{HC}\equiv\text{CH} + 2\text{D}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{DC}\equiv\text{CD}\text{ (Dideuterioethyne)} + 2\text{HOD}
$$

  
*(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{1\text{ Mark}}$ for both balanced equations)*

***

#### 3. Theoretical Justification (Hybridization & Electronegativity):
- In an alkane ($\text{C--H}$), carbon is $sp^3$ hybridized ($25\%$ s-character).
- In an alkyne ($\equiv\text{C--H}$), carbon is $sp$ hybridized ($50\%$ s-character).
- Since s-orbitals are spherical and penetrate closer to the nucleus, electrons in an $sp$ orbital experience higher effective nuclear charge ($Z_{\text{eff}}$). The $sp$ carbon becomes significantly more electronegative than $sp^2$ and $sp^3$ carbons:
  

$$
\text{Electronegativity: } \text{C}(sp) > \text{C}(sp^2) > \text{C}(sp^3)
$$

- This polarizes the $\equiv\text{C--H}$ bond strongly, allowing the proton ($\text{H}^+$) to be abstracted by strong bases like $\text{NaNH}_2$. In alkanes, the conjugate carbanion ($\text{R--CH}_2^-$) is extremely unstable due to low s-character, making alkane $\text{C--H}$ bonds non-acidic ($\text{p}K_a \approx 50$ vs $\text{p}K_a \approx 25$ for ethyne).  
*(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{1\text{ Mark}}$ for s-character, relative electronegativity, and carbanion stability)*

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{--C}(=\text{O})\text{CH}_3\text{ (Butan-2-one)} + \text{CH}_3\text{--C}(=\text{O})\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3\text{ (Pentan-2-one)}

\text{CH}_2=\text{C}(\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3)_2 \xrightarrow[(2)\text{ Zn/H}_2\text{O}]{(1)\text{ O}_3} \text{HCHO (Methanal)} + \text{O}=\text{C}(\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3)_2\text{ (Pentan-3-one)}

$$
\text{H--Br} \rightleftharpoons \text{H}^+ + \text{Br}^-

$$

### Question 10: Exemplar Advanced Problem — Conformational Dynamics of Ethane
**Tag:** [NCERT Exemplar Problems, 3 Marks]  
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ [High-Yield]

#### Problem Statement:
1. Represent the conformation of ethane that has maximum torsional strain and the conformation that has minimum torsional strain using both **Sawhorse** and **Newman** projections.
2. The energy difference between the staggered and eclipsed conformations of ethane is $12.55\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$. Explain why ethane cannot be separated into its individual conformers at room temperature ($298\text{ K}$).

***

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Scheme:

#### 1. Conformations of Ethane:
- **Eclipsed Conformation:** Maximum torsional strain (dihedral angle $\theta = 0^\circ$).
- **Staggered Conformation:** Minimum torsional strain (dihedral angle $\theta = 60^\circ$).

```
       SAWHORSE PROJECTIONS                   NEWMAN PROJECTIONS
       --------------------                   ------------------

        H            H                                H
         \          /                              H / \ H
          C--------C                                C1---C2 (behind)
         / \      / \                              /     \
        H   H    H   H                            H       H
       (Eclipsed Conformation)               (Eclipsed: theta = 0 deg)

            H                                         H
           /                                       H / \ H
          C--------H                                |   |
         / \      /                                /     \
        H   H    H                                H       H
       (Staggered Conformation)              (Staggered: theta = 60 deg)
```
*(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{1\text{ Mark}}$ for Sawhorse drawings; $\mathbf{1\text{ Mark}}$ for Newman projections)*

***

#### 2. Barrier to Rotation & Dynamic Equilibrium at $298\text{ K}$:
- The energy barrier for interconversion between staggered and eclipsed ethane is only $12.55\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ ($3\text{ kcal mol}^{-1}$).
- At room temperature ($298\text{ K}$), the average thermal kinetic energy ($RT$) of molecular collisions is:
  

$$
\text{Thermal Energy} \approx RT = (8.314\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}) \times 298\text{ K} \approx 2.48\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}
$$

- Thermal collisions provide more than enough energy to overcome this small $12.55\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ rotational barrier. The carbon-carbon single bond rotates millions of times per second ($> 10^{10}\text{ s}^{-1}$).
- Consequently, the conformers rapidly interconvert, making physical separation of individual conformers impossible at ambient conditions.  
*(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{1\text{ Mark}}$ for comparing the $12.55\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ barrier to thermal energy $RT$)*

### Question 11: Exemplar Multi-Concept Synthesis Problem
**Tag:** [NCERT Exemplar Problems, 5 Marks]  
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

#### Problem Statement:
An unsaturated hydrocarbon $\mathbf{A}$ ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}$) undergoes the following series of reactions:
1. $\mathbf{A}$ does not show geometrical isomerism.
2. Treatment of $\mathbf{A}$ with acidic $\text{KMnO}_4$ produces a single ketone $\mathbf{B}$ ($\text{C}_3\text{H}_6\text{O}$) as the only organic product.
3. Addition of $\text{HBr}$ to $\mathbf{A}$ in the presence of benzoyl peroxide gives compound $\mathbf{C}$ ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{13}\text{Br}$).
4. When $\mathbf{A}$ is treated with concentrated sulfuric acid followed by hydrolysis with water, it produces alcohol $\mathbf{D}$ ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{14}\text{O}$).
5. Reaction of $\mathbf{A}$ with $\text{Cl}_2$ at $773\text{ K}$ (high-temperature allylic substitution) gives monochloro derivative $\mathbf{E}$.

#### Tasks:
- Deduce the structural formulas and write IUPAC names of compounds $\mathbf{A}$, $\mathbf{B}$, $\mathbf{C}$, $\mathbf{D}$, and $\mathbf{E}$.
- Write the balanced equation for the oxidative cleavage of $\mathbf{A}$ with acidic $\text{KMnO}_4$.
- Explain why addition of $\text{HBr}$ to $\mathbf{A}$ in the presence of peroxide gives $\mathbf{C}$, but does NOT invert regiochemistry compared to Markovnikov's addition in this particular structure.

#### Model Solution & CBSE Step-Marking Scheme:

#### 1. Identification of Compounds A through E:

  $\mathbf{A}$ ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}$) yields **only one ketone** $\mathbf{B}$ ($\text{C}_3\text{H}_6\text{O}$, propan-2-one) upon vigorous oxidation with $\text{KMnO}_4/\text{H}^+$.
  

$$
\text{Acetone} = \text{CH}_3\text{--C}(=\text{O})\text{--CH}_3

$$
(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{C}=\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)_2 \quad (2,3\text{-dimethylbut-2-ene})
$$

  - Check geometrical isomerism: Each double-bonded carbon carries two identical methyl groups; therefore, it **cannot** exhibit cis-trans isomerism.
  

$$
\mathbf{A} = 2,3\text{-dimethylbut-2-ene}
$$

  

$$
\mathbf{B} = \text{Propan-2-one (Acetone)}
$$

- **Deduction of C (Reaction with $\text{HBr}$ / Peroxide):**  
  

$$
\mathbf{A}\text{ is symmetrical: } (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{C}=\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)_2 + \text{HBr} \xrightarrow{\text{peroxide}} (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CH--C(Br)}(\text{CH}_3)_2

$$
\mathbf{C} = 2\text{-bromo-2,3-dimethylbutane}

(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{C}=\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+} (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CH--C(OH)}(\text{CH}_3)_2

$$
\mathbf{D} = 2,3\text{-dimethylbutan-2-ol}
$$

$$
(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{C}=\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)_2 + \text{Cl}_2 \xrightarrow{773\text{ K}} \text{CH}_2=\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)\text{--C}(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{Cl} \text{ or } \text{ClCH}_2\text{--C}(\text{CH}_3)=\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)_2
$$

  

$$
\mathbf{E} = 1\text{-chloro-2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene}
$$

(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{C}=\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)_2 + 2[\text{O}] \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{KMnO}_4/\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4} 2\text{CH}_3\text{--C}(=\text{O})\text{--CH}_3

#### 3. Regiochemical Symmetry Analysis:
The alkene $2,3\text{-dimethylbut-2-ene}$ is **completely symmetrical**. 
- In electrophilic addition (Markovnikov), protonation at either carbon gives an identical tertiary carbocation:
  

$$
(\text{CH}_3)_2\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{--CH}(\text{CH}_3)_2
$$

  followed by $\text{Br}^-$ attack to give $2\text{-bromo-2,3-dimethylbutane}$.
- In free radical addition (anti-Markovnikov), $\text{Br}^\bullet$ attack at either carbon yields an identical tertiary radical:
  

$$
(\text{CH}_3)_2\dot{\text{C}}\text{--C(Br)}(\text{CH}_3)_2
$$

  followed by $\text{H}^\bullet$ abstraction from $\text{HBr}$ to produce $2\text{-bromo-2,3-dimethylbutane}$.
- Because both olefinic carbons have identical substitution (zero hydrogens, two methyl groups each), regiochemical difference between Markovnikov and anti-Markovnikov pathways is degenerate.  
*(CBSE Rubric: $\mathbf{1\frac{1}{2}\text{ Marks}}$ for carbocation and free radical intermediate symmetry analysis)*

## 1. Speed Hacks & Fast Solving Shortcuts

### A. Rapid Stereochemical & Product-Counting Shortcuts
1. **Stereoisomers from Addition across $C=C$ (CAR / TAM Rules):**
   * **CAR:** **C**is-alkene $+$ **A**nti-addition (e.g., $\text{Br}_2/\text{CCl}_4$) $\rightarrow$ **R**acemic mixture ($\pm$ pair).
   * **CIS:** **C**is-alkene $+$ **S**yn-addition (e.g., cold dil. alk. $\text{KMnO}_4$ / Baeyer's reagent) $\rightarrow$ **M**eso compound (if symmetrical).
   * **TAM:** **T**rans-alkene $+$ **A**nti-addition $\rightarrow$ **M**eso compound (if symmetrical).
   * **TRA:** **T**rans-alkene $+$ **S**yn-addition $\rightarrow$ **R**acemic mixture.

2. **Degree of Unsaturation / Index of Hydrogen Deficiency (DoU / IHD):**
   

$$
\text{DoU} = C + 1 - \frac{H + X - N}{2}
$$

   * Quickly confirms cyclic systems or multiple bonds:
     * $\text{DoU} = 1 \implies 1\text{ double bond or } 1\text{ ring}$.
     * $\text{DoU} = 2 \implies 1\text{ triple bond, } 2\text{ double bonds, } 1\text{ ring } + 1\text{ double bond, or } 2\text{ rings}$.
     * $\text{DoU} \ge 4 \implies$ Strong hint of a benzene ring ($\text{DoU} = 4$: 1 ring + 3 $\pi$-bonds).

3. **Total Monochloro Derivatives Shortcut:**
   * Count unique sets of chemically equivalent hydrogens using symmetry planes ($\sigma_v, \sigma_h, C_2$).
   * *Trap check:* Always count stereocenters! If a chiral carbon is generated by substitution, add **2** isomers ($d + l$) instead of 1.

4. **Combustion Stoichiometry Fast Factor:**
   

$$
\text{C}_x\text{H}_y + \left(x + \frac{y}{4}\right)\text{O}_2 \longrightarrow x\,\text{CO}_2 + \frac{y}{2}\,\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

   * To find hydrocarbon formula rapidly from eudiometry:
     

$$
\frac{\Delta V_{\text{contraction}}}{V_{\text{hydrocarbon}}} = 1 + \frac{y}{4} \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{V_{\text{CO}_2}}{V_{\text{hydrocarbon}}} = x
$$

### B. Instant Reaction Mechanism & Reagent Identification Hacks
* **Wurtz vs. Frankland vs. Corey-House:**
  * $\text{R-X} + \text{Na}/\text{dry ether} \longrightarrow \text{Symmetrical alkane with even carbons}$. (Gives poor yields with odd/unsymmetrical alkanes due to radical cross-coupling).
  * Unsymmetrical alkanes ($\text{R}-\text{R}'$) are synthesized smoothly via the **Corey-House synthesis** ($\text{R}_2\text{CuLi} + \text{R}'\text{X}$).
* **Anti-Markovnikov (Peroxide Effect / Kharasch Effect):**
  * Operates **ONLY** for $\text{HBr}$ in the presence of organic peroxides ($(\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CO})_2\text{O}_2$).
  * Never apply peroxide effect to $\text{HCl}$ (H–Cl bond too strong: $\Delta H^\circ > 0$ in chain propagation) or $\text{HI}$ (I–I bond formation is preferred: iodine free radicals dimerize into $\text{I}_2$ rather than adding across double bond).
* **Markovnikov Regiochemistry & Carbocation Shifts:**
  * Attack of electrophile ($E^+$) creates the **most stable carbocation** ($3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ$).
  * Check for **1,2-hydride** or **1,2-methyl** shifts before nucleophilic attack whenever an adjacent carbon is more substituted.
* **Ozonolysis Direct Cleavage Trick:**
  * Erase the $C=C$ or $C\equiv C$ bond and cap each carbon end with $=O$:
    * $\text{R}-\text{CH}=\text{CH}-\text{R}' \xrightarrow[\text{Zn}/\text{H}_2\text{O}]{\text{O}_3} \text{R}-\text{CHO} + \text{R}'-\text{CHO}$
    * Tetrasubstituted alkene $\rightarrow 2$ Ketones.
    * Ozonide intermediate ($\text{ozonide ring}$) must be drawn if explicitly asked; if reductive ($\text{Zn}/\text{H}_2\text{O}$ or $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{S}$), aldehydes are isolated. If oxidative ($\text{O}_3/\text{H}_2\text{O}$ or $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$), aldehydes oxidize to carboxylic acids.
* **Alkynes Hydration ($Hg^{2+}/H^+$):**
  * Alkyne $+ \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{HgSO}_4 / \text{dil. } \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4, 333\text{ K}}$ Enol $\xrightarrow{\text{tautomerism}}$ Carbonyl.
  * **Ethyne** is the **only** alkyne that gives **acetaldehyde** ($\text{CH}_3\text{CHO}$). All higher alkynes yield **ketones**.

## 2. Examiner Traps & Common Board Evaluation Mistakes

### A. Fatal Board Deduction Pitfalls (Where $1/2$ and $1$ Mark are Lost)
1. **Omission of Specific Reaction Conditions on Arrows:**
   * Writing $\text{AlCl}_3$ instead of **anhydrous** $\text{AlCl}_3$ in Friedel-Crafts alkylation/acylation: **$-1/2$ mark**. (Water hydrolyzes $\text{AlCl}_3$ to $\text{Al(OH)}_3$, destroying its Lewis acid capability).
   * Writing $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ instead of **conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ at $443\text{ K}$** for alkene synthesis from alcohols (at $413\text{ K}$, ether is formed!).

   * Forgetting **$\text{Fe}$ / dark** in electrophilic aromatic halogenation vs. **$h\nu$ / UV light** (which causes side-chain free radical halogenation).

2. **Incorrect Terminal Alkyne Test Observations:**
   * When asked to distinguish between **but-1-yne** and **but-2-yne**:
     * Merely stating "Tollens' reagent reacts" gets **$0$ marks**.
     * **Full Mark Requirement:** Specify the reagent and the precise visual observation:
       

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{C}\equiv\text{CH} + [\text{Ag}(\text{NH}_3)_2]^+ \longrightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{C}\equiv\text{CAg}\downarrow (White precipitate) + \text{NH}_4^+ + \text{NH}_3

$$

     * But-2-yne gives **no precipitate** (absence of acidic terminal acetylenic proton).

3. **Conformations of Ethane / Butane - Dihedral Angle & Energy Ordering:**
   * Dihedral angle of staggered form is **$60^\circ$**; eclipsed is **$0^\circ$**.
   * Conformation energy: $\text{Eclipsed} > \text{Staggered}$ (by $12.5\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ due to torsional strain).
   * **Trap:** Conformations cannot be separated at room temperature because the energy barrier ($12.5\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$) is easily overcome by thermal collisions.

4. **Aromaticity / Hückel’s Rule Criteria:**
   * Mentioning only "$(4n+2)\,\pi\text{ electrons}$" scores only $1/2$ mark.
   * **Mandatory 4 Criteria for Aromaticity:**
     1. Planar geometry (all ring atoms $sp^2$ or $sp$ hybridized).
     2. Complete delocalization of $\pi$-electrons throughout the closed ring (cyclic conjugation).
     3. Obeying Hückel's rule: $(4n+2)\,\pi\text{ electrons}$ where $n = 0, 1, 2, 3\dots$
     4. Cyclic closed ring system.
   * **Cyclooctatetraene ($\text{C}_8\text{H}_8$):** $8\,\pi$ electrons, but it is **non-planar (tub-shaped)**, hence **non-aromatic**, not anti-aromatic.

5. **Electrophilic Substitution of Toluene vs. Nitrobenzene:**
   * $-\text{CH}_3$ is activating and $o/p$-directing via **hyperconjugation ($+H$)** and inductive ($+I$).
   * $-\text{NO}_2$ is deactivating and $m$-directing via **$-R$ and $-I$** effects.
   * **Trap:** In Friedel-Crafts reaction, **nitrobenzene does not undergo alkylation/acylation** because the strong deactivating $-\text{NO}_2$ group complexes with and deactivates the catalyst $\text{AlCl}_3$.

## 3. Golden Points to Remember (NCERT In-Text Lines & High-Yield Truths)

1. **Boiling Point & Melting Point Anomalies:**
   * **Boiling Point:** $\text{Straight-chain alkane} > \text{Branched-chain isomer}$. (Branching makes the molecule spherical, decreasing surface area and minimizing van der Waals dispersion forces).
   * **Melting Point (Alternation Effect):** Alkanes with an **even** number of carbon atoms have higher melting points than the immediately adjacent alkanes with an **odd** number of carbon atoms. Reason: Even-numbered carbons pack better in the crystal lattice due to terminal methyl groups lying on opposite sides.
   * **Alkene Geometrical Isomers:**
     * **Boiling Point:** $\text{Cis} > \text{Trans}$ (Cis has a net non-zero dipole moment $\mu > 0$, causing stronger dipole-dipole attractions).
     * **Melting Point:** $\text{Trans} > \text{Cis}$ (Trans isomer is more symmetrical, fits tightly into the crystal lattice, and has higher crystal lattice energy).

2. **Kolbe’s Electrolytic Synthesis:**
   * At Anode: $2\text{RCOO}^- \longrightarrow \text{R}-\text{R} + 2\text{CO}_2 + 2e^-$
   * At Cathode: $2\text{H}_2\text{O} + 2e^- \longrightarrow \text{H}_2 + 2\text{OH}^-$
   * **Crucial NCERT In-Text Fact:** The aqueous solution becomes **basic/alkaline** during Kolbe's electrolysis because $\text{OH}^-$ ions accumulate ($\text{pH}$ rises).
   * **Methane ($\text{CH}_4$) cannot be prepared** by Kolbe's electrolytic method or Wurtz reaction.

3. **Controlled Oxidation of Methane:**
   * $2\text{CH}_4 + \text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Cu} / 523\text{ K} / 100\text{ atm}} 2\text{CH}_3\text{OH}$ (Methanol)
   * $\text{CH}_4 + \text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Mo}_2\text{O}_3 / \Delta} \text{HCHO} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$ (Methanal)
   * $2\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_3 + 3\text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{(\text{CH}_3\text{COO})_2\text{Mn} / \Delta} 2\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (Ethanoic acid)
   * $3^\circ$ alkanes with a tertiary hydrogen undergo oxidation by alkaline $\text{KMnO}_4$ to alcohols:
     

$$
(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{CH} \xrightarrow{\text{KMnO}_4, [\text{O}]} (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-OH} \quad (\text{2-Methylpropan-2-ol})
$$

4. **Aromatization (Catalytic Dehydro-cyclization):**
   * $n\text{-Hexane} \xrightarrow[\text{773 K},\, 10\text{--}20\text{ atm}]{\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_3 / \text{V}_2\text{O}_5 / \text{Mo}_2\text{O}_3\text{ on }\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3} \text{Benzene} + 4\text{H}_2$
   * $n\text{-Heptane} \longrightarrow \text{Toluene} + 4\text{H}_2$

5. **Stereospecific Trans vs. Cis Additions:**
   * Birch Reduction:
     

$$
\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-\text{R}' \xrightarrow{\text{Na} / \text{liq. } \text{NH}_3} trans-alkene
$$

   * Lindlar's Catalyst:
     

$$
\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-\text{R}' \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2,\, \text{Pd/CaCO}_3\text{ poisoned with quinoline or sulfur}} cis-alkene
$$

6. **Cyclic Polymerization of Alkynes:**
   * $3\,\text{CH}\equiv\text{CH} \xrightarrow{\text{Red hot iron tube},\, 873\text{ K}} \text{Benzene}$
   * $3\,\text{CH}_3-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH} \xrightarrow{\text{Red hot iron tube},\, 873\text{ K}} Mesitylene$ (1,3,5-Trimethylbenzene)
