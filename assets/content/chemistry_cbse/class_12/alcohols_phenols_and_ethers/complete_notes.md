# Classification, Nomenclature, and Structural Features of Alcohols, Phenols, and Ethers

# Alcohols, Phenols, and Ethers: Classification, Nomenclature, and Structural Features
**Class 12 Chemistry | Core Pedagogical Reference Note**

***

## 1. Pedagogical Theory & Foundational Concepts

### 1.1 Structural Definitions and Functional Groups (⭐⭐⭐⭐)
*   **Alcohols:** Organic compounds formed when a hydrogen atom in an aliphatic hydrocarbon is replaced by a hydroxyl group ($-\text{OH}$). General formula: $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+1}\text{OH}$ or $\text{R}-\text{OH}$.
*   **Phenols:** Organic compounds formed when a hydrogen atom in an aromatic system (arene ring) is replaced by a hydroxyl group ($-\text{OH}$) bonded directly to an $\text{sp}^2$-hybridised carbon atom of the benzene ring. General formula: $\text{Ar}-\text{OH}$.
*   **Ethers:** Organic compounds derived by replacing a hydrogen atom of a hydrocarbon by an alkoxy ($-\text{OR}$) or aryloxy ($-\text{OAr}$) group; alternatively viewed as dialkyl/diaryl/alkyl-aryl derivatives of water. General formula: $\text{R}-\text{O}-\text{R}'$.

***

### 1.2 Classification Systems (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

#### A. Alcohols & Phenols: By Number of Hydroxyl Groups ($-\text{OH}$)
1.  **Monohydric:** Contain one $-\text{OH}$ group.
    

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH} \quad (\text{Ethanol}), \quad \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} \quad (\text{Phenol})
$$

2.  **Dihydric (Diols / Glycols):** Contain two $-\text{OH}$ groups on adjacent or distinct carbons.
    

$$
\text{CH}_2(\text{OH})-\text{CH}_2(\text{OH}) \quad (\text{Ethane-1,2-diol}), \quad \text{Benzene-1,2-diol} \quad (\text{Catechol})
$$

3.  **Trihydric (Triols / Glycerols):** Contain three $-\text{OH}$ groups.
    

$$
\text{CH}_2(\text{OH})-\text{CH}(\text{OH})-\text{CH}_2(\text{OH}) \quad (\text{Propane-1,2,3-triol / Glycerol})
$$

4.  **Polyhydric:** Contain four or more $-\text{OH}$ groups (e.g., Sorbitol, $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{14}\text{O}_6$).

#### B. Classification Based on Hybridisation of the Carbon Bearing $-\text{OH}$

##### 1. Compounds Containing $\text{C}_{\text{sp}^3}-\text{OH}$ Bonds (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
*   **Alkyl Alcohols (Primary $1^\circ$, Secondary $2^\circ$, Tertiary $3^\circ$):**
    *   $1^\circ$: $-\text{OH}$ attached to a primary carbon ($\text{R}-\text{CH}_2-\text{OH}$).
    *   $2^\circ$: $-\text{OH}$ attached to a secondary carbon ($\text{R}_2\text{CH}-\text{OH}$).
    *   $3^\circ$: $-\text{OH}$ attached to a tertiary carbon ($\text{R}_3\text{C}-\text{OH}$).
*   **Allylic Alcohols:** $-\text{OH}$ group bonded to an $\text{sp}^3$-hybridised carbon adjacent to a carbon-carbon double bond (the allylic carbon):
    

$$
\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH} \quad (1^\circ \text{ allylic})
$$

    

$$
\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}-\text{CH}(\text{OH})\text{CH}_3 \quad (2^\circ \text{ allylic})
$$

    

$$
\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}-\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{OH} \quad (3^\circ \text{ allylic})
$$

*   **Benzylic Alcohols:** $-\text{OH}$ group bonded to an $\text{sp}^3$-hybridised carbon atom attached to an aromatic ring:
    

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{CH}_2\text{OH} \quad (1^\circ \text{ benzylic})
$$

    

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{CH}(\text{OH})\text{CH}_3 \quad (2^\circ \text{ benzylic})
$$

    

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{C}(\text{OH})(\text{CH}_3)_2 \quad (3^\circ \text{ benzylic})
$$

##### 2. Compounds Containing $\text{C}_{\text{sp}^2}-\text{OH}$ Bonds (⭐⭐⭐⭐)
*   **Vinylic Alcohols:** $-\text{OH}$ group bonded directly to a vinylic carbon ($\text{C}=\text{C}$):
    

$$
\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}-\text{OH} \quad (\text{Ethenol; tautomerises to Ethanal})
$$

*   **Phenols:** $-\text{OH}$ group bonded directly to an $\text{sp}^2$ ring carbon of an arene.

#### C. Classification of Ethers (⭐⭐⭐)
*   **Symmetrical / Simple Ethers:** Both alkyl/aryl groups attached to the oxygen atom are identical:
    

$$
\text{R}-\text{O}-\text{R} \quad \text{e.g., } \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2-\text{O}-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3 \quad (\text{Ethoxyethane})
$$

*   **Unsymmetrical / Mixed Ethers:** The two groups attached to oxygen are non-identical:
    

$$
\text{R}-\text{O}-\text{R}' \quad \text{e.g., } \text{CH}_3-\text{O}-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3 \quad (\text{Methoxyethane}), \quad \text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{O}-\text{CH}_3 \quad (\text{Methoxybenzene/Anisole})
$$

## 2. IUPAC and Common Nomenclature

### 2.1 Rules for IUPAC Naming
1.  **Alcohols:** Replace the terminal '-e' of the parent alkane with the suffix **'-ol'**. Number the parent chain from the end closer to the $-\text{OH}$ group. If multiple $-\text{OH}$ groups exist, retain the terminal '-e' and use suffixes like '-diol', '-triol' (e.g., Ethane-1,2-diol).
2.  **Phenols:** The parent compound is phenol ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH}$). Substituents are indicated by numerical locants ($2-, 3-, 4-$ in IUPAC; $o-, m-, p-$ in common nomenclature).
3.  **Ethers:** Named as **alkoxyalkanes**. The larger or parent hydrocarbon chain is taken as the base alkane, and the smaller alkyl group along with the ethereal oxygen is treated as an **alkoxy** substituent prefix.

### 3.2 Pedagogical Explanations of Structural Anomalies

#### 1. Why is the $\text{C}-\text{O}-\text{H}$ bond angle in Methanol ($108.9^\circ$) slightly less than the tetrahedral angle ($109^\circ 28'$)?
*   **Reason:** The oxygen atom contains two bond pairs and two unshared electron pairs (lone pairs). 
*   According to VSEPR theory, the electrostatic repulsion between **lone pair - lone pair** is greater than **lone pair - bond pair**, which in turn exceeds **bond pair - bond pair**:
    

$$
\text{Repulsion: } (\text{lp}-\text{lp}) > (\text{lp}-\text{bp}) > (\text{bp}-\text{bp})
$$

*   The mutual repulsion between the two bulky lone pairs on oxygen compresses the $\angle \text{C}-\text{O}-\text{H}$ angle slightly downward to $108.9^\circ$.

#### 2. Why is the $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond length in Phenol ($136\text{ pm}$) significantly shorter than in Methanol ($142\text{ pm}$)? (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
Two contributing factors dictate this:
1.  **Partial Double Bond Character via Resonance:** The unshared electron pair on the oxygen atom conjugates with the $\pi$-electron system of the aromatic ring. Delocalisation results in resonance structures showing partial double bond character:
    

$$
\Delta d = d(\text{C}-\text{O})_{\text{alcohol}} - d(\text{C}-\text{O})_{\text{phenol}} = 142\text{ pm} - 136\text{ pm} = 6\text{ pm}
$$

2.  **Hybridisation of Carbon:** In phenol, oxygen is attached to an $\text{sp}^2$-hybridised carbon ($33.3\% \text{ s-character}$), whereas in methanol, it is attached to an $\text{sp}^3$-hybridised carbon ($25\% \text{ s-character}$). Higher s-character brings the electrons closer to the carbon nucleus, decreasing the covalent radius and shortening the bond.

#### 3. Why is the $\text{C}-\text{O}-\text{C}$ bond angle in Ethers ($111.7^\circ$) greater than the tetrahedral angle? (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
*   **Reason:** The steric hindrance and mutual electrostatic repulsive forces between the two bulky alkyl groups (e.g., $-\text{CH}_3$) attached to oxygen override the lone-pair compression forces, pushing the bonds outward:
    

$$
\angle \text{C}-\text{O}-\text{C} = 111.7^\circ > 109.5^\circ
$$

## 4. Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

| Error Category | Common Student Mistake | Scientifically Correct NCERT Principle |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Locant Rules in Diols** | Writing `Ethanediol` or `Propane-1-ol-2-ol` | Retain the parent alkane suffix 'e' when using 'd', 't' prefixes: **Ethane-1,2-diol** (not *Ethandi-1,2-ol*). |
| **IUPAC of Ethers** | Calling $\text{CH}_3-\text{O}-\text{CH}(\text{CH}_3)_2$ "2-Methoxypropane" as "Methyl isopropyl ether" in IUPAC answers | "Methyl isopropyl ether" is the **common name**. The IUPAC name is **2-Methoxypropane**. |
| **Phenol Classification** | Classifying Benzyl alcohol ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$) as a "Phenol" | It is an **aromatic primary alcohol** (benzylic alcohol), not a phenol, because the $-\text{OH}$ group is bonded to an $\text{sp}^3$ aliphatic carbon, not directly to the benzene ring. |
| **Bond Length Reasoning** | Stating "Phenol has a double bond" | It possesses **partial double bond character** due to $+R$ resonance delocalisation; it is not a true, fixed double bond. |
| **Dipole Moment Vector Slip** | Stating ethers have zero dipole moment because they look linear ($\text{R}-\text{O}-\text{R}$) | Ethers are **bent / V-shaped** ($\approx 111.7^\circ$). The bond dipole vectors do not cancel: $\mu_{\text{ether}} \neq 0\text{ D}$ ($\mu \approx 1.15 - 1.3\text{ D}$). |

## 5. Five-Year Solved CBSE Board Examination Questions (2020–2025)

### Question 1 [CBSE 2024, 1 Mark]
**Write the IUPAC name of the following compound:**

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}(\text{OH})-\text{CH}_2-\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)_2-\text{OH}
$$

**Solution:**
1.  **Identify Principal Functional Group:** Both ends feature hydroxyl groups ($-\text{OH}$), so it is a diol.
2.  **Longest Carbon Chain:** Contains 5 carbon atoms (parent: **pentane**).
3.  **Numbering (Lowest Locant Set Rule):**
    *   Left to right: $-\text{OH}$ at $2, 4$; methyls at $4, 4 \rightarrow \text{Locant set: } (2, 4, 4, 4)$.
    *   Right to left: $-\text{OH}$ at $2, 4$; methyls at $2, 2 \rightarrow \text{Locant set: } (2, 2, 2, 4)$ **(Lowest set selected)**.
4.  **Assembly:** Substituents: 4-methyl, 4-methyl $\rightarrow$ 4,4-dimethyl. Parent chain: pentane-2,4-diol.

$$
\mathbf{\text{IUPAC Name: 4,4-Dimethylpentane-2,4-diol}}
$$

***

### Question 2 [CBSE 2023, 2 Marks]
**(a) Account for the following: The $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond length in phenol is shorter than that in methanol.**  
**(b) Classify the following alcohol as $1^\circ, 2^\circ,$ or $3^\circ$ and allylic/benzylic:**

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{CH}=\text{CH}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}
$$

**Solution:**
**(a)** The $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond length in phenol ($136\text{ pm}$) is shorter than in methanol ($142\text{ pm}$) due to:
1.  **Resonance Effect ($+R$):** Delocalisation of the unshared electron pair of oxygen over the benzene ring imparts **partial double bond character** to the $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond.
2.  **Difference in Hybridisation:** In phenol, oxygen is linked to an $\text{sp}^2$-hybridised carbon (higher electronegativity and smaller covalent radius due to $33.3\%$ s-character), whereas in methanol, it is linked to an $\text{sp}^3$-hybridised carbon ($25\%$ s-character).

**(b)**
*   The $-\text{OH}$ is attached to a carbon bonded to one other carbon $\rightarrow \mathbf{1^\circ \text{ (Primary)}}$.
*   The carbon bearing $-\text{OH}$ is an $\text{sp}^3$ carbon adjacent to an aliphatic carbon-carbon double bond ($\text{C}=\text{C}$) $\rightarrow \mathbf{\text{Allylic alcohol}}$ *(Note: It is not a benzylic alcohol because the $-\text{OH}$ is not directly on the benzylic carbon).*

### Question 5 [CBSE 2020, 3 Marks]
**(a) Draw the resonance structures of Phenol showing the partial double bond character of the $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond.**  
**(b) Explain why the dipole moment of methanol ($\mu = 1.71\text{ D}$) is higher than that of phenol ($\mu = 1.54\text{ D}$).**

**Solution:**
**(a) Resonance Structures:**
The lone pair on the oxygen atom participates in resonance with the $\pi$-cloud of the benzene ring:
```
    :O-H              :O-H (+)          :O-H (+)          :O-H (+)             :O-H
   /   \             //   \            //   \            //   \             /   \
  /     \    <-->   /     \    <-->   /     \    <-->   /     \    <-->   /     \
 |       |         |       |         |       |(-)       |       |         |       |
  \     /           \     / (-)       \     /            \     /           \     /
   \___/             \___/             \___/              \___/ (-)         \___/
   (I)                (II)              (III)              (IV)              (V)
```
*Structures (II), (III), and (IV) establish partial double bond character between carbon and oxygen with excess electron density at the ortho- and para-positions.*

**(b) Dipole Moment Comparison:**
*   In methanol, the methyl group ($+\text{I}$ inductive effect) pushes electron density toward the electronegative oxygen atom; the $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond moment and $\text{O}-\text{H}$ bond moment reinforce each other:
    

$$
\vec{\mu}_{\text{net}}(\text{CH}_3\text{OH}) = 1.71\text{ D}
$$

*   In phenol, the $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond moment opposes the resonance electron shift: the phenyl group exerts a strong electron-withdrawing resonance effect ($-R$) and inductive effect ($-\text{I}$, $\text{sp}^2$ carbon). This attenuates the net charge separation along the $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond axis, reducing the net dipole moment:
    

$$
\vec{\mu}_{\text{net}}(\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH}) = 1.54\text{ D}
$$

$$
\text{Single } -\text{OH} \longrightarrow \text{Drop '-e'} \quad (\text{Alkan} + \text{ol} = \mathbf{\text{Alkanol}})

$$
\text{Multiple } -\text{OH} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{Keep '-e'}} \quad (\text{Alkane} + \text{diol} = \mathbf{\text{Alkanediol}})

### 6.2 The Ether Formula Shortcut: "Small Alkoxy, Big Alkane"

$$
\text{R}(\text{fewer carbons})-\text{O}-\text{R}'(\text{more carbons}) \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{[Alkoxy]}} + \mathbf{\text{[Parent Alkane]}}
$$

*   *Example:* $\text{CH}_3-\text{O}-\text{C}_3\text{H}_7 \rightarrow \text{Methoxy (1C) + propane (3C)} = \mathbf{\text{1-Methoxypropane}}$

$$

$$

*   **Mnemonic:** **"E-T-P-M"** $\rightarrow$ **E**ther $>$ **T**etrahedral $>$ **P**henol $>$ **M**ethanol.

## 1. Preparation of Alcohols from Alkenes

### Method 1A: By Acid-Catalysed Hydration
**Priority Tag:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (Very High Board Frequency)

#### Concept & Principle
Alkenes react with water in the presence of an acid catalyst (commonly concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$) to yield alcohols.
- For **symmetrical alkenes**, addition across the double bond gives a unique product:

$$
\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+} \text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}
$$

- For **unsymmetrical alkenes**, addition follows **Markovnikov’s Rule**: the electrophile ($\text{H}^+$) adds to the carbon having the greater number of hydrogen atoms, forming the more stable carbocation intermediate. The nucleophile ($-\text{OH}$) then binds to the more substituted carbon.

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+} \text{CH}_3-\text{CH}(\text{OH})-\text{CH}_3
$$

***

#### Detailed Reaction Mechanism (NCERT Core Derivation)
The reaction proceeds in three distinct reversible steps:

##### Step 1: Electrophilic attack by hydronium ion on alkene to form a carbocation intermediate
The acid dissociates in water to generate the hydronium ion:

$$
\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{H}^+ \rightleftharpoons \text{H}_3\text{O}^+
$$

The $\pi$-electrons of the alkene attack the electrophilic proton of $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$:

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 + \text{H}-\text{O}^+\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3-\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}-\text{CH}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

*(Formation of the secondary carbocation, which is more stable than the primary carbocation).*

##### Step 2: Nucleophilic attack of water on the carbocation
Water acts as a Lewis base/nucleophile and attacks the electron-deficient planar carbocation carbon:

$$
\text{CH}_3-\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}-\text{CH}_3 + {:}\text{O}\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3-\underset{\substack{\mid \\ \text{O}^+\text{H}_2}}{\text{CH}}-\text{CH}_3
$$

*(Formation of protonated alcohol / alkyloxonium ion).*

##### Step 3: Deprotonation to yield the alcohol
A second water molecule acts as a base and abstracts a proton from the oxonium ion, regenerating the $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ catalyst:

$$
\text{CH}_3-\underset{\substack{\mid \\ \text{O}^+\text{H}_2}}{\text{CH}}-\text{CH}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3-\underset{\substack{\mid \\ \text{OH}}}{\text{CH}}-\text{CH}_3 + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Overall: } \text{CH}_3\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}(\text{OH})\text{CH}_3}
$$

***

### Method 1B: By Hydroboration–Oxidation
**Priority Tag:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (Very High Board Frequency)

#### Concept & Principle
Diboran $(\text{B}_2\text{H}_6 \text{ or } \text{BH}_3)$ reacts with alkenes to give trialkylboranes as addition products. Oxidation of trialkylborane by alkaline hydrogen peroxide ($\text{H}_2\text{O}_2 / \text{OH}^-$) yields alcohols.
- **Regioselectivity:** The net addition corresponds to **Anti-Markovnikov hydration** (addition of $\text{H}$ and $\text{OH}$).
- **Stereospecificity:** *Syn*-addition of $-\text{H}$ and $-\text{BH}_2$ across the double bond.
- **Absence of Skeletal Rearrangement:** Proceeds via a concerted 4-membered cyclic transition state without a free carbocation.

#### Step-by-Step Equations

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 + (\text{H}-\text{BH}_2) \longrightarrow \text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\text{BH}_2
$$

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{BH}_2 + \text{CH}_3\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 \longrightarrow (\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2)_2\text{BH}
$$

$$
(\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2)_2\text{BH} + \text{CH}_3\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 \longrightarrow (\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2)_3\text{B} \quad \text{(Tripropylborane)}
$$

Oxidation stage:

$$
(\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2)_3\text{B} + 3\,\text{H}_2\text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{OH}^-} 3\,\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{OH} + \text{B}(\text{OH})_3
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Net Result: } \text{R}-\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) } \text{H}_2\text{O}_2,\,\text{OH}^-]{\text{(i) } \text{B}_2\text{H}_6,\,\text{THF}} \text{R}-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}}
$$

***

## 2. Preparation from Carbonyl Compounds

### Method 2A: By Reduction of Aldehydes and Ketones
**Priority Tag:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### Reducing Agents
1. **Catalytic Hydrogenation:** Molecular hydrogen in the presence of finely divided metal catalysts like $\text{Pt}$, $\text{Pd}$, or $\text{Ni}$.
2. **Complex Metal Hydrides:**
   - **Sodium borohydride ($\text{NaBH}_4$):** Mild reducing agent in ethanol or methanol solvent; selectively reduces aldehydes and ketones without affecting carboxylic acids or esters.
   - **Lithium aluminium hydride ($\text{LiAlH}_4$):** Strong reducing agent in dry ether; reduces aldehydes, ketones, esters, and carboxylic acids.

#### Reactions
- **Aldehydes yield Primary ($1^\circ$) Alcohols:**

$$
\text{R}-\text{CHO} + \text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Pd}} \text{R}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}
$$

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CHO} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) }\text{H}_2\text{O}]{\text{(i) }\text{NaBH}_4} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH}
$$

- **Ketones yield Secondary ($2^\circ$) Alcohols:**

$$
\text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{R}' \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) } \text{H}^+]{\text{(i) } \text{NaBH}_4} \text{R}-\underset{\substack{\mid \\ \text{OH}}}{\text{CH}}-\text{R}'
$$

***

### Method 2B: By Reduction of Carboxylic Acids and Esters
**Priority Tag:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

- **Reduction with $\text{LiAlH}_4$:**
Carboxylic acids are reduced to primary alcohols in high yield using $\text{LiAlH}_4$:

$$
\text{R}-\text{COOH} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) } \text{H}_2\text{O}]{\text{(i) } \text{LiAlH}_4 / \text{dry ether}} \text{R}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}
$$

- **Industrial Commercial Process (Esterification followed by Catalytic Hydrogenation):**
Because $\text{LiAlH}_4$ is an expensive reagent, commercial preparation converts the carboxylic acid into an ester first, followed by catalytic reduction with hydrogen:

$$
\text{R}-\text{COOH} + \text{R}'\text{OH} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+} \text{R}-\text{COOR}' + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

$$
\text{R}-\text{COOR}' + 2\,\text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Catalyst}} \text{R}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH} + \text{R}'\text{OH}
$$

***

## 3. Preparation from Grignard Reagents
**Priority Tag:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (Highest Board Frequency)

### Concept & Reaction Sequence
A Grignard reagent ($\text{RMgX}$) acts as a carbanion source ($\text{R}^{\delta-}-\text{Mg}^{\delta+}\text{X}$). The nucleophilic carbon adds to the electrophilic carbonyl carbon to form a magnesium alkoxide adduct, which on acid hydrolysis furnishes the corresponding alcohol.

$$
\text{R}-\text{MgX} + \backslash\text{C}=\text{O} \longrightarrow \left[ \backslash\text{C}\underset{\substack{\mid \\ \text{R}}}{\overset{\substack{\text{O}^-\text{Mg}^{2+}\text{X} \\ \mid}}{-}} \right] \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{O} / \text{H}^+} \backslash\text{C}\underset{\substack{\mid \\ \text{R}}}{\overset{\substack{\text{OH} \\ \mid}}{-}} + \text{Mg}(\text{OH})\text{X}
$$

### Summary of Classifications
1. **Formaldehyde (Methanal, $\text{HCHO}$)** $\longrightarrow$ **$1^\circ$ Alcohol:**

$$
\text{HCHO} + \text{RMgX} \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} \text{R}-\text{CH}_2\text{OMgX} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{R}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH} + \text{Mg}(\text{OH})\text{X}
$$

2. **Any other Aldehyde ($\text{R}'\text{CHO}$, where $\text{R}' \neq \text{H}$)** $\longrightarrow$ **$2^\circ$ Alcohol:**

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CHO} + \text{CH}_3\text{MgBr} \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} \text{CH}_3-\underset{\substack{\mid \\ \text{CH}_3}}{\text{CH}}-\text{OMgBr} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{CH}_3-\underset{\substack{\mid \\ \text{CH}_3}}{\text{CH}}-\text{OH} + \text{Mg}(\text{OH})\text{Br}
$$

3. **Ketones ($\text{R}'\text{COR}''$)** $\longrightarrow$ **$3^\circ$ Alcohol:**

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_3 + \text{CH}_3\text{MgBr} \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}-\text{OMgBr} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}-\text{OH} + \text{Mg}(\text{OH})\text{Br}
$$

***

# Part 2: Methods of Preparation of Phenols

Phenol ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH}$), historically termed **carbolic acid**, is synthesized on laboratory and industrial scales via four principal routes:

***

## 1. From Haloarenes (Dow's Process)
**Priority Tag:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### Principle & Conditions
Chlorobenzene is treated with aqueous $\text{NaOH}$ at **$623\,\text{K}$** and **$300\,\text{atm}$** pressure. Under these drastic conditions, nucleophilic aromatic substitution occurs to form sodium phenoxide, which on acidification with dilute $\text{HCl}$ yields phenol.

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} + 2\,\text{NaOH} \xrightarrow[300\,\text{atm}]{623\,\text{K}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{ONa} + \text{NaCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{ONa} + \text{HCl} \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{NaCl}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Overall: } \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) } \text{H}^+]{\text{(i) } \text{NaOH},\,623\,\text{K},\,300\,\text{atm}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH}}
$$

***

## 2. From Benzenesulphonic Acid
**Priority Tag:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

### Principle & Step-by-Step Equations
Benzene is sulphonated with **oleum** (fuming sulphuric acid, $\text{H}_2\text{S}_2\text{O}_7$) to form benzenesulphonic acid. Heating with molten sodium hydroxide ($\text{NaOH}$) converts it into sodium phenoxide, followed by acidification to produce phenol.

1. **Sulphonation:**

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{SO}_3 \ (\text{in } \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4) \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_3\text{H}
$$

2. **Fusion with caustic alkali:**

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_3\text{H} + 3\,\text{NaOH} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{ONa} + \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_3 + 2\,\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

3. **Acidification:**

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{ONa} + \text{H}^+ \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH}
$$

***

## 3. From Diazonium Salts
**Priority Tag:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### Principle & Step-by-Step Equations
Aniline is diazotized at **$273\text{--}278\,\text{K}$ ($0\text{--}5^\circ\text{C}$)** using nitrous acid ($\text{NaNO}_2 + \text{HCl}$). The resulting benzenediazonium chloride is hydrolysed by warming with water or dilute acid to yield phenol with quantitative nitrogen gas evolution.

1. **Diazotisation:**

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{NaNO}_2 + 2\,\text{HCl} \xrightarrow{273-278\,\text{K}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{NaCl} + 2\,\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

2. **Hydrolysis:**

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{Warm}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{N}_2\uparrow + \text{HCl}
$$

***

## 4. From Cumene (Isopropylbenzene)
**Priority Tag:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (Most Tested Industrial Method)

### Principle & Reaction Scheme
Cumene is oxidized in the presence of air/molecular oxygen to form **cumene hydroperoxide**. Treatment with dilute acid ($\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$) causes rearrangement, cleaving it into **phenol** and **propan-2-one (acetone)**. Acetone is obtained as an economically valuable by-product in equimolar amounts.

1. **Preparation of Cumene (Friedel–Crafts Alkylation):**

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{CH}_3\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4 \text{ or } \text{AlCl}_3} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{CH}(\text{CH}_3)_2
$$

2. **Autoxidation to Cumene Hydroperoxide:**

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{CH}(\text{CH}_3)_2 + \text{O}_2 \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\underset{\substack{\mid \\ \text{CH}_3}}{\overset{\substack{\text{CH}_3 \\ \mid}}{\text{C}}}-\text{O}-\text{O}-\text{H}
$$

3. **Acid-Catalysed Cleavage:**

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)_2-\text{O}-\text{O}-\text{H} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+ / \text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_3
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Net: } \text{Cumene} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) } \text{H}^+ / \text{H}_2\text{O}]{\text{(i) } \text{O}_2} \text{Phenol} + \text{Acetone}}
$$

***

# Part 3: High-Yield Examples & 5-Year Solved CBSE Board PYQs (2020–2025)

### Q1 [CBSE 2024, 3 Marks]
**(a)** Write the mechanism of acid-catalysed hydration of ethene to ethanol.  
**(b)** How do you convert propanone to 2-methylpropan-2-ol?

**Solution:**
- **(a)** Write the three steps as detailed above:
  1. Protonation of ethene by $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ to form ethyl carbocation:
     

$$
\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}_2 + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+ \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3-\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

  2. Nucleophilic attack of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ on the carbocation:
     

$$
\text{CH}_3-\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{O}^+\text{H}_2
$$

  3. Deprotonation by water to form ethanol:
     

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{O}^+\text{H}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{OH} + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+
$$

- **(b)** Treat propanone with methylmagnesium bromide in dry ether, followed by acid hydrolysis:
  

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_3 + \text{CH}_3\text{MgBr} \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}-\text{OMgBr} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}-\text{OH} + \text{Mg}(\text{OH})\text{Br}
$$

### Q2 [CBSE 2023, 2 Marks]
Explain the preparation of phenol from cumene with balanced chemical equations. Name the by-product and state its industrial significance.

**Solution:**
- **Reactions:**
  1. Cumene undergoes oxidation with atmospheric oxygen to form cumene hydroperoxide:
     

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}(\text{CH}_3)_2 + \text{O}_2 \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{OOH}
$$

  2. Cumene hydroperoxide on treatment with dilute sulphuric acid gives phenol and propanone:
     

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{OOH} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{O} / \text{H}^+} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_3
$$

- **By-product:** Propanone (Acetone).
- **Industrial Significance:** It is an important industrial solvent and starting material, produced in equimolar yield alongside phenol, making this route the most economical industrial method.

### Q3 [CBSE 2022 Term-2, 2 Marks]
Show how you would synthesize:
1. Pentan-1-ol using a suitable alkene.
2. Cyclohexylmethanol using a Grignard reagent.

1. **Pentan-1-ol from Pent-1-ene:** Use Hydroboration–Oxidation to achieve Anti-Markovnikov addition:

6\,\text{CH}_3(\text{CH}_2)_2\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 + \text{B}_2\text{H}_6 \longrightarrow 2\,[\text{CH}_3(\text{CH}_2)_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2]_3\text{B}
$$

   

$$
[\text{CH}_3(\text{CH}_2)_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2]_3\text{B} + 3\,\text{H}_2\text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{OH}^-} 3\,\text{CH}_3(\text{CH}_2)_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH} + \text{B}(\text{OH})_3
$$

2. **Cyclohexylmethanol:** React cyclohexylmagnesium bromide with methanal (formaldehyde) followed by hydrolysis:
   

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_{11}\text{MgBr} + \text{HCHO} \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_{11}\text{CH}_2\text{OMgBr} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{C}_6\text{H}_{11}\text{CH}_2\text{OH} + \text{Mg}(\text{OH})\text{Br}
$$

### Q4 [CBSE 2021, 1 Mark]
Why cannot chlorobenzene be converted to phenol by simple boiling with aqueous $\text{NaOH}$ under ordinary conditions?

**Solution:**
Due to:
1. **Resonance Effect:** The lone pair of electrons on chlorine delocalizes with the $\pi$-system of the benzene ring, imparting partial double-bond character to the $\text{C}-\text{Cl}$ bond.
2. **$\text{sp}^2$ Hybridisation:** The $\text{sp}^2$ hybridised carbon of the ring is more electronegative than the $\text{sp}^3$ carbon of alkyl halides, holding the halogen tighter.
3. Instability of the phenyl cation and steric/electronic repulsion of attacking nucleophiles by the benzene $\pi$-electron cloud.

1. Conversion of aniline to phenol.
2. Hydroboration of propene.

# 1. Pedagogical Theory & Concepts

## 1.1 Physical Properties of Alcohols

### Boiling Points ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
* **Definition & Trend:** The boiling points of alcohols increase monotonically with increase in molecular mass (due to the progressive increase in the magnitude of Van der Waals forces/London dispersion forces as the molecular surface area increases).
* **Branching Effect:** Among isomeric alcohols, the boiling point decreases with an increase in branching of the alkyl chain.  
  

$$
\text{Boiling Point Order: } 1^\circ \text{ alcohol} > 2^\circ \text{ alcohol} > 3^\circ \text{ alcohol}
$$

  
  *Molecular Reason:* Increased branching causes the molecule to acquire an increasingly spherical geometry. A sphere possesses minimum surface area for a given volume, leading to decreased area of contact between adjacent molecules and consequently attenuated intermolecular Van der Waals forces.
* **Intermolecular Hydrogen Bonding:** Alcohols exhibit anomalously high boiling points compared to hydrocarbons, haloalkanes, and ethers of comparable molecular masses.  
  *Molecular Interpretation:* The highly electronegative oxygen atom ($\chi_{\text{O}} \approx 3.44$) polarizes the $\mathrm{O-H}$ bond, creating a substantial permanent dipole ($\mathrm{R-O^{\delta-}-H^{\delta+}}$). Strong intermolecular hydrogen bonds form extensive associative networks in the liquid state:
  

$$
\cdots \mathrm{O(R)-H} \cdots \mathrm{O(R)-H} \cdots \mathrm{O(R)-H} \cdots
$$

  Cleavage of these cohesive associative interactions requires significant thermal energy input ($10\text{--}40\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ per H-bond).

### Solubility in Water ⭐⭐⭐⭐
* **Mechanism:** The lower molecular mass alcohols ($\mathrm{CH_3OH}$, $\mathrm{C_2H_5OH}$, $\mathrm{C_3H_7OH}$) are miscible with water in all proportions. This miscibility arises directly from the thermodynamic favorability of forming solute-solvent intermolecular hydrogen bonds between the hydroxyl ($-\mathrm{OH}$) group of the alcohol and polar $\mathrm{H_2O}$ molecules.
* **Hydrophobic Cutoff:** As the length of the hydrocarbon radical ($\mathrm{R}-$) increases, solubility in water drops precipitously. The alkyl group acts as a bulky, non-polar, hydrophobic moiety that disrupts the tetrahedral hydrogen-bonded structure of water without forming compensating electrostatic interactions.

***

## 1.2 Structure & Reactivity of the Functional Group

Alcohols display amphoteric chemical reactivity arising from dual reaction sites:

```
          Cleavage of O-H bond (Alcohol as Nucleophile / Weak Acid)
                     ↓
             R — O — H
                 ↑
          Cleavage of C-O bond (Protonated Alcohol as Electrophile)
```

1. **Reactions Involving Cleavage of $\mathrm{O-H}$ Bond:** The alcohol acts as a **nucleophile** or a Bronsted acid.
2. **Reactions Involving Cleavage of $\mathrm{C-O}$ Bond:** Protonation of the hydroxyl group transforms the poor leaving group ($\mathrm{OH^-}$) into an excellent leaving group ($\mathrm{H_2O}$), allowing the protonated species ($\mathrm{R-OH_2^+}$) to act as an **electrophile**.

***

# 2. Complete Step-by-Step Chemical Reactions, Derivations & Mechanisms

***

## 2.1 Reactions Involving Cleavage of $\mathrm{O-H}$ Bond

### A. Acidity of Alcohols & Reaction with Active Metals ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Alcohols react with electropositive alkali and alkaline earth metals ($\mathrm{Na, K, Mg, Al}$) with the brisk effervescence of hydrogen gas:

$$
2\mathrm{R-O-H} + 2\mathrm{Na} \longrightarrow 2\mathrm{R-O^-Na^+} + \mathrm{H_2}\uparrow
$$

$$
6\left(\mathrm{CH_3}\right)_3\mathrm{C-OH} + 2\mathrm{Al} \longrightarrow 2\left[\left(\mathrm{CH_3}\right)_3\mathrm{C-O}\right]_3\mathrm{Al} + 3\mathrm{H_2}\uparrow
$$

*(Aluminium tert-butoxide)*

#### Acidic Character Comparison:

$$
\text{Acidic Strength: } \mathrm{H_2O} > 1^\circ \text{ alcohol} > 2^\circ \text{ alcohol} > 3^\circ \text{ alcohol}
$$

*Thermodynamic & Electronic Origin:*  
The acidic dissociation in aqueous media corresponds to:

$$
\mathrm{R-OH} + \mathrm{H_2O} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{R-O^-} + \mathrm{H_3O^+}
$$

Alkyl groups ($\mathrm{R}$) are electron-releasing via inductive ($+I$) and hyperconjugative effects. An increasing number of alkyl groups attached to the carbinol carbon intensifies the negative charge density on the conjugate base (alkoxide ion, $\mathrm{R-O^-}$), destabilizing it:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-O^-} \quad \text{vs} \quad (\mathrm{CH_3})_2\mathrm{CH-O^-} \quad \text{vs} \quad (\mathrm{CH_3})_3\mathrm{C-O^-}
$$

Consequently, $3^\circ$ alkoxide is the most basic and least stable conjugate base, rendering $3^\circ$ alcohols the least acidic.  
*Key Exception:* **Methanol ($\mathrm{CH_3OH}$)** is a marginally stronger acid than water ($K_a = 2.9 \times 10^{-16}$ for water vs $K_a = 3.2 \times 10^{-16}$ for methanol at $298\text{ K}$).

***

### B. Esterification Reaction ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Reversible condensation between an alcohol and a carboxylic acid, acid anhydride, or acid chloride in the presence of an acid/base catalyst.

#### 1. With Carboxylic Acid (Fischer-Speier Esterification):

$$
\mathrm{R-COOH} + \mathrm{R'-OH} \overset{\mathrm{H_2SO_4 \text{ (conc.)}}}{\rightleftharpoons} \mathrm{R-COOR'} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

#### 2. With Acid Anhydride:

$$
(\mathrm{RCO})_2\mathrm{O} + \mathrm{R'-OH} \overset{\mathrm{H^+}}{\rightleftharpoons} \mathrm{R-COOR'} + \mathrm{R-COOH}
$$

#### 3. With Acyl Chloride:

$$
\mathrm{R-COCl} + \mathrm{R'-OH} \overset{\text{Pyridine}}{\longrightarrow} \mathrm{R-COOR'} + \mathrm{HCl}
$$

*Function of Pyridine:* Pyridine is a tertiary organic amine base. It neutralizes the $\mathrm{HCl}$ byproduct forming pyridinium chloride ($\mathrm{C_5H_5NH^+Cl^-}$), which shifts the dynamic equilibrium toward the product side according to Le Chatelier's Principle.

#### Complete Step-by-Step Mechanism for Fischer Esterification:

**Step 1: Protonation of the Carbonyl Oxygen**  
The mineral acid ($\mathrm{H^+}$) protonates the carbonyl oxygen of the carboxylic acid, generating a resonance-stabilized electrophilic carbocation center:

$$
\mathrm{R-C(=O)-OH} + \mathrm{H^+} \rightleftharpoons \left[ \mathrm{R-C(=O^+H)-OH} \longleftrightarrow \mathrm{R-C^+(OH)_2} \right]
$$

**Step 2: Nucleophilic Attack by the Alcohol Molecule**  
The unshared electron pair on the oxygen atom of the alcohol attacks the electrophilic carbonyl carbon to form a tetrahedral intermediate:

$$
\mathrm{R-C^+(OH)_2} + \mathrm{R'-OH} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{R-C(OH)_2-(O^+H-R')}
$$

**Step 3: Proton Transfer (Tautomerization of the Intermediate)**  
Proton transfer occurs intramolecularly or via solvent mediation from the alkonium oxygen to one of the hydroxyl oxygens, turning it into a good leaving group ($\mathrm{-O^+H_2}$):

$$
\mathrm{R-C(OH)_2-(O^+H-R')} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{R-C(OH)(O^+H_2)(OR')}
$$

**Step 4: Elimination of Water Molecule ($\mathrm{-H_2O}$)**  
The lone pair on the remaining $-\mathrm{OH}$ group assists in the expulsion of neutral $\mathrm{H_2O}$, generating a protonated ester:

$$
\mathrm{R-C(OH)(O^+H_2)(OR')} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{R-C(=O^+H)(OR')} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

**Step 5: Deprotonation (Regeneration of the Acid Catalyst)**  
Loss of a proton yields the neutral ester product:

$$
\mathrm{R-C(=O^+H)(OR')} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{R-COOR'} + \mathrm{H^+}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Steric Reactivity Order: } \mathrm{CH_3OH} > 1^\circ > 2^\circ > 3^\circ \text{ (Alcohols)}; \quad \mathrm{HCOOH} > \mathrm{CH_3COOH} > \mathrm{R_2CHCOOH} > \mathrm{R_3CCOOH}}
$$

***

## 2.2 Reactions Involving Cleavage of $\mathrm{C-O}$ Bond

***

### A. Reaction with Hydrogen Halides ($\mathrm{HX}$) & Lucas Test ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

$$
\mathrm{R-OH} + \mathrm{HX} \overset{\mathrm{ZnCl_2}}{\longrightarrow} \mathrm{R-X} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

#### Lucas Reagent:
An equimolar mixture of **concentrated $\mathrm{HCl}$ and anhydrous $\mathrm{ZnCl_2}$**.

#### Mechanism & Principles:
Anhydrous $\mathrm{ZnCl_2}$ acts as a Lewis acid and coordinates with the oxygen of the alcohol:

$$
\mathrm{R-\ddot{O}-H} + \mathrm{ZnCl_2} \longrightarrow \mathrm{R-\overset{+}{O}(H)-[ZnCl_2]^-}
$$

This weakens the $\mathrm{C-O}$ bond, facilitating heterolytic cleavage to produce a carbocation intermediate ($\mathrm{R^+}$), followed by fast nucleophilic attack by chloride ion ($\mathrm{Cl^-}$):

$$
\mathrm{R-\overset{+}{O}(H)-[ZnCl_2]^-} \longrightarrow \mathrm{R^+} + [\mathrm{ZnCl_2(OH)}]^- \overset{\mathrm{Cl^-}}{\longrightarrow} \mathrm{R-Cl} + \mathrm{ZnCl_2} + \mathrm{OH^-}
$$

Since alkyl chlorides are insoluble in water, their formation causes **turbidity / cloudiness**.

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
3^\circ \text{ Alcohols} &\longrightarrow \text{Instant turbidity at room temperature via stable } 3^\circ \text{ carbocation } (\mathrm{S_N1})\\
2^\circ \text{ Alcohols} &\longrightarrow \text{Turbidity appears within } 5 \text{ minutes}\\
1^\circ \text{ Alcohols} &\longrightarrow \text{No turbidity at room temperature; appears only upon sustained heating}
\end{aligned}
}
$$

***

### B. Reaction with Phosphorus Halides and Thionyl Chloride ⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### 1. With Phosphorus Trichloride ($\mathrm{PCl_3}$):

$$
3\mathrm{R-OH} + \mathrm{PCl_3} \longrightarrow 3\mathrm{R-Cl} + \mathrm{H_3PO_3} \quad \text{(Phosphorous acid)}
$$

#### 2. With Phosphorus Pentachloride ($\mathrm{PCl_5}$):

$$
\mathrm{R-OH} + \mathrm{PCl_5} \longrightarrow \mathrm{R-Cl} + \mathrm{POCl_3} + \mathrm{HCl}\uparrow \quad \text{(Phosphoryl chloride)}
$$

#### 3. With Thionyl Chloride ($\mathrm{SOCl_2}$ - Darzens Process):

$$
\mathrm{R-OH} + \mathrm{SOCl_2} \overset{\text{Pyridine or Reflux}}{\longrightarrow} \mathrm{R-Cl} + \mathrm{SO_2}\uparrow + \mathrm{HCl}\uparrow
$$

$$
\boxed{Industrial & Preparative Preference:  \mathrm{SOCl_2} \text{ is the superior reagent because both byproducts } (\mathrm{SO_2} \text{ and } \mathrm{HCl}) \text{ are gaseous and escape, leaving pure alkyl halide without tedious purification.}}
$$

***

### C. Acid-Catalyzed Dehydration of Alcohols to Alkenes ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

When heated with protic acids ($\mathrm{H_2SO_4, H_3PO_4}$), alcohols undergo unimolecular/bimolecular elimination ($\beta$-elimination) to yield alkenes with the loss of a water molecule.

#### Relative Ease of Dehydration:

$$
3^\circ \text{ Alcohol } (20\%\ \mathrm{H_3PO_4}, 358\text{ K}) > 2^\circ \text{ Alcohol } (85\%\ \mathrm{H_3PO_4}, 440\text{ K}) > 1^\circ \text{ Alcohol } (\text{conc. }\mathrm{H_2SO_4}, 443\text{ K})
$$

#### Complete 3-Step Reaction Mechanism for Ethanol Dehydration ($1^\circ$ Alcohol):

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH} \xrightarrow[\mathrm{443\ K}]{\text{conc. }\mathrm{H_2SO_4}} \mathrm{CH_2=CH_2} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

**Step 1: Formation of Protonated Alcohol (Ethyl Oxonium Ion) — Fast Equilibrium**  
The lone pair on the oxygen atom of ethanol accepts a proton from sulfuric acid:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-\ddot{O}-H} + \mathrm{H^+} \xrightleftharpoons[\text{fast}]{\quad} \mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-\overset{+}{O}H_2}
$$

**Step 2: Formation of Carbocation Intermediate — Slow, Rate-Determining Step (RDS)**  
The $\mathrm{C-O}$ bond cleaves heterolytically with the departure of the neutral, stable water leaving group:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-\overset{+}{O}H_2} \xrightarrow[\text{slow (RDS)}]{\quad} \mathrm{CH_3-\overset{+}{C}H_2} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

**Step 3: Elimination of a Proton ($\beta$-Elimination) — Fast Step**  
A conjugate base ($\mathrm{H_2O}$ or $\mathrm{HSO_4^-}$) abstracts a proton from the $\beta$-carbon atom, generating the $\mathrm{C=C}$ double bond:

$$
\mathrm{H-CH_2-\overset{+}{C}H_2} + \mathrm{H_2O} \xrightarrow[\text{fast}]{\quad} \mathrm{CH_2=CH_2} + \mathrm{H_3O^+}
$$

*Regiochemical Note (Saytzeff/Zaitsev Rule):* For unsymmetrical secondary and tertiary alcohols, dehydration follows Saytzeff's rule: the predominant alkene is the more highly substituted, thermodynamically stable alkene containing the greater number of hyperconjugative $\alpha$-hydrogens.

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CH(OH)-CH_3} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\mathrm{H^+}} \underset{\text{But-2-ene (Major, 80\%)}}{\mathrm{CH_3-CH=CH-CH_3}} + \underset{\text{But-1-ene (Minor, 20\%)}}{\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CH=CH_2}}
$$

***

## 2.3 Oxidation Reactions of Alcohols ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Oxidation of alcohols involves the cleavage of both an $\mathrm{O-H}$ bond and a $\mathrm{C-H}$ bond, with the formal generation of a carbonyl ($\mathrm{C=O}$) bond. Hence, these are also referred to as **dehydrogenation reactions**.

$$
\begin{array}{rcccl}
1^\circ \text{ Alcohol} & \xrightarrow{[\mathrm{O}]} & \text{Aldehyde} & \xrightarrow{[\mathrm{O}]} & \text{Carboxylic Acid (Same carbon count)} \\
2^\circ \text{ Alcohol} & \xrightarrow{[\mathrm{O}]} & \text{Ketone} & \xrightarrow[\text{Vigorous conditions}]{[\mathrm{O}]} & \text{Carboxylic Acids (Fewer carbons - Popoff's Rule)} \\
3^\circ \text{ Alcohol} & \xrightarrow[\text{Neutral/Alkaline}]{[\mathrm{O}]} & \text{No Reaction} & \xrightarrow[\text{Acidic } \mathrm{KMnO_4}/\Delta]{[\mathrm{O}]} & \text{Alkene intermediate } \to \text{ Degraded acids}
\end{array}
$$

### Selective Reagents:

#### 1. Pyridinium Chlorochromate ($\mathrm{PCC}$):
A complex of chromium trioxide with pyridine and $\mathrm{HCl}$ ($\mathrm{C_5H_5NH^+CrO_3Cl^-}$) in anhydrous dichloromethane ($\mathrm{CH_2Cl_2}$).
* Stoppage at aldehyde without over-oxidation to carboxylic acid:

$$
\mathrm{R-CH_2OH} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{PCC,\ CH_2Cl_2}} \mathrm{R-CHO}
$$

* Does not affect isolated aliphatic non-conjugated carbon-carbon double bonds:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH=CH-CH_2OH} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{PCC}} \mathrm{CH_3-CH=CH-CHO} \quad \text{(Crotonaldehyde)}
$$

#### 2. Collins Reagent ($\mathrm{CrO_3 \cdot 2C_5H_5N}$) / Jones Reagent ($\mathrm{CrO_3 / \text{aq. } H_2SO_4 \text{ in acetone}}$):
Jones reagent quantitatively oxidizes $1^\circ$ alcohols to carboxylic acids, and $2^\circ$ alcohols to ketones.

#### 3. Acidified Potassium Permanganate ($\mathrm{KMnO_4 / H^+}$) or Potassium Dichromate ($\mathrm{K_2Cr_2O_7 / H_2SO_4}$):
Powerful strong oxidants converting primary alcohols directly to carboxylic acids:

$$
\mathrm{R-CH_2OH} \xrightarrow{\text{alk. }\mathrm{KMnO_4} \text{ or } \mathrm{H^+/K_2Cr_2O_7}} \mathrm{R-COOH}
$$

***

## 2.4 Catalytic Dehydrogenation over Heated Copper at $573\text{ K}$ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

When vapors of alcohols are passed over finely divided metallic copper catalyst maintained at $573\text{ K}$ ($300^\circ\text{C}$):

1. **Primary Alcohol ($1^\circ$): Dehydrogenation to Aldehyde**
   

$$
\mathrm{R-CH_2OH} \xrightarrow[\mathrm{573\ K}]{\mathrm{Cu}} \mathrm{R-CHO} + \mathrm{H_2}\uparrow
$$

   

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH} \xrightarrow[\mathrm{573\ K}]{\mathrm{Cu}} \mathrm{CH_3CHO} + \mathrm{H_2}\uparrow
$$

2. **Secondary Alcohol ($2^\circ$): Dehydrogenation to Ketone**
   

$$
\mathrm{R-CH(OH)-R'} \xrightarrow[\mathrm{573\ K}]{\mathrm{Cu}} \mathrm{R-CO-R'} + \mathrm{H_2}\uparrow
$$

   

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH(OH)-CH_3} \xrightarrow[\mathrm{573\ K}]{\mathrm{Cu}} \mathrm{CH_3-CO-CH_3} + \mathrm{H_2}\uparrow
$$

3. **Tertiary Alcohol ($3^\circ$): Dehydration (Elimination) to Alkene**  
   *Because tertiary alcohols lack an $\alpha$-hydrogen atom*, they cannot undergo dehydrogenation. Instead, under the influence of hot $\mathrm{Cu}$ at $573\text{ K}$, intramolecular $\beta$-elimination occurs:
   

$$
\left(\mathrm{CH_3}\right)_3\mathrm{C-OH} \xrightarrow[\mathrm{573\ K}]{\mathrm{Cu}} \left(\mathrm{CH_3}\right)_2\mathrm{C=CH_2} + \mathrm{H_2O} \quad \text{(2-Methylpropene / Isobutylene)}
$$

# 3. Commercially Important Alcohols ⭐⭐⭐⭐

***

## 3.1 Methanol ($\mathrm{CH_3OH}$, "Wood Spirit")

### Industrial Synthesis:
Catalytic hydrogenation of carbon monoxide (synthesis gas/water gas) at elevated pressure and temperature over a mixed metal-oxide catalyst:

$$
\mathrm{CO(g)} + 2\mathrm{H_2(g)} \xrightarrow[\substack{200\text{--}300\text{ atm} \\ 573\text{--}673\text{ K}}]{\mathrm{ZnO - Cr_2O_3}} \mathrm{CH_3OH(l)}
$$

*Operating Parameters:*
* **Catalyst:** Zinc oxide - Chromium trioxide mixture ($\mathrm{ZnO - Cr_2O_3}$)
* **Pressure:** $200\text{ to }300\text{ atm}$ ($20\text{ to }30\text{ MPa}$)
* **Temperature:** $573\text{ to }673\text{ K}$ ($300\text{ to }400^\circ\text{C}$)

### Physiological Toxicity:
* Ingestion of even minute quantities of methanol ($10\text{--}30\text{ mL}$) causes acute poisoning leading to irreversible blindness, and $\ge 30\text{ mL}$ is fatal.
* **Biochemical Mechanism of Action:** In the human liver, methanol is enzymatically oxidized by alcohol dehydrogenase into **methanal (formaldehyde, $\mathrm{HCHO}$)**, which is rapidly transformed into **methanoic acid (formic acid, $\mathrm{HCOOH}$)**:
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3OH} \xrightarrow{\text{Alcohol dehydrogenase}} \mathrm{HCHO} \longrightarrow \mathrm{HCOOH}
$$

  Methanal acts as a powerful electrophilic cross-linking agent that coagulates cellular protoplasm (similar to the hard-boiling of an egg). Methanoic acid causes systemic metabolic acidosis and selectively induces optic nerve demyelination, causing irreversible retinal cell necrosis and blindness.

***

## 3.2 Ethanol ($\mathrm{C_2H_5OH}$)

### Large-Scale Production by Fermentation:
Ethanol is produced industrially by the enzymatic anaerobic fermentation of carbohydrates derived from molasses, sugarcane juice, or starchy grains.

#### Biochemical Transformations:
1. **Hydrolysis of Sucrose (Inversion):**  
   The enzyme **invertase** hydrolyzes sucrose into an equimolar mixture of D-glucose and D-fructose:
   

$$
\mathrm{C_{12}H_{22}O_{11}} + \mathrm{H_2O} \xrightarrow{\text{Invertase}} \underset{\text{D-Glucose}}{\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}} + \underset{\text{D-Fructose}}{\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}}
$$

2. **Zymase-Mediated Fermentation:**  
   The intracellular multi-enzyme complex **zymase** (secreted by active yeast cells, *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*) ferments glucose/fructose into ethanol and carbon dioxide:
   

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6} \xrightarrow{\text{Zymase}} 2\mathrm{C_2H_5OH} + 2\mathrm{CO_2}\uparrow
$$

*Physicochemical Reaction Limits:*  
* Fermentation must proceed under strict **anaerobic conditions**. In the presence of atmospheric oxygen, aerobic *Acetobacter aceti* bacteria oxidize the ethanol into ethanoic acid ($\mathrm{CH_3COOH}$), souring the mixture.
* When the ethanol concentration reaches approximately $14\%$, the physiological toxicity of the alcohol terminates yeast enzymatic action. Higher concentrations cannot be obtained by direct fermentation.
* **Rectified Spirit:** Fractional distillation of the fermented broth yields an azeotropic mixture containing $95.57\%$ ethanol and $4.43\%$ water (Boiling Point: $351.15\text{ K}$). It cannot be separated further by ordinary fractional distillation.
* **Absolute Alcohol:** $100\%$ anhydrous ethanol obtained via azeotropic distillation with benzene or chemical drying with quicklime ($\mathrm{CaO}$).

### Denaturation of Industrial Alcohol:
To prevent the diversion and misuse of duty-free or subsidized industrial ethanol for intoxicating beverage purposes, it is rendered unfit for human consumption by adding:
* **Methanol** ($~5\%$, toxic denaturant)
* **Pyridine** (foul-smelling organic base)
* **Copper sulfate ($\mathrm{CuSO_4}$)** (imparts a characteristic warning blue color)

### Question 2 [CBSE 2023, 2 Marks]
Give simple chemical tests to distinguish between the following pairs of compounds:  
**(i)** Butan-1-ol and 2-Methylpropan-2-ol  
**(ii)** Ethanol and Propan-1-ol

**(i) Distinction between Butan-1-ol ($1^\circ$) and 2-Methylpropan-2-ol ($3^\circ$):**

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH} + 4\mathrm{I_2} + 6\mathrm{NaOH} \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Iodoform (Yellow ppt)}}{\mathrm{CHI_3}\downarrow} + \mathrm{HCOONa} + 5\mathrm{NaI} + 5\mathrm{H_2O}

### Question 3 [CBSE 2022 Term-2, 3 Marks]
An organic compound $(A)$ with molecular formula $\mathrm{C_4H_{10}O}$ does not react with Lucas reagent at room temperature. When vapors of compound $(A)$ are passed over heated copper at $573\text{ K}$, it forms compound $(B)$ ($\mathrm{C_4H_8O}$). Compound $(B)$ reduces Tollens' reagent. Identify $(A)$ and $(B)$, and write the chemical reactions involved.

**Solution:**  
1. *Deduction of Functional Group of $(A)$:* Molecular formula $\mathrm{C_4H_{10}O}$ conforms to $\mathrm{C_nH_{2n+2}O}$, indicating an alcohol or ether. Since it reacts over hot $\mathrm{Cu}$ to form a carbonyl compound, $(A)$ is an alcohol.  
2. *Lucas Reactivity:* Compound $(A)$ does not react with Lucas reagent at room temperature, which identifies $(A)$ as a **primary ($1^\circ$) alcohol**.  
3. *Identification of $(B)$:* $(A)$ is oxidized over $\mathrm{Cu}/573\text{ K}$ via catalytic dehydrogenation to form $(B)$ ($\mathrm{C_4H_8O}$). Since $(B)$ reduces Tollens' reagent, $(B)$ must be an **aldehyde**.  
4. There are two isomeric $1^\circ$ alcohols with formula $\mathrm{C_4H_{10}O}$: Butan-1-ol and 2-Methylpropan-1-ol.  
   * Using Butan-1-ol:
     

$$
\underset{(A)\ \text{Butan-1-ol}}{\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2OH}} \xrightarrow[\mathrm{573\ K}]{\mathrm{Cu}} \underset{(B)\ \text{Butanal}}{\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2CHO}} + \mathrm{H_2}\uparrow
$$

   * Tollens' Reaction for $(B)$:
     

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2CHO} + 2[\mathrm{Ag(NH_3)_2}]^+ + 3\mathrm{OH^-} \longrightarrow \mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2COO^-} + \underset{\text{Silver Mirror}}{2\mathrm{Ag}\downarrow} + 4\mathrm{NH_3} + 2\mathrm{H_2O}
$$

*Conclusion:*  
Compound $(A) = \text{Butan-1-ol (or 2-Methylpropan-1-ol)}$  
Compound $(B) = \text{Butanal (or 2-Methylpropanal)}$

### Question 4 [CBSE 2021, 2 Marks]
How do you account for the following?  
**(a)** Propan-1-ol has a significantly higher boiling point than Butane, despite their comparable molecular weights.  
**(b)** The dipole moment of methanol is $1.71\text{ D}$, whereas the $\mathrm{C-O-H}$ bond angle is $108.9^\circ$, slightly less than the tetrahedral angle.

**Solution:**  
**(a)** Propan-1-ol ($\mathrm{M_r} \approx 60\text{ g mol}^{-1}$) possesses a polar hydroxyl group that forms extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonds, requiring considerable thermal energy to break. Butane ($\mathrm{M_r} \approx 58\text{ g mol}^{-1}$) is entirely non-polar, possessing only weak Van der Waals dispersion forces.  
**(b)** The central oxygen atom in methanol is $\mathrm{sp^3}$-hybridized. The expected ideal tetrahedral angle is $109.5^\circ$. However, the presence of **two unshared electron lone pairs** on the oxygen atom exerts strong lone pair-lone pair (lp-lp) and lone pair-bond pair (lp-bp) repulsions (VSEPR theory), which compresses the $\mathrm{C-O-H}$ bond angle down to $108.9^\circ$.

# 5. Examiner Traps & Common Mistakes

| Concept / Reaction | The Fatal Student Trap | The Chemically Rigorous NCERT Reality |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |

| **Ethanol Dehydration Temperature** | Conflating $443\text{ K}$ and $413\text{ K}$ with $\mathrm{conc.\ H_2SO_4}$. | At $\mathbf{443\text{ K}}$ ($170^\circ\text{C}$): Intramolecular dehydration gives **alkene** ($\mathrm{CH_2=CH_2}$).
At $\mathbf{413\text{ K}}$ ($140^\circ\text{C}$): Intermolecular dehydration gives **ether** ($\mathrm{C_2H_5OC_2H_5}$). |

| **Copper at $573\text{ K}$ on $3^\circ$ Alcohols** | Writing dehydrogenation to give a ketone with one less carbon. | Tertiary alcohols have **no $\alpha$-hydrogen**. They undergo **dehydration** to an alkene ($\mathrm{R_2C=CH_2}$), not dehydrogenation! |
| **Methanol vs Water Acidity** | Stating "all alcohols are weaker acids than water." | Methanol ($\mathrm{CH_3OH}$) is slightly **more acidic** than water ($K_a = 3.2 \times 10^{-16}$ vs $1.8 \times 10^{-16}$). All other aliphatic alcohols are weaker acids than water. |
| **PCC vs Aqueous Chromic Acid** | Using Jones Reagent or acidified $\mathrm{KMnO_4}$ when asked to convert primary alcohols to aldehydes. | Strong aqueous oxidants over-oxidize $1^\circ$ alcohols to **carboxylic acids**. PCC in anhydrous $\mathrm{CH_2Cl_2}$ must be specified for aldehydes. |
| **Esterification Reversibility** | Writing a unidirectional forward arrow ($\rightarrow$) without removing products. | Esterification is a **reversible equilibrium** ($\rightleftharpoons$). Conc. $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ acts both as a catalyst and as a **dehydrating agent** to drive equilibrium forward. |
| **Lucas Test Observations** | Claiming $1^\circ$ alcohols produce no reaction at all. | $1^\circ$ alcohols *do* react via an $\mathrm{S_N2}$ pathway, but reaction is imperceptibly slow at room temperature; turbidity appears **upon heating**. |

# 6. Speed Hacks & Golden Points

### 1. The Carbinol Hydrogen Count Rule for Copper Dehydrogenation
Count the number of hydrogens directly bound to the carbinol carbon ($\mathrm{C-OH}$):
* **Two $\alpha$-H's ($1^\circ$ alcohol):** Remove $\mathrm{H_2} \longrightarrow$ **Aldehyde**
* **One $\alpha$-H ($2^\circ$ alcohol):** Remove $\mathrm{H_2} \longrightarrow$ **Ketone**
* **Zero $\alpha$-H's ($3^\circ$ alcohol):** Remove $\mathrm{H_2O}$ (from $\beta$-carbon) $\longrightarrow$ **Alkene**

### 2. Acid/Base Reactivity Mnemonics
* **"Protons Out, Less Crowded Wins":** Acidity drops with branching ($1^\circ > 2^\circ > 3^\circ$) due to $+I$ destabilization of the alkoxide anion.
* **"Leaving Out, More Crowded Wins":** Cleavage of $\mathrm{C-O}$ (dehydration, Lucas reaction) accelerates with branching ($3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ$) due to carbocation stability.

### 3. Industrial Alcohol Nomenclature Flashcards
* **Rectified Spirit:** $95.6\%\ \mathrm{C_2H_5OH} + 4.4\%\ \mathrm{H_2O}$ (Constant boiling azeotropic mixture).
* **Absolute Alcohol:** $100\%$ pure ethanol.
* **Denatured Alcohol / Methylated Spirit:** Ethanol made poisonous with $\mathrm{CH_3OH}$ ($~5\%$) + pyridine (smell) + $\mathrm{CuSO_4}$ (color).
* **Power Alcohol:** Mixture of $20\%$ absolute alcohol $+ 80\%$ petrol/gasoline (used as internal combustion motor fuel).

# 1. Pedagogical Theory, Structure, and Acidity of Phenols

***

### 1.1 Structural Features & Resonance Hybrid of Phenol
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

* **Molecular Formula:** $\mathrm{C_6H_5OH}$ (Benzenol / Hydroxybenzene)
* **Hybridization:** The oxygen atom of the hydroxyl group ($-\mathrm{OH}$) is bonded to an $sp^2$-hybridized carbon atom of the aromatic ring.
* **Bond Characteristics:**
  * The $\mathrm{C-O}$ bond length in phenol is **$136\text{ pm}$**, which is significantly shorter than the $\mathrm{C-O}$ bond length in methanol (**$142\text{ pm}$**).
  * **Two Underlying Physical Causes:**
    1. **Partial Double Bond Character:** Conjugation of the unshared electron pairs of oxygen with the $\pi$-electron system of the aromatic ring.
    2. **Electronegativity Difference of Carbon:** The oxygen is attached to an $sp^2$-hybridized carbon (higher $s$-character, $33.3\%$, more electronegative) compared to an $sp^3$-hybridized carbon in aliphatic alcohols ($25\%$, less electronegative).

```
Resonance Structures of Phenol:
  :O-H            +O-H            +O-H            +O-H            :O-H
   ..              ..              ..              ..              ..
 /    \         /    \(-)       /    \           /(-) \         /    \
|      |  <->  |      |   <->  |      |   <->   |      |  <->  |      |
 \    /         \    /          \(-) /           \    /         \    /
   (I)            (II)            (III)            (IV)            (V)
```

$$
\text{Dipole moment of phenol } (\mu = 1.54\text{ D}) < \text{Dipole moment of methanol } (\mu = 1.71\text{ D})
$$

*Reason:* The aromatic ring exerts a strong electron-withdrawing inductive/resonance effect ($-I$ and $+R$), pulling electron density away from oxygen toward the ring, opposing the $\mathrm{C}^{\delta+}-\mathrm{O}^{\delta-}$ bond dipole.

***

### 1.2 Nature of Acidity: Comparative Analysis
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### Phenol vs. Aliphatic Alcohols
Phenols react with electropositive metals ($\mathrm{Na}$, $\mathrm{K}$) as well as aqueous alkali ($\mathrm{NaOH}$), whereas aliphatic alcohols react only with reactive metals ($\mathrm{Na}$, $\mathrm{K}$) and do **not** react with aqueous $\mathrm{NaOH}$.

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5OH + NaOH(aq) \rightleftharpoons C_6H_5O^-Na^+ + H_2O}
$$

$$
\mathrm{R-OH + NaOH(aq) \rightarrow \text{No Reaction (alcohols are weaker acids than water)}}
$$

```
Resonance Structures of Phenoxide Ion:
  :O:(-)          :O:             :O:             :O:             :O:(-)
   ..              ..              ..              ..              ..
 /    \         /    \(-)       /    \           /(-) \         /    \
|      |  <->  |      |   <->  |      |   <->   |      |  <->  |      |
 \    /         \    /          \(-) /           \    /         \    /
   (I)            (II)            (III)            (IV)            (V)
```

**Rigorous Resonance Interpretation (NCERT Criterion):**
1. In un-ionized phenol, structures II, III, and IV involve **charge separation** (positive charge on the highly electronegative oxygen atom and negative charge dispersed on ring carbons). Charge separation destabilizes the canonical forms.
2. In the phenoxide ion ($\mathrm{C_6H_5O^-}$), there is **no charge separation**. The negative charge is delocalized over the ortho and para positions of the benzene ring.
3. Therefore, the phenoxide ion is **more resonance-stabilized** than neutral phenol. The equilibrium shifts toward ionization:
   

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5OH + H_2O \rightleftharpoons C_6H_5O^- + H_3O^+} \quad (pK_a \approx 10.0)
$$

#### Quantitative Acidity Comparison ($pK_a$ Values at $298\text{ K}$)

$$
\text{Picric acid } (0.78) < 2,4\text{-Dinitrophenol } (4.0) < 4\text{-Nitrophenol } (7.15) < 2\text{-Nitrophenol } (7.23) < \text{Phenol } (9.98) < m\text{-Cresol } (10.08) < p\text{-Cresol } (10.26) < \text{Ethanol } (15.9)
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Acid Strength} \propto K_a \propto \frac{1}{pK_a}}
$$

***

### 1.3 Substituent Effects on Acidity
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

1. **Electron-Withdrawing Groups (EWGs) ($-I, -M/-R$):**
   * Groups like $-\mathrm{NO_2}, -\mathrm{CN}, -\mathrm{X}, -\mathrm{CHO}, -\mathrm{COOH}$.
   * **Mechanism:** Disperse the negative charge of the phenoxide ion by drawing electron density through resonance and inductive pathways.
   * **Position Dependency:** The $-R$ effect operates exclusively at **ortho** and **para** positions. At the **meta** position, $-\mathrm{NO_2}$ stabilizes the phenoxide ion solely via the weaker $-I$ effect.
   * *Order:* $4\text{-nitrophenol} > 2\text{-nitrophenol} > 3\text{-nitrophenol} > \text{phenol}$
   * *Anomaly (ortho vs. para):* 4-Nitrophenol ($pK_a = 7.15$) is slightly more acidic than 2-nitrophenol ($pK_a = 7.23$) due to **intramolecular hydrogen bonding** (chelation) in 2-nitrophenol, which stabilizes the undissociated molecule and hinders the release of the proton.

2. **Electron-Donating Groups (EDGs) ($+I, +H, +R$):**
   * Groups like $-\mathrm{CH_3}, -\mathrm{OCH_3}, -\mathrm{NH_2}$.
   * **Mechanism:** Intensify the negative charge on the phenoxide ion, destabilizing it and decreasing acid strength.
   * For cresols (methylphenols), hyperconjugation ($+H$) and $+I$ operate at ortho/para, while only $+I$ operates at meta:
     

$$
\text{Acidic Strength: } \text{Phenol} > m\text{-cresol} > p\text{-cresol} > o\text{-cresol}
$$

# 2. Complete Chemical Reactions, Mechanisms & Industrial Processes

### 2.1 Reactions Involving Cleavage of $\mathrm{O-H}$ Bond (Acylation & Esterification)
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Phenols react with acid chlorides or acid anhydrides in the presence of a base (pyridine) to yield phenyl esters. Pyridine neutralizes the byproduct $\mathrm{HCl}$, shifting the equilibrium forward.

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5OH + CH_3COCl \xrightarrow{\text{Pyridine}} C_6H_5OCOCH_3 + HCl}
$$

#### Synthesis of Aspirin (Acetylation of Salicylic Acid)
Salicylic acid (2-hydroxybenzoic acid) reacts with acetic anhydride in the presence of concentrated $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ acting as an acid catalyst:

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_4(OH)(COOH) + (CH_3CO)_2O \xrightarrow{\text{conc. } H_2SO_4} C_6H_4(OCOCH_3)(COOH) + CH_3COOH}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Salicylic Acid} + \text{Acetic Anhydride} \rightarrow \text{Acetylsalicylic Acid (Aspirin)} + \text{Ethanoic Acid}}
$$

* **Key Detail:** The phenolic $-\mathrm{OH}$ group is acetylated, **not** the carboxylic acid $-\mathrm{COOH}$ group.

### 2.2 Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution (EAS) of Phenols
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

The $-\mathrm{OH}$ group is a **strongly activating group** and **ortho/para-directing** because the lone pairs on oxygen provide high electron density to the ring via the $+R$ effect, specifically enriching the 2-, 4-, and 6-positions.

***

#### A. Halogenation (Solvent-Controlled Regioselectivity)
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

* **Case 1: Monobromination in Non-Polar / Low Polarity Media**
  * **Reagents:** $\mathrm{Br_2}$ dissolved in $\mathrm{CS_2}$ or $\mathrm{CHCl_3}$ at low temperature ($T = 273\text{ K}$).
  * **Lewis Acid Requirement:** **No $\mathrm{FeBr_3}$ catalyst is needed** because the ring is already strongly activated by the $-\mathrm{OH}$ group.
  * **Solvent Effect:** In low dielectric media ($\mathrm{CS_2}, \epsilon \approx 2.6$), phenol does not ionize and remains as neutral $\mathrm{C_6H_5OH}$. The ring is moderately activated, leading selectively to mono-substitution.

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5OH + Br_2 \xrightarrow[\text{273 K}]{\mathrm{CS_2}} \underset{\text{(Minor, sterically hindered)}}{o\text{-Bromophenol}} + \underset{\text{(Major, sterically favored)}}{p\text{-Bromophenol}} + HBr}
$$

* **Case 2: Polybromination in Polar Protane Medium (Bromine Water)**
  * **Reagents:** $\mathrm{Br_2}/\mathrm{H_2O}$ (excess bromine water) at room temperature.
  * **Solvent Effect:** Water ($\epsilon \approx 80$) facilitates complete ionization of phenol into the **phenoxide ion** ($\mathrm{C_6H_5O^-}$). The phenoxide ion has an oxygen with a full negative charge, exerting an exceptionally powerful $+R$ effect that hyper-activates all ortho and para positions.

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5OH + 3Br_2(aq) \rightarrow \underset{\text{2,4,6-Tribromophenol (White Precipitate)}}{\mathrm{C_6H_2Br_3OH}} \downarrow + 3HBr}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Phenol} + 3\mathrm{Br_2(aq)} \rightarrow \text{2,4,6-tribromophenol}\downarrow \text{ (White ppt)} + 3\mathrm{HBr}}
$$

*Application:* Diagnostic test to distinguish phenol from aliphatic alcohols.

***

#### B. Nitration
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

* **Case 1: Dilute Nitric Acid**
  * **Conditions:** $\text{dil. }\mathrm{HNO_3}$ at low temperature ($293\text{ K}$).

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5OH + \text{dil. } HNO_3 \xrightarrow{293\text{ K}} \underset{\text{(Steam volatile)}}{o\text{-Nitrophenol}} + \underset{\text{(Non-steam volatile)}}{p\text{-Nitrophenol}} + H_2O}
$$

* **Separation Principle (Steam Distillation):**
  * **$o$-Nitrophenol:** Displays **intramolecular hydrogen bonding** (chelation), forming a planar 6-membered ring. This prevents intermolecular associations, lowering boiling point and enhancing volatility in steam.
  * **$p$-Nitrophenol:** Displays extensive **intermolecular hydrogen bonding** causing molecular association, giving it a much higher boiling point and low volatility.

```
Intramolecular H-bonding in o-Nitrophenol:
         O - H ····· O
        /             \\
      (Ar)             N = O
        \             /
         -------------
```

* **Case 2: Concentrated Nitric Acid**
  * **Conditions:** $\text{conc. }\mathrm{HNO_3} + \text{conc. }\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$.

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5OH + 3\text{ conc. } HNO_3 \xrightarrow{\text{conc. } H_2SO_4} \underset{\text{2,4,6-Trinitrophenol (Picric Acid)}}{\mathrm{C_6H_2(NO_2)_3OH}} + 3H_2O}
$$

* *Crucial NCERT Caveat:* The yield of picric acid via direct nitration is **very poor** because concentrated $\mathrm{HNO_3}$ oxidizes the electron-rich aromatic ring to benzoquinone and tarry matter.
* *Modern Industrial Route:*
  1. Sulfonation of phenol with conc. $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ yields phenol-2,4-disulfonic acid.
  2. Treatment with conc. $\mathrm{HNO_3}$ replaces the $-\mathrm{SO_3H}$ groups with $-\mathrm{NO_2}$ via ipso-substitution, preventing oxidative decomposition and yielding high-purity picric acid.

### 2.3 Named Reactions of Phenol

***

#### Kolbe's Reaction (Carboxylation)
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

* **Step 1:** Phenol is converted to sodium phenoxide by treatment with $\mathrm{NaOH}$. The phenoxide ion is more nucleophilic than neutral phenol toward weak electrophiles.
* **Step 2:** Sodium phenoxide is treated with carbon dioxide ($\mathrm{CO_2}$, a weak electrophile) under elevated pressure ($4\text{ to }7\text{ atm}$) and temperature ($400\text{ K}$).
* **Step 3:** Acidification of the intermediate yields salicylic acid.

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5OH + NaOH \rightarrow C_6H_5O^-Na^+ + H_2O}
$$

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5O^-Na^+ + CO_2 \xrightarrow[400\text{ K}]{4-7\text{ atm}} \text{Sodium Salicylate Intermediate} \xrightarrow{H^+} \underset{\text{Salicylic Acid (2-Hydroxybenzoic acid)}}{\mathrm{C_6H_4(OH)(COOH)}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Major Product: Salicylic Acid (ortho isomer predominates due to sodium ion chelation)}}
$$

***

#### Reimer-Tiemann Reaction (Formylation)
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

* **Reagents:** Chloroform ($\mathrm{CHCl_3}$) and aqueous sodium hydroxide ($\mathrm{NaOH}$) at $340\text{ K}$, followed by acid hydrolysis.
* **Active Electrophile:** **Dichlorocarbene ($:\mathrm{CCl_2}$)**.
* **Generation of Electrophile ($\alpha$-elimination):**

$$
\mathrm{OH^- + CHCl_3 \rightleftharpoons H_2O + ^-:\mathrm{CCl_3} \xrightarrow{\text{slow (-Cl^-)}} :\mathrm{CCl_2} \quad (\text{Neutral, 6-electron, electron-deficient species})}
$$

* **Detailed Step-by-Step Mechanism:**
  1. Attack of phenoxide ion on $:\mathrm{CCl_2}$ produces an intermediate carbanion at the ortho position.
  2. Proton transfer restores aromaticity, yielding a benzylidene dichloride intermediate.
  3. Alkaline hydrolysis replaces the two chlorines with a geminal diol, which eliminates water to form an aldehyde group.
  4. Acidification produces **salicylaldehyde** (2-hydroxybenzaldehyde).

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5OH \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) } H^+]{\text{(i) } CHCl_3 + NaOH} \underset{\text{Salicylaldehyde (Major)}}{\mathrm{C_6H_4(OH)(CHO)}} + 2NaCl + 2H_2O}
$$

* *Variation:* If **carbon tetrachloride ($\mathrm{CCl_4}$)** is used instead of $\mathrm{CHCl_3}$, the product formed after hydrolysis is **salicylic acid**:

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5OH \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) } H^+]{\text{(i) } CCl_4 + NaOH} \mathrm{C_6H_4(OH)(COOH)}}
$$

***

### 2.4 Oxidation and Reduction Reactions
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### Reaction with Zinc Dust (Deoxygenation / Reduction)
Vapor of phenol passed over heated zinc dust removes the oxygen atom completely, producing benzene:

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5OH + Zn \xrightarrow{\Delta} C_6H_6 + ZnO}
$$

$$
\boxed{\Delta H < 0, \quad \text{Conversion of Phenol to Benzene}}
$$

#### Oxidation with Chromic Acid / Alkaline $\mathrm{K_2S_2O_8}$
Phenol oxidizes slowly in the presence of air or rapidly with acidified sodium dichromate ($\mathrm{Na_2Cr_2O_7} / \mathrm{H_2SO_4}$) to give a conjugated diketone:

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5OH + [O] \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Na_2Cr_2O_7 / H_2SO_4}} \underset{\text{benzo-1,4-quinone (p-Benzoquinone)}}{\mathrm{O=C_6H_4=O}}}
$$

*Physical Consequence:* Pure phenol is colorless, but turns reddish-pink upon prolonged exposure to air due to the formation of $p$-benzoquinone and its charge-transfer complexes (phenoquinone).

### Question 1 [CBSE Board 2024, 3 Marks]
**(a)** How do you explain that phenol is more acidic than ethanol?  
**(b)** Write the chemical reaction for the preparation of Aspirin from salicylic acid.  
**(c)** Give a chemical test to distinguish between Phenol and Benzyl alcohol.

**Answer & Marking Scheme:**
*(a) Acidity Rationale [1 Mark]:*  
Ionization of phenol generates the phenoxide ion ($\mathrm{C_6H_5O^-}$), which is stabilized by resonance over the ortho/para positions of the benzene ring. Ionization of ethanol generates the ethoxide ion ($\mathrm{CH_3CH_2O^-}$), where the negative charge is localized on the oxygen and destabilized by the $+I$ inductive effect of the ethyl group. Thus, phenoxide is significantly more stable than ethoxide.

*(b) Aspirin Synthesis [1 Mark]:*

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_4(OH)(COOH) + (CH_3CO)_2O \xrightarrow{\text{conc. } H_2SO_4} C_6H_4(OCOCH_3)(COOH) + CH_3COOH}
$$

*(c) Diagnostic Distinguishing Test [1 Mark]:*  
Add neutral $\mathrm{FeCl_3}$ solution to both compounds:
* **Phenol:** Gives a characteristic **violet/purple coloration** due to formation of the complex ion $[\mathrm{Fe(OC_6H_5)_6}]^{3-}$.
* **Benzyl alcohol ($\mathrm{C_6H_5CH_2OH}$):** Being an aliphatic/aralkyl alcohol, it gives **no characteristic color change** with neutral $\mathrm{FeCl_3}$.
*(Alternative test: Add bromine water; phenol gives a white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromophenol, benzyl alcohol does not).*

### Question 2 [CBSE Board 2023, 2 Marks]
Account for the following:
**(i)** $o$-Nitrophenol is more steam-volatile than $p$-nitrophenol.  
**(ii)** Phenol does not give a protonation reaction readily like aliphatic alcohols.

**Answer & Marking Scheme:**
*(i) [1 Mark]:*  
$o$-Nitrophenol has **intramolecular hydrogen bonding** (within the same molecule), preventing association with neighboring molecules. $p$-Nitrophenol exhibits extensive **intermolecular hydrogen bonding**, causing high molecular association and a higher boiling point. Hence, $o$-nitrophenol is steam volatile.

*(ii) [1 Mark]:*  
In phenol, the lone pairs of electrons on the oxygen atom are delocalized into the aromatic ring through resonance ($+R$ effect), acquiring a partial positive charge ($\delta+$). This significantly reduces the electron density on oxygen, making it unavailable to accept a proton ($\mathrm{H^+}$). In contrast, aliphatic alcohols have localized lone pairs on oxygen, further enhanced by the $+I$ effect of alkyl groups.

### Quick Mnemonics

* **Reimer-Tiemann Electrophile Generation:**
  

$$

# Preparation, Physical Properties, and Cleavage Reactions of Ethers

# MASTER TEACHING NOTE: ALCOHOLS, PHENOLS, AND ETHERS

## Subtopic: Ethers — Preparation, Physical Properties, and Cleavage Reactions
**Target:** CBSE Class 12 Board Examination & Competitive Entrances (JEE/NEET)  
**Pedagogical Framework:** Strict NCERT Alignment, Mechanistic Clarity, Step-by-Step Problem Solving

***

# SECTION 1: IN-DEPTH PEDAGOGICAL THEORY & FUNDAMENTAL CONCEPTS

### 1.1 Structural Classification and Nomenclature
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐  
Ethers are organic compounds possessing the general structural formula:

$$
\mathrm{R-O-R'} \quad \text{or} \quad \mathrm{Ar-O-R} \quad \text{or} \quad \mathrm{Ar-O-Ar'}
$$

They are regarded as dialkyl derivatives of water or monoalkyl derivatives of alcohols.

* **Symmetrical (Simple) Ethers:** Identical alkyl or aryl groups attached to the oxygen atom.
  

$$
\mathrm{R} = \mathrm{R'} \quad (\text{e.g., } \mathrm{CH_3CH_2-O-CH_2CH_3} \text{ : Ethoxyethane})
$$

* **Unsymmetrical (Mixed) Ethers:** Differing alkyl or aryl groups attached to the oxygen atom.
  

$$
\mathrm{R} \neq \mathrm{R'} \quad (\text{e.g., } \mathrm{CH_3-O-CH_2CH_3} \text{ : Methoxyethane})
$$

#### NCERT Molecular Geometry
* **Hybridization:** The central oxygen atom is $\mathrm{sp^3}$ hybridized.
* **Bond Geometry:** Bent/V-shaped geometry due to the presence of two bond pairs and two lone pairs.
* **Bond Angle Anomaly:** In dimethyl ether ($\mathrm{CH_3-O-CH_3}$), the $\mathrm{C-O-C}$ bond angle is $111.7^\circ$.  
  * *Interpretation:* This angle is notably larger than the standard tetrahedral angle ($109.5^\circ$) and the bond angle in water ($104.5^\circ$). The deviation arises from strong steric repulsive interactions between the two bulky alkyl ($-\mathrm{CH_3}$) groups, which overcome the lone pair-lone pair repulsions.
* **Bond Length:** The $\mathrm{C-O}$ bond length in ethers is approximately $141\text{ pm}$, nearly identical to that in alcohols.

***

### 1.2 Preparation Method I: Dehydration of Alcohols
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (Core CBSE Board Focus)

Intermolecular dehydration of primary alcohols in the presence of protic acids ($\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ or $\mathrm{H_3PO_4}$) yields symmetrical ethers under strictly controlled temperature regimes.

#### The Temperature Bifurcation Trap

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH} &\xrightarrow{\mathrm{conc.\ H_2SO_4,\ 413\ K\ (140^\circ C)}} \mathrm{CH_3CH_2-O-CH_2CH_3} + \mathrm{H_2O} \quad [\mathrm{S_N2\ Substitution}] \\
\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH} &\xrightarrow{\mathrm{conc.\ H_2SO_4,\ 443\ K\ (170^\circ C)}} \mathrm{CH_2=CH_2} + \mathrm{H_2O} \quad [\mathrm{E1\ Elimination}]
\end{aligned}
$$

#### Fundamental Limitations
1. **Applicability:** Feasible *only* for primary ($1^\circ$) unhindered alcohols.
2. **Substrate Restrictions:** Secondary ($2^\circ$) and tertiary ($3^\circ$) alcohols yield alkenes as the major product because the intermediate carbocation undergoes preferential elimination rather than bimolecular nucleophilic attack ($\mathrm{E1} > \mathrm{S_N2}$).
3. **Symmetry Restrictions:** Unsuitable for the preparation of unsymmetrical ethers ($\mathrm{R-O-R'}$). A mixture of two different alcohols ($\mathrm{ROH} + \mathrm{R'OH}$) produces an inseparable mixture of three distinct ethers:
   

$$
\mathrm{R-O-R} + \mathrm{R'-O-R'} + \mathrm{R-O-R'}
$$

***

### 1.3 Preparation Method II: Williamson Ether Synthesis
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (Highest Board Frequency)

Williamson synthesis is an $\mathrm{S_N2}$ displacement of a halide ion from an alkyl halide by an alkoxide/phenoxide ion.

$$
\mathrm{R-X} + \mathrm{R'-\bar{O}Na^+} \xrightarrow{} \mathrm{R'-O-R} + \mathrm{NaX}
$$

#### Mechanistic Principles & Substrate Requirements
* **Kinetic Mechanism:** Bimolecular Nucleophilic Substitution ($\mathrm{S_N2}$). Attack of the alkoxide ion on the primary alkyl halide occurs from the backside in a concerted single step.
* **Alkyl Halide Requirement:** The alkyl halide ($\mathrm{R-X}$) **must be primary ($1^\circ$)** or methyl ($\mathrm{CH_3-X}$).
* **Behavior with $2^\circ$ and $3^\circ$ Halides:**
  * Alkoxide ions are not only potent nucleophiles but also strong **Brønsted bases**.
  * If a tertiary ($3^\circ$) alkyl halide is used, steric hindrance prevents $\mathrm{S_N2}$ attack; **$\mathrm{E2}$ elimination dominates exclusively**, producing an alkene.

$$
\mathrm{(CH_3)_3C-Br} + \mathrm{CH_3CH_2O^-Na^+} \xrightarrow{} \mathrm{(CH_3)_2C=CH_2} + \mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH} + \mathrm{NaBr}
$$

$$
\text{Product: 2-Methylprop-1-ene (100\% yield; Ether yield = 0\%) }
$$

* **Preparation of Alkyl-Aryl Ethers (e.g., Anisole):** Phenols are converted to sodium phenoxide using aqueous $\mathrm{NaOH}$, followed by nucleophilic attack on an alkyl halide:
  

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5OH} + \mathrm{NaOH} \xrightarrow{} \mathrm{C_6H_5O^-Na^+} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

  

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5O^-Na^+} + \mathrm{CH_3-I} \xrightarrow{} \mathrm{C_6H_5-O-CH_3} + \mathrm{NaI}
$$

* **Aryl Halides / Vinyl Halides Limitation:** Ethers cannot be prepared using haloarenes (e.g., chlorobenzene) and sodium alkoxide under normal conditions because the $\mathrm{C-X}$ bond in haloarenes possesses partial double-bond character due to resonance, making it inert to nucleophilic displacement.

***

### 1.4 Physical Properties of Ethers
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Boiling Points
* Ethers have a polar $\mathrm{C-O-C}$ linkage with a net molecular dipole moment ($\mu \approx 1.18\text{ D}$ for diethyl ether).
* **Intermolecular Forces:** Ethers exhibit weak dipole-dipole interactions. They **cannot form intermolecular hydrogen bonds** with themselves due to the absence of an $-\mathrm{OH}$ moiety.
* **Comparative Trend (for comparable molecular masses):**
  

$$
\text{Alcohols} \gg \text{Ethers} \approx \text{Alkanes}
$$

  
  *Comparative Data Table:*
  

$$
\begin{array}{|l|c|c|l|}
  \hline
  Compound & Formula & Molar Mass\ (\text{g/mol}) & Boiling Point\ (\text{K}) \\
  \hline
  \text{n-Butane} & \mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_3} & 58 & 272.5 \\
  \text{Ethoxyethane} & \mathrm{CH_3CH_2OCH_2CH_3} & 74 & 307.6 \\
  \text{Pentan-1-ol} & \mathrm{CH_3(CH_2)_3CH_2OH} & 74 & 390.0 \\
  \hline
  \end{array}
$$

#### B. Miscibility in Water
* Lower ethers (e.g., methoxymethane, ethoxyethane) are miscible with water in proportions comparable to their isomeric alcohols (diethyl ether: $\approx 7.5\text{ g}/100\text{ g }\mathrm{H_2O}$ at $298\text{ K}$).
* **Origin:** The ethereal oxygen acts as a hydrogen-bond acceptor, forming intermolecular hydrogen bonds with the polar hydrogen atoms of water molecules ($\mathrm{R_2O}\cdots\mathrm{H-O-H}$).
* **Hydrophobic Effect:** Miscibility declines sharply as the hydrocarbon chain length increases due to the predominant hydrophobic non-polar alkyl group interactions.

# SECTION 2: STEP-BY-STEP REACTION MECHANISMS

### 2.1 Mechanism: Acid-Catalyzed Dehydration of Ethanol to Ethoxyethane
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  
*Reaction Condition:* $\mathrm{conc.\ H_2SO_4}$ at $413\text{ K}$.

$$
\mathrm{2\,CH_3CH_2OH} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{H^+,\ 413\ K}} \mathrm{CH_3CH_2-O-CH_2CH_3} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

#### Step 1: Protonation of Alcohol (Formation of Oxonium Ion)
Ethanol acts as a weak Lewis base, accepting a proton from sulfuric acid in a rapid, reversible equilibrium:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CH_2-\ddot{O}-H} + \mathrm{H^+} \xrightleftharpoons{\text{Fast}} \mathrm{CH_3CH_2-\overset{+}{\underset{\mathbf{H}}{\ddot{O}}}-H}
$$

#### Step 2: Nucleophilic Attack by Unprotonated Alcohol ($\mathrm{S_N2}$ Pathway - Rate Determining Step)
An unprotonated ethanol molecule attacks the $\alpha$-carbon of the protonated ethanol species from the rear, with simultaneous departure of a water molecule:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CH_2-\ddot{O}-H} + \mathrm{CH_3CH_2-\overset{+}{O}H_2} \xrightarrow[\text{RDS}]{\text{Slow}} \mathrm{CH_3CH_2-\overset{+}{\underset{\mathbf{H}}{O}}-CH_2CH_3} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

#### Step 3: Deprotonation to Regenerate Acid Catalyst
The protonated ether transfers a proton to a base (water or bisulfate ion) to form the neutral ether:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CH_2-\overset{+}{\underset{\mathbf{H}}{O}}-CH_2CH_3} + \mathrm{H_2O} \xrightleftharpoons{\text{Fast}} \mathrm{CH_3CH_2-O-CH_2CH_3} + \mathrm{H_3O^+}
$$

***

### 2.2 Mechanism: Cleavage of $\mathrm{C-O}$ Bond by Halogen Acids ($\mathrm{HX}$)
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (Crucial Board Concept)

Ethers are cleaved by strong halogen acids ($\mathrm{HI} > \mathrm{HBr} \gg \mathrm{HCl}$). The reaction rate depends on the nucleophilicity of the halide ion and the acid strength:

$$
\text{Reactivity Order: } \mathrm{HI} > \mathrm{HBr} > \mathrm{HCl}
$$

#### Case A: Cleavage of Primary and Secondary Alkyl Ethers ($\mathrm{S_N2}$ Mechanism)
When both alkyl groups are $1^\circ$ or $2^\circ$, cleavage proceeds via an $\mathrm{S_N2}$ pathway. The halide ion preferentially attacks the **less sterically hindered** carbon.

##### Step 1: Protonation of the Ethereal Oxygen

$$
\mathrm{R-O-R'} + \mathrm{HX} \xrightleftharpoons{\text{Fast}} \mathrm{R-\overset{+}{\underset{\mathbf{H}}{O}}-R'} + \mathrm{X^-}
$$

##### Step 2: Nucleophilic Displacement ($\mathrm{S_N2}$)
The halide ion ($\mathrm{X^-}$) attacks the least hindered alkyl group:

$$
\mathrm{X^-} + \mathrm{CH_3-\overset{+}{\underset{\mathbf{H}}{O}}-CH_2CH_3} \xrightarrow[\text{RDS}]{\text{Slow}} \left[\mathrm{X}\cdots\mathrm{CH_3}\cdots\mathrm{O(H)CH_2CH_3}\right]^\ddagger \xrightarrow{} \mathrm{CH_3X} + \mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH}
$$

##### Step 3: Reaction with Excess Acid
If $\mathrm{HX}$ is present in **excess** at elevated temperature, the formed alcohol reacts further to yield a second equivalent of alkyl halide:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH} + \mathrm{HX} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathrm{CH_3CH_2X} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

**Net Equation (Excess $\mathrm{HX}$):**

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{R-O-R'} + 2\,\mathrm{HX} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathrm{R-X} + \mathrm{R'-X} + \mathrm{H_2O}}
$$

***

#### Case B: Cleavage Involving a Tertiary ($3^\circ$) Alkyl Group ($\mathrm{S_N1}$ Mechanism)
When one of the alkyl groups is tertiary, the cleavage proceeds via an **$\mathrm{S_N1}$ mechanism**. The halide ion selectively binds to the **tertiary carbon atom**, irrespective of steric factors, because of the high thermodynamic stability of the intermediate tertiary carbocation.

**Reaction:**

$$
\mathrm{(CH_3)_3C-O-CH_3} + \mathrm{HI} \xrightarrow{373\ K} \mathrm{(CH_3)_3C-I} + \mathrm{CH_3OH}
$$

##### Step 1: Rapid Protonation

$$
\mathrm{(CH_3)_3C-O-CH_3} + \mathrm{H^+} \xrightleftharpoons{\text{Fast}} \mathrm{(CH_3)_3C-\overset{+}{\underset{\mathbf{H}}{O}}-CH_3}
$$

##### Step 2: Heterolytic Cleavage to form Stable Carbocation (Rate-Determining Step)
The $\mathrm{C-O}$ bond attached to the tertiary group cleaves heterolytically:

$$
\mathrm{(CH_3)_3C-\overset{+}{\underset{\mathbf{H}}{O}}-CH_3} \xrightarrow[\text{RDS}]{\text{Slow}} \mathrm{(CH_3)_3C^+} + \mathrm{CH_3OH}
$$

*Note:* The $3^\circ$ carbocation ($\mathrm{(CH_3)_3C^+}$) is stabilized by 9 hyperconjugative $\alpha$-hydrogens and inductive effects.

##### Step 3: Nucleophilic Attack by Halide

$$
\mathrm{(CH_3)_3C^+} + \mathrm{I^-} \xrightarrow{\text{Fast}} \mathrm{(CH_3)_3C-I}
$$

***

#### Case C: Cleavage of Alkyl-Aryl Ethers (e.g., Anisole)
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  
Cleavage of anisole ($\mathrm{C_6H_5-O-CH_3}$) with $\mathrm{HI}$ yields **Phenol and Methyl Iodide**, never Iodobenzene and Methanol.

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5-O-CH_3} + \mathrm{HI} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathrm{C_6H_5OH} + \mathrm{CH_3I}
$$

##### Fundamental Chemical Rationale
1. **Resonance Stabilization / Partial Double Bond Character:** In alkyl aryl ethers, the unshared electron pair on oxygen is delocalized over the aromatic ring:
   

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5-O-CH_3} \longleftrightarrow \text{Resonance structures displaying } \mathrm{C_{aryl}=O^+}
$$

   This imparts partial double bond character to the $\mathrm{sp^2\ C_{aryl}-O}$ bond ($136\text{ pm}$), making it shorter, stronger, and far more resistant to cleavage than the $\mathrm{sp^3\ C_{alkyl}-O}$ bond ($141\text{ pm}$).
2. **Instability of Phenyl Cation:** An $\mathrm{S_N1}$ dissociation to form a phenyl cation ($\mathrm{C_6H_5^+}$) is energetically unfavorable due to high ring strain.
3. **Steric / Electronic Resistance to $\mathrm{S_N2}$:** Backside $\mathrm{S_N2}$ attack on the $\mathrm{sp^2}$ aromatic carbon is stereochemically blocked. Thus, nucleophilic attack by $\mathrm{I^-}$ occurs exclusively on the less hindered $\mathrm{sp^3}$ methyl carbon.

### Problem 2: Synthetic Route Design via Williamson Synthesis
**[CBSE Board 2023, 2 Marks]**

**Detailed Solution:**

* **Analysis:** The molecule is an unsymmetrical ether with one $3^\circ$ alkyl group and one $1^\circ$ alkyl group.
* **Selection of Precursors:** In Williamson synthesis, the alkyl halide must be primary ($1^\circ$) to suppress elimination.

# SECTION 5: SPEED HACKS & GOLDEN MNEMONICS

### 1. The "HI Cleavage Rule Matrix"
When cleaving an unsymmetrical ether $\mathrm{R_1-O-R_2}$ with $1\text{ equivalent of cold }\mathrm{HI}$:
* **Is there a $3^\circ$, Benzylic, or Allylic Carbon?**
  * **YES:** $\longrightarrow \mathbf{S_N1\text{ Pathway}}$.  
    Halide ($\mathrm{I^-}$) attaches to the **MORE STABLE CARBOCATION** ($3^\circ$ / benzyl). The remaining fragment forms an alcohol.
  * **NO (Both groups are $1^\circ$ or $2^\circ$):** $\longrightarrow \mathbf{S_N2\text{ Pathway}}$.  
    Halide ($\mathrm{I^-}$) attacks the **SMALLER, LESS HINDERED ALKYL GROUP**. The bulkier fragment retains the oxygen to form the alcohol.
* **Is an Aromatic Ring directly bound to Oxygen?**
  * The $\mathrm{Ar-O}$ bond **NEVER CLEAVES**. The product is always a **Phenol** ($\mathrm{Ar-OH}$).

### 2. Williamson's "Primary Halide" Directive

$$
\mathbf{L-H-B-A}
$$

$$
L\text{ess Hindered Halide} \quad + \quad B\text{ulky }A\text{lkoxide} \quad = \quad Ether Synthesized!
$$

$$
\text{If Halide is } 3^\circ \implies ALKENE IS THE ONLY PRODUCT.
$$

### 3. Boiling Point vs. Solubility Shorthand
* **Ethers are like Alkanes in Boiling Point:** No self $\mathrm{H}$-bonding $\implies$ Low boiling point ($\text{Ether B.P.} \approx \text{Alkane B.P.}$ of same molar mass).
* **Ethers are like Alcohols in Solubility:** Can accept $\mathrm{H}$-bonds from water $\implies$ Soluble in water (up to 4 carbons).

### Question A1 [CBSE Delhi 2024, 1 Mark] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Question:**
Anisole undergoes cleavage on heating with concentrated $\text{HI}$ to yield:
- (a) Phenol and Methyl iodide
- (b) Iodobenzene and Methanol
- (c) Phenol and Methanol
- (d) Iodobenzene and Methyl iodide

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
* **Correct Option:** **(a) Phenol and Methyl iodide** [$\frac{1}{2}$ Mark]
* **Examiner Rationale/Mechanism:**
  Protonation of anisole gives methylphenyl oxonium ion:
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\overset{+}{\text{O}}(\text{H})-\text{CH}_3
$$

  The $\text{C}_\text{aromatic}-\text{O}$ bond acquires partial double-bond character due to resonance and has a higher bond dissociation enthalpy than the alkyl $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond. Thus, the nucleophile $\text{I}^-$ attacks the less sterically hindered methyl carbon via an $\text{S}_\text{N}2$ mechanism to yield $\text{CH}_3\text{I}$ and $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH}$:
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{O}-\text{CH}_3 + \text{HI} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{CH}_3\text{I}
$$

 [$\frac{1}{2}$ Mark]

### Question A2 [CBSE All India 2023, 1 Mark] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Question:**
Which of the following compounds gives a positive Lucas test (immediate turbidity at room temperature)?
- (a) Butan-1-ol
- (b) Butan-2-ol
- (c) 2-Methylpropan-2-ol
- (d) 2-Methylpropan-1-ol

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
* **Correct Option:** **(c) 2-Methylpropan-2-ol** [$\frac{1}{2}$ Mark]
* **Examiner Rationale:**
  Lucas reagent ($\text{conc. HCl} + \text{anhyd. ZnCl}_2$) reacts via an $\text{S}_\text{N}1$ pathway. 2-Methylpropan-2-ol is a tertiary ($3^\circ$) alcohol that forms a stable tertiary carbocation:
  

$$
(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}^+
$$

  Turbidity appears immediately due to the formation of insoluble $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}-\text{Cl}$. Primary alcohols do not produce turbidity at room temperature. [$\frac{1}{2}$ Mark]

### Question A3 [CBSE 2023 Compartment, 1 Mark] ⭐⭐⭐⭐
**Question:**
The correct decreasing order of boiling points for isomeric alcohols is:
- (a) $1^\circ > 2^\circ > 3^\circ$
- (b) $3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ$
- (c) $2^\circ > 1^\circ > 3^\circ$
- (d) $3^\circ > 1^\circ > 2^\circ$

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
* **Correct Option:** **(a) $1^\circ > 2^\circ > 3^\circ$** [$\frac{1}{2}$ Mark]
* **Examiner Rationale:**
  With increased branching, the surface area of the molecule decreases, leading to a decrease in the magnitude of intermolecular van der Waals forces. Primary alcohols have the highest surface area and form more unhindered intermolecular hydrogen bonding, resulting in the highest boiling point. [$\frac{1}{2}$ Mark]

### Question A5 [CBSE Foreign 2023, 1 Mark] ⭐⭐⭐⭐
**Question:**
Phenol on treatment with excess bromine water ($\text{Br}_2/\text{H}_2\text{O}$) gives a white precipitate of:
- (a) 2-Bromophenol
- (b) 4-Bromophenol
- (c) 2,4,6-Tribromophenol

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
* **Correct Option:** **(c) 2,4,6-Tribromophenol** [$\frac{1}{2}$ Mark]

\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + 3\text{Br}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_2\text{Br}_3\text{OH}\downarrow (\text{white ppt}) + 3\text{HBr}
$$

 [$\frac{1}{2}$ Mark]

### Question A6 [CBSE 2025 Sample Paper / Pattern, 1 Mark] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Question:**
Given below are two statements: an **Assertion (A)** and a **Reason (R)**:
- **Assertion (A):** The $\text{C}-\text{O}-\text{H}$ bond angle in alcohols is slightly less than the tetrahedral angle ($109.5^\circ$).
- **Reason (R):** It is due to the repulsive interaction between the two unshared lone pairs of electrons on the oxygen atom.

Select the correct alternative:
- (a) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
- (b) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).
- (c) (A) is true, but (R) is false.
- (d) (A) is false, but (R) is true.

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
* **Correct Option:** **(a) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).** [1 Mark]
* **Examiner Note:** The bond angle is approx $108.9^\circ$ in methanol. According to VSEPR theory, lone pair-lone pair repulsion is greater than bond pair-bond pair repulsion, compressing the tetrahedral angle.

### Question B1 [CBSE All India 2024, 2 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Question:**
Write the chemical equations involved in the following reactions:
1. Kolbe’s reaction
2. Acid-catalysed hydration of propene

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
1. **Kolbe’s Reaction:**
   Phenol is treated with $\text{NaOH}$ to form sodium phenoxide, followed by reaction with $\text{CO}_2$ at $400\,\text{K}$ and $4-7\,\text{atm}$, then acidification to yield 2-hydroxybenzoic acid (salicylic acid):
   

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} \xrightarrow{\text{NaOH}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{ONa} \xrightarrow[(ii)\,\text{H}^+]{(i)\,\text{CO}_2,\,400\text{K},\,4-7\text{atm}} o\text{-}\text{HO}-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4-\text{COOH}
$$

   *(Salicylic acid / 2-Hydroxybenzoic acid)* [1 Mark]

2. **Acid-catalysed hydration of propene:**
   Reaction follows Markovnikov’s rule:
   

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+} \text{CH}_3-\text{CH}(\text{OH})-\text{CH}_3
$$

   *(Propan-2-ol)* [1 Mark]

### Question B2 [CBSE 2023, 2 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐
**Question:**
Give reasons for the following observations:
1. *o*-Nitrophenol has a lower boiling point and is steam volatile, whereas *p*-nitrophenol has a higher boiling point and is less volatile.
2. The $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond length in phenol is shorter ($136\,\text{pm}$) than that in methanol ($142\,\text{pm}$).

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
1. ***o*-Nitrophenol vs *p*-Nitrophenol:**
   - In *o*-nitrophenol, intramolecular hydrogen bonding exists between the $-\text{OH}$ and $-\text{NO}_2$ groups, forming a closed 6-membered chelate ring. This prevents intermolecular association, making it steam-volatile. [$\frac{1}{2}$ Mark]
   - In *p*-nitrophenol, the groups are far apart, leading to extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding. This causes molecular association, raising its boiling point. [$\frac{1}{2}$ Mark]
2. **$\text{C}-\text{O}$ Bond Length Difference:**
   - In phenol, the unshared electron pair on oxygen is delocalized into the benzene ring through resonance ($+R$ effect), conferring partial double-bond character to the $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond. [$\frac{1}{2}$ Mark]
   - Also, in phenol, oxygen is attached to an $\text{sp}^2$ hybridised aromatic carbon (higher s-character, more electronegative, smaller covalent radius), whereas in methanol, oxygen is attached to an $\text{sp}^3$ hybridised alkyl carbon. [$\frac{1}{2}$ Mark]

### Question B5 [CBSE Delhi 2020, 2 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐
**Question:**
Distinguish chemically between the following pairs of compounds:
1. Ethanol and Dimethyl ether
2. Phenol and Benzyl alcohol

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
1. **Ethanol vs Dimethyl ether:**
   - **Sodium metal test:** Ethanol reacts with $\text{Na}$ metal with effervescence releasing hydrogen gas ($\text{H}_2\uparrow$), whereas dimethyl ether does not react:
     

$$
2\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH} + 2\text{Na} \rightarrow 2\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{ONa} + \text{H}_2\uparrow
$$

 [1 Mark]

2. **Phenol vs Benzyl alcohol:**
   - **Neutral $\text{FeCl}_3$ test:** Phenol produces a characteristic violet coloration due to the formation of an iron-phenoxide coordination complex:
     

$$
6\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{Fe}^{3+} \rightarrow [\text{Fe}(\text{OC}_6\text{H}_5)_6]^{3-} + 6\text{H}^+
$$

     Benzyl alcohol does not produce any violet coloration. [1 Mark]

### Question C1 [CBSE Delhi 2024, 3 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Question:**
Write the mechanism of the acid-catalysed dehydration of ethanol to yield ethene at $443\,\text{K}$.

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
The dehydration of ethanol involves three distinct sequential steps:

* **Step 1: Protonation of alcohol to form ethyl oxonium ion (Fast step):**
  The lone pair on oxygen acts as a Lewis base and accepts a proton from the acid catalyst:
  

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\overset{\cdot\cdot}{\text{O}}\text{H} + \text{H}^+ \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\overset{+}{\underset{\text{H}}{\text{O}}}-\text{H}
$$

 [1 Mark]

* **Step 2: Formation of carbocation (Slow, rate-determining step):**
  Cleavage of the $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond with departure of water:
  

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\overset{+}{\text{O}}\text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{slow}} \text{CH}_3-\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

 [1 Mark]

* **Step 3: Elimination of a proton to form ethene (Fast step):**
  Elimination of a $\beta$-hydrogen restores the acid catalyst ($\text{H}^+$):
  

$$
\text{H}-\text{CH}_2-\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{fast}} \text{CH}_2=\text{CH}_2 + \text{H}^+
$$

 [1 Mark]

### Question C2 [CBSE All India 2023, 3 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Question:**
Explain the mechanism of the reaction of an ether with concentrated $\text{HI}$ when:
1. Methoxyethane is treated with 1 equivalent of $\text{HI}$ at room temperature.
2. *tert*-Butyl methyl ether is treated with 1 equivalent of $\text{HI}$.

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
1. **Methoxyethane $+$ 1 eq. $\text{HI}$:**
   - **Step 1:** Protonation of ether oxygen to form dialkyl oxonium ion:
     

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{O}-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3 + \text{H}^+ \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3-\overset{+}{\text{O}}(\text{H})-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3
$$

 [$\frac{1}{2}$ Mark]
   - **Step 2:** Nucleophilic attack of $\text{I}^-$. Both alkyl groups are primary. The reaction proceeds via an $\text{S}_\text{N}2$ mechanism where the iodide ion attacks the less sterically hindered carbon (methyl group):
     

$$
\text{I}^- + \text{CH}_3-\overset{+}{\text{O}}(\text{H})-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3 \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{I} + \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH}
$$

     Products: **Iodomethane** and **Ethanol**. [1 Mark]

2. ***tert*-Butyl methyl ether $+$ 1 eq. $\text{HI}$:**
   - Protonation forms the oxonium ion:
     

$$
(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}-\overset{+}{\text{O}}(\text{H})-\text{CH}_3
$$

 [$\frac{1}{2}$ Mark]
   - Because one of the alkyl groups is tertiary ($3^\circ$), the reaction proceeds via an $\text{S}_\text{N}1$ pathway. Heterolytic cleavage produces the highly stable $3^\circ$ carbocation:
     

$$
(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}-\overset{+}{\text{O}}(\text{H})-\text{CH}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{slow}} (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}^+ + \text{CH}_3\text{OH}
$$

     Followed by rapid capture by $\text{I}^-$:
     

$$
(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}^+ + \text{I}^- \xrightarrow{\text{fast}} (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}-\text{I}
$$

     Products: ***tert*-Butyl iodide** and **Methanol**. [1 Mark]

### Question D1 [CBSE All India 2024, 4 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Read the passage below and answer the following questions:**

> The acidic character of alcohols and phenols is dictated by the polarity of the $\text{O}-\text{H}$ bond and the stability of the alkoxide or phenoxide ion formed after deprotonation. Alcohols act as Brønsted bases and acids. In aqueous solutions, alcohols are even weaker acids than water due to the electron-donating inductive effect ($+\text{I}$) of alkyl groups, which intensifies the electron density on oxygen and destabilizes the alkoxide anion. Phenols, on the other hand, are significantly more acidic than alcohols ($\text{p}K_a$ values for phenols are typically around 10, whereas alcohols have $\text{p}K_a \approx 16-18$). The phenoxide ion is stabilized by resonance delocalization of the negative charge over the benzene ring. Substituents on the aromatic ring dramatically alter acidity: electron-withdrawing groups ($-\text{NO}_2$, $-\text{CN}$) enhance acidity, particularly at *ortho* and *para* positions, whereas electron-donating groups ($-\text{CH}_3$, $-\text{OCH}_3$) decrease acidity.

1. Arrange the following compounds in increasing order of their $\text{p}K_a$ values:
   Phenol, *p*-Cresol, *p*-Nitrophenol, Ethanol. (1 Mark)
2. Explain why propan-1-ol has a lower boiling point than propanoic acid despite both having comparable molecular masses. (1 Mark)
3. **(Attempt either subquestion A or B):**
   - **(A)** Account for the fact that 2,4,6-trinitrophenol (picric acid) is acidic enough to liberate $\text{CO}_2$ from sodium bicarbonate ($\text{NaHCO}_3$), whereas phenol cannot. (2 Marks)
   *OR*
   - **(B)** Write the structure and IUPAC name of the compound formed when phenol is treated with concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$ in the presence of concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$. Why is the yield of this reaction poor? (2 Marks)

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
1. **Order of $\text{p}K_a$ values:**
   - Note: Higher acidity means lower $\text{p}K_a$.
   - Acidity order: *p*-Nitrophenol > Phenol > *p*-Cresol > Ethanol
   - Therefore, increasing order of $\text{p}K_a$:
     

$$
\text{\textit{p}-Nitrophenol} < \text{Phenol} < \text{\textit{p}-Cresol} < \text{Ethanol}
$$

 [1 Mark]
     *(Full 1 mark if correct order; deduct $\frac{1}{2}$ mark if reverse order is written without clarifying relation to $\text{p}K_a$ vs acidity).*

2. **Propan-1-ol vs Propanoic Acid:**
   - Both undergo intermolecular hydrogen bonding, but propanoic acid forms extensive, stable **cyclic hydrogen-bonded dimers** in which two hydrogen bonds connect a pair of molecules. The hydrogen bonding in carboxylic acids is stronger than in alcohols because the carbonyl oxygen polarizes the $\text{O}-\text{H}$ bond to a greater extent. [1 Mark]

3. **Subquestion (A):**
   - In 2,4,6-trinitrophenol (picric acid), three strong electron-withdrawing nitro groups ($-\text{I}$ and $-\text{M}$ effects) are present at two *ortho* and one *para* positions. [$\frac{1}{2}$ Mark]

   - Consequently, picric acid decomposes $\text{NaHCO}_3$ to release $\text{CO}_2$, whereas unsubstituted phenol ($\text{p}K_a \approx 10$) is a weaker acid than carbonic acid and cannot displace it. [$\frac{1}{2}$ Mark]
   *OR*
   **Subquestion (B):**
   - Structure & IUPAC Name:
     

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_2(\text{NO}_2)_3\text{OH}\quad (\text{2,4,6-Trinitrophenol / Picric acid})
$$

 [1 Mark]
   - Reason for poor yield:
     Concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$ is a strong oxidizing agent; it oxidizes the electron-rich aromatic ring of phenol to tarry quinone oxidation products, resulting in low yields of picric acid. [1 Mark]

## 1. Speed Hacks & Fast Solving Shortcuts

### Organic Reaction Shortcuts & Mechanism Decision Matrix
* **HI Cleavage of Ethers ($R\text{—}O\text{—}R'$): The "3° vs 1°/2°" 2-Second Rule**
  

$$
\text{Is either alkyl group } 3^\circ \text{ (or resonance-stabilised, e.g., benzyl/allyl)?}
$$

  * **YES $\rightarrow \text{S}_\text{N}1$ pathway:** The halide ion ($\text{I}^-$) attacks the **more substituted / stable carbocation**.
    

$$
\text{e.g., }(CH_3)_3C\text{—}O\text{—}CH_3 + HI \xrightarrow{\text{cold}} (CH_3)_3C\text{—}I + CH_3OH
$$

  * **NO (both are $1^\circ$ or $2^\circ$) $\rightarrow \text{S}_\text{N}2$ pathway:** Halide attacks the **less sterically hindered carbon**.
    

$$
\text{e.g., } CH_3CH_2\text{—}O\text{—}CH_3 + HI \xrightarrow{\text{cold}} CH_3I + CH_3CH_2OH
$$

  * **Phenolic Oxygen Rule:** Bond cleavage of $\text{Ar—O—R}$ **never** breaks the $\text{Ar—O}$ bond due to partial double bond character ($sp^2$-hybridised carbon). Product is always **Phenol + Alkyl Halide**.

* **Acid-Catalyzed Dehydration of Alcohols: Temperature Discriminator**
  

$$
\text{Ethanol} + \text{conc. } H_2SO_4: \begin{cases} \mathbf{443\text{ K}} \longrightarrow CH_2=CH_2 \text{ (Elimination - } \text{E}1\text{)} \\ \mathbf{413\text{ K}} \longrightarrow C_2H_5\text{—}O\text{—}C_2H_5 \text{ (Nucleophilic substitution - } \text{S}_\text{N}2\text{)} \end{cases}
$$

  * *Shortcut memory aid:* Higher Temperature ($443\text{ K}$) $\rightarrow$ higher energy barrier $\rightarrow$ alkene (bond breakage/elimination).

* **Reagent Elimination / Conversion Map**
  * **$1^\circ \text{ Alcohol} \rightarrow \text{Aldehyde}$:** Use **PCC** ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_5\text{NH}^+\text{CrO}_3\text{Cl}^-$) or **anhydrous $\text{CrO}_3$**. *Trap:* $\text{KMnO}_4/\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$ over-oxidizes directly to carboxylic acid.
  * **$2^\circ \text{ Alcohol} \rightarrow \text{Ketone}$:** Use $\text{CrO}_3$ or $\text{PCC}$.
  * **$3^\circ \text{ Alcohol} + \text{Cu} / 573\text{ K}$:** Dehydration yields **Alkene** (not oxidation):
    

$$
(CH_3)_3C\text{—}OH \xrightarrow{Cu/573\text{ K}} (CH_3)_2C=CH_2 + H_2O
$$

* **Hydroboration–Oxidation vs. Acid Hydration**
  * $\text{Alkene} \xrightarrow{H_2O/H^+} \text{Markovnikov hydration (carbocation rearrangement possible)}$.
  * $\text{Alkene} \xrightarrow{1.\ B_2H_6,\ 2.\ H_2O_2/\bar{O}H} \mathbf{Anti-Markovnikov hydration} \text{ (overall, no rearrangement)}$.

### Where Students Lose $\frac{1}{2}$ to $1$ Mark in Evaluation

| Evaluator Trap | Common Mistake by Candidate | Correct Board Standard Presentation | Mark Penalty |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Williamson Ether Synthesis** | Attempting: $\text{Ar—X} + R\text{—}O^-Na^+$ or $3^\circ \text{ halide} + R'\text{—}O^-Na^+$ | Must use **$1^\circ$ alkyl halide** and **alkoxide/phenoxide**. If $3^\circ$ halide is used, the predominant product is an **alkene (elimination)**. | $-1$ mark |
| **Reimer-Tiemann Intermediate** | Writing directly to salicylaldehyde without showing the dichlorocarbene intermediate ($\text{:CCl}_2$) or benzylidene intermediate ($-\text{CHCl}_2$ on ring). | Show the intermediate: $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{O}^- \text{Na}^+ \text{ with } \text{—CHCl}_2 \text{ at } ortho\text{-position}$. | $-\frac{1}{2}$ mark |
| **Kolbe's Reaction Specifics** | Forgetting the two distinct steps: (1) $\text{CO}_2, 400\text{ K}, 4\text{--}7\text{ atm}$, (2) $\text{H}^+$. | Always mention: (i) $\text{CO}_2 / 4\text{--}7\text{ atm} / 400\text{ K}$, followed by (ii) $H^+$ acidification to yield salicylic acid. | $-\frac{1}{2}$ mark |
| **Bromine Water vs $\text{CS}_2$ on Phenol** | Writing 2,4,6-tribromophenol when non-polar solvent ($\text{CS}_2$ or $\text{CHCl}_3$ at $273\text{ K}$) is given. | $\text{Br}_2 / \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \mathbf{2,4,6\text{-tribromophenol (white ppt)}}$; $\text{Br}_2 / \text{CS}_2 (273\text{ K}) \rightarrow \mathbf{p\text{-bromophenol (major)}}$. | $-1$ mark |
| **Esterification Reversibility** | Omitting the reversible double arrow $(\rightleftharpoons)$ or omitting conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$. | $R\text{—}OH + R'\text{—}COOH \xrightleftharpoons{\text{conc. } H_2SO_4} R'\text{—}COOR + H_2O$. (Water removed continuously). | $-\frac{1}{2}$ mark |
| **Lucas Test Observations** | Stating "all alcohols react immediately" or failing to specify **turbidity appearance time**. | $3^\circ$: Immediate turbidity; $2^\circ$: Turbidity appears within 5 mins; $1^\circ$: Turbidity only upon heating. | $-\frac{1}{2}$ mark |
| **Carbocation Shift in Acid Dehydration** | Writing $3,3\text{-dimethylbutan-2-ol} \xrightarrow{H^+} 3,3\text{-dimethylbut-1-ene}$. | Rearrangement via 1,2-methyl shift occurs to yield the more stable carbocation $\rightarrow$ **2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene** (Saytzeff product). | $-1$ mark |

### How Examiners Grade Step-Marking
1. **Mechanisms (3-mark standard question):**
   * *Step 1:* Protonation of alcohol / formation of oxonium ion ($\frac{1}{2}$ mark).
   * *Step 2:* Carbocation formation / cleavage of $\text{C—O}$ bond (**Rate-determining step**) ($\mathbf{1}$ mark).
   * *Step 3:* Deprotonation / Nucleophilic attack ($\mathbf{1}$ mark).
   * *Electrons & Formal Charges:* Missing curved arrows representing electron flow or missing $(+)$ charge on oxonium/carbocation leads to deduction of $\frac{1}{2}$ mark per step.
2. **Reasoning Questions:** Examiners look for standard keywords (e.g., *"Intermolecular H-bonding"*, *"Electronegativity"*, *"-I effect vs +R effect"*, *"Resonance stabilization of phenoxide ion"*). Vague conversational answers receive zero.

## 3. Golden Points to Remember (NCERT In-Text Lines & High-Yield Truths)

1. **Acidity Order of Alcohols vs Phenols vs Water:**
   

$$
\text{Phenol} > \text{Water} > 1^\circ \text{ Alcohol} > 2^\circ \text{ Alcohol} > 3^\circ \text{ Alcohol}
$$

   * *NCERT Highlight:* Water is a stronger acid than alcohols, **except methanol** ($CH_3OH > H_2O$).
   * Alkoxide ion ($RO^-$) is a stronger base than hydroxide ion ($OH^-$).

2. **Substituted Phenol Acidity:**
   * Electron-withdrawing groups (EWGs: $-\text{NO}_2, -\text{CN}, -X$) stabilize phenoxide ion $\rightarrow$ **increase acidity** ($o\text{-}, p\text{-} > m\text{-}$).
   * $p\text{-nitrophenol} > o\text{-nitrophenol} > m\text{-nitrophenol} > \text{phenol} > \text{cresols}$.
   * *Intramolecular vs Intermolecular H-bonding:*
     * **$o$-Nitrophenol:** Chelation / Intramolecular H-bonding $\rightarrow$ lower boiling point, **steam-volatile**.
     * **$p$-Nitrophenol:** Intermolecular H-bonding $\rightarrow$ association, higher boiling point, **less volatile**. Separation done via steam distillation.

3. **Azeotropic Composition (Rectified Spirit):**
   * Fractional distillation of ethanol-water mixture gives constant boiling azeotrope containing **$95.6\%$ ethanol by volume**. Absolute ($100\%$) alcohol cannot be prepared by simple fractional distillation.

4. **Preparation of Phenol from Cumene (Industrial Method):**
   

$$
\text{Cumene (Isopropylbenzene)} + O_2 \xrightarrow{\text{air}} \text{Cumene Hydroperoxide} \xrightarrow{H^+ / H_2O} \text{Phenol} + \mathbf{CH_3COCH_3 \text{ (Acetone - valuable byproduct)}}
$$

5. **Aspirin Synthesis (Acetylation of Salicylic Acid):**
   

$$
\text{Salicylic acid} + (CH_3CO)_2O \xrightarrow{H^+} \text{Acetylsalicylic acid (Aspirin)} + CH_3COOH
$$

6. **Ethers Dipole Moment:**
   * Ethers have a bent geometry ($C\text{—}O\text{—}C$ angle $\approx 111.7^\circ$, greater than tetrahedral $109.5^\circ$ due to repulsive interaction between bulky alkyl groups); hence, **net dipole moment $\mu \neq 0$**.

### Must-Know Named Reactions Checklist

* **Kolbe's Reaction:**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{NaOH} \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{O}^-\text{Na}^+ \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) } \text{H}^+]{\text{(i) } \text{CO}_2,\ 400\text{ K},\ 4\text{--}7\text{ atm}} \text{2-Hydroxybenzoic acid (Salicylic acid)}
$$

* **Reimer-Tiemann Reaction:**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{CHCl}_3 + 3\text{NaOH} \xrightarrow{340\text{ K}} \text{Salicylaldehyde (2-Hydroxybenzaldehyde)} + 3\text{NaCl} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

  

$$
\text{Electrophile involved: } \mathbf{\text{:CCl}_2} \text{ (Dichlorocarbene)}
$$

* **Williamson Ether Synthesis:**
  

$$
R\text{—}X + R'\text{—}O^-\text{Na}^+ \longrightarrow R\text{—}O\text{—}R' + \text{Na}X \quad [R = 1^\circ \text{ alkyl group}]
$$

* **Dehydrogenation with Heated Copper:**
  

$$
R\text{—}CH_2OH \xrightarrow{\text{Cu},\ 573\text{ K}} R\text{—}CHO + H_2
$$

  

$$
R\text{—}CH(OH)\text{—}R' \xrightarrow{\text{Cu},\ 573\text{ K}} R\text{—}CO\text{—}R' + H_2
$$

  

$$
(CH_3)_3C\text{—}OH \xrightarrow{\text{Cu},\ 573\text{ K}} (CH_3)_2C=CH_2 + H_2O
$$

* **Oxidation of Phenol with Chromic Acid:**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} \xrightarrow{\text{Na}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7 / \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4} \text{Benzo-1,4-quinone (conjugated diketone)}
$$

* **Reduction with Zinc Dust:**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{Zn} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Benzene} + \text{ZnO}
$$
