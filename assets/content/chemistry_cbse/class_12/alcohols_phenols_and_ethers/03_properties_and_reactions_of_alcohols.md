# 1. Pedagogical Theory & Concepts

## 1.1 Physical Properties of Alcohols

### Boiling Points ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
* **Definition & Trend:** The boiling points of alcohols increase monotonically with increase in molecular mass (due to the progressive increase in the magnitude of Van der Waals forces/London dispersion forces as the molecular surface area increases).
* **Branching Effect:** Among isomeric alcohols, the boiling point decreases with an increase in branching of the alkyl chain.  
  

$$
\text{Boiling Point Order: } 1^\circ \text{ alcohol} > 2^\circ \text{ alcohol} > 3^\circ \text{ alcohol}
$$

  
  *Molecular Reason:* Increased branching causes the molecule to acquire an increasingly spherical geometry. A sphere possesses minimum surface area for a given volume, leading to decreased area of contact between adjacent molecules and consequently attenuated intermolecular Van der Waals forces.
* **Intermolecular Hydrogen Bonding:** Alcohols exhibit anomalously high boiling points compared to hydrocarbons, haloalkanes, and ethers of comparable molecular masses.  
  *Molecular Interpretation:* The highly electronegative oxygen atom ($\chi_{\text{O}} \approx 3.44$) polarizes the $\mathrm{O-H}$ bond, creating a substantial permanent dipole ($\mathrm{R-O^{\delta-}-H^{\delta+}}$). Strong intermolecular hydrogen bonds form extensive associative networks in the liquid state:
  

$$
\cdots \mathrm{O(R)-H} \cdots \mathrm{O(R)-H} \cdots \mathrm{O(R)-H} \cdots
$$

  Cleavage of these cohesive associative interactions requires significant thermal energy input ($10\text{--}40\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ per H-bond).

### Solubility in Water ⭐⭐⭐⭐
* **Mechanism:** The lower molecular mass alcohols ($\mathrm{CH_3OH}$, $\mathrm{C_2H_5OH}$, $\mathrm{C_3H_7OH}$) are miscible with water in all proportions. This miscibility arises directly from the thermodynamic favorability of forming solute-solvent intermolecular hydrogen bonds between the hydroxyl ($-\mathrm{OH}$) group of the alcohol and polar $\mathrm{H_2O}$ molecules.
* **Hydrophobic Cutoff:** As the length of the hydrocarbon radical ($\mathrm{R}-$) increases, solubility in water drops precipitously. The alkyl group acts as a bulky, non-polar, hydrophobic moiety that disrupts the tetrahedral hydrogen-bonded structure of water without forming compensating electrostatic interactions.

## 1.2 Structure & Reactivity of the Functional Group

Alcohols display amphoteric chemical reactivity arising from dual reaction sites:

```
          Cleavage of O-H bond (Alcohol as Nucleophile / Weak Acid)
                     ↓
             R — O — H
                 ↑
          Cleavage of C-O bond (Protonated Alcohol as Electrophile)
```

1. **Reactions Involving Cleavage of $\mathrm{O-H}$ Bond:** The alcohol acts as a **nucleophile** or a Bronsted acid.
2. **Reactions Involving Cleavage of $\mathrm{C-O}$ Bond:** Protonation of the hydroxyl group transforms the poor leaving group ($\mathrm{OH^-}$) into an excellent leaving group ($\mathrm{H_2O}$), allowing the protonated species ($\mathrm{R-OH_2^+}$) to act as an **electrophile**.

## 2.1 Reactions Involving Cleavage of $\mathrm{O-H}$ Bond

### A. Acidity of Alcohols & Reaction with Active Metals ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Alcohols react with electropositive alkali and alkaline earth metals ($\mathrm{Na, K, Mg, Al}$) with the brisk effervescence of hydrogen gas:

$$
2\mathrm{R-O-H} + 2\mathrm{Na} \longrightarrow 2\mathrm{R-O^-Na^+} + \mathrm{H_2}\uparrow
$$

$$
6\left(\mathrm{CH_3}\right)_3\mathrm{C-OH} + 2\mathrm{Al} \longrightarrow 2\left[\left(\mathrm{CH_3}\right)_3\mathrm{C-O}\right]_3\mathrm{Al} + 3\mathrm{H_2}\uparrow
$$

*(Aluminium tert-butoxide)*

#### Acidic Character Comparison:

$$
\text{Acidic Strength: } \mathrm{H_2O} > 1^\circ \text{ alcohol} > 2^\circ \text{ alcohol} > 3^\circ \text{ alcohol}
$$

*Thermodynamic & Electronic Origin:*  
The acidic dissociation in aqueous media corresponds to:

$$
\mathrm{R-OH} + \mathrm{H_2O} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{R-O^-} + \mathrm{H_3O^+}
$$

Alkyl groups ($\mathrm{R}$) are electron-releasing via inductive ($+I$) and hyperconjugative effects. An increasing number of alkyl groups attached to the carbinol carbon intensifies the negative charge density on the conjugate base (alkoxide ion, $\mathrm{R-O^-}$), destabilizing it:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-O^-} \quad \text{vs} \quad (\mathrm{CH_3})_2\mathrm{CH-O^-} \quad \text{vs} \quad (\mathrm{CH_3})_3\mathrm{C-O^-}
$$

Consequently, $3^\circ$ alkoxide is the most basic and least stable conjugate base, rendering $3^\circ$ alcohols the least acidic.  
*Key Exception:* **Methanol ($\mathrm{CH_3OH}$)** is a marginally stronger acid than water ($K_a = 2.9 \times 10^{-16}$ for water vs $K_a = 3.2 \times 10^{-16}$ for methanol at $298\text{ K}$).

***

### B. Esterification Reaction ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Reversible condensation between an alcohol and a carboxylic acid, acid anhydride, or acid chloride in the presence of an acid/base catalyst.

#### 1. With Carboxylic Acid (Fischer-Speier Esterification):

$$
\mathrm{R-COOH} + \mathrm{R'-OH} \overset{\mathrm{H_2SO_4 \text{ (conc.)}}}{\rightleftharpoons} \mathrm{R-COOR'} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

#### 2. With Acid Anhydride:

$$
(\mathrm{RCO})_2\mathrm{O} + \mathrm{R'-OH} \overset{\mathrm{H^+}}{\rightleftharpoons} \mathrm{R-COOR'} + \mathrm{R-COOH}
$$

#### 3. With Acyl Chloride:

$$
\mathrm{R-COCl} + \mathrm{R'-OH} \overset{\text{Pyridine}}{\longrightarrow} \mathrm{R-COOR'} + \mathrm{HCl}
$$

*Function of Pyridine:* Pyridine is a tertiary organic amine base. It neutralizes the $\mathrm{HCl}$ byproduct forming pyridinium chloride ($\mathrm{C_5H_5NH^+Cl^-}$), which shifts the dynamic equilibrium toward the product side according to Le Chatelier's Principle.

#### Complete Step-by-Step Mechanism for Fischer Esterification:

**Step 1: Protonation of the Carbonyl Oxygen**  
The mineral acid ($\mathrm{H^+}$) protonates the carbonyl oxygen of the carboxylic acid, generating a resonance-stabilized electrophilic carbocation center:

$$
\mathrm{R-C(=O)-OH} + \mathrm{H^+} \rightleftharpoons \left[ \mathrm{R-C(=O^+H)-OH} \longleftrightarrow \mathrm{R-C^+(OH)_2} \right]
$$

**Step 2: Nucleophilic Attack by the Alcohol Molecule**  
The unshared electron pair on the oxygen atom of the alcohol attacks the electrophilic carbonyl carbon to form a tetrahedral intermediate:

$$
\mathrm{R-C^+(OH)_2} + \mathrm{R'-OH} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{R-C(OH)_2-(O^+H-R')}
$$

**Step 3: Proton Transfer (Tautomerization of the Intermediate)**  
Proton transfer occurs intramolecularly or via solvent mediation from the alkonium oxygen to one of the hydroxyl oxygens, turning it into a good leaving group ($\mathrm{-O^+H_2}$):

$$
\mathrm{R-C(OH)_2-(O^+H-R')} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{R-C(OH)(O^+H_2)(OR')}
$$

**Step 4: Elimination of Water Molecule ($\mathrm{-H_2O}$)**  
The lone pair on the remaining $-\mathrm{OH}$ group assists in the expulsion of neutral $\mathrm{H_2O}$, generating a protonated ester:

$$
\mathrm{R-C(OH)(O^+H_2)(OR')} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{R-C(=O^+H)(OR')} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

**Step 5: Deprotonation (Regeneration of the Acid Catalyst)**  
Loss of a proton yields the neutral ester product:

$$
\mathrm{R-C(=O^+H)(OR')} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{R-COOR'} + \mathrm{H^+}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Steric Reactivity Order: } \mathrm{CH_3OH} > 1^\circ > 2^\circ > 3^\circ \text{ (Alcohols)}; \quad \mathrm{HCOOH} > \mathrm{CH_3COOH} > \mathrm{R_2CHCOOH} > \mathrm{R_3CCOOH}}
$$

## 2.2 Reactions Involving Cleavage of $\mathrm{C-O}$ Bond

***

### A. Reaction with Hydrogen Halides ($\mathrm{HX}$) & Lucas Test ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

$$
\mathrm{R-OH} + \mathrm{HX} \overset{\mathrm{ZnCl_2}}{\longrightarrow} \mathrm{R-X} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

#### Lucas Reagent:
An equimolar mixture of **concentrated $\mathrm{HCl}$ and anhydrous $\mathrm{ZnCl_2}$**.

#### Mechanism & Principles:
Anhydrous $\mathrm{ZnCl_2}$ acts as a Lewis acid and coordinates with the oxygen of the alcohol:

$$
\mathrm{R-\ddot{O}-H} + \mathrm{ZnCl_2} \longrightarrow \mathrm{R-\overset{+}{O}(H)-[ZnCl_2]^-}
$$

This weakens the $\mathrm{C-O}$ bond, facilitating heterolytic cleavage to produce a carbocation intermediate ($\mathrm{R^+}$), followed by fast nucleophilic attack by chloride ion ($\mathrm{Cl^-}$):

$$
\mathrm{R-\overset{+}{O}(H)-[ZnCl_2]^-} \longrightarrow \mathrm{R^+} + [\mathrm{ZnCl_2(OH)}]^- \overset{\mathrm{Cl^-}}{\longrightarrow} \mathrm{R-Cl} + \mathrm{ZnCl_2} + \mathrm{OH^-}
$$

Since alkyl chlorides are insoluble in water, their formation causes **turbidity / cloudiness**.

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
3^\circ \text{ Alcohols} &\longrightarrow \text{Instant turbidity at room temperature via stable } 3^\circ \text{ carbocation } (\mathrm{S_N1})\\
2^\circ \text{ Alcohols} &\longrightarrow \text{Turbidity appears within } 5 \text{ minutes}\\
1^\circ \text{ Alcohols} &\longrightarrow \text{No turbidity at room temperature; appears only upon sustained heating}
\end{aligned}
}
$$

***

### B. Reaction with Phosphorus Halides and Thionyl Chloride ⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### 1. With Phosphorus Trichloride ($\mathrm{PCl_3}$):

$$
3\mathrm{R-OH} + \mathrm{PCl_3} \longrightarrow 3\mathrm{R-Cl} + \mathrm{H_3PO_3} \quad \text{(Phosphorous acid)}
$$

#### 2. With Phosphorus Pentachloride ($\mathrm{PCl_5}$):

$$
\mathrm{R-OH} + \mathrm{PCl_5} \longrightarrow \mathrm{R-Cl} + \mathrm{POCl_3} + \mathrm{HCl}\uparrow \quad \text{(Phosphoryl chloride)}
$$

#### 3. With Thionyl Chloride ($\mathrm{SOCl_2}$ - Darzens Process):

$$
\mathrm{R-OH} + \mathrm{SOCl_2} \overset{\text{Pyridine or Reflux}}{\longrightarrow} \mathrm{R-Cl} + \mathrm{SO_2}\uparrow + \mathrm{HCl}\uparrow
$$

$$
\boxed{Industrial & Preparative Preference:  \mathrm{SOCl_2} \text{ is the superior reagent because both byproducts } (\mathrm{SO_2} \text{ and } \mathrm{HCl}) \text{ are gaseous and escape, leaving pure alkyl halide without tedious purification.}}
$$

### C. Acid-Catalyzed Dehydration of Alcohols to Alkenes ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

When heated with protic acids ($\mathrm{H_2SO_4, H_3PO_4}$), alcohols undergo unimolecular/bimolecular elimination ($\beta$-elimination) to yield alkenes with the loss of a water molecule.

#### Relative Ease of Dehydration:

$$
3^\circ \text{ Alcohol } (20\%\ \mathrm{H_3PO_4}, 358\text{ K}) > 2^\circ \text{ Alcohol } (85\%\ \mathrm{H_3PO_4}, 440\text{ K}) > 1^\circ \text{ Alcohol } (\text{conc. }\mathrm{H_2SO_4}, 443\text{ K})
$$

#### Complete 3-Step Reaction Mechanism for Ethanol Dehydration ($1^\circ$ Alcohol):

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH} \xrightarrow[\mathrm{443\ K}]{\text{conc. }\mathrm{H_2SO_4}} \mathrm{CH_2=CH_2} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

**Step 1: Formation of Protonated Alcohol (Ethyl Oxonium Ion) — Fast Equilibrium**  
The lone pair on the oxygen atom of ethanol accepts a proton from sulfuric acid:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-\ddot{O}-H} + \mathrm{H^+} \xrightleftharpoons[\text{fast}]{\quad} \mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-\overset{+}{O}H_2}
$$

**Step 2: Formation of Carbocation Intermediate — Slow, Rate-Determining Step (RDS)**  
The $\mathrm{C-O}$ bond cleaves heterolytically with the departure of the neutral, stable water leaving group:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-\overset{+}{O}H_2} \xrightarrow[\text{slow (RDS)}]{\quad} \mathrm{CH_3-\overset{+}{C}H_2} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

**Step 3: Elimination of a Proton ($\beta$-Elimination) — Fast Step**  
A conjugate base ($\mathrm{H_2O}$ or $\mathrm{HSO_4^-}$) abstracts a proton from the $\beta$-carbon atom, generating the $\mathrm{C=C}$ double bond:

$$
\mathrm{H-CH_2-\overset{+}{C}H_2} + \mathrm{H_2O} \xrightarrow[\text{fast}]{\quad} \mathrm{CH_2=CH_2} + \mathrm{H_3O^+}
$$

*Regiochemical Note (Saytzeff/Zaitsev Rule):* For unsymmetrical secondary and tertiary alcohols, dehydration follows Saytzeff's rule: the predominant alkene is the more highly substituted, thermodynamically stable alkene containing the greater number of hyperconjugative $\alpha$-hydrogens.

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CH(OH)-CH_3} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\mathrm{H^+}} \underset{\text{But-2-ene (Major, 80\%)}}{\mathrm{CH_3-CH=CH-CH_3}} + \underset{\text{But-1-ene (Minor, 20\%)}}{\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CH=CH_2}}
$$

## 2.3 Oxidation Reactions of Alcohols ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Oxidation of alcohols involves the cleavage of both an $\mathrm{O-H}$ bond and a $\mathrm{C-H}$ bond, with the formal generation of a carbonyl ($\mathrm{C=O}$) bond. Hence, these are also referred to as **dehydrogenation reactions**.

$$
\begin{array}{rcccl}
1^\circ \text{ Alcohol} & \xrightarrow{[\mathrm{O}]} & \text{Aldehyde} & \xrightarrow{[\mathrm{O}]} & \text{Carboxylic Acid (Same carbon count)} \\
2^\circ \text{ Alcohol} & \xrightarrow{[\mathrm{O}]} & \text{Ketone} & \xrightarrow[\text{Vigorous conditions}]{[\mathrm{O}]} & \text{Carboxylic Acids (Fewer carbons - Popoff's Rule)} \\
3^\circ \text{ Alcohol} & \xrightarrow[\text{Neutral/Alkaline}]{[\mathrm{O}]} & \text{No Reaction} & \xrightarrow[\text{Acidic } \mathrm{KMnO_4}/\Delta]{[\mathrm{O}]} & \text{Alkene intermediate } \to \text{ Degraded acids}
\end{array}
$$

### Selective Reagents:

#### 1. Pyridinium Chlorochromate ($\mathrm{PCC}$):
A complex of chromium trioxide with pyridine and $\mathrm{HCl}$ ($\mathrm{C_5H_5NH^+CrO_3Cl^-}$) in anhydrous dichloromethane ($\mathrm{CH_2Cl_2}$).
* Stoppage at aldehyde without over-oxidation to carboxylic acid:

$$
\mathrm{R-CH_2OH} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{PCC,\ CH_2Cl_2}} \mathrm{R-CHO}
$$

* Does not affect isolated aliphatic non-conjugated carbon-carbon double bonds:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH=CH-CH_2OH} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{PCC}} \mathrm{CH_3-CH=CH-CHO} \quad \text{(Crotonaldehyde)}
$$

#### 2. Collins Reagent ($\mathrm{CrO_3 \cdot 2C_5H_5N}$) / Jones Reagent ($\mathrm{CrO_3 / \text{aq. } H_2SO_4 \text{ in acetone}}$):
Jones reagent quantitatively oxidizes $1^\circ$ alcohols to carboxylic acids, and $2^\circ$ alcohols to ketones.

#### 3. Acidified Potassium Permanganate ($\mathrm{KMnO_4 / H^+}$) or Potassium Dichromate ($\mathrm{K_2Cr_2O_7 / H_2SO_4}$):
Powerful strong oxidants converting primary alcohols directly to carboxylic acids:

$$
\mathrm{R-CH_2OH} \xrightarrow{\text{alk. }\mathrm{KMnO_4} \text{ or } \mathrm{H^+/K_2Cr_2O_7}} \mathrm{R-COOH}
$$

***

## 2.4 Catalytic Dehydrogenation over Heated Copper at $573\text{ K}$ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

When vapors of alcohols are passed over finely divided metallic copper catalyst maintained at $573\text{ K}$ ($300^\circ\text{C}$):

1. **Primary Alcohol ($1^\circ$): Dehydrogenation to Aldehyde**
   

$$
\mathrm{R-CH_2OH} \xrightarrow[\mathrm{573\ K}]{\mathrm{Cu}} \mathrm{R-CHO} + \mathrm{H_2}\uparrow
$$

   

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH} \xrightarrow[\mathrm{573\ K}]{\mathrm{Cu}} \mathrm{CH_3CHO} + \mathrm{H_2}\uparrow
$$

2. **Secondary Alcohol ($2^\circ$): Dehydrogenation to Ketone**
   

$$
\mathrm{R-CH(OH)-R'} \xrightarrow[\mathrm{573\ K}]{\mathrm{Cu}} \mathrm{R-CO-R'} + \mathrm{H_2}\uparrow
$$

   

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH(OH)-CH_3} \xrightarrow[\mathrm{573\ K}]{\mathrm{Cu}} \mathrm{CH_3-CO-CH_3} + \mathrm{H_2}\uparrow
$$

3. **Tertiary Alcohol ($3^\circ$): Dehydration (Elimination) to Alkene**  
   *Because tertiary alcohols lack an $\alpha$-hydrogen atom*, they cannot undergo dehydrogenation. Instead, under the influence of hot $\mathrm{Cu}$ at $573\text{ K}$, intramolecular $\beta$-elimination occurs:
   

$$
\left(\mathrm{CH_3}\right)_3\mathrm{C-OH} \xrightarrow[\mathrm{573\ K}]{\mathrm{Cu}} \left(\mathrm{CH_3}\right)_2\mathrm{C=CH_2} + \mathrm{H_2O} \quad \text{(2-Methylpropene / Isobutylene)}
$$

## 3.1 Methanol ($\mathrm{CH_3OH}$, "Wood Spirit")

### Industrial Synthesis:
Catalytic hydrogenation of carbon monoxide (synthesis gas/water gas) at elevated pressure and temperature over a mixed metal-oxide catalyst:

$$
\mathrm{CO(g)} + 2\mathrm{H_2(g)} \xrightarrow[\substack{200\text{--}300\text{ atm} \\ 573\text{--}673\text{ K}}]{\mathrm{ZnO - Cr_2O_3}} \mathrm{CH_3OH(l)}
$$

*Operating Parameters:*
* **Catalyst:** Zinc oxide - Chromium trioxide mixture ($\mathrm{ZnO - Cr_2O_3}$)
* **Pressure:** $200\text{ to }300\text{ atm}$ ($20\text{ to }30\text{ MPa}$)
* **Temperature:** $573\text{ to }673\text{ K}$ ($300\text{ to }400^\circ\text{C}$)

### Physiological Toxicity:
* Ingestion of even minute quantities of methanol ($10\text{--}30\text{ mL}$) causes acute poisoning leading to irreversible blindness, and $\ge 30\text{ mL}$ is fatal.
* **Biochemical Mechanism of Action:** In the human liver, methanol is enzymatically oxidized by alcohol dehydrogenase into **methanal (formaldehyde, $\mathrm{HCHO}$)**, which is rapidly transformed into **methanoic acid (formic acid, $\mathrm{HCOOH}$)**:
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3OH} \xrightarrow{\text{Alcohol dehydrogenase}} \mathrm{HCHO} \longrightarrow \mathrm{HCOOH}
$$

  Methanal acts as a powerful electrophilic cross-linking agent that coagulates cellular protoplasm (similar to the hard-boiling of an egg). Methanoic acid causes systemic metabolic acidosis and selectively induces optic nerve demyelination, causing irreversible retinal cell necrosis and blindness.

***

## 3.2 Ethanol ($\mathrm{C_2H_5OH}$)

### Large-Scale Production by Fermentation:
Ethanol is produced industrially by the enzymatic anaerobic fermentation of carbohydrates derived from molasses, sugarcane juice, or starchy grains.

#### Biochemical Transformations:
1. **Hydrolysis of Sucrose (Inversion):**  
   The enzyme **invertase** hydrolyzes sucrose into an equimolar mixture of D-glucose and D-fructose:
   

$$
\mathrm{C_{12}H_{22}O_{11}} + \mathrm{H_2O} \xrightarrow{\text{Invertase}} \underset{\text{D-Glucose}}{\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}} + \underset{\text{D-Fructose}}{\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}}
$$

2. **Zymase-Mediated Fermentation:**  
   The intracellular multi-enzyme complex **zymase** (secreted by active yeast cells, *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*) ferments glucose/fructose into ethanol and carbon dioxide:
   

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6} \xrightarrow{\text{Zymase}} 2\mathrm{C_2H_5OH} + 2\mathrm{CO_2}\uparrow
$$

*Physicochemical Reaction Limits:*  
* Fermentation must proceed under strict **anaerobic conditions**. In the presence of atmospheric oxygen, aerobic *Acetobacter aceti* bacteria oxidize the ethanol into ethanoic acid ($\mathrm{CH_3COOH}$), souring the mixture.
* When the ethanol concentration reaches approximately $14\%$, the physiological toxicity of the alcohol terminates yeast enzymatic action. Higher concentrations cannot be obtained by direct fermentation.
* **Rectified Spirit:** Fractional distillation of the fermented broth yields an azeotropic mixture containing $95.57\%$ ethanol and $4.43\%$ water (Boiling Point: $351.15\text{ K}$). It cannot be separated further by ordinary fractional distillation.
* **Absolute Alcohol:** $100\%$ anhydrous ethanol obtained via azeotropic distillation with benzene or chemical drying with quicklime ($\mathrm{CaO}$).

### Denaturation of Industrial Alcohol:
To prevent the diversion and misuse of duty-free or subsidized industrial ethanol for intoxicating beverage purposes, it is rendered unfit for human consumption by adding:
* **Methanol** ($~5\%$, toxic denaturant)
* **Pyridine** (foul-smelling organic base)
* **Copper sulfate ($\mathrm{CuSO_4}$)** (imparts a characteristic warning blue color)

### Question 1 [CBSE 2024, 3 Marks]
**(a)** Write the mechanism of acid-catalyzed dehydration of ethanol to yield ethene at $443\text{ K}$.  
**(b)** What major organic product is formed when ethanol is heated with concentrated sulfuric acid at $413\text{ K}$?

**Solution:**  
**(a)** Refer directly to Section 2.2(C) for the detailed 3-step mechanism:  
* *Step 1:* Protonation of ethanol by $\mathrm{H^+}$ to yield the ethyl oxonium ion ($\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH_2^+}$) [Fast].  
* *Step 2:* Heterolytic cleavage of the $\mathrm{C-O}$ bond to form the carbocation intermediate ($\mathrm{CH_3CH_2^+}$) with loss of $\mathrm{H_2O}$ [Slow, Rate-Determining Step].  
* *Step 3:* Loss of a proton ($\beta$-elimination) to water yielding ethene ($\mathrm{CH_2=CH_2}$) and regenerating $\mathrm{H_3O^+}$ [Fast].

**(b)** At the lower temperature of $413\text{ K}$ ($140^\circ\text{C}$), bimolecular nucleophilic substitution ($\mathrm{S_N2}$) predominates over elimination, yielding **ethoxyethane (diethyl ether)** as the major product:

$$
2\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH} \xrightarrow[\mathrm{413\ K}]{\text{conc. }\mathrm{H_2SO_4}} \mathrm{CH_3CH_2-O-CH_2CH_3} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

### Question 2 [CBSE 2023, 2 Marks]
Give simple chemical tests to distinguish between the following pairs of compounds:  
**(i)** Butan-1-ol and 2-Methylpropan-2-ol  
**(ii)** Ethanol and Propan-1-ol

**Solution:**  
**(i) Distinction between Butan-1-ol ($1^\circ$) and 2-Methylpropan-2-ol ($3^\circ$):**  
*Test:* **Lucas Test** (Conc. $\mathrm{HCl} + \text{anhydrous } \mathrm{ZnCl_2}$).  
*Observation:* 2-Methylpropan-2-ol ($3^\circ$ alcohol) reacts immediately at room temperature producing distinct cloudiness/turbidity ($\mathrm{R-Cl}$ formation). Butan-1-ol ($1^\circ$ alcohol) shows no turbidity at room temperature; turbidity appears only upon sustained heating.

**(ii) Distinction between Ethanol and Propan-1-ol:**  
*Test:* **Iodoform Test** ($\mathrm{I_2 / NaOH}$ or $\mathrm{NaOI}$).  
*Observation:* Ethanol ($\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH}$) possesses the mandatory terminal $\mathrm{CH_3-CH(OH)-}$ structural unit. Upon warming with iodine and sodium hydroxide, it yields a yellow crystalline precipitate of iodoform ($\mathrm{CHI_3}$) characterized by an antiseptic odor:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH} + 4\mathrm{I_2} + 6\mathrm{NaOH} \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Iodoform (Yellow ppt)}}{\mathrm{CHI_3}\downarrow} + \mathrm{HCOONa} + 5\mathrm{NaI} + 5\mathrm{H_2O}
$$

Propan-1-ol ($\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2OH}$) lacks the $\mathrm{CH_3-CH(OH)-}$ group and does not give the iodoform reaction.

### Question 3 [CBSE 2022 Term-2, 3 Marks]
An organic compound $(A)$ with molecular formula $\mathrm{C_4H_{10}O}$ does not react with Lucas reagent at room temperature. When vapors of compound $(A)$ are passed over heated copper at $573\text{ K}$, it forms compound $(B)$ ($\mathrm{C_4H_8O}$). Compound $(B)$ reduces Tollens' reagent. Identify $(A)$ and $(B)$, and write the chemical reactions involved.

**Solution:**  
1. *Deduction of Functional Group of $(A)$:* Molecular formula $\mathrm{C_4H_{10}O}$ conforms to $\mathrm{C_nH_{2n+2}O}$, indicating an alcohol or ether. Since it reacts over hot $\mathrm{Cu}$ to form a carbonyl compound, $(A)$ is an alcohol.  
2. *Lucas Reactivity:* Compound $(A)$ does not react with Lucas reagent at room temperature, which identifies $(A)$ as a **primary ($1^\circ$) alcohol**.  
3. *Identification of $(B)$:* $(A)$ is oxidized over $\mathrm{Cu}/573\text{ K}$ via catalytic dehydrogenation to form $(B)$ ($\mathrm{C_4H_8O}$). Since $(B)$ reduces Tollens' reagent, $(B)$ must be an **aldehyde**.  
4. There are two isomeric $1^\circ$ alcohols with formula $\mathrm{C_4H_{10}O}$: Butan-1-ol and 2-Methylpropan-1-ol.  
   * Using Butan-1-ol:
     

$$
\underset{(A)\ \text{Butan-1-ol}}{\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2OH}} \xrightarrow[\mathrm{573\ K}]{\mathrm{Cu}} \underset{(B)\ \text{Butanal}}{\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2CHO}} + \mathrm{H_2}\uparrow
$$

   * Tollens' Reaction for $(B)$:
     

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2CHO} + 2[\mathrm{Ag(NH_3)_2}]^+ + 3\mathrm{OH^-} \longrightarrow \mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2COO^-} + \underset{\text{Silver Mirror}}{2\mathrm{Ag}\downarrow} + 4\mathrm{NH_3} + 2\mathrm{H_2O}
$$

*Conclusion:*  
Compound $(A) = \text{Butan-1-ol (or 2-Methylpropan-1-ol)}$  
Compound $(B) = \text{Butanal (or 2-Methylpropanal)}$

### Question 4 [CBSE 2021, 2 Marks]
How do you account for the following?  
**(a)** Propan-1-ol has a significantly higher boiling point than Butane, despite their comparable molecular weights.  
**(b)** The dipole moment of methanol is $1.71\text{ D}$, whereas the $\mathrm{C-O-H}$ bond angle is $108.9^\circ$, slightly less than the tetrahedral angle.

**Solution:**  
**(a)** Propan-1-ol ($\mathrm{M_r} \approx 60\text{ g mol}^{-1}$) possesses a polar hydroxyl group that forms extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonds, requiring considerable thermal energy to break. Butane ($\mathrm{M_r} \approx 58\text{ g mol}^{-1}$) is entirely non-polar, possessing only weak Van der Waals dispersion forces.  
**(b)** The central oxygen atom in methanol is $\mathrm{sp^3}$-hybridized. The expected ideal tetrahedral angle is $109.5^\circ$. However, the presence of **two unshared electron lone pairs** on the oxygen atom exerts strong lone pair-lone pair (lp-lp) and lone pair-bond pair (lp-bp) repulsions (VSEPR theory), which compresses the $\mathrm{C-O-H}$ bond angle down to $108.9^\circ$.

### Question 5 [CBSE 2020, 3 Marks]
Complete and write the major organic products in each of the following transformations:  
**(i)** $\mathrm{CH_3-CH(OH)-CH_3} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\mathrm{K_2Cr_2O_7 / H_2SO_4}}$  
**(ii)** $\left(\mathrm{CH_3}\right)_3\mathrm{C-OH} \xrightarrow[\mathrm{573\ K}]{\mathrm{Cu}}$  
**(iii)** $\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CH_2-OH} + \mathrm{SOCl_2} \xrightarrow{\text{Pyridine}}$

**Solution:**  
**(i)** Secondary alcohol undergoes oxidation to a ketone:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH(OH)-CH_3} \xrightarrow{[\mathrm{O}]} \underset{\text{Propan-2-one (Acetone)}}{\mathrm{CH_3-CO-CH_3}}
$$

**(ii)** Tertiary alcohol undergoes catalytic dehydration (elimination) rather than dehydrogenation:

$$
\left(\mathrm{CH_3}\right)_3\mathrm{C-OH} \xrightarrow[\mathrm{573\ K}]{\mathrm{Cu}} \underset{\text{2-Methylpropene}}{\left(\mathrm{CH_3}\right)_2\mathrm{C=CH_2}} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

**(iii)** Thionyl chloride substitution (Darzens reaction):

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2OH} + \mathrm{SOCl_2} \xrightarrow{\text{Pyridine}} \underset{\text{1-Chloropropane}}{\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2Cl}} + \mathrm{SO_2}\uparrow + \mathrm{HCl}\uparrow
$$

# 5. Examiner Traps & Common Mistakes

| Concept / Reaction | The Fatal Student Trap | The Chemically Rigorous NCERT Reality |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ethanol Dehydration Temperature** | Conflating $443\text{ K}$ and $413\text{ K}$ with $\mathrm{conc.\ H_2SO_4}$. | At $\mathbf{443\text{ K}}$ ($170^\circ\text{C}$): Intramolecular dehydration gives **alkene** ($\mathrm{CH_2=CH_2}$).
At $\mathbf{413\text{ K}}$ ($140^\circ\text{C}$): Intermolecular dehydration gives **ether** ($\mathrm{C_2H_5OC_2H_5}$). |
| **Copper at $573\text{ K}$ on $3^\circ$ Alcohols** | Writing dehydrogenation to give a ketone with one less carbon. | Tertiary alcohols have **no $\alpha$-hydrogen**. They undergo **dehydration** to an alkene ($\mathrm{R_2C=CH_2}$), not dehydrogenation! |
| **Methanol vs Water Acidity** | Stating "all alcohols are weaker acids than water." | Methanol ($\mathrm{CH_3OH}$) is slightly **more acidic** than water ($K_a = 3.2 \times 10^{-16}$ vs $1.8 \times 10^{-16}$). All other aliphatic alcohols are weaker acids than water. |
| **PCC vs Aqueous Chromic Acid** | Using Jones Reagent or acidified $\mathrm{KMnO_4}$ when asked to convert primary alcohols to aldehydes. | Strong aqueous oxidants over-oxidize $1^\circ$ alcohols to **carboxylic acids**. PCC in anhydrous $\mathrm{CH_2Cl_2}$ must be specified for aldehydes. |
| **Esterification Reversibility** | Writing a unidirectional forward arrow ($\rightarrow$) without removing products. | Esterification is a **reversible equilibrium** ($\rightleftharpoons$). Conc. $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ acts both as a catalyst and as a **dehydrating agent** to drive equilibrium forward. |
| **Lucas Test Observations** | Claiming $1^\circ$ alcohols produce no reaction at all. | $1^\circ$ alcohols *do* react via an $\mathrm{S_N2}$ pathway, but reaction is imperceptibly slow at room temperature; turbidity appears **upon heating**. |

# 6. Speed Hacks & Golden Points

### 1. The Carbinol Hydrogen Count Rule for Copper Dehydrogenation
Count the number of hydrogens directly bound to the carbinol carbon ($\mathrm{C-OH}$):
* **Two $\alpha$-H's ($1^\circ$ alcohol):** Remove $\mathrm{H_2} \longrightarrow$ **Aldehyde**
* **One $\alpha$-H ($2^\circ$ alcohol):** Remove $\mathrm{H_2} \longrightarrow$ **Ketone**
* **Zero $\alpha$-H's ($3^\circ$ alcohol):** Remove $\mathrm{H_2O}$ (from $\beta$-carbon) $\longrightarrow$ **Alkene**

### 2. Acid/Base Reactivity Mnemonics
* **"Protons Out, Less Crowded Wins":** Acidity drops with branching ($1^\circ > 2^\circ > 3^\circ$) due to $+I$ destabilization of the alkoxide anion.
* **"Leaving Out, More Crowded Wins":** Cleavage of $\mathrm{C-O}$ (dehydration, Lucas reaction) accelerates with branching ($3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ$) due to carbocation stability.

### 3. Industrial Alcohol Nomenclature Flashcards
* **Rectified Spirit:** $95.6\%\ \mathrm{C_2H_5OH} + 4.4\%\ \mathrm{H_2O}$ (Constant boiling azeotropic mixture).
* **Absolute Alcohol:** $100\%$ pure ethanol.
* **Denatured Alcohol / Methylated Spirit:** Ethanol made poisonous with $\mathrm{CH_3OH}$ ($~5\%$) + pyridine (smell) + $\mathrm{CuSO_4}$ (color).
* **Power Alcohol:** Mixture of $20\%$ absolute alcohol $+ 80\%$ petrol/gasoline (used as internal combustion motor fuel).
