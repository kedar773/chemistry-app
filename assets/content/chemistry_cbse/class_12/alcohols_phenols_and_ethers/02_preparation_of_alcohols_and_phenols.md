## 1. Preparation of Alcohols from Alkenes

### Method 1A: By Acid-Catalysed Hydration
**Priority Tag:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (Very High Board Frequency)

#### Concept & Principle
Alkenes react with water in the presence of an acid catalyst (commonly concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$) to yield alcohols.
- For **symmetrical alkenes**, addition across the double bond gives a unique product:

$$
\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+} \text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}
$$

- For **unsymmetrical alkenes**, addition follows **Markovnikov’s Rule**: the electrophile ($\text{H}^+$) adds to the carbon having the greater number of hydrogen atoms, forming the more stable carbocation intermediate. The nucleophile ($-\text{OH}$) then binds to the more substituted carbon.

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+} \text{CH}_3-\text{CH}(\text{OH})-\text{CH}_3
$$

***

#### Detailed Reaction Mechanism (NCERT Core Derivation)
The reaction proceeds in three distinct reversible steps:

##### Step 1: Electrophilic attack by hydronium ion on alkene to form a carbocation intermediate
The acid dissociates in water to generate the hydronium ion:

$$
\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{H}^+ \rightleftharpoons \text{H}_3\text{O}^+
$$

The $\pi$-electrons of the alkene attack the electrophilic proton of $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$:

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 + \text{H}-\text{O}^+\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3-\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}-\text{CH}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

*(Formation of the secondary carbocation, which is more stable than the primary carbocation).*

##### Step 2: Nucleophilic attack of water on the carbocation
Water acts as a Lewis base/nucleophile and attacks the electron-deficient planar carbocation carbon:

$$
\text{CH}_3-\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}-\text{CH}_3 + {:}\text{O}\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3-\underset{\substack{\mid \\ \text{O}^+\text{H}_2}}{\text{CH}}-\text{CH}_3
$$

*(Formation of protonated alcohol / alkyloxonium ion).*

##### Step 3: Deprotonation to yield the alcohol
A second water molecule acts as a base and abstracts a proton from the oxonium ion, regenerating the $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ catalyst:

$$
\text{CH}_3-\underset{\substack{\mid \\ \text{O}^+\text{H}_2}}{\text{CH}}-\text{CH}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3-\underset{\substack{\mid \\ \text{OH}}}{\text{CH}}-\text{CH}_3 + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Overall: } \text{CH}_3\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}(\text{OH})\text{CH}_3}
$$

***

### Method 1B: By Hydroboration–Oxidation
**Priority Tag:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (Very High Board Frequency)

#### Concept & Principle
Diboran $(\text{B}_2\text{H}_6 \text{ or } \text{BH}_3)$ reacts with alkenes to give trialkylboranes as addition products. Oxidation of trialkylborane by alkaline hydrogen peroxide ($\text{H}_2\text{O}_2 / \text{OH}^-$) yields alcohols.
- **Regioselectivity:** The net addition corresponds to **Anti-Markovnikov hydration** (addition of $\text{H}$ and $\text{OH}$).
- **Stereospecificity:** *Syn*-addition of $-\text{H}$ and $-\text{BH}_2$ across the double bond.
- **Absence of Skeletal Rearrangement:** Proceeds via a concerted 4-membered cyclic transition state without a free carbocation.

#### Step-by-Step Equations

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 + (\text{H}-\text{BH}_2) \longrightarrow \text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\text{BH}_2
$$

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{BH}_2 + \text{CH}_3\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 \longrightarrow (\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2)_2\text{BH}
$$

$$
(\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2)_2\text{BH} + \text{CH}_3\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 \longrightarrow (\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2)_3\text{B} \quad \text{(Tripropylborane)}
$$

Oxidation stage:

$$
(\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2)_3\text{B} + 3\,\text{H}_2\text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{OH}^-} 3\,\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{OH} + \text{B}(\text{OH})_3
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Net Result: } \text{R}-\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) } \text{H}_2\text{O}_2,\,\text{OH}^-]{\text{(i) } \text{B}_2\text{H}_6,\,\text{THF}} \text{R}-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}}
$$

## 2. Preparation from Carbonyl Compounds

### Method 2A: By Reduction of Aldehydes and Ketones
**Priority Tag:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### Reducing Agents
1. **Catalytic Hydrogenation:** Molecular hydrogen in the presence of finely divided metal catalysts like $\text{Pt}$, $\text{Pd}$, or $\text{Ni}$.
2. **Complex Metal Hydrides:**
   - **Sodium borohydride ($\text{NaBH}_4$):** Mild reducing agent in ethanol or methanol solvent; selectively reduces aldehydes and ketones without affecting carboxylic acids or esters.
   - **Lithium aluminium hydride ($\text{LiAlH}_4$):** Strong reducing agent in dry ether; reduces aldehydes, ketones, esters, and carboxylic acids.

#### Reactions
- **Aldehydes yield Primary ($1^\circ$) Alcohols:**

$$
\text{R}-\text{CHO} + \text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Pd}} \text{R}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}
$$

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CHO} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) }\text{H}_2\text{O}]{\text{(i) }\text{NaBH}_4} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH}
$$

- **Ketones yield Secondary ($2^\circ$) Alcohols:**

$$
\text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{R}' \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) } \text{H}^+]{\text{(i) } \text{NaBH}_4} \text{R}-\underset{\substack{\mid \\ \text{OH}}}{\text{CH}}-\text{R}'
$$

***

### Method 2B: By Reduction of Carboxylic Acids and Esters
**Priority Tag:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

- **Reduction with $\text{LiAlH}_4$:**
Carboxylic acids are reduced to primary alcohols in high yield using $\text{LiAlH}_4$:

$$
\text{R}-\text{COOH} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) } \text{H}_2\text{O}]{\text{(i) } \text{LiAlH}_4 / \text{dry ether}} \text{R}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}
$$

- **Industrial Commercial Process (Esterification followed by Catalytic Hydrogenation):**
Because $\text{LiAlH}_4$ is an expensive reagent, commercial preparation converts the carboxylic acid into an ester first, followed by catalytic reduction with hydrogen:

$$
\text{R}-\text{COOH} + \text{R}'\text{OH} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+} \text{R}-\text{COOR}' + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

$$
\text{R}-\text{COOR}' + 2\,\text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Catalyst}} \text{R}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH} + \text{R}'\text{OH}
$$

## 3. Preparation from Grignard Reagents
**Priority Tag:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (Highest Board Frequency)

### Concept & Reaction Sequence
A Grignard reagent ($\text{RMgX}$) acts as a carbanion source ($\text{R}^{\delta-}-\text{Mg}^{\delta+}\text{X}$). The nucleophilic carbon adds to the electrophilic carbonyl carbon to form a magnesium alkoxide adduct, which on acid hydrolysis furnishes the corresponding alcohol.

$$
\text{R}-\text{MgX} + \backslash\text{C}=\text{O} \longrightarrow \left[ \backslash\text{C}\underset{\substack{\mid \\ \text{R}}}{\overset{\substack{\text{O}^-\text{Mg}^{2+}\text{X} \\ \mid}}{-}} \right] \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{O} / \text{H}^+} \backslash\text{C}\underset{\substack{\mid \\ \text{R}}}{\overset{\substack{\text{OH} \\ \mid}}{-}} + \text{Mg}(\text{OH})\text{X}
$$

### Summary of Classifications
1. **Formaldehyde (Methanal, $\text{HCHO}$)** $\longrightarrow$ **$1^\circ$ Alcohol:**

$$
\text{HCHO} + \text{RMgX} \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} \text{R}-\text{CH}_2\text{OMgX} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{R}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH} + \text{Mg}(\text{OH})\text{X}
$$

2. **Any other Aldehyde ($\text{R}'\text{CHO}$, where $\text{R}' \neq \text{H}$)** $\longrightarrow$ **$2^\circ$ Alcohol:**

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CHO} + \text{CH}_3\text{MgBr} \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} \text{CH}_3-\underset{\substack{\mid \\ \text{CH}_3}}{\text{CH}}-\text{OMgBr} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{CH}_3-\underset{\substack{\mid \\ \text{CH}_3}}{\text{CH}}-\text{OH} + \text{Mg}(\text{OH})\text{Br}
$$

3. **Ketones ($\text{R}'\text{COR}''$)** $\longrightarrow$ **$3^\circ$ Alcohol:**

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_3 + \text{CH}_3\text{MgBr} \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}-\text{OMgBr} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}-\text{OH} + \text{Mg}(\text{OH})\text{Br}
$$

# Part 2: Methods of Preparation of Phenols

Phenol ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH}$), historically termed **carbolic acid**, is synthesized on laboratory and industrial scales via four principal routes:

***

## 1. From Haloarenes (Dow's Process)
**Priority Tag:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### Principle & Conditions
Chlorobenzene is treated with aqueous $\text{NaOH}$ at **$623\,\text{K}$** and **$300\,\text{atm}$** pressure. Under these drastic conditions, nucleophilic aromatic substitution occurs to form sodium phenoxide, which on acidification with dilute $\text{HCl}$ yields phenol.

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} + 2\,\text{NaOH} \xrightarrow[300\,\text{atm}]{623\,\text{K}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{ONa} + \text{NaCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{ONa} + \text{HCl} \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{NaCl}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Overall: } \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) } \text{H}^+]{\text{(i) } \text{NaOH},\,623\,\text{K},\,300\,\text{atm}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH}}
$$

***

## 2. From Benzenesulphonic Acid
**Priority Tag:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

### Principle & Step-by-Step Equations
Benzene is sulphonated with **oleum** (fuming sulphuric acid, $\text{H}_2\text{S}_2\text{O}_7$) to form benzenesulphonic acid. Heating with molten sodium hydroxide ($\text{NaOH}$) converts it into sodium phenoxide, followed by acidification to produce phenol.

1. **Sulphonation:**

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{SO}_3 \ (\text{in } \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4) \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_3\text{H}
$$

2. **Fusion with caustic alkali:**

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_3\text{H} + 3\,\text{NaOH} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{ONa} + \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_3 + 2\,\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

3. **Acidification:**

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{ONa} + \text{H}^+ \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH}
$$

***

## 3. From Diazonium Salts
**Priority Tag:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### Principle & Step-by-Step Equations
Aniline is diazotized at **$273\text{--}278\,\text{K}$ ($0\text{--}5^\circ\text{C}$)** using nitrous acid ($\text{NaNO}_2 + \text{HCl}$). The resulting benzenediazonium chloride is hydrolysed by warming with water or dilute acid to yield phenol with quantitative nitrogen gas evolution.

1. **Diazotisation:**

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{NaNO}_2 + 2\,\text{HCl} \xrightarrow{273-278\,\text{K}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{NaCl} + 2\,\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

2. **Hydrolysis:**

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{Warm}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{N}_2\uparrow + \text{HCl}
$$

***

## 4. From Cumene (Isopropylbenzene)
**Priority Tag:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (Most Tested Industrial Method)

### Principle & Reaction Scheme
Cumene is oxidized in the presence of air/molecular oxygen to form **cumene hydroperoxide**. Treatment with dilute acid ($\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$) causes rearrangement, cleaving it into **phenol** and **propan-2-one (acetone)**. Acetone is obtained as an economically valuable by-product in equimolar amounts.

1. **Preparation of Cumene (Friedel–Crafts Alkylation):**

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{CH}_3\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4 \text{ or } \text{AlCl}_3} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{CH}(\text{CH}_3)_2
$$

2. **Autoxidation to Cumene Hydroperoxide:**

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{CH}(\text{CH}_3)_2 + \text{O}_2 \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\underset{\substack{\mid \\ \text{CH}_3}}{\overset{\substack{\text{CH}_3 \\ \mid}}{\text{C}}}-\text{O}-\text{O}-\text{H}
$$

3. **Acid-Catalysed Cleavage:**

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)_2-\text{O}-\text{O}-\text{H} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+ / \text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_3
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Net: } \text{Cumene} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) } \text{H}^+ / \text{H}_2\text{O}]{\text{(i) } \text{O}_2} \text{Phenol} + \text{Acetone}}
$$

# Part 3: High-Yield Examples & 5-Year Solved CBSE Board PYQs (2020–2025)

### Q1 [CBSE 2024, 3 Marks]
**(a)** Write the mechanism of acid-catalysed hydration of ethene to ethanol.  
**(b)** How do you convert propanone to 2-methylpropan-2-ol?

**Solution:**
- **(a)** Write the three steps as detailed above:
  1. Protonation of ethene by $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ to form ethyl carbocation:
     

$$
\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}_2 + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+ \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3-\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

  2. Nucleophilic attack of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ on the carbocation:
     

$$
\text{CH}_3-\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{O}^+\text{H}_2
$$

  3. Deprotonation by water to form ethanol:
     

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{O}^+\text{H}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{OH} + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+
$$

- **(b)** Treat propanone with methylmagnesium bromide in dry ether, followed by acid hydrolysis:
  

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_3 + \text{CH}_3\text{MgBr} \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}-\text{OMgBr} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}-\text{OH} + \text{Mg}(\text{OH})\text{Br}
$$

***

### Q2 [CBSE 2023, 2 Marks]
Explain the preparation of phenol from cumene with balanced chemical equations. Name the by-product and state its industrial significance.

**Solution:**
- **Reactions:**
  1. Cumene undergoes oxidation with atmospheric oxygen to form cumene hydroperoxide:
     

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}(\text{CH}_3)_2 + \text{O}_2 \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{OOH}
$$

  2. Cumene hydroperoxide on treatment with dilute sulphuric acid gives phenol and propanone:
     

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{OOH} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{O} / \text{H}^+} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_3
$$

- **By-product:** Propanone (Acetone).
- **Industrial Significance:** It is an important industrial solvent and starting material, produced in equimolar yield alongside phenol, making this route the most economical industrial method.

### Q3 [CBSE 2022 Term-2, 2 Marks]
Show how you would synthesize:
1. Pentan-1-ol using a suitable alkene.
2. Cyclohexylmethanol using a Grignard reagent.

**Solution:**
1. **Pentan-1-ol from Pent-1-ene:** Use Hydroboration–Oxidation to achieve Anti-Markovnikov addition:
   

$$
6\,\text{CH}_3(\text{CH}_2)_2\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 + \text{B}_2\text{H}_6 \longrightarrow 2\,[\text{CH}_3(\text{CH}_2)_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2]_3\text{B}
$$

   

$$
[\text{CH}_3(\text{CH}_2)_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2]_3\text{B} + 3\,\text{H}_2\text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{OH}^-} 3\,\text{CH}_3(\text{CH}_2)_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH} + \text{B}(\text{OH})_3
$$

2. **Cyclohexylmethanol:** React cyclohexylmagnesium bromide with methanal (formaldehyde) followed by hydrolysis:
   

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_{11}\text{MgBr} + \text{HCHO} \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_{11}\text{CH}_2\text{OMgBr} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{C}_6\text{H}_{11}\text{CH}_2\text{OH} + \text{Mg}(\text{OH})\text{Br}
$$

### Q4 [CBSE 2021, 1 Mark]
Why cannot chlorobenzene be converted to phenol by simple boiling with aqueous $\text{NaOH}$ under ordinary conditions?

**Solution:**
Due to:
1. **Resonance Effect:** The lone pair of electrons on chlorine delocalizes with the $\pi$-system of the benzene ring, imparting partial double-bond character to the $\text{C}-\text{Cl}$ bond.
2. **$\text{sp}^2$ Hybridisation:** The $\text{sp}^2$ hybridised carbon of the ring is more electronegative than the $\text{sp}^3$ carbon of alkyl halides, holding the halogen tighter.
3. Instability of the phenyl cation and steric/electronic repulsion of attacking nucleophiles by the benzene $\pi$-electron cloud.

***

### Q5 [CBSE 2020, 3 Marks]
Give equations for:
1. Conversion of aniline to phenol.
2. Hydroboration of propene.
3. Reduction of but-2-enal to but-2-en-1-ol.

**Solution:**
1. **Aniline to Phenol:**
   

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 \xrightarrow[273-278\,\text{K}]{\text{NaNO}_2 + \text{HCl}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{N}_2 + \text{HCl}
$$

2. **Hydroboration of Propene:**
   

$$
3\,\text{CH}_3\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{BH}_3/\text{THF}} (\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2)_3\text{B} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{O}_2/\text{OH}^-} 3\,\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{OH}
$$

3. **Selective Reduction:**
   $\text{NaBH}_4$ reduces only the carbonyl group while keeping the $\text{C}=\text{C}$ double bond intact:
   

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}=\text{CH}-\text{CHO} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) }\text{H}_2\text{O}]{\text{(i) }\text{NaBH}_4} \text{CH}_3-\text{CH}=\text{CH}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}
$$

# Part 4: Examiner Traps & Common Student Mistakes

| S.No. | Reaction / Topic | Common Mistake Made by Students | NCERT Correct Concept / Examiner Expectation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Dow's Process Conditions** | Writing generic "$\text{NaOH}, \Delta$" without specifying temperature and pressure. | Marks are deducted if explicit values **$623\,\text{K}$** and **$300\,\text{atm}$** are omitted. |
| **2** | **Diazotisation Temperature** | Writing room temperature or forgetting cooling conditions. | Must specify **$273\text{--}278\,\text{K}$** ($0\text{--}5^\circ\text{C}$); at higher temperatures, diazonium salts decompose prematurely. |
| **3** | **Acid Hydration vs. Hydroboration** | Confusing regiochemical additions on unsymmetrical alkenes. | Acid hydration gives **Markovnikov** product ($2^\circ$ alcohol); Hydroboration–Oxidation gives **Anti-Markovnikov** product ($1^\circ$ alcohol). |
| **4** | **Grignard Preparation Medium** | Omitting the solvent "dry ether". | Grignard reagents react explosively or irreversibly with traces of active hydrogen ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{ROH}$), destroying the reagent: $\text{RMgX} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{RH} + \text{Mg}(\text{OH})\text{X}$. |
| **5** | **Reducing $\alpha,\beta$-Unsaturated Carbonyls** | Using catalytic $\text{H}_2/\text{Ni}$ to prepare unsaturated alcohols. | $\text{H}_2/\text{Ni}$ or $\text{Pd}$ also hydrogenates the carbon–carbon double bond; use **$\text{NaBH}_4$** to selectively reduce only $-\text{CHO}$ without reducing $-\text{C}=\text{C}-$. |
| **6** | **Cumene Intermediate Name** | Writing "Cumene peroxide" instead of "Cumene hydroperoxide". | The peroxy functional group is linked to a hydrogen ($-\text{O}-\text{O}-\text{H}$), making it a **hydroperoxide**. |

# Part 5: Speed Hacks & Golden Points

### 1. Grignard Alcohol Synthesis Matrix

| Carbonyl Reactant | Reagent | Product Class | Specific Example Product |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\text{HCHO}$ (Formaldehyde) | $\text{RMgX}$ | **$1^\circ$ Alcohol** | Ethanol, Propan-1-ol |
| $\text{R}'\text{CHO}$ (Other Aldehydes) | $\text{RMgX}$ | **$2^\circ$ Alcohol** | Propan-2-ol, Butan-2-ol |
| $\text{R}'\text{COR}''$ (Ketones) | $\text{RMgX}$ | **$3^\circ$ Alcohol** | 2-Methylpropan-2-ol |

> **Memory Mnemonic:**
> *"Formal is One, Other is Two, Ketone is Three"*  
> ($\text{Formaldehyde} \to 1^\circ$, $\text{Other Aldehydes} \to 2^\circ$, $\text{Ketones} \to 3^\circ$)

### 2. Alkene Hydration Cheat Rule
- $\text{Alkene} + \text{H}_2\text{O}/\text{H}^+ \Longrightarrow$ **Markovnikov** product with possible carbocation rearrangement.
- $\text{Alkene} + \text{B}_2\text{H}_6 \text{ then } \text{H}_2\text{O}_2/\text{OH}^- \Longrightarrow$ **Anti-Markovnikov**, clean $1^\circ$ alcohol, no rearrangements.

### 3. Phenol Industrial Preparation Mnemonic
> **"C-D-D-S" Route:**
> - **C**umene (Air $\text{O}_2$, then $\text{H}^+$) $\to$ Most popular industrial route (Acetone by-product).
> - **D**ow's Process ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl}$, $623\,\text{K}$, $300\,\text{atm}$).
> - **D**iazonium Salt ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^-$, warm with $\text{H}_2\text{O}$).
> - **S**ulphonic Acid ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_3\text{H}$, fuse with $\text{NaOH}$, then $\text{H}^+$).
