#### Question 1 [CBSE Delhi 2024, 1 Mark] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Which of the following transition metal ions has the highest spin-only magnetic moment?**
(A) $\text{Co}^{2+} \; (Z = 27)$  
(B) $\text{Ni}^{2+} \; (Z = 28)$  
(C) $\text{Fe}^{3+} \; (Z = 26)$  
(D) $\text{Cu}^{2+} \; (Z = 29)$

**Model Solution:**
* **Electronic Configurations:**
  * $\text{Co}^{2+} \; (3d^7) \implies n = 3$ unpaired electrons
  * $\text{Ni}^{2+} \; (3d^8) \implies n = 2$ unpaired electrons
  * $\text{Fe}^{3+} \; (3d^5) \implies n = 5$ unpaired electrons
  * $\text{Cu}^{2+} \; (3d^9) \implies n = 1$ unpaired electron
* **Spin-only formula:**
  

$$
\mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)} \text{ B.M.}
$$

  For $n = 5$, $\mu = \sqrt{5(5+2)} = \sqrt{35} \approx 5.92\text{ B.M.}$ (Highest).
* **Correct Option:** **(C) $\text{Fe}^{3+}$**

**CBSE Marking Scheme:**
* Stating number of unpaired electrons / applying $\mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)}$: $[1/2 \text{ Mark}]$
* Identifying correct option (C): $[1/2 \text{ Mark}]$

#### Question 3 [CBSE 2023, 1 Mark] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**The basic character of the transition metal monoxides follows the order:**
(A) $\text{TiO} > \text{VO} > \text{CrO} > \text{FeO}$  
(B) $\text{FeO} > \text{CrO} > \text{VO} > \text{TiO}$

(D) $\text{CrO} > \text{TiO} > \text{FeO} > \text{VO}$

$$
\text{TiO} > \text{VO} > \text{CrO} > \text{FeO}
$$

* **Correct Option:** **(A)**

* Correct option selection: $[1 \text{ Mark}]$

#### Question 4 [CBSE 2023 Compartment, 1 Mark] ⭐⭐⭐⭐
**Assertion (A):** The standard reduction potential ($E^\circ$) for $\text{Mn}^{3+}/\text{Mn}^{2+}$ couple is much more positive than that for $\text{Cr}^{3+}/\text{Cr}^{2+}$.  
**Reason (R):** $\text{Mn}^{2+}$ has a stable half-filled $d^5$ configuration, whereas $\text{Cr}^{3+}$ has a stable half-filled $t_{2g}^3$ configuration in aqueous solution.  
(A) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).  
(B) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).  
(C) (A) is true, but (R) is false.

#### Question 8 [CBSE Delhi 2024, 2 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Account for the following:**
**(i) Transition metals and their compounds exhibit high catalytic activity.**
**(ii) Transition metals have high enthalpies of atomization.**

**Model Solution:**
* **(i)** Transition metals show catalytic activity due to:
  1. Their ability to exhibit **variable oxidation states** and form unstable intermediate complexes that lower activation energy.
  2. Providing a **large surface area** with active sites/unfilled $d$-orbitals for reactants to adsorb.
* **(ii)** Transition metals have high enthalpies of atomization because:
  * They have a large number of unpaired electrons in their $(n-1)d$ orbitals in addition to $ns$ electrons.
  * This results in extensive interatomic $d\text{-}d$ overlapping and very strong metallic bonding.

**CBSE Marking Scheme:**
* (i) Variable oxidation states / intermediate complex formation / surface adsorption: $[1 \text{ Mark}]$
* (ii) Presence of unpaired $d$-electrons leading to strong metallic bonding: $[1 \text{ Mark}]$

#### Question 9 [CBSE All India 2023, 2 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Give reasons:**
**(i) $\text{E}^\circ$ value for the $\text{Cu}^{2+}/\text{Cu}$ couple is positive ($+0.34\text{ V}$) unlike other $3d$ series elements.**
**(ii) $\text{Cr}^{2+}$ is a strong reducing agent, whereas $\text{Mn}^{3+}$ is a strong oxidizing agent though both have $d^4$ configuration.**

**Model Solution:**
* **(i)** The sum of the first and second ionization enthalpies of copper ($\Delta_i H_1 + \Delta_i H_2$) is extremely high and is **not compensated** by its negative enthalpy of hydration ($\Delta_{\text{hyd}}H^\circ$). Hence, reduction of $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ to $\text{Cu}$ is thermodynamically favorable and $E^\circ$ is positive.
* **(ii)** 
  * $\text{Cr}^{2+} (d^4)$ gets oxidized to $\text{Cr}^{3+} (d^3)$. In aqueous solution, $d^3$ represents a stable, half-filled $t_{2g}^3$ configuration in the crystal field. Thus, $\text{Cr}^{2+}$ is strongly reducing.
  * $\text{Mn}^{3+} (d^4)$ gets reduced to $\text{Mn}^{2+} (d^5)$, which has an extra-stable half-filled $d^5$ subshell. Thus, $\text{Mn}^{3+}$ acts as an oxidizing agent.

**CBSE Marking Scheme:**
* (i) High enthalpy of atomization/ionization not balanced by hydration energy: $[1 \text{ Mark}]$
* (ii) $\text{Cr}^{3+}$ reaches stable $t_{2g}^3$ and $\text{Mn}^{2+}$ reaches stable $d^5$: $[1 \text{ Mark}]$

#### Question 12 [CBSE 2020 All India, 2 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**What is Lanthanoid Contraction? What is its main cause, and what is its effect on the chemistry of the second and third transition series elements?**

**Model Solution:**
* **Definition:** The steady and progressive decrease in the atomic and ionic radii of lanthanoid elements ($\text{La} \to \text{Lu}$) with increasing atomic number is known as **Lanthanoid Contraction**.
* **Cause:** With each successive element, the additional electron enters the inner $4f$ subshell. The $4f$-orbitals have diffused shapes and offer **imperfect / poor shielding** of the outer electrons from the increasing nuclear charge. Consequently, effective nuclear charge increases and pulls the valence shell inwards.
* **Consequence:** The atomic radii of elements of the $4d$ (second) and $5d$ (third) transition series belonging to the same vertical group become nearly identical (e.g., $\text{Zr} \approx \text{Hf}$, $\text{Nb} \approx \text{Ta}$).

**CBSE Marking Scheme:**
* Definition of steady decrease: $[1/2 \text{ Mark}]$
* Cause (poor shielding of $4f$ electrons): $[1/2 \text{ Mark}]$
* Consequence (identical radii of $4d/5d$ pairs like $\text{Zr/Hf}$): $[1 \text{ Mark}]$

#### Question 13 [CBSE 2024 Delhi Set-1, 3 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Explain the following observations:**
**(i) The highest oxidation state of a transition metal is shown in its oxides and fluorides only.**
**(ii) $\text{Cu}^+$ ion is not stable in aqueous solution.**
**(iii) The $+2$ oxidation state becomes more and more stable in the first half of the first row transition elements with increasing atomic number.**

**Model Solution:**
* **(i)** Oxygen and fluorine have **small atomic sizes** and **very high electronegativities**. Additionally, oxygen has the ability to form **multiple bonds** ($p\pi\text{-}d\pi$ bonding) with transition metals (e.g., in $\text{MnO}_4^-$ and $\text{CrO}_4^{2-}$), stabilizing high formal oxidation states like $+7$ and $+6$.
* **(ii)** In aqueous solution, $\text{Cu}^+$ undergoes spontaneous **disproportionation**:
  

$$
2\text{Cu}^+_{(\text{aq})} \longrightarrow \text{Cu}^{2+}_{(\text{aq})} + \text{Cu}_{(\text{s})}
$$

  This is because the much higher (more negative) hydration enthalpy of $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ compensates for the second ionization enthalpy of copper.
* **(iii)** Across the $3d$ series, electrons are added to the $(n-1)d$ subshell. Removal of two $4s$ electrons leaves the $3d$ electrons intact. As atomic number increases, the effective nuclear charge increases, binding the remaining $3d$ electrons more tightly. In the first half, reaching half-filled stability ($3d^5$ in $\text{Mn}^{2+}$) also reinforces the $+2$ state.

**CBSE Marking Scheme:**
* (i) Small size, high electronegativity, and oxygen's capability of multiple bonding: $[1 \text{ Mark}]$
* (ii) Disproportionation reaction and high hydration enthalpy of $\text{Cu}^{2+}$: $[1 \text{ Mark}]$
* (iii) Increase in sum of IE values & stability of configurations like $d^5$: $[1 \text{ Mark}]$

#### Question 15 [CBSE 2023 Compartment, 3 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐
**Compare the chemistry of Lanthanoids and Actinoids with respect to:**
**(i) Electronic configuration**
**(ii) Oxidation states**
**(iii) Chemical reactivity**

**Model Solution:**
* **(i) Electronic Configuration:**
  * **Lanthanoids:** Involve filling of $4f$ subshell; general configuration is $[\text{Xe}] 4f^{1-14} 5d^{0-1} 6s^2$.
  * **Actinoids:** Involve filling of $5f$ subshell; general configuration is $[\text{Rn}] 5f^{1-14} 6d^{0-1} 7s^2$. The $5f$ electrons do not shield as effectively as $4f$.
* **(ii) Oxidation States:**
  * **Lanthanoids:** Exhibit primarily $+3$ oxidation state. Occasionally $+2$ and $+4$ are shown only if stable configurations ($f^0, f^7, f^{14}$) are attained (e.g., $\text{Eu}^{2+}, \text{Ce}^{4+}$).
  * **Actinoids:** Exhibit a wider variety of oxidation states ($+3, +4, +5, +6, +7$) because the energy gap between $5f, 6d$, and $7s$ subshells is very narrow.
* **(iii) Chemical Reactivity:**
  * **Lanthanoids:** Moderately reactive; resemble calcium in reactivity.
  * **Actinoids:** Far more reactive, especially when finely divided. They are attacked by boiling water to form oxide and hydride, and react readily with non-metals at moderate temperatures.

**CBSE Marking Scheme:**
* (i) Accurate configuration difference: $[1 \text{ Mark}]$
* (ii) Comparison of oxidation states with energy levels explanation: $[1 \text{ Mark}]$
* (iii) Higher reactivity of actinoids vs lanthanoids: $[1 \text{ Mark}]$

#### Question 16 [CBSE 2022 Term-2, 3 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐
**Give appropriate chemical reasons for the following:**
**(i) Actinoid contraction is greater from element to element than lanthanoid contraction.**
**(ii) $\text{Ce}^{4+}$ is a good analytical oxidizing agent.**
**(iii) Transition metals form a large number of complex compounds.**

**Model Solution:**
* **(i)** $5f$ orbitals extend further in space than $4f$ orbitals and provide **poorer shielding** against the increasing nuclear charge. As a result, the effective nuclear charge felt by the outer electrons increases more rapidly across the actinoid series, making actinoid contraction greater.
* **(ii)** The most stable oxidation state of all lanthanoids is $+3$. Although $\text{Ce}^{4+}$ has a stable noble gas configuration ($4f^0$), it has a strong thermodynamic tendency to gain an electron to reach the $+3$ state ($E^\circ(\text{Ce}^{4+}/\text{Ce}^{3+}) = +1.74\text{ V}$). Hence, it acts as a strong oxidizing agent.
* **(iii)** Transition metals form abundant coordination complexes due to:
  1. **Small cationic size** and **high ionic charge density**.
  2. Availability of **vacant $(n-1)d$ orbitals** of appropriate energy to accept lone pairs from ligands.

**CBSE Marking Scheme:**
* (i) Poorer shielding of $5f$ compared to $4f$: $[1 \text{ Mark}]$
* (ii) Tendency of $\text{Ce}^{4+}$ to revert to dominant $+3$ state ($E^\circ = +1.74\text{ V}$): $[1 \text{ Mark}]$
* (iii) High charge density + vacant $d$-orbitals: $[1 \text{ Mark}]$

#### Question 17 [CBSE Sample Paper 2024 / Board Pattern 2023-24, 4 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Read the passage given below and answer the following questions:**

> Transition elements are characterized by partially filled $d$-orbitals. These elements display characteristic properties such as variable oxidation states, high melting points, formation of coloured ions, magnetic behaviour, and catalytic properties. 
> The ionization enthalpies of transition elements increase across a series, but the increase is not regular. The standard electrode potential values ($E^\circ_{\text{M}^{2+}/\text{M}}$) show irregularities across the $3d$ series due to the interplay of enthalpies of sublimation, ionization, and hydration.
> Potassium permanganate ($\text{KMnO}_4$) and potassium dichromate ($\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$) are two widely used oxidizing agents prepared from the minerals pyrolusite and chromite ore, respectively.

**(a) Why do transition metals exhibit variable oxidation states?**  
**(b) Why is $\text{Cr}^{2+}$ reducing whereas $\text{Mn}^{3+}$ is oxidizing when both have $d^4$ configuration?**  
**(c) [Attempt either sub-part (i) or (ii)]**  
**(i) How does the yellow chromate ion ($\text{CrO}_4^{2-}$) change to orange dichromate ion ($\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}$) with change in pH? Write the balanced equilibrium reaction.**  
*OR*  
**(ii) Write the steps involved in the preparation of potassium permanganate from pyrolusite ore ($\text{MnO}_2$).**

***

**Model Solution:**

* **(a)** Transition elements exhibit variable oxidation states because the energy difference between $(n-1)d$ and $ns$ orbitals is very small. As a result, both $ns$ and $(n-1)d$ electrons can participate in bond formation.

* **(b)** 
  * $\text{Cr}^{2+}$ ($d^4$) loses an electron to form $\text{Cr}^{3+}$ ($d^3$), which has a stable, half-filled $t_{2g}^3$ configuration in aqueous medium. Thus, it easily acts as a reducing agent.
  * $\text{Mn}^{3+}$ ($d^4$) gains an electron to form $\text{Mn}^{2+}$ ($d^5$), which achieves a stable half-filled $d$-subshell configuration. Thus, it acts as an oxidizing agent.

* **(c) (i)** 
  * In acidic solution, yellow chromate ($\text{CrO}_4^{2-}$) is converted to orange dichromate ($\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}$). In basic solution, orange dichromate reverts to yellow chromate.
  * Balanced reversible equilibrium:
    

$$
2\text{CrO}_4^{2-} + 2\text{H}^+ \rightleftharpoons \text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-} + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

    

$$
\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-} + 2\text{OH}^- \rightleftharpoons 2\text{CrO}_4^{2-} + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

* **OR**

* **(c) (ii)**
  * **Step 1: Conversion of pyrolusite ore ($\text{MnO}_2$) into potassium manganate:**  
    Finely powdered $\text{MnO}_2$ is fused with potassium hydroxide ($\text{KOH}$) in the presence of atmospheric oxygen or an oxidizing agent like $\text{KNO}_3$:
    

$$
2\text{MnO}_2 + 4\text{KOH} + \text{O}_2 \longrightarrow 2\text{K}_2\text{MnO}_4\text{ (dark green)} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

  * **Step 2: Oxidation of potassium manganate to potassium permanganate:**  
    The green potassium manganate is oxidized electrolytically (or disproportionated in acidic solution):
    

$$
\text{MnO}_4^{2-} \xrightarrow{\text{electrolytic oxidation in alkaline medium}} \text{MnO}_4^-\text{ (purple)} + e^-
$$

    *(Or via chemical disproportionation: $3\text{MnO}_4^{2-} + 4\text{H}^+ \to 2\text{MnO}_4^- + \text{MnO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$)*

***

**CBSE Marking Scheme:**
* (a) Comparable energies of $(n-1)d$ and $ns$ orbitals: $[1 \text{ Mark}]$
* (b) Reference to $t_{2g}^3$ stability of $\text{Cr}^{3+}$ and $d^5$ stability of $\text{Mn}^{2+}$: $[1 \text{ Mark}]$
* (c) (i) Correct explanation of pH effect ($1 \text{ mark}$) + balanced equilibrium equation ($1 \text{ mark}$): $[2 \text{ Marks}]$  
  *OR*  
  (c) (ii) Step 1 fusion equation ($1 \text{ mark}$) + Step 2 electrolytic/acidic oxidation ($1 \text{ mark}$): $[2 \text{ Marks}]$

#### Question 18 [CBSE 2024 All India, 5 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**(a) Account for the following:**
**(i) Of the $d^4$ species, $\text{Cr}^{2+}$ is strongly reducing while $\text{Mn}^{3+}$ is strongly oxidizing.**
**(ii) Cobalt(II) is stable in aqueous solution, but in the presence of strong ligands and complexing agents, it is readily oxidized to Cobalt(III).**
**(iii) The $d^1$ configuration is very unstable in ions of transition metals.**

**(b) Describe the preparation of potassium dichromate ($\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$) from chromite ore ($\text{FeCr}_2\text{O}_4$). Write all balanced chemical equations.**

**Model Solution:**

* **(a) (i)** $\text{Cr}^{2+} (d^4)$ oxidizes to $\text{Cr}^{3+} (d^3)$, which has a stable half-filled $t_{2g}^3$ crystal field configuration. $\text{Mn}^{3+} (d^4)$ reduces to $\text{Mn}^{2+} (d^5)$, which achieves an extra-stable half-filled $d$-subshell configuration.
* **(a) (ii)** In the presence of strong field ligands, the crystal field splitting energy ($\Delta_\circ$) is very large. This large stabilization energy (CFSE) overcomes the third ionization enthalpy of cobalt, making $\text{Co(III)}$ with low-spin $t_{2g}^6$ (completely filled sub-level) far more stable than $\text{Co(II)}$ ($d^7$).
* **(a) (iii)** An ion with $d^1$ configuration has a strong tendency either to lose this single electron to acquire a stable noble gas configuration (e.g., $\text{Ti}^{3+} \to \text{Ti}^{4+}$) or to undergo disproportionation to more stable states (e.g., $\text{Cr}^{5+}$).

* **(b) Preparation of $\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$ from Chromite Ore:**

  * **Step 1: Fusion of chromite ore with sodium carbonate in excess of air:**  
    Powdered chromite ore is fused with $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ in a reverberatory furnace in the presence of free air to form yellow sodium chromate:
    

$$
4\text{FeCr}_2\text{O}_4 + 8\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 + 7\text{O}_2 \longrightarrow 8\text{Na}_2\text{CrO}_4 + 2\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 + 8\text{CO}_2\uparrow
$$

  * **Step 2: Conversion of sodium chromate into sodium dichromate:**  
    The yellow solution of sodium chromate is filtered and acidified with concentrated sulphuric acid to yield orange sodium dichromate:
    

$$
2\text{Na}_2\text{CrO}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \longrightarrow \text{Na}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7 + \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

    Sodium sulphate crystallizes out as $\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4 \cdot 10\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and is removed.

  * **Step 3: Conversion of sodium dichromate to potassium dichromate:**  
    The orange sodium dichromate solution is treated with potassium chloride ($\text{KCl}$). Due to the lower solubility of $\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$ compared to $\text{Na}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$, orange crystals of potassium dichromate separate upon cooling:
    

$$
\text{Na}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7 + 2\text{KCl} \longrightarrow \text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7 + 2\text{NaCl}
$$

#### Question 19 [CBSE 2023 All India / Foreign, 5 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**(a) Complete and balance the following chemical equations:**
**(i) $\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-} + 3\text{Sn}^{2+} + 14\text{H}^+ \longrightarrow$**  
**(ii) $2\text{MnO}_4^- + 5\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-} + 16\text{H}^+ \longrightarrow$**  
**(iii) $2\text{MnO}_4^- + 10\text{I}^- + 16\text{H}^+ \longrightarrow$**  

**(b) Assign reasons for the following:**
**(i) Transition metals show paramagnetic behaviour.**  
**(ii) The enthalpies of atomization of the transition elements of $5d$ series are higher than those of corresponding elements of $3d$ and $4d$ series.**

**Model Solution:**

* **(a) Balanced Ionic Equations:**
  * **(i)** Oxidation of tin(II) by dichromate:
    

$$
\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-} + 3\text{Sn}^{2+} + 14\text{H}^+ \longrightarrow 2\text{Cr}^{3+} + 3\text{Sn}^{4+} + 7\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

  * **(ii)** Oxidation of oxalate ion by acidified permanganate:
    

$$
2\text{MnO}_4^- + 5\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-} + 16\text{H}^+ \longrightarrow 2\text{Mn}^{2+} + 10\text{CO}_2\uparrow + 8\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

  * **(iii)** Oxidation of iodide ion by acidified permanganate:
    

$$
2\text{MnO}_4^- + 10\text{I}^- + 16\text{H}^+ \longrightarrow 2\text{Mn}^{2+} + 5\text{I}_2 + 8\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

* **(b) Reasons:**
  * **(i)** Transition metal atoms/ions possess one or more **unpaired electrons** in their $(n-1)d$ subshells. Each unpaired electron has a permanent magnetic dipole moment arising from its spin and orbital angular momentum, resulting in paramagnetism (attraction to magnetic fields).
  * **(ii)** The $5d$ transition elements have intervening $4f$-orbitals that cause **Lanthanoid Contraction**. This results in a higher effective nuclear charge, smaller atomic sizes, and much stronger metallic bonding involving extensive interatomic $5d\text{-}5d$ orbital overlap compared to $3d$ and $4d$ series. Hence, their enthalpies of atomization are significantly higher.

#### Question 20 [CBSE 2020 Delhi, 5 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐
**(a) Illustrate with appropriate reasons:**
**(i) Copper(I) compounds are generally diamagnetic and white, whereas Copper(II) compounds are paramagnetic and coloured.**
**(ii) Zinc, Cadmium, and Mercury are usually not regarded as true transition elements.**
**(iii) $\text{V}_2\text{O}_5$ acts as an amphoteric oxide.**

**(b) What happens when:**
**(i) A neutral solution of potassium permanganate reacts with potassium iodide? Write balanced equation.**
**(ii) Potassium dichromate is heated with concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ and $\text{NaCl}$? (Chromyl chloride test). Write balanced reaction.**

**Model Solution:**

* **(a) (i)** 
  * $\text{Cu}^+$ has electronic configuration $[\text{Ar}] 3d^{10}$. Since all orbitals are completely filled ($n = 0$ unpaired electrons), it is **diamagnetic** and cannot undergo $d\text{-}d$ transitions; hence it is **white/colourless**.
  * $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ has configuration $[\text{Ar}] 3d^9$. It contains one unpaired electron ($n=1$), which imparts **paramagnetism** and allows absorption of visible light via $d\text{-}d$ electron transitions, making its compounds **coloured** (usually blue).

* **(a) (ii)** 
  * According to IUPAC, a transition element is defined as an element that has an incompletely filled $d$-subshell either in its ground state or in any of its common oxidation states.
  * $\text{Zn}, \text{Cd}$, and $\text{Hg}$ have completely filled $d$-orbitals ($d^{10}$) in their ground states as well as in their common $+2$ oxidation states ($\text{Zn}^{2+}: 3d^{10}, \text{Cd}^{2+}: 4d^{10}, \text{Hg}^{2+}: 5d^{10}$). Hence, they are not regarded as true transition metals.

* **(a) (iii)**
  * In $\text{V}_2\text{O}_5$, vanadium is in its maximum oxidation state ($+5$). It reacts with acids to produce dioxovanadium(V) salts and with alkalis to form vanadate ions:
    

$$
\text{V}_2\text{O}_5 + 6\text{HCl} \longrightarrow 2\text{VO}_2^+ + 4\text{Cl}^- + 3\text{H}_2\text{O} \quad (\text{Basic action})
$$

    

$$
\text{V}_2\text{O}_5 + 6\text{OH}^- \longrightarrow 2\text{VO}_4^{3-} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O} \quad (\text{Acidic action})
$$

  * Because it neutralizes both acids and bases, $\text{V}_2\text{O}_5$ is amphoteric.

* **(b) (i)** 
  * In neutral or faintly alkaline medium, permanganate oxidizes iodide to **iodate** ($\text{IO}_3^-$):
    

$$
2\text{MnO}_4^- + \text{I}^- + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow 2\text{MnO}_2 + \text{IO}_3^- + 2\text{OH}^-
$$

* **(b) (ii)** 
  * When potassium dichromate is heated with solid $\text{NaCl}$ and concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$, oily red/orange vapors of **Chromyl Chloride** ($\text{CrO}_2\text{Cl}_2$) are evolved:
    

$$
\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7 + 4\text{NaCl} + 6\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \longrightarrow 2\text{KHSO}_4 + 4\text{NaHSO}_4 + 2\text{CrO}_2\text{Cl}_2\uparrow + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$
