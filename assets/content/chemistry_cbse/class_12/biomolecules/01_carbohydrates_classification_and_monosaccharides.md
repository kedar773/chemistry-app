# 1. Pedagogical Theory, Classifications & Fundamental Definitions

### 1.1 Definition of Carbohydrates
Historically defined as "hydrates of carbon" with empirical formula $\mathrm{C}_x(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O})_y$.
- **Failure of Historical Definition:**
  - Acetic acid ($\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{COOH} \equiv \mathrm{C}_2(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O})_2$) fits the formula but is **not** a carbohydrate.
  - Formaldehyde ($\mathrm{HCHO} \equiv \mathrm{C}(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O})$) fits the formula but is **not** a carbohydrate.
  - Rhamnose ($\mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_{12}\mathrm{O}_5$) and 2-deoxyribose ($\mathrm{C}_5\mathrm{H}_{10}\mathrm{O}_4$) are carbohydrates but **do not** conform to $\mathrm{C}_x(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O})_y$.

> **NCERT Classical Definition (Strict Marking Standard):** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  
> **Carbohydrates** are defined as **optically active polyhydroxy aldehydes or polyhydroxy ketones**, or compounds which produce such units on hydrolysis.

***

### 1.2 Classification Systems

#### A. Based on Hydrolysis Behavior ⭐⭐⭐⭐
1. **Monosaccharides:** Carbohydrates that cannot be hydrolyzed further into simpler polyhydroxy aldehyde or ketone units. (General formula: $(\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{O})_n$, where $n = 3\text{ to }7$).  
   *Examples:* Glucose, Fructose, Galactose, Ribose.
2. **Oligosaccharides:** Carbohydrates yielding $2\text{ to }10$ monosaccharide molecules on hydrolysis.
   - **Disaccharides** ($n=2$): Yield two units.
     

$$
\mathrm{C}_{12}\mathrm{H}_{22}\mathrm{O}_{11} + \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{H}^+} \mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_{12}\mathrm{O}_6 + \mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_{12}\mathrm{O}_6
$$

     - Sucrose $\rightarrow$ $\mathrm{D}\text{-(+)-Glucose} + \mathrm{D}\text{-(-)-Fructose}$
     - Maltose $\rightarrow$ $2 \text{ molecules of } \mathrm{D}\text{-(+)-Glucose}$
     - Lactose $\rightarrow$ $\mathrm{D}\text{-(+)-Glucose} + \mathrm{D}\text{-(+)-Galactose}$
   - **Trisaccharides** ($n=3$): e.g., Raffinose ($\mathrm{C}_{18}\mathrm{H}_{32}\mathrm{O}_{16} \rightarrow \text{Glucose} + \text{Fructose} + \text{Galactose}$).
3. **Polysaccharides:** High molecular mass polymers yielding a very large number of monosaccharide units on hydrolysis. General formula: $(\mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_{10}\mathrm{O}_5)_n$.  
   *Examples:* Starch, Cellulose, Glycogen.

#### B. Based on Reducing Character (Board Priority: High) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
1. **Reducing Sugars:** Carbohydrates containing a **free aldehydic or ketonic group** capable of reducing Fehling’s solution ($\mathrm{Cu}^{2+} \rightarrow \mathrm{Cu}_2\mathrm{O}\downarrow$, red) and Tollen's reagent ($\mathrm{Ag}^+ \rightarrow \mathrm{Ag}\downarrow$, silver mirror).
   - **All monosaccharides** (both aldoses and ketoses) are reducing sugars.  
     *(Note: Ketoses reduce Fehling's/Tollen's because they tautomerize to aldoses via enediol intermediates under alkaline conditions).*
   - **Disaccharides** with free hemiacetal/hemiketal groups: Maltose, Lactose.
2. **Non-Reducing Sugars:** Carbohydrates in which the reducing groups (aldehydic or ketonic carbonyl carbons) are engaged in **glycosidic linkage formation**.
   - *Example:* **Sucrose** (C-1 of $\alpha$-D-glucose is linked to C-2 of $\beta$-D-fructose).
   - All **polysaccharides** are non-reducing sugars.

#### C. Physical Classification ⭐⭐⭐
- **Sugars:** Crystalline, sweet in taste, soluble in water (Monosaccharides, Oligosaccharides).
- **Non-Sugars:** Amorphous, tasteless, insoluble or sparingly soluble in water (Polysaccharides).

# 2. Structural Elucidation of D-(+)-Glucose (Aldohexose)

Molecular Formula of Glucose: $\mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_{12}\mathrm{O}_6$.

```
           CHO          (C-1)
            |
     H ———— C ———— OH   (C-2)
            |
    HO ———— C ———— H    (C-3)
            |
     H ———— C ———— OH   (C-4)
            |
     H ———— C ———— OH   (C-5)
            |
          CH2OH         (C-6)
```

### 2.1 Step-by-Step Proof of Structure (Reactions & Deductions) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### Step 1: Presence of an unbranched 6-carbon straight chain
- **Reaction:** Prolonged heating of glucose with hydroiodic acid ($\mathrm{HI}$) in the presence of red phosphorus at $373\text{ K}$.
- **Equation:**
  

$$
\mathrm{CHO-(\!CHOH\!)}_4\mathrm{-CH_2OH} + 14\,\mathrm{HI} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{Red }\mathrm{P},\, 373\,\text{K}} \mathrm{CH_3-(CH_2)_4-CH_3} + 7\,\mathrm{I}_2 + 6\,\mathrm{H_2O}
$$

- **Deduction:** Formation of **n-hexane** proves that all 6 carbon atoms are joined in a continuous, unbranched open chain.

***

#### Step 2: Confirmation of the presence of a Carbonyl Group ($>\!\mathrm{C\!=\!O}$)
Glucose reacts with nucleophiles targeting carbonyl carbon:
1. **Reaction with Hydroxylamine ($\mathrm{NH_2OH}$):**
   

$$
\mathrm{CHO-(\!CHOH\!)}_4\mathrm{-CH_2OH} + \mathrm{NH_2OH} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{H}^+} \mathrm{CH\!=\!N-OH-(\!CHOH\!)}_4\mathrm{-CH_2OH} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

   *(Product: Glucose oxime)*
2. **Reaction with Hydrogen Cyanide ($\mathrm{HCN}$):**
   

$$
\mathrm{CHO-(\!CHOH\!)}_4\mathrm{-CH_2OH} + \mathrm{HCN} \rightarrow \mathrm{CH(OH)(CN)-(\!CHOH\!)}_4\mathrm{-CH_2OH}
$$

   *(Product: Glucose cyanohydrin)*
- **Deduction:** These reactions confirm the presence of a carbonyl group ($>\!\mathrm{C\!=\!O}$).

***

#### Step 3: Confirmation that the Carbonyl Group is an Aldehyde ($-\mathrm{CHO}$)
- **Reaction:** Mild oxidation using Bromine water ($\mathrm{Br_2}/\mathrm{H_2O}$).
- **Equation:**
  

$$
\mathrm{CHO-(\!CHOH\!)}_4\mathrm{-CH_2OH} + [\mathrm{O}] \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Br_2 \,/\, H_2O}} \mathrm{COOH-(\!CHOH\!)}_4\mathrm{-CH_2OH}
$$

  *(Product: Gluconic acid / Aldonic acid)*
- **Deduction:** Bromine water is a mild oxidizing agent that oxidizes aldehydes to carboxylic acids but does **not** oxidize ketones or secondary alcohols. Hence, the carbonyl group is an **aldehyde group** situated at C-1.

***

#### Step 4: Confirmation of Five Hydroxyl ($-OH$) Groups
- **Reaction:** Acetylation with Acetic Anhydride ($\mathrm{(CH_3CO)_2O}$) in the presence of pyridine catalyst.
- **Equation:**
  

$$
\mathrm{CHO-(\!CHOH\!)}_4\mathrm{-CH_2OH} + 5\,(\mathrm{CH_3CO})_2\mathrm{O} \xrightarrow{\text{Pyridine}} \mathrm{CHO-(\!CHOCOCH_3\!)}_4\mathrm{-CH_2OCOCH_3} + 5\,\mathrm{CH_3COOH}
$$

  *(Product: Glucose pentaacetate)*
- **Deduction:** Formation of a stable **pentaacetate** derivative confirms the presence of **5 hydroxyl groups**. Because glucose is a stable compound and geminal polyols ($\mathrm{C(OH)_2}$) are unstable and spontaneously eliminate water, each of the 5 $-OH$ groups must be attached to a **different carbon atom**.

***

#### Step 5: Confirmation of a Primary Alcoholic Group ($-\mathrm{CH_2OH}$) at C-6
- **Reaction:** Strong oxidation with concentrated Nitric Acid ($\mathrm{HNO}_3$).
- **Equation:**
  

$$
\mathrm{CHO-(\!CHOH\!)}_4\mathrm{-CH_2OH} + 3[\mathrm{O}] \xrightarrow{\text{conc. }\mathrm{HNO}_3} \mathrm{COOH-(\!CHOH\!)}_4\mathrm{-COOH} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

  *(Product: Saccharic acid / Glucaric acid, a dicarboxylic acid)*
- Also, oxidation of gluconic acid yields the same saccharic acid:
  

$$
\mathrm{COOH-(\!CHOH\!)}_4\mathrm{-CH_2OH} + 2[\mathrm{O}] \xrightarrow{\text{conc. }\mathrm{HNO}_3} \mathrm{COOH-(\!CHOH\!)}_4\mathrm{-COOH} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

- **Deduction:** The aldehyde group at C-1 and the terminal primary alcohol at C-6 are oxidized to carboxyl groups ($-\mathrm{COOH}$), confirming the terminal position of a **primary alcohol** ($-\mathrm{CH_2OH}$).

***

### 2.2 D/L Notations and Optical Activity ⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Absolute Reference Compound:** Glyceraldehyde ($\mathrm{CH_2OH-CHOH-CHO}$).
  - In Fischer projection with the most oxidized carbon ($-\mathrm{CHO}$) at the top:
    - If the $-OH$ group on the lowest asymmetric (chiral) carbon is on the **RIGHT** $\rightarrow$ **$\mathrm{D}$-configuration**.
    - If the $-OH$ group on the lowest asymmetric carbon is on the **LEFT** $\rightarrow$ **$\mathrm{L}$-configuration**.
- For Glucose, the lowest asymmetric carbon is **C-5**.
  - Naturally occurring glucose has the $-OH$ group on the right at C-5 $\rightarrow$ **$\mathrm{D}$-family**.
  - It rotates plane-polarized light to the right (dextrorotatory) $\rightarrow$ designated by **$(+)$**.
  - **Complete Correct Name:** **$\mathrm{D}\text{-(+)-Glucose}$**.
  > **Examiner Note:** '$\mathrm{D}$' and '$\mathrm{L}$' denote spatial stereochemical configuration relative to glyceraldehyde; they have **no mathematical correlation** with optical rotation signs $(+)$ [dextro] or $(-)$ [laevo].

# 3. Cyclic Structure, Anomers, and Mutarotation of D-Glucose

### 3.1 Limitations of the Open-Chain Structure of Glucose ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
The open-chain Fischer structure fails to explain several experimental facts:
1. **No addition compound with $\mathrm{NaHSO}_3$:** Despite having an aldehyde group, glucose does not form a bisulfite addition product or react with Schiff’s reagent.
2. **Pentaacetate does not react with $\mathrm{NH}_2\mathrm{OH}$:** Glucose pentaacetate does not form an oxime, proving that in the pentaacetate, there is **no free $-\mathrm{CHO}$ group**.
3. **Existence of two isomeric crystalline forms ($\alpha$ and $\beta$):**
   - $\alpha\text{-D-glucose}$: Crystallized from concentrated solution at $303\text{ K}$ ($\text{m.p.} = 419\text{ K}$, $[\alpha]_D = +112^\circ$).
   - $\beta\text{-D-glucose}$: Crystallized from hot, saturated aqueous solution at $371\text{ K}$ ($\text{m.p.} = 423\text{ K}$, $[\alpha]_D = +19^\circ$).

***

### 3.2 Mechanism of Intramolecular Hemiacetal Ring Formation
The $-\mathrm{OH}$ group at **C-5** adds intramolecularly across the carbonyl carbon (**C-1**), forming a 6-membered oxygen-containing heterocycle (**Pyranose ring**).

$$
\mathrm{C}_1(=\!\mathrm{O}) + \mathrm{C}_5\mathrm{-OH} \rightleftharpoons \text{Intramolecular Cyclic Hemiacetal}
$$

```
        alpha-D-(+)-Glucopyranose                beta-D-(+)-Glucopyranose
                  H — C — OH                             HO — C — H
                      |                                       |
               H ———— C ———— OH                        H ———— C ———— OH
                      |                                       |
              HO ———— C ———— H                        HO ———— C ———— H
                      |          O                            |          O
               H ———— C ———— OH   |                    H ———— C ———— OH   |
                      |           |                           |           |
               H ———— C ——————————+                    H ———— C ——————————+
                      |                                       |
                    CH2OH                                   CH2OH
```

### 3.3 Anomers & The Anomeric Carbon ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- During cyclization, **C-1** becomes an asymmetric center (chiral carbon). It is called the **anomeric carbon**.
- **Anomers:** Diastereomers that differ in stereochemical configuration **only at C-1** (the anomeric carbon).
  - **$\alpha$-D-Glucopyranose:** The $-OH$ at C-1 is directed **downward** (trans to $-\mathrm{CH_2OH}$ in Haworth projection; right in Fischer).
  - **$\beta$-D-Glucopyranose:** The $-OH$ at C-1 is directed **upward** (cis to $-\mathrm{CH_2OH}$ in Haworth projection; left in Fischer).

***

### 3.4 Haworth Projections of D-Glucopyranose

```
        alpha-D-Glucopyranose                     beta-D-Glucopyranose
                CH2OH                                     CH2OH
               /     \                                   /     \
            H /   O   \ H                             H /   O   \ OH
             |---------|  OH                           |---------|  H
            OH\       /                               OH\       /
               \     /                                   \     /
              H   |   H                                 H   |   H
                 OH                                        OH

  At C-1: H is UP, OH is DOWN              At C-1: OH is UP, H is DOWN
  At C-2: H is UP, OH is DOWN              At C-2: H is UP, OH is DOWN
  At C-3: OH is UP, H is DOWN              At C-3: OH is UP, H is DOWN
  At C-4: H is UP, OH is DOWN              At C-4: H is UP, OH is DOWN
  At C-5: -CH2OH is UP, H is DOWN          At C-5: -CH2OH is UP, H is DOWN
```

### 3.5 Mutarotation (Quantitative Derivation) ⭐⭐⭐⭐
**Mutarotation:** Spontaneous change in the specific optical rotation of an optically active sugar in solution until reaching a constant equilibrium value.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha\text{-D-(+)-Glucose} \quad &\rightleftharpoons \quad \text{Open-chain intermediate} \quad \rightleftharpoons \quad \beta\text{-D-(+)-Glucose} \\
([\alpha]_D = +112^\circ) \quad & \qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad ([\alpha]_D = +19^\circ)
\end{aligned}
$$

At equilibrium ($298\text{ K}$), the constant specific rotation is **$[\alpha]_D = +52.7^\circ$**.

**Quantitative Equilibrium Composition Derivation:**
Let fraction of $\alpha$-anomer $= x$; then fraction of $\beta$-anomer $= 1 - x$ (neglecting the trace $<0.02\%$ open-chain form):

$$
112^\circ(x) + 19^\circ(1 - x) = 52.7^\circ
$$

$$
112x + 19 - 19x = 52.7
$$

$$
93x = 33.7 \implies x = \frac{33.7}{93} \approx 0.362 \implies \mathbf{36\% \; \alpha\text{-anomer}}
$$

$$
1 - x = 1 - 0.362 = 0.638 \implies \mathbf{64\% \; \beta\text{-anomer}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Equilibrium Mixture: } \approx 36\%\; \alpha\text{-D-glucose} + \approx 64\%\; \beta\text{-D-glucose}}
$$

*Thermodynamic Reason:* In $\beta$-D-glucopyranose, all bulky substituents ($-OH$ at C-1, C-2, C-3, C-4 and $-\mathrm{CH_2OH}$ at C-5) occupy equatorial positions in the chair conformation, minimizing 1,3-diaxial steric strain.

# 4. Structure of D-(-)-Fructose (Ketohexose)

Molecular Formula: $\mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_{12}\mathrm{O}_6$.  
Functional Group: **Ketone at C-2** ($>\!\mathrm{C\!=\!O}$).

### 4.1 Open-Chain Structure
```
          CH2OH        (C-1)
            |
            C === O    (C-2, Carbonyl carbon)
            |
   HO ———— C ———— H   (C-3)
            |
    H ———— C ———— OH  (C-4)
            |
    H ———— C ———— OH  (C-5, Configuration determining)
            |
          CH2OH        (C-6)
```
- Configuration at C-5 corresponds to $\mathrm{D}$-series ($-OH$ on right).
- It is strongly **laevorotatory** ($[\alpha]_D = -92.4^\circ$).
- Complete systematic name: **$\mathrm{D}\text{-(-)-Fructose}$**.

***

### 4.2 Cyclic Hemiketal Structure (Furanose Ring) ⭐⭐⭐⭐
- Formed by intramolecular nucleophilic attack of $-OH$ at **C-5** onto the ketonic carbonyl carbon at **C-2**.
- Results in a 5-membered cyclic hemiketal: **Furanose ring**.
- The newly created asymmetric anomeric center is **C-2**.

```
           alpha-D-(-)-Fructofuranose               beta-D-(-)-Fructofuranose
                    CH2OH                                      OH
                      |                                        |
               HO — C — (C-2)                           HOH2C — C — (C-2)
                      |                                        |
             HO ———— C ———— H                         HO ———— C ———— H
                      |          O                             |          O
              H ———— C ———— OH   |                     H ———— C ———— OH   |
                      |          |                             |          |
              H ———— C ——————————+                     H ———— C ——————————+
                      |                                        |
                    CH2OH                                    CH2OH
```

### 4.3 Haworth Projections of D-Fructofuranose

```
        alpha-D-(-)-Fructofuranose                 beta-D-(-)-Fructofuranose
             HOH2C    O   CH2OH                         HOH2C    O   OH
                 \   / \ /                                  \   / \ /
               H  \_/   \ OH                              H  \_/   \ CH2OH
                 / \     /                                  / \     /
               OH   \___/ H                               OH   \___/ H
                     OH                                         OH

  At C-2: -CH2OH is UP, -OH is DOWN        At C-2: -OH is UP, -CH2OH is DOWN
  At C-3: -OH is UP, -H is DOWN            At C-3: -OH is UP, -H is DOWN
  At C-4: -H is UP, -OH is DOWN            At C-4: -H is UP, -OH is DOWN
  At C-5: -CH2OH is UP, -H is DOWN         At C-5: -CH2OH is UP, -H is DOWN
```

# 5. High-Yield Solved Board PYQs (2020–2025)

### Question 1 [CBSE Board 2024, 3 Marks]
**(a)** Write the chemical reaction showing that glucose contains:
1. Five hydroxyl groups.
2. A straight chain of six carbons.  
**(b)** State why glucose pentaacetate does not form an oxime with hydroxylamine.

**Complete Step-by-Step Solution:**
**(a)**
1. Glucose reacts with acetic anhydride in the presence of pyridine to give glucose pentaacetate:
   

$$
\mathrm{CHO-(\!CHOH\!)}_4\mathrm{-CH_2OH} + 5\,(\mathrm{CH_3CO})_2\mathrm{O} \xrightarrow{\text{Pyridine}} \mathrm{CHO-(\!CHOCOCH_3\!)}_4\mathrm{-CH_2OCOCH_3} + 5\,\mathrm{CH_3COOH}
$$

   *Evaluation Marker:* Confirms 5 $-OH$ groups on different carbon atoms. [1 Mark]
2. Prolonged heating of glucose with $\mathrm{HI}$ and red phosphorus yields n-hexane:
   

$$
\mathrm{CHO-(\!CHOH\!)}_4\mathrm{-CH_2OH} + 14\,\mathrm{HI} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{Red }\mathrm{P}} \mathrm{CH_3-(CH_2)_4-CH_3} + 7\,\mathrm{I}_2 + 6\,\mathrm{H_2O}
$$

   *Evaluation Marker:* Confirms an unbranched six-carbon straight chain. [1 Mark]

**(b)** In glucose pentaacetate, the ring cannot open into an open-chain structure because the $-OH$ group at C-1 is locked as an acetate ester ($-\mathrm{OCOCH_3}$). Therefore, it lacks a free aldehyde group ($-\mathrm{CHO}$) at C-1 and does not undergo nucleophilic addition with hydroxylamine ($\mathrm{NH_2OH}$). [1 Mark]

### Question 2 [CBSE Board 2023, 2 Marks]
**(a)** What are anomers? Give one example.  
**(b)** Fructose is a ketohexose, yet it reduces Fehling’s solution and Tollen's reagent. Explain why.

**Complete Step-by-Step Solution:**
**(a)** **Anomers** are cyclic stereoisomers (diastereomers) of sugars that differ only in the spatial configuration around the anomeric carbon (C-1 in aldoses, C-2 in ketoses).  
*Example:* $\alpha\text{-D-glucopyranose}$ and $\beta\text{-D-glucopyranose}$. [1 Mark]

**(b)** In an alkaline medium (such as Fehling’s or Tollen’s reagent), fructose undergoes base-catalyzed keto-enol tautomerism via an enediol intermediate (Lobry de Bruyn–Alberda van Ekenstein transformation) to isomerize into aldoses (glucose and mannose), which have free $-\mathrm{CHO}$ groups capable of reducing the reagents. [1 Mark]

### Question 3 [CBSE Board 2022 Term-2, 2 Marks]
Differentiate between the following:
1. $\mathrm{D}\text{-series}$ and $\mathrm{L}\text{-series}$ sugars.
2. Reducing and Non-reducing sugars.

**Complete Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. **$\mathrm{D}$- and $\mathrm{L}$-Sugars:** Monosaccharides are assigned $\mathrm{D}$-configuration if the $-OH$ group on the lowest asymmetric (penultimate) carbon atom lies on the **right-hand side** in a standard Fischer projection. If it lies on the **left-hand side**, it is assigned $\mathrm{L}$-configuration. [1 Mark]
2. **Reducing vs Non-reducing Sugars:** Reducing sugars possess a free aldehydic or ketonic carbonyl carbon (free hemiacetal/hemiketal) and reduce Tollen's/Fehling's reagents (e.g., Maltose, Glucose). Non-reducing sugars have their anomeric carbonyl groups tied up in glycosidic bonds and cannot reduce these reagents (e.g., Sucrose). [1 Mark]

### Question 4 [CBSE Board 2021 Compartment, 3 Marks]
What happens when D-glucose is treated with:
1. Bromine water ($\mathrm{Br_2}/\mathrm{H_2O}$)
2. Concentrated $\mathrm{HNO}_3$
3. Hydrogen Cyanide ($\mathrm{HCN}$)  
Write balanced chemical reactions for each.

**Complete Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. **With Bromine water:** Mild oxidation selectively oxidizes the $-\mathrm{CHO}$ group to a carboxyl group, forming **Gluconic acid**:
   

$$
\mathrm{CHO-(\!CHOH\!)}_4\mathrm{-CH_2OH} + [\mathrm{O}] \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Br_2/H_2O}} \mathrm{COOH-(\!CHOH\!)}_4\mathrm{-CH_2OH}
$$

 [1 Mark]
2. **With conc. $\mathrm{HNO}_3$:** Strong oxidation simultaneously oxidizes both the terminal $-\mathrm{CHO}$ group (C-1) and the primary alcohol $-\mathrm{CH_2OH}$ group (C-6) to form **Saccharic acid** (Glucaric acid):
   

$$
\mathrm{CHO-(\!CHOH\!)}_4\mathrm{-CH_2OH} + 3[\mathrm{O}] \xrightarrow{\text{conc. }\mathrm{HNO}_3} \mathrm{COOH-(\!CHOH\!)}_4\mathrm{-COOH} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

 [1 Mark]
3. **With $\mathrm{HCN}$:** Nucleophilic addition to the carbonyl group yields **Glucose cyanohydrin**:
   

$$
\mathrm{CHO-(\!CHOH\!)}_4\mathrm{-CH_2OH} + \mathrm{HCN} \rightarrow \mathrm{CH(OH)(CN)-(\!CHOH\!)}_4\mathrm{-CH_2OH}
$$

 [1 Mark]

# 6. Examiner Traps & Common Board Pitfalls

| Topic / Reaction | Common Student Mistake | Accurate NCERT Standard / Examiner Correction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Oxidation with $\mathrm{Br_2}/\mathrm{H_2O}$ vs $\mathrm{HNO}_3$** | Writing Saccharic acid for $\mathrm{Br_2}/\mathrm{H_2O}$ oxidation. | $\mathrm{Br_2}/\mathrm{H_2O}$ is a **mild** oxidant: oxidizes **only** C-1 $-\mathrm{CHO}$ to $-\mathrm{COOH}$ (yields **Gluconic acid**). Strong $\mathrm{HNO}_3$ oxidizes **both** C-1 and C-6 to form dicarboxylic **Saccharic acid**. |
| **D/L vs (+)/(-)** | Assuming '$\mathrm{D}$' means dextrorotatory and '$\mathrm{L}$' means laevorotatory. | $\mathrm{D}/\mathrm{L}$ describes the absolute configuration at the lowest asymmetric carbon relative to glyceraldehyde. $(+)/(-)$ represents the experimentally determined direction of optical rotation. Fructose is $\mathrm{D}\text{-(-)-fructose}$ ($\mathrm{D}$-configuration, but laevorotatory). |
| **Pentaacetate Oxime Absence** | Stating "all $-OH$ groups are protected so it cannot form oxime." | Oxime formation occurs at the **carbonyl group**, not at $-OH$ groups! The correct explanation: acetylation locks the hemiacetal hydroxyl at C-1, preventing the pyranose ring from opening to regenerate the free $-\mathrm{CHO}$ group. |
| **Anomeric Carbon Identity** | Calling C-5 the anomeric carbon. | The anomeric carbon is the original carbonyl carbon: **C-1** in aldoses (glucose) and **C-2** in ketoses (fructose). C-5 determines $\mathrm{D}/\mathrm{L}$ assignment. |
| **Haworth C-3 Position** | Placing the C-3 $-OH$ group down. | In D-glucose, the $-OH$ at **C-3 is UP** in Haworth projection (LEFT in Fischer projection). Mnemonic: **GLUCOSE = Down-Down-Up-Down** for $-OH$ groups at C-1, C-2, C-3, C-4 in the $\alpha$-anomer. |

# 7. Speed Hacks & Memory Mnemonics

### 1. Fischer Projection of D-Glucose (C-2 to C-5 $-OH$ orientations)

$$
"R - L - R - R" (Right - Left - Right - Right)
$$

- **C-2:** Right
- **C-3:** Left
- **C-4:** Right
- **C-5:** Right

***

### 2. Haworth Projection of $\alpha$-D-Glucopyranose (C-1 to C-4 $-OH$ groups)

$$
"D - D - U - D" (Down - Down - Up - Down)
$$

- **C-1 ($\alpha$):** Down
- **C-2:** Down
- **C-3:** Up
- **C-4:** Down
- *(For $\beta$-D-glucopyranose, C-1 flips to UP $\rightarrow$ **"U - D - U - D"**).*

***

### 3. Anomer Mnemonic
- **$\alpha$ (alpha):** $-\mathrm{OH}$ points **"Away"** / **"down"** relative to the $-\mathrm{CH_2OH}$ group (trans).
- **$\beta$ (beta):** $-\mathrm{OH}$ is **"Better friends"** / **"up"** on the same side as $-\mathrm{CH_2OH}$ (cis).
