#### Question 6
**[CBSE 2023, 1 Mark]** | Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**Assertion (A):** All naturally occurring $\alpha\text{-amino acids}$ except glycine are optically active.  
**Reason (R):** Most naturally occurring amino acids have L-configuration.  
(a) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).  
(b) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).  
(c) (A) is true, but (R) is false.  
(d) (A) is false, but (R) is true.

All other amino acids possess an asymmetric $\alpha\text{-carbon}$ atom. The correct explanation for optical activity is chirality/asymmetric carbon, not the L-configuration convention.  
- **Marking Breakdown:**  
  - Correct choice: $[1\text{ Mark}]$

#### Question 9
**[CBSE 2024, 2 Marks]** | Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]  
**Write the chemical equations when D-glucose reacts with:**  
**(i) Bromine water ($\text{Br}_2/\text{H}_2\text{O}$)**  
**(ii) Concentrated Nitric acid ($\text{conc. HNO}_3$)**  

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**  
1. **Reaction with Bromine water:**  
   

$$
\begin{matrix} \text{CHO} \\ | \\ (\text{CHOH})_4 \\ | \\ \text{CH}_2\text{OH} \end{matrix} + \text{Br}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \begin{matrix} \text{COOH} \\ | \\ (\text{CHOH})_4 \\ | \\ \text{CH}_2\text{OH} \end{matrix} + 2\text{HBr}
$$

  
   *(Conversion of aldehydic group to carboxylic acid group forming Gluconic acid)*  

2. **Reaction with conc. $\text{HNO}_3$:**  
   

$$
\begin{matrix} \text{CHO} \\ | \\ (\text{CHOH})_4 \\ | \\ \text{CH}_2\text{OH} \end{matrix} \xrightarrow{[\text{O}]/\text{conc. HNO}_3} \begin{matrix} \text{COOH} \\ | \\ (\text{CHOH})_4 \\ | \\ \text{COOH} \end{matrix}
$$

  
   *(Oxidation of both terminal $-\text{CHO}$ and primary alcoholic $-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$ group forming Saccharic acid / Glucaric acid)*  

- **Marking Breakdown:**  
  - Correct equation & product name for (i) (Gluconic acid): $[1\text{ Mark}]$  
  - Correct equation & product name for (ii) (Saccharic acid): $[1\text{ Mark}]$

#### Question 11
**[CBSE 2022 Term-2, 2 Marks]** | Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**Define the following terms:**  
**(i) Invert sugar**  
**(ii) Anomers**  

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**  
- **(i) Invert Sugar:**  
  Sucrose is dextrorotatory ($[\alpha]_D = +66.5^\circ$). Upon hydrolysis with dilute acid or invertase enzyme, it gives an equimolar mixture of $\text{D-(+)-glucose}$ ($+52.5^\circ$) and $\text{D-(-)-fructose}$ ($-92.4^\circ$). Because the negative rotation of fructose is greater than the positive rotation of glucose, the resulting mixture becomes levorotatory. The product mixture is known as **invert sugar**.  

- **(ii) Anomers:**  
  Pairs of cyclic diastereomers of monosaccharides that differ in stereochemical configuration *only* at the carbonyl/hemiacetal carbon atom ($C_1$ in aldoses or $C_2$ in ketoses). This reference carbon is called the **anomeric carbon** (e.g., $\alpha\text{-D-glucopyranose}$ and $\beta\text{-D-glucopyranose}$).  

- **Marking Breakdown:**  
  - Correct definition and optical explanation of invert sugar: $[1\text{ Mark}]$  
  - Definition of anomers with explicit reference to $C_1/C_2$ carbon: $[1\text{ Mark}]$

#### Question 13
**[CBSE 2024 Delhi Set-1, 3 Marks]** | Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]  
**(a) Write the chemical evidence to prove that glucose contains:**  
**(i) A straight chain of six carbon atoms.**  
**(ii) An aldehyde group as the carbonyl carbon.**  
**(b) Why does glucose not give 2,4-DNP test or Schiff's test despite having a free aldehyde group in open-chain structure?**  

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**  
- **(a) Chemical Evidence:**  
  1. **Straight chain of 6 carbons:** Heating glucose with hydroiodic acid ($\text{HI}$) and red phosphorus for a prolonged time yields n-hexane:  
     

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{HI, Red P}} \text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_3 \quad (\text{n-hexane})
$$

  
     *(Confirms all 6 carbons are linked in an unbranched chain).* $[1\text{ Mark}]$  

  2. **Presence of aldehyde group:** Mild oxidation of glucose with bromine water ($\text{Br}_2/\text{H}_2\text{O}$) converts it to gluconic acid (a six-carbon monocarboxylic acid). Since $\text{Br}_2$ water oxidizes $-\text{CHO}$ but not ketones, it confirms the presence of an aldehydic carbonyl group. $[1\text{ Mark}]$  

- **(b) Explanation for negative Schiff's / 2,4-DNP tests:**  
  Glucose exists predominantly ($>99\%$) in stable cyclic six-membered ring structures (hemiacetal pyranose forms: $\alpha$ and $\beta$). The $-\text{CHO}$ group at $C_1$ is involved in forming an intramolecular hemiacetal linkage with the $-\text{OH}$ group at $C_5$. Because no free $-\text{CHO}$ group exists in the cyclic hemiacetal form, glucose fails to react with mild reagents like Schiff's reagent, $\text{NaHSO}_3$, and 2,4-DNP. $[1\text{ Mark}]$  

- **Marking Breakdown:**  
  - Reaction with $\text{HI}$ yielding n-hexane: $[1\text{ Mark}]$  
  - Reaction with bromine water to gluconic acid: $[1\text{ Mark}]$  
  - Explanation involving cyclic hemiacetal structure: $[1\text{ Mark}]$

#### Question 14
**[CBSE 2023, 3 Marks]** | Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]  
**(a) What is the effect of denaturation on the structure and biological activity of proteins?**  
**(b) Define peptide linkage with the help of a general chemical equation.**  
**(c) Name one water-soluble vitamin that cannot be stored in the human body and must be supplied regularly.**  

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**  
- **(a) Effect of Denaturation:**  
  When a protein in its native state is subjected to physical change (temperature change) or chemical change ($\text{pH}$ variation), the hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic interactions are disrupted. Globules unfold and helices get uncoiled.  
  - **Structural effect:** Secondary ($2^\circ$) and tertiary ($3^\circ$) structures are completely destroyed, while the primary ($1^\circ$) structure (covalent peptide sequence) remains intact.  
  - **Biological effect:** The protein precipitates or coagulates and loses its specific biological activity. $[1\text{ Mark}]$  

- **(b) Peptide Linkage:**  
  A peptide linkage is an amide bond ($-\text{CO}-\text{NH}-$) formed between the carboxyl group ($-\text{COOH}$) of one $\alpha\text{-amino acid}$ and the amino group ($-\text{NH}_2$) of an adjacent $\alpha\text{-amino acid}$ with the elimination of a water molecule:  
  

$$
\text{H}_2\text{N}-\text{CH}(R_1)-\text{COOH} + \text{H}_2\text{N}-\text{CH}(R_2)-\text{COOH} \xrightarrow{-\text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{H}_2\text{N}-\text{CH}(R_1)-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{NH}-\text{CH}(R_2)-\text{COOH}
$$

  
  The $-\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}{\text{C}}-\text{NH}-$ bond is the **peptide linkage**. $[1\text{ Mark}]$  

- **(c) Water-soluble Vitamin:**  
  **Vitamin C** (ascorbic acid) or any member of **Vitamin B-complex** (except Vitamin $\text{B}_{12}$). Because they are water-soluble, they are readily excreted in urine and cannot be stored in large quantities in the body. $[1\text{ Mark}]$  

- **Marking Breakdown:**  
  - Mentioning loss of $2^\circ, 3^\circ$ structures while $1^\circ$ remains intact + biological inactivation: $[1\text{ Mark}]$  
  - Definition and chemical equation for peptide linkage: $[1\text{ Mark}]$  
  - Correct vitamin identified with reason: $[1\text{ Mark}]$

#### Question 15
**[CBSE 2020 All India, 3 Marks]** | Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**(a) Explain what is meant by the secondary structure of proteins. Differentiate between $\alpha\text{-helix}$ and $\beta\text{-pleated sheet}$ structures.**  
**(b) What are essential and non-essential amino acids? Give one example of each.**  

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**  
- **(a) Secondary Structure of Proteins:**  
  The secondary structure refers to the local conformational shape in which a long polypeptide chain exists, stabilized by hydrogen bonding between the $>\text{C}=\text{O}$ and $-\text{NH}-$ groups of the peptide backbone.  
  - **$\alpha\text{-Helix}$:** Polypeptide chain coils into a right-handed screw structure where each $-\text{NH}-$ group forms an intramolecular hydrogen bond with the $>\text{C}=\text{O}$ group of the 4th amino acid residue further along the chain.  
  - **$\beta\text{-Pleated Sheet}$:** All peptide chains are stretched out to nearly maximum extension and laid side-by-side, held together by intermolecular hydrogen bonds forming a pleated sheet structure. $[1\frac{1}{2}\text{ Marks}]$  

- **(b) Essential vs Non-essential Amino Acids:**  
  - **Essential Amino Acids:** Amino acids that cannot be synthesized in the human body and must be supplied through the diet.  
    *Example:* Valine, Leucine, Lysine.  
  - **Non-essential Amino Acids:** Amino acids that can be synthesized by the human body from metabolic intermediates.  
    *Example:* Glycine, Alanine, Glutamic acid. $[1\frac{1}{2}\text{ Marks}]$  

- **Marking Breakdown:**  
  - Secondary structure definition + clear difference between $\alpha\text{-helix}$ and $\beta\text{-pleated sheet}$: $[1\frac{1}{2}\text{ Marks}]$  
  - Definition of essential and non-essential amino acids with one example each: $[1\frac{1}{2}\text{ Marks}]$

#### Question 16
**[CBSE 2024 Foreign & Delhi Model, 4 Marks]** | Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]  
**Read the passage below and answer the following questions:**

> Nucleic acids are biopolymers essential for all forms of life. Deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) and Ribonucleic acid (RNA) are polymers made of repeating monomers called nucleotides. Each nucleotide consists of three chemical parts: a nitrogenous heterocyclic base (purines and pyrimidines), a five-carbon pentose sugar, and a phosphoric acid residue. In DNA, the double helix model proposed by Watson and Crick explains replication and transcription. In this structure, the two strands are complementary and antiparallel, stabilized by specific hydrogen bonding between base pairs.

**(i) What type of linkage joins a nitrogenous base to the $C_1'$ position of a pentose sugar to form a nucleoside?** $[1\text{ Mark}]$  
**(ii) Explain why the two strands of a DNA double helix are complementary to each other.** $[1\text{ Mark}]$  
**(iii) [Attempt either subquestion (A) or (B)]**

Write the complementary base sequence on the opposite strand from $3'$ to $5'$ direction and state how many hydrogen bonds form between Adenine and Thymine, and between Guanine and Cytosine.** $[2\text{ Marks}]$

**1. Pentose sugar component**

#### Question 17
**[CBSE 2023, 5 Marks]** | Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]  
**(a) How do you explain the following chemical properties of D-glucose? Write chemical equations where applicable:**  
**(i) Glucose contains five hydroxyl ($-\text{OH}$) groups.**  
**(ii) Glucose contains one primary alcoholic group ($-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$).**  
**(iii) The pentaacetate of glucose does not react with hydroxylamine ($\text{NH}_2\text{OH}$).** $[3\text{ Marks}]$  
**(b) Write the Fischer projection and Haworth cyclic structures for $\alpha\text{-D-(+)-glucopyranose}$.** $[2\text{ Marks}]$  

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**  
- **(a) Chemical Explanations:**  
  1. **Five $-\text{OH}$ groups:**  
     Acetylation of glucose with acetic anhydride in the presence of anhydrous $\text{ZnCl}_2$ or pyridine yields **glucose pentaacetate**, confirming the presence of five $-\text{OH}$ groups attached to five different carbon atoms (as a stable gem-diol is not possible):  
     

$$
\begin{matrix} \text{CHO} \\ | \\ (\text{CHOH})_4 \\ | \\ \text{CH}_2\text{OH} \end{matrix} + 5(\text{CH}_3\text{CO})_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{Pyridine}} \begin{matrix} \text{CHO} \\ | \\ (\text{CHOCOCH}_3)_4 \\ | \\ \text{CH}_2\text{OCOCH}_3 \end{matrix} + 5\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}
$$

  
     $[1\text{ Mark}]$  

  2. **One primary alcoholic group ($-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$):**  
     Oxidation of both glucose and gluconic acid with concentrated nitric acid ($\text{HNO}_3$, a stronger oxidizing agent than $\text{Br}_2$ water) yields the same dicarboxylic acid, **saccharic acid** (glucaric acid). This indicates the presence of a primary alcoholic group ($-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$ at $C_6$):  
     

$$
\begin{matrix} \text{CHO} \\ | \\ (\text{CHOH})_4 \\ | \\ \text{CH}_2\text{OH} \end{matrix} \xrightarrow{\text{conc. HNO}_3} \begin{matrix} \text{COOH} \\ | \\ (\text{CHOH})_4 \\ | \\ \text{COOH} \end{matrix} \quad (\text{Saccharic acid})
$$

  
     $[1\text{ Mark}]$  

  3. **Pentaacetate of glucose does not react with $\text{NH}_2\text{OH}$:**  
     Because glucose pentaacetate cannot revert back to the open-chain form containing a free $-\text{CHO}$ group (as the $-\text{OH}$ at $C_1$ is converted to an acetate ester $-\text{OCOCH}_3$, locking the cyclic hemiacetal ring), it cannot undergo nucleophilic addition with hydroxylamine. This proves glucose exists in a cyclic hemiacetal form involving the $C_1-\text{OH}$. $[1\text{ Mark}]$  

- **(b) Structures of $\alpha\text{-D-(+)-glucopyranose}$:**  
  - **Fischer Projection (Open to cyclic representation):**  
    $C_1$ has $-\text{H}$ on left and $-\text{OH}$ on right; oxygen bridge connects $C_1$ and $C_5$:  
    - $C_1$: $\text{H}-\text{C}-\text{OH}$  
    - $C_2$: $\text{H}-\text{C}-\text{OH}$  
    - $C_3$: $\text{HO}-\text{C}-\text{H}$  
    - $C_4$: $\text{H}-\text{C}-\text{OH}$  
    - $C_5$: $\text{H}-\text{C}-$ (bridged to $C_1$ via $-\text{O}-$)  
    - $C_6$: $\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$  
    $[1\text{ Mark}]$  

  - **Haworth Pyranose Ring Projection:**  
    Six-membered pyranose ring with heterocyclic oxygen at top-right corner:  
    - $C_1$: $-\text{H}$ (up), $-\text{OH}$ (down)  
    - $C_2$: $-\text{H}$ (up), $-\text{OH}$ (down)  
    - $C_3$: $-\text{OH}$ (up), $-\text{H}$ (down)  
    - $C_4$: $-\text{H}$ (up), $-\text{OH}$ (down)  
    - $C_5$: $-\text{H}$ (down), $-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$ (up at position 6)  
    *(In $\alpha\text{-D-glucose}$, the anomeric $-\text{OH}$ at $C_1$ is positioned downwards / trans to the $-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$ group at $C_5$).* $[1\text{ Mark}]$  

- **Marking Breakdown:**  
  - Part (a)(i), (ii), (iii): $1$ mark each $[3\text{ Marks}]$  
  - Part (b): Fischer cyclic representation $[1\text{ Mark}]$, Haworth pyranose diagram $[1\text{ Mark}]$

#### Question 18
**[CBSE 2022 Term-2 / 2025 Compartment Predictor, 5 Marks]** | Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]  
**(a) What are vitamins? How are they classified into two main groups based on their solubility? Name two vitamins belonging to each group.** $[2\frac{1}{2}\text{ Marks}]$  
**(b) Mention one disease caused by the deficiency of each of the following vitamins:**  
**(i) Vitamin A**  
**(ii) Vitamin D**  
**(iii) Vitamin K** $[1\frac{1}{2}\text{ Marks}]$  
**(c) What happens when protein undergoes denaturation? Is denaturation reversible or irreversible? Give one exception.** $[1\text{ Mark}]$  

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**  
- **(a) Definition & Classification of Vitamins:**  
  Vitamins are organic compounds required in small dietary amounts to perform specific biological functions for the normal maintenance of optimum health, growth, and reproduction of an organism.  
  - **Fat-soluble Vitamins:** Vitamins that are soluble in fats and oils but insoluble in water. They are stored in the liver and adipose (fat-storing) tissues.  
    *Examples:* **Vitamin A, D, E, and K.**  
  - **Water-soluble Vitamins:** Vitamins that are soluble in water. They cannot be stored in the body in large amounts (except $\text{B}_{12}$) and must be supplied regularly in the diet as they are readily excreted in urine.  
    *Examples:* **Vitamin B-complex ($\text{B}_1, \text{B}_2, \text{B}_6, \text{B}_{12}$), Vitamin C.**  
  - **Marks Breakdown:** Definition ($\frac{1}{2}$ mark), Fat-soluble description + 2 examples ($1$ mark), Water-soluble description + 2 examples ($1$ mark). $[2\frac{1}{2}\text{ Marks}]$  

- **(b) Deficiency Diseases:**  
  - **(i) Vitamin A:** Xerophthalmia (hardening of the cornea of the eye) or Night blindness. $[\frac{1}{2}\text{ Mark}]$  
  - **(ii) Vitamin D:** Rickets (bone deformities in children) or Osteomalacia (soft/brittle bones in adults). $[\frac{1}{2}\text{ Mark}]$  
  - **(iii) Vitamin K:** Increased blood clotting time / prolonged bleeding / haemorrhage. $[\frac{1}{2}\text{ Mark}]$  

- **(c) Denaturation Dynamics:**  
  During denaturation, the $2^\circ$ and $3^\circ$ structures unravel due to disruption of non-covalent bonds, leading to loss of biological function while $1^\circ$ structure stays intact.  
  Denaturation is generally **irreversible** (e.g., coagulation of egg white on boiling; curdling of milk).  
  *Exception/Reversibility:* In certain mild conditions, some proteins can undergo **renaturation** upon removal of the denaturing agent (e.g., ribonuclease enzyme regains its shape and catalytic function when urea/mercaptoethanol is removed). $[1\text{ Mark}]$
