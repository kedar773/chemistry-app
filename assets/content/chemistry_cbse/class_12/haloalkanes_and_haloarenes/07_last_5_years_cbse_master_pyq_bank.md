### Q1. [CBSE 2024 (Delhi), 1 Mark] | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Question:**  
Which of the following alkyl halides undergoes $\text{S}_\text{N}1$ reaction most rapidly?  
(A) $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{Br}$  
(B) $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CHBr}$  
(C) $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{CBr}$  
(D) $\text{CH}_3\text{Br}$

**Model Answer / Marking Scheme:**  
- **Correct Option:** **(C)** $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{CBr}$ `[1/2 Mark]`  
- **Explanation:** The rate-determining step in an $\text{S}_\text{N}1$ mechanism involves the formation of a carbocation intermediate. The tertiary carbocation $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}^+$ formed from 2-bromo-2-methylpropane is exceptionally stabilized by $+I$ inductive effects and 9 hyperconjugative $\alpha\text{-H}$ structures:

$$
\text{Stability: } 3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ > \text{CH}_3^+
$$

Hence, $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{CBr}$ reacts fastest. `[1/2 Mark]`

### Q2. [CBSE 2024 (All India), 1 Mark] | ⭐⭐⭐⭐
**Question:**  
A hydrocarbon $\text{C}_5\text{H}_{12}$ gives only one monochloro derivative upon photochemical chlorination. The hydrocarbon is:  
(A) $n$-pentane  
(B) Isopentane  
(C) Neopentane  
(D) Cyclopentane

**Model Answer / Marking Scheme:**  
- **Correct Option:** **(C)** Neopentane (2,2-dimethylpropane) `[1/2 Mark]`  
- **Explanation:** Neopentane, $\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)_4$, possesses 12 chemically and symmetrically equivalent primary hydrogen atoms. Chlorination at any methyl group yields an identical product, 1-chloro-2,2-dimethylpropane:

$$
\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)_4 + \text{Cl}_2 \xrightarrow{h\nu} (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}-\text{CH}_2\text{Cl} + \text{HCl}
$$

`[1/2 Mark]`

### Q3. [CBSE 2023 (Delhi), 1 Mark] | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Question:**  
The reaction of methyl bromide with aqueous sodium hydroxide involves:  
(A) Retention of configuration via carbocation  
(B) Inversion of configuration via single-step bimolecular nucleophilic substitution  
(C) Racemisation via planar intermediate  
(D) Electrophilic addition  

**Model Answer / Marking Scheme:**  
- **Correct Option:** **(B)** Inversion of configuration via single-step bimolecular nucleophilic substitution `[1/2 Mark]`  
- **Explanation:** Methyl bromide ($\text{CH}_3\text{Br}$) is a primary alkyl halide possessing negligible steric hindrance; it proceeds through a concerted $\text{S}_\text{N}2$ mechanism where hydroxide attacks from the backside directly opposite to the leaving bromide, resulting in complete Walden inversion. `[1/2 Mark]`

### Q7. [CBSE 2024 (Foreign), 1 Mark] | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Assertion-Reason Question:**  
Given below are two statements labeled as Assertion (A) and Reason (R):  
- **Assertion (A):** The boiling points of alkyl halides follow the order: $\text{R-I} > \text{R-Br} > \text{R-Cl} > \text{R-F}$.  
- **Reason (R):** With the increase in size and mass of halogen atom, the magnitude of van der Waals forces increases.  

Select the correct answer from the following choices:  
(A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).  
(B) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).  
(C) (A) is true but (R) is false.  
(D) (A) is false but (R) is true.  

**Model Answer / Marking Scheme:**  
- **Correct Option:** **(A)** `[1/2 Mark]`  
- **Explanation:** Van der Waals dispersion forces are directly proportional to the molecular mass and polarizability of the electron cloud. As halogen size increases ($\text{F} < \text{Cl} < \text{Br} < \text{I}$), intermolecular forces strengthen, requiring higher thermal energy to overcome the liquid state. `[1/2 Mark]`

### Q8. [CBSE 2023 (All India), 1 Mark] | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Assertion-Reason Question:**  
- **Assertion (A):** Haloarenes are far less reactive towards nucleophilic substitution reactions than haloalkanes.  
- **Reason (R):** In haloarenes, the $\text{C}-\text{X}$ bond possesses partial double bond character due to resonance stabilization.  

**Model Answer / Marking Scheme:**  
- **Correct Option:** **(A)** `[1/2 Mark]`  
- **Explanation:** The lone pair of electrons on halogen undergoes delocalization with $\pi$-electrons of the benzene ring ($+R$ effect), conferring partial double-bond character to the $\text{C}-\text{X}$ bond and shortening its length ($169\text{ pm}$ vs $178\text{ pm}$ in alkyl chloride), which makes bond cleavage difficult. `[1/2 Mark]`

### Q9. [CBSE 2025 Sample Paper, 1 Mark] | ⭐⭐⭐⭐
**Assertion-Reason Question:**  
- **Assertion (A):** Alkyl iodides darken on standing in presence of light.  
- **Reason (R):** Alkyl iodides are photochemically cleaved into iodine ($\text{I}_2$).  

**Model Answer / Marking Scheme:**  
- **Correct Option:** **(A)** `[1/2 Mark]`

$$
2\text{R-I} \xrightarrow{h\nu} \text{R-R} + \text{I}_2
$$

The released $\text{I}_2$ imparts a brown or violet-brown colour to the liquid. `[1/2 Mark]`

### Q11. [CBSE 2024 (Delhi), 2 Marks] | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Question:**  
Give reasons for the following:  
(a) The dipole moment of chlorobenzene ($\mu = 1.69\text{ D}$) is lower than that of cyclohexyl chloride ($\mu = 1.83\text{ D}$).  
(b) Alkyl halides, though polar, are practically immiscible with water.  

**Model Answer / Marking Scheme:**  
- **(a)**  
  1. In chlorobenzene, the $\text{C}$ atom attached to $\text{Cl}$ is $\text{sp}^2$ hybridised with higher $s$-character ($33.3\%$), making it more electronegative and less electron-donating towards $\text{Cl}$ than the $\text{sp}^3$ hybridised $\text{C}$ ($25\%\; s\text{-character}$) in cyclohexyl chloride.  
  2. The $+R$ resonance effect causes a partial double-bond character in $\text{C}-\text{Cl}$ of chlorobenzene, reducing the bond length and the dipole moment ($\mu = q \times d$). `[1 Mark]`  
- **(b)** For an alkyl halide to dissolve in water, energy is required to break dipole-dipole interactions among alkyl halide molecules and hydrogen bonds among water molecules. The new attractive forces established between alkyl halide and water molecules are weaker than the existing hydrogen bonds in water; thus, the dissolution process is energetically unfavorable. `[1 Mark]`

### Q12. [CBSE 2024 (All India), 2 Marks] | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Question:**  
Write chemical equations for the following named conversions:  
(a) Finkelstein Reaction  
(b) Swarts Reaction  

**Model Answer / Marking Scheme:**  
- **(a) Finkelstein Reaction:**  
Alkyl chlorides or bromides react with sodium iodide in dry acetone to form alkyl iodides:

$$
\text{R}-\text{Cl} + \text{NaI} \xrightarrow{\text{dry acetone}} \text{R}-\text{I} + \text{NaCl}\downarrow
$$

*(or with $\text{R}-\text{Br}$, $\text{NaCl}/\text{NaBr}$ precipitates out, driving the equilibrium forward)* `[1 Mark]`  

- **(b) Swarts Reaction:**  
Heating alkyl bromides or chlorides in the presence of heavy metallic fluorides ($\text{AgF}$, $\text{Hg}_2\text{F}_2$, $\text{CoF}_3$, or $\text{SbF}_3$) produces alkyl fluorides:

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{Br} + \text{AgF} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{CH}_3\text{F} + \text{AgBr}\downarrow
$$

`[1 Mark]`

### Q13. [CBSE 2023 (Delhi), 2 Marks] | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Question:**  
Explain why:  
(a) Haloalkanes react with $\text{KCN}$ to give alkyl cyanides as main product, whereas with $\text{AgCN}$ they form alkyl isocyanides as the major product.  
(b) Grignard reagents must be prepared under strictly anhydrous conditions.  

**Model Answer / Marking Scheme:**  
- **(a)**  
  - $\text{KCN}$ is predominantly ionic in nature and provides cyanide ions ($[:\text{C}\equiv\text{N}:]^-$), an ambident nucleophile. Attack occurs through carbon because the $\text{C}-\text{C}$ bond is thermodynamically more stable than the $\text{C}-\text{N}$ bond. `[1/2 Mark]`  
  - $\text{AgCN}$ is predominantly covalent; only the unshared electron pair on the nitrogen atom is readily available for nucleophilic attack, yielding isocyanides ($\text{R}-\text{N}\equiv\text{C}$). `[1/2 Mark]`  
- **(b)** Grignard reagents ($\text{R-MgX}$) are strong bases and carbanion sources. They react instantly with any proton donor (water, alcohol, or amines) to form hydrocarbons:

$$
\text{R}-\text{MgX} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{R}-\text{H} + \text{Mg(OH)X}
$$

Thus, even traces of moisture destroy the reagent. `[1 Mark]`

### Q14. [CBSE 2023 (Foreign), 2 Marks] | ⭐⭐⭐⭐
**Question:**  
Out of the following pairs, which compound will react faster in an $\text{S}_\text{N}2$ reaction with $\text{OH}^-$? Give a reason for each:  
(a) $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{Br}$ or $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}-\text{CH}_2\text{Br}$  
(b) $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{Br}$ or $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{I}$  

**Model Answer / Marking Scheme:**  
- **(a)** $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{Br}$ reacts faster.  
  *Reason:* $\text{S}_\text{N}2$ reaction rate is governed by steric hindrance around the reaction center. Neopentyl bromide, $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}-\text{CH}_2\text{Br}$, has a bulky tert-butyl group adjacent to the $\alpha$-carbon, which impedes backside attack by the nucleophile. `[1 Mark]`  
- **(b)** $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{I}$ reacts faster.  
  *Reason:* Iodide ion ($\text{I}^-$) is a better leaving group than bromide ion ($\text{Br}^-$) due to its larger ionic size, lower base strength, and lower $\text{C}-\text{I}$ bond dissociation energy ($234\text{ kJ/mol}$ vs $293\text{ kJ/mol}$ for $\text{C}-\text{Br}$). `[1 Mark]`

### Q15. [CBSE 2022 Term-2, 2 Marks] | ⭐⭐⭐⭐
**Question:**  
Predict the major alkene formed in each of the following $\beta$-elimination reactions:  
(a) 2-Bromopentane treated with alcoholic $\text{KOH}$.  
(b) 1-Bromo-1-methylcyclohexane treated with sodium ethoxide in ethanol.  

**Model Answer / Marking Scheme:**  
- **(a)** According to Zaitsev's (Saytzeff's) rule, the preferred alkene is the one with the greater number of alkyl substituents attached to the double-bonded carbon atoms.  

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}(\text{Br})-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{alc. KOH, }\Delta} \text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}=\text{CH}-\text{CH}_3 \text{ (Pent-2-ene, major, 81\%)}
$$

`[1 Mark]`  
- **(b)** Elimination of $\text{HBr}$ yields the more substituted, endocyclic double-bond isomer:  

$$
\text{1-bromo-1-methylcyclohexane} \xrightarrow{\text{NaOEt/EtOH, }\Delta} \text{1-methylcyclohex-1-ene (Major)}
$$

`[1 Mark]`

### Q17. [CBSE 2024 (Delhi), 3 Marks] | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Question:**  
An organic compound (A) with molecular formula $\text{C}_4\text{H}_9\text{Br}$ on treatment with $\text{KOH}(\text{alc.})$ gives compound (B). Compound (B) reacts with $\text{HBr}$ in the presence of organic peroxide to give compound (C), which is an isomer of (A). When (A) is reacted with metallic sodium in dry ether, it gives 2,5-dimethylhexane.  
Identify (A), (B), and (C). Write the chemical equations involved.  

**Model Answer / Marking Scheme:**  
- **Identification:**  
  1. (A) gives 2,5-dimethylhexane via Wurtz reaction:
  

$$
\text{Structure of 2,5-dimethylhexane: } (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CH}-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}(\text{CH}_3)_2
$$

  Splitting this symmetric dimer across the central bond gives the isobutyl radical. Therefore, compound (A) is **1-bromo-2-methylpropane** (isobutyl bromide): $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CH}-\text{CH}_2\text{Br}$. `[1/2 Mark]`  
  2. Elimination of (A) gives (B):
  

$$
(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CH}-\text{CH}_2\text{Br} \xrightarrow{\text{alc. KOH, }\Delta} (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{C}=\text{CH}_2 \text{ (2-Methylprop-1-ene)}
$$

  Therefore, (B) is **2-methylprop-1-ene**. `[1/2 Mark]`  
  3. Anti-Markovnikov addition of $\text{HBr}$ in the presence of peroxide to (B):
  

$$
(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{C}=\text{CH}_2 + \text{HBr} \xrightarrow{\text{Peroxide}} (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CH}-\text{CH}_2\text{Br} \text{ (Compound A)}
$$

  *Wait! Let us re-verify (A) vs its secondary isomer:*  
  If (A) is 2-bromobutane $\rightarrow$ Wurtz yields 3,4-dimethylhexane.  
  If (A) is 1-bromo-2-methylpropane $\rightarrow$ Wurtz yields 2,5-dimethylhexane:
  

$$
2(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CHCH}_2\text{Br} + 2\text{Na} \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CHCH}_2-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}(\text{CH}_3)_2 + 2\text{NaBr}
$$

  Equation verified.  
  Then dehydrohalogenation gives isobutylene (B).  
  When (B) reacts with $\text{HBr}$ in the absence of peroxide, it yields tert-butyl bromide; in the presence of peroxide, it yields isobutyl bromide.  
  Where (C) is an isomer of (A), when (A) is treated with alc $\text{KOH}$, 2-methylprop-1-ene forms.  
  Alternatively, if (A) is 2-bromo-2-methylpropane, it does not give 2,5-dimethylhexane.  
  Thus:
  - Compound (A) = **1-bromo-2-methylpropane** `[1/2 Mark]`
  - Compound (B) = **2-methylprop-1-ene** `[1/2 Mark]`
  - Compound (C) = **1-bromo-2-methylpropane** (or 2-bromo-2-methylpropane if Markovnikov product was stated).  
- **Marking Distribution for Chemical Equations:**  
  - Wurtz equation: `[1/2 Mark]`  
  - Elimination reaction: `[1/2 Mark]`

### Q18. [CBSE 2024 (All India), 3 Marks] | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Question:**  
Account for the following observations:  
(a) The presence of a nitro ($-\text{NO}_2$) group at the ortho- and para-positions increases the reactivity of haloarenes towards nucleophilic substitution, whereas no effect is observed at the meta-position.  
(b) Racemic mixture is optically inactive.  
(c) Chloroform is treated with $1\%$ ethanol before being stored for anaesthetic use.  

**Model Answer / Marking Scheme:**  
- **(a)** Nucleophilic attack on haloarenes generates a carbanion (Meisenheimer complex) stabilized by resonance.  
  - When the $-\text{NO}_2$ group is at ortho- and para-positions, the negative charge is delocalized directly onto the electronegative oxygen atoms of the nitro group via resonance, stabilizing the intermediate. `[1/2 Mark]`  
  - At the meta-position, the negative charge never delocalizes onto the ring carbon bearing the nitro group; hence, the nitro group cannot stabilize the carbanion via its strong $-R$ resonance effect. `[1/2 Mark]`  
- **(b)** A racemic mixture contains equimolar quantities of two enantiomers (dextrorotatory and laevorotatory). The optical rotation caused by the molecules of one enantiomer is canceled by the equal and opposite optical rotation caused by the other enantiomer due to **external compensation**. `[1 Mark]`  
- **(c)** Small amounts ($1\%$) of ethanol convert any traces of poisonous phosgene ($\text{COCl}_2$), formed by aerial oxidation, into non-toxic diethyl carbonate:

$$
\text{COCl}_2 + 2\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \longrightarrow (\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{O})_2\text{C}=\text{O} \text{ (Diethyl carbonate)} + 2\text{HCl}
$$

`[1 Mark]`

### Q19. [CBSE 2023 (All India), 3 Marks] | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Question:**  
Complete and balance the following chemical equations:  
(a) $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{Cu}_2\text{Cl}_2 / \text{HCl} \longrightarrow$  
(b) $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 + \text{HBr} \xrightarrow{\text{Peroxide}}$  
(c) $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} + \text{CH}_3\text{Cl} + 2\text{Na} \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}}$  

**Model Answer / Marking Scheme:**  
- **(a) Sandmeyer Reaction:**

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- \xrightarrow{\text{Cu}_2\text{Cl}_2 / \text{HCl}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} + \text{N}_2\uparrow
$$

Product: Chlorobenzene + Nitrogen gas. `[1 Mark]`  
- **(b) Anti-Markovnikov Addition (Peroxide / Kharasch Effect):**

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 + \text{HBr} \xrightarrow{(\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CO})_2\text{O}_2} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{Br}
$$

Product: 1-Bromobutane. `[1 Mark]`  
- **(c) Wurtz-Fittig Reaction:**

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} + \text{CH}_3\text{Cl} + 2\text{Na} \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{CH}_3 \text{ (Toluene)} + 2\text{NaCl}
$$

Product: Toluene (methylbenzene). `[1 Mark]`

### Q20. [CBSE 2023 (Delhi), 3 Marks] | ⭐⭐⭐⭐
**Question:**  
How will you bring about the following organic transformations? (In not more than two steps):  
(a) Toluene to Benzyl alcohol  
(b) Propene to Propyne  
(c) Ethanol to But-1-yne  

**Model Answer / Marking Scheme:**  
- **(a) Toluene to Benzyl alcohol:**  
  1. Side-chain chlorination using chlorine in the presence of UV light or heat:
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_3 + \text{Cl}_2 \xrightarrow{h\nu / \Delta} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_2\text{Cl} + \text{HCl}
$$

  2. Nucleophilic substitution using aqueous $\text{KOH}$:
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_2\text{Cl} + \text{KOH}(\text{aq}) \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_2\text{OH} + \text{KCl}
$$

  `[1 Mark]`  

- **(b) Propene to Propyne:**  
  1. Bromination with bromine in $\text{CCl}_4$:
  

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 + \text{Br}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{CCl}_4} \text{CH}_3-\text{CH(Br)}-\text{CH}_2\text{Br}
$$

  2. Double dehydrohalogenation using $\text{NaNH}_2$ in liquid ammonia (or alc. $\text{KOH}$ followed by $\text{NaNH}_2$):
  

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH(Br)}-\text{CH}_2\text{Br} \xrightarrow{2\text{NaNH}_2 / \text{liq. NH}_3} \text{CH}_3-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH} + 2\text{NaBr} + 2\text{NH}_3
$$

  `[1 Mark]`  

- **(c) Ethanol to But-1-yne:**  
  1. Halogenation of ethanol with $\text{SOCl}_2$:
  

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH} + \text{SOCl}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Pyridine}} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{Cl} + \text{SO}_2\uparrow + \text{HCl}\uparrow
$$

  2. Reaction with sodium acetylide:
  

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{Cl} + \text{Na}^+ {}^-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH} \longrightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH} + \text{NaCl}
$$

  `[1 Mark]`

### Q21. [CBSE 2021 (Term-1 Exam Pattern Modified), 3 Marks] | ⭐⭐⭐⭐
**Question:**  
(a) Which alkyl halide in each pair is more reactive towards $\text{S}_\text{N}1$ solvolysis?  
    (i) $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_2\text{Cl}$ or $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}(\text{Cl})\text{CH}_3$  
    (ii) $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}-\text{Cl}$ or $(\text{C}_6\text{H}_5)_3\text{C}-\text{Cl}$  
(b) Explain why vinyl chloride ($\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}-\text{Cl}$) is hydrolysed much more slowly than ethyl chloride ($\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{Cl}$).  

**Model Answer / Marking Scheme:**  
- **(a)**  
  (i) $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}(\text{Cl})\text{CH}_3$ reacts faster because it generates a secondary benzylic carbocation $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{HCH}_3$, which is stabilized by both phenyl ring resonance and methyl hyperconjugation/$+I$ effects, compared to the primary benzylic carbocation $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2$. `[1 Mark]`  
  (ii) $(\text{C}_6\text{H}_5)_3\text{C}-\text{Cl}$ reacts faster because the trityl carbocation $[(\text{C}_6\text{H}_5)_3\text{C}]^+$ is stabilized by resonance across three aromatic rings (9 canonical forms). `[1 Mark]`  
- **(b)** In vinyl chloride, the lone pairs on the chlorine atom are delocalized with the carbon-carbon double bond via resonance:

$$
\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}-\ddot{\text{Cl}}: \longleftrightarrow \overset{-}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2-\text{CH}=\overset{+}{\text{Cl}}:
$$

This imparts partial double-bond character to the $\text{C}-\text{Cl}$ bond and makes the carbon $\text{sp}^2$ hybridised, making cleavage difficult relative to the single $\text{C}(\text{sp}^3)-\text{Cl}$ bond in ethyl chloride. `[1 Mark]`

### Q23. [CBSE 2024 Board Examination, 4 Marks] | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Passage:**  
Nucleophilic substitution reactions are classified into two broad kinetic categories: $\text{S}_\text{N}1$ and $\text{S}_\text{N}2$. The $\text{S}_\text{N}2$ mechanism proceeds through a single concerted bimolecular transition state, characterized by backside nucleophilic attack, inversion of configuration (Walden Inversion), and absence of rearrangements. In contrast, the $\text{S}_\text{N}1$ reaction is a two-step unimolecular process where the leaving group departs to generate a planar, $\text{sp}^2$-hybridized intermediate carbocation, which can be attacked by a nucleophile from either face with equal probability, leading to racemisation. The solvent's polarity, leaving group ability, steric congestion around the electrophilic carbon, and nucleophile strength dictate the operative mechanism.

Answer the following questions:  
**(a)** Why does optically active 2-bromobutane yield an optically inactive product mixture when treated with aqueous acetone? `[1 Mark]`  
**(b)** Arrange the isomeric bromobutanes in increasing order of reactivity towards $\text{S}_\text{N}2$ substitution. `[1 Mark]`  
**(c)** [Attempt either sub-part (i) or (ii)]:  
*(i)* Optically active compound (X) with molecular formula $\text{C}_7\text{H}_{15}\text{Br}$ reacts with aqueous alkali by $\text{S}_\text{N}1$ pathway to give an optically inactive alcohol. Draw the structure of (X) and account for the loss of optical activity. `[2 Marks]`  
**OR**  
*(ii)* Allyl chloride ($\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}-\text{CH}_2\text{Cl}$) undergoes hydrolysis much faster than $n$-propyl chloride ($\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{Cl}$) under $\text{S}_\text{N}1$ conditions. Justify using canonical structures. `[2 Marks]`

***

**Model Answer / Marking Scheme:**  
- **(a)** 2-Bromobutane undergoes solvolysis via an $\text{S}_\text{N}1$ mechanism. The intermediate formed is a planar, achiral sec-butyl carbocation $\text{CH}_3\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{HCH}_2\text{CH}_3$. Water molecules attack from either the front face or back face with equal probability, forming an equimolar enantiomeric mixture ($50\%\; (R)\text{-butan-2-ol}$ and $50\%\; (S)\text{-butan-2-ol}$). This racemic mixture is optically inactive due to external compensation. `[1 Mark]`  
- **(b)** $\text{S}_\text{N}2$ reactivity depends inversely on steric congestion around the $\alpha$-carbon:

$$
(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{CBr} < (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CHCH}_2\text{Br} \approx \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH(Br)CH}_3 < \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{Br}
$$

Strict Order:

$$
3^\circ < 2^\circ < 1^\circ \text{ (isobutyl)} < 1^\circ \text{ (n-butyl)}
$$

$$
\text{tert-butyl bromide} < \text{sec-butyl bromide} < \text{isobutyl bromide} < n\text{-butyl bromide}
$$

`[1 Mark]`  
- **(c) (i)**  
  Structure of (X): 3-Bromo-3-methylhexane or an asymmetric tertiary alkyl halide:
  

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\overset{\overset{\text{CH}_3}{\displaystyle|}}{\underset{\underset{\text{Br}}{\displaystyle|}}{\text{C}}}-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_3 \quad \text{or} \quad \text{CH}_3-\overset{\overset{\text{C}_2\text{H}_5}{\displaystyle|}}{\underset{\underset{\text{Br}}{\displaystyle|}}{\text{C}}}-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_3
$$

  *(Notice $\text{C}^*$ has 4 distinct groups: $-\text{CH}_3$, $-\text{C}_2\text{H}_5$, $-\text{C}_3\text{H}_7$, and $-\text{Br}$.)* `[1 Mark]`  
  *Reason for loss of optical activity:* The departure of $\text{Br}^-$ produces a planar, $\text{sp}^2$-hybridised carbocation intermediate with a plane of symmetry. Attack of $\text{OH}^-$ from either face yields an equimolar racemic mixture of enantiomers, destroying optical activity. `[1 Mark]`  
  **OR**  
  **(c) (ii)**  
  Ionization of allyl chloride gives an allylic carbocation that is resonance-stabilized:
  

$$
\big[\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}-\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2 \longleftrightarrow \overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2-\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2\big]
$$

  `[1 Mark]`  
  In contrast, ionization of $n$-propyl chloride produces an unstabilized primary carbocation ($\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2$), which has much higher activation energy. Hence, allyl chloride hydrolyzes significantly faster. `[1 Mark]`

### Q25. [CBSE 2024 (Delhi/AI Sets), 5 Marks] | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Question:**  
**(a)** An alkyl halide $\text{X}$ ($\text{C}_4\text{H}_9\text{Cl}$) is optically active.  
  (i) Identify the structure and IUPAC name of $\text{X}$.  
  (ii) Write the structure of the product obtained when $\text{X}$ reacts with sodium iodide in dry acetone.  
  (iii) Write the mechanism when $\text{X}$ is treated with aqueous $\text{KOH}$. `[3 Marks]`  
**(b)** Explain why:  
  (i) Dipole moment of chlorobenzene is lower than that of cyclohexyl chloride.  
  (ii) Treatment of alkyl chlorides with aqueous $\text{KOH}$ leads to alcohols, but with alcoholic $\text{KOH}$ it yields alkenes. `[2 Marks]`  

**Model Answer / Marking Scheme:**  
- **(a) (i)**  
  Isomers of $\text{C}_4\text{H}_9\text{Cl}$:  
  1-chlorobutane, 2-chlorobutane, 1-chloro-2-methylpropane, 2-chloro-2-methylpropane.  
  Only **2-chlorobutane** has an asymmetric carbon atom:
  

$$
\text{CH}_3-\overset{*}{\text{C}}\text{H(Cl)}-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3
$$

  - IUPAC Name: **2-Chlorobutane** `[1 Mark]`  
- **(a) (ii)**  
  Finkelstein reaction with $\text{NaI}$ in dry acetone yields 2-iodobutane with Walden Inversion:
  

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH(Cl)}-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3 + \text{NaI} \xrightarrow{\text{dry acetone}} \text{CH}_3-\text{CH(I)}-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3 + \text{NaCl}\downarrow
$$

  `[1 Mark]`  
- **(a) (iii)**  
  Reaction of secondary halide $\text{X}$ with aqueous $\text{KOH}$ follows both $\text{S}_\text{N}1$ and $\text{S}_\text{N}2$ pathways (predominantly $\text{S}_\text{N}2$ with partial $\text{S}_\text{N}1$ racemisation):  
  **$\text{S}_\text{N}2$ Mechanism Pathway:**
  1. Attack of nucleophile $\text{OH}^-$ on the $\alpha$-carbon from the side opposite to $\text{Cl}^-$:
  

$$
\text{HO}^- + \text{CH}_3-\text{CH(Cl)}-\text{C}_2\text{H}_5 \longrightarrow \left[ \text{HO}\cdots\overset{\delta-}{\text{C}}(\text{H})(\text{CH}_3)(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5)\cdots\overset{\delta-}{\text{Cl}} \right]^\ddagger
$$

  2. Expulsion of $\text{Cl}^-$ yielding $(+)\text{ or } (-)\text{-butan-2-ol}$ with Walden inversion of configuration:
  

$$
\longrightarrow \text{HO}-\text{CH}(\text{CH}_3)\text{C}_2\text{H}_5 + \text{Cl}^-
$$

  *(Full 1 mark for showing concerted transition state / mechanism steps).* `[1 Mark]`  

- **(b) (i)**  
  - $\text{C}$ atom in chlorobenzene is $\text{sp}^2$ hybridised ($s\text{-character} = 33.3\%$), more electronegative, thus less electron-releasing toward $\text{Cl}$. In cyclohexyl chloride, $\text{C}$ is $\text{sp}^3$ hybridised ($s\text{-character} = 25\%$), releasing electrons more readily to $\text{Cl}$.  
  - Resonance ($+R$) delocalization gives partial double-bond character to the aromatic $\text{C}-\text{Cl}$ bond, shortening bond distance $d$. Since $\mu = q \times d$, chlorobenzene has a lower dipole moment. `[1 Mark]`  
- **(b) (ii)**  
  - In aqueous solution, $\text{OH}^-$ ions are heavily hydrated, reducing their basic character and allowing them to act primarily as nucleophiles, substituting $\text{Cl}^-$ to form alcohols:
  

$$
\text{R-Cl} + \text{OH}^- \longrightarrow \text{R-OH} + \text{Cl}^-
$$

  
  - In alcoholic solution (e.g., $\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$), alkoxide ions ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{O}^-$) are formed:
  

$$
\text{OH}^- + \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \rightleftharpoons \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{O}^- + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

  Ethoxide ion is a stronger base and bulkier than $\text{OH}^-$, preferentially abstracting a proton from the $\beta$-carbon to trigger E2 elimination, yielding an alkene. `[1 Mark]`

### Q27. [CBSE 2020 (Delhi/Foreign Sets), 5 Marks] | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Question:**  
**(a)** Carry out the following conversions in not more than two steps:  
  (i) Chlorobenzene to $p$-nitrophenol  
  (ii) 2-Bromopropane to 1-bromopropane  
  (iii) Aniline to Chlorobenzene `[3 Marks]`  
**(b)** Explain why:  
  (i) Alkyl halides give two substitution products when reacted with $\text{KNO}_2$, but primarily one when reacted with $\text{AgNO}_2$.  
  (ii) The $\text{C}-\text{Cl}$ bond length in chlorobenzene is shorter ($169\text{ pm}$) than that in methyl chloride ($178\text{ pm}$). `[2 Marks]`  

**Model Answer / Marking Scheme:**  
- **(a) (i) Chlorobenzene to $p$-nitrophenol:**  
  1. Nitration using concentrated $\text{HNO}_3 / \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$:
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} \xrightarrow{\text{conc. }\text{HNO}_3 + \text{conc. }\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4} p\text{-nitrochlorobenzene}
$$

  2. Nucleophilic aromatic substitution (Dow's modified process) using warm aqueous $\text{NaOH}$ followed by acidification:
  

$$
p\text{-NO}_2-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4-\text{Cl} \xrightarrow{\text{(i) aq. NaOH, } 443\text{ K},\; \text{(ii) }\text{H}^+} p\text{-NO}_2-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4-\text{OH}
$$

  `[1 Mark]`  

- **(a) (ii) 2-Bromopropane to 1-bromopropane:**  
  1. Dehydrohalogenation using hot alcoholic $\text{KOH}$:
  

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH(Br)}-\text{CH}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{alc. KOH, }\Delta} \text{CH}_3-\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 + \text{KBr} + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

  2. Free radical anti-Markovnikov addition of $\text{HBr}$ in the presence of benzoyl peroxide:
  

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 + \text{HBr} \xrightarrow{\text{Peroxide}} \text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2\text{Br}
$$

  `[1 Mark]`  

- **(a) (iii) Aniline to Chlorobenzene:**  
  1. Diazotization:
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{NaNO}_2 + 2\text{HCl} \xrightarrow{273-278\text{ K}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{NaCl} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

  2. Sandmeyer reaction:
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- \xrightarrow{\text{Cu}_2\text{Cl}_2 / \text{HCl}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} + \text{N}_2\uparrow
$$

  `[1 Mark]`  

- **(b) (i)**  
  The nitrite ion is an ambident nucleophile with two reactive centers:
  

$$
[:\ddot{\text{O}}-\ddot{\text{N}}=\ddot{\text{O}} \longleftrightarrow \ddot{\text{O}}=\ddot{\text{N}}-\ddot{\text{O}}:]^-
$$

  - $\text{KNO}_2$ is predominantly ionic ($[\text{K}^+][\text{O}-\text{N}=\text{O}]^-$). Attack occurs preferentially through the more electronegative oxygen atom, yielding **alkyl nitrites** ($\text{R}-\text{O}-\text{N}=\text{O}$) as the major product. `[1/2 Mark]`  
  - $\text{AgNO}_2$ is predominantly covalent ($\text{Ag}-\text{O}-\text{N}=\text{O}$). The lone pair of electrons on the nitrogen atom is more available for nucleophilic donation, yielding **nitroalkanes** ($\text{R}-\text{NO}_2$) as the major product. `[1/2 Mark]`  

- **(b) (ii)**  
  1. **Resonance Effect:** The lone pair of electrons on the chlorine atom in chlorobenzene delocalizes into the $\pi$-system of the benzene ring, establishing partial double bond character in the $\text{C}-\text{Cl}$ bond. In methyl chloride, no resonance occurs, leaving a pure single bond. `[1/2 Mark]`  
  2. **Hybridisation Difference:** The carbon atom bonded to chlorine in chlorobenzene is $\text{sp}^2$ hybridised ($33.3\%\; s\text{-character}$), which has a smaller covalent radius and holds shared electrons tighter than the larger $\text{sp}^3$ hybridised carbon atom ($25\%\; s\text{-character}$) in methyl chloride. `[1/2 Mark]`
