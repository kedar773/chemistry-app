## 1. Speed Hacks & Fast Solving Shortcuts

### Mechanism Determination Flowchart (3-Second Rule)
To avoid confusion between $\text{S}_\text{N}1$, $\text{S}_\text{N}2$, $\text{E}1$, and $\text{E}2$:

$$
\text{Substrate} \longrightarrow \begin{cases}
1^\circ \text{ Alkyl Halide} & \xrightarrow{\text{Strong Nucleophile (e.g., } \text{OH}^-, \text{CN}^-)} \mathbf{S_N2} \text{ (Inversion)} \\
1^\circ \text{ Alkyl Halide} & \xrightarrow{\text{Bulky Base (e.g., } t\text{-BuO}^- \text{ or alc. KOH + }\Delta)} \mathbf{E2} \text{ (Saytzeff/Hofmann)} \\
2^\circ \text{ Alkyl Halide} & \xrightarrow{\text{Polar Aprotic (DMSO, Acetone) / Strong Nu}^-} \mathbf{S_N2} \\
2^\circ \text{ Alkyl Halide} & \xrightarrow{\text{Polar Protic (}\text{H}_2\text{O, EtOH)} \text{ / Weak Nu}} \mathbf{S_N1} \\
3^\circ \text{ Alkyl Halide} & \xrightarrow{\text{Nucleophile in Protic medium (}\text{H}_2\text{O, MeOH)}} \mathbf{S_N1} \text{ (Racemisation)} \\
3^\circ \text{ Alkyl Halide} & \xrightarrow{\text{Base + Heat / alc. KOH}} \mathbf{E2/E1} \text{ (Alkene, Saytzeff preferred)}
\end{cases}
$$

* **Steric vs Electronic Dominance:**
  * $\text{S}_\text{N}2$ rate: $\text{CH}_3\text{X} > 1^\circ > 2^\circ \gg 3^\circ$ (Driven entirely by steric hindrance).
  * $\text{S}_\text{N}1$ rate: $3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ > \text{CH}_3\text{X}$ (Driven entirely by carbocation stability).
  * **Resonance override:** Allylic ($\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}-\text{CH}_2\text{X}$) and benzylic ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_2\text{X}$) substrates undergo **both** $\text{S}_\text{N}1$ (resonance-stabilised carbocation) and $\text{S}_\text{N}2$ ($1^\circ$ sterics) at exceptionally fast rates.

### Ambident Nucleophile Rule
* **$\text{KCN}$ vs $\text{AgCN}$:**
  * $\text{KCN}$ is predominantly **ionic** $\implies \text{C}$ is nucleophilic $\implies \mathbf{R-CN}$ (Alkyl cyanide/Nitrile).
  * $\text{AgCN}$ is predominantly **covalent** $\implies \text{lone pair on N}$ attacks $\implies \mathbf{R-NC}$ (Isocyanide/Carbylamine).
* **$\text{KNO}_2$ vs $\text{AgNO}_2$:**
  * $\text{KNO}_2$ is **ionic** ($\text{K}^+ \, ^-\text{O}-\text{N}=\text{O}$) $\implies \text{O}$ attacks $\implies \mathbf{R-ONO}$ (Alkyl nitrite).
  * $\text{AgNO}_2$ is **covalent** ($\text{Ag}-\text{O}-\text{N}=\text{O}$) $\implies \text{N}$ attacks $\implies \mathbf{R-NO}_2$ (Nitroalkane).

### Elimination Regiochemistry (Saytzeff vs Hofmann)
* **Saytzeff Rule:** In dehydrohalogenation, base abstracts $\beta\text{-H}$ from the carbon bearing fewer hydrogens to yield the **more substituted, thermodynamically more stable alkene** (major).
* **Hofmann Exception:** Use of sterically hindered base (e.g., potassium $t$-butoxide) or a poor leaving group ($-\text{F}$) yields the **less substituted alkene** as the major product.

## 2. Examiner Traps & Common Board Evaluation Mistakes

Examiners strictly adhere to the official CBSE Marking Scheme. Below are exact areas where deductions occur:

### 1. Missing Reagents and Conditions (0.5 to 1 Mark Loss)
* **Finkelstein Reaction:** Writing only $\text{NaI}$ without specifying **dry acetone** leads to a $1/2$ mark deduction. (Acetone drives equilibrium forward via precipitation of $\text{NaCl}/\text{NaBr}$).
* **Swarts Reaction:** Writing generic fluorinating conditions instead of specific heavy metal fluorides ($\text{AgF}$, $\text{Hg}_2\text{F}_2$, $\text{CoF}_3$, or $\text{SbF}_3$).
* **Free-radical halogenation:** Writing $\text{Cl}_2$ without $\mathbf{h\nu}$ / **sunlight** or **heat** loses the entire mark for the step.
* **Aromatic electrophilic substitution:** Omitting anhydrous Lewis acid catalysts like **anh. $\text{AlCl}_3$** or **anh. $\text{FeCl}_3$**.

### 2. Ambiguity in Explaining Low Reactivity of Haloarenes (Frequent 2-3 Mark Question)
Students often write one generic point. CBSE marking schemes mandate at least **three distinct points** with diagrams/reasons for full credit:
1. **Resonance Effect:** Lone pair of halogen conjugates with the ring, imparting **partial double bond character** to the $\text{C}-\text{Cl}$ bond (requires drawing canonical structures).
2. **Hybridisation of Carbon:** In haloarenes, carbon is $\mathbf{sp^2}$ hybridised (greater $s$-character, shorter bond length of $169\text{ pm}$, held more tightly) vs $\mathbf{sp^3}$ carbon in haloalkanes ($177\text{ pm}$).
3. **Instability of Phenyl Cation:** Self-ionisation to form a phenyl cation is not stabilised by resonance.
4. **Electronic Repulsion:** Electron-rich nucleophile faces repulsion from the electron-rich $\pi$-electron cloud of the benzene ring.

### 3. Stereochemical Inaccuracies ($\text{S}_\text{N}1$ vs $\text{S}_\text{N}2$)
* Stating that $\text{S}_\text{N}1$ gives "100% racemic mixture".  
  * *Correction for Full Marks:* Mention that $\text{S}_\text{N}1$ proceeds with **racemisation accompanied by slight inversion** due to the leaving group shielding the front side immediately after ionisation.
* For $\text{S}_\text{N}2$, failing to write **"complete inversion of configuration"** (Walden inversion) or not showing the planar pentacoordinate transition state in mechanism questions.

### 4. Grignard Reagent Handling Pitfalls
* Forgetting to write **"dry ether"** over the arrow:
  

$$
\text{R}-\text{X} + \text{Mg} \xrightarrow{dry ether} \text{RMgX}
$$

* Writing water in the preparation step destroys the reagent:
  

$$
\text{RMgX} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{R}-\text{H} + \text{Mg(OH)X}
$$

  *(Examiner trap: Explaining why Grignard reagents must be prepared under anhydrous conditions).*

## 3. Golden Points to Remember (NCERT In-Text Lines & High-Yield Truths)

1. **Dipole Moment Anomaly:**
   

$$
\mathbf{\text{CH}_3\text{Cl} > \text{CH}_3\text{F} > \text{CH}_3\text{Br} > \text{CH}_3\text{I}}
$$

   *Reason:* Even though fluorine is more electronegative than chlorine, the $\text{C}-\text{Cl}$ bond length is significantly longer than $\text{C}-\text{F}$. Since $\mu = q \times d$, the larger bond length ($d$) in $\text{CH}_3\text{Cl}$ overcompensates for the slight difference in charge separation ($q$).

2. **Melting Point of Dichlorobenzene Isomers:**
   

$$
\text{Melting Point: } \mathbf{\text{para} > \text{ortho} > \text{meta}}
$$

   *Reason:* The $p$-isomer is symmetrical and packs closely in the crystal lattice, leading to stronger intermolecular forces and a significantly higher melting point compared to $o$- and $m$-isomers.
   *Boiling Point:* Nearly identical across isomers ($o \approx p > m$).

3. **Boiling Point Trends of Alkyl Halides:**
   * For the same alkyl group: $\mathbf{\text{R}-\text{I} > \text{R}-\text{Br} > \text{R}-\text{Cl} > \text{R}-\text{F}}$ (Due to increasing molecular mass, size, and London dispersion forces).
   * For isomeric alkyl halides: $\mathbf{\text{Primary} > \text{Secondary} > \text{Tertiary}}$ (Branching decreases surface area $\implies$ reduces van der Waals forces $\implies$ lowers boiling point).

4. **Chloroform Storage & Phosgene Trap:**
   * Chloroform ($\text{CHCl}_3$) slowly oxidises in air and light to form the poisonous gas **phosgene** ($\text{COCl}_2$):
     

$$
2\text{CHCl}_3 + \text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{light}} 2\text{COCl}_2 + 2\text{HCl}
$$

   * *Prevention:* Stored in dark, amber-coloured bottles filled to the brim. Addition of **1% ethanol** acts as a negative catalyst and converts any formed phosgene into harmless diethyl carbonate:
     

$$
\text{COCl}_2 + 2\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \longrightarrow (\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{O})_2\text{C}=\text{O} + 2\text{HCl}
$$

5. **Reactivity towards $\text{AgNO}_3$ Test:**
   * Haloarenes and vinyl halides do **not** give a precipitate of $\text{AgX}$ with alcoholic $\text{AgNO}_3$ at room temperature (partial double-bond character inhibits ionisation).
   * Allylic, benzylic, and $3^\circ$ alkyl halides form precipitates instantaneously.

### 2. Named Reactions Master Checklist

* **Finkelstein Reaction:**
  

$$
\text{R}-\text{X} + \text{NaI} \xrightarrow{dry acetone} \text{R}-\text{I} + \text{NaX} \downarrow \quad (\text{X} = \text{Cl, Br})
$$

* **Swarts Reaction:**
  

$$
\text{R}-\text{X} + \text{AgF} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{R}-\text{F} + \text{AgX} \downarrow \quad (\text{also using } \text{Hg}_2\text{F}_2, \text{CoF}_3, \text{SbF}_3)
$$

* **Darzens Halogenation (Best method for pure alkyl chlorides):**
  

$$
\text{R}-\text{OH} + \text{SOCl}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{pyridine}} \text{R}-\text{Cl} + \text{SO}_2 \uparrow + \text{HCl} \uparrow
$$

  *(By-products $\text{SO}_2$ and $\text{HCl}$ are gaseous, leaving behind pure alkyl halide).*

* **Sandmeyer Reaction:**
  

$$
\text{Ar}-\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- \xrightarrow{\text{Cu}_2\text{Cl}_2 / \text{HCl}} \text{Ar}-\text{Cl} + \text{N}_2 \uparrow
$$

  

$$
\text{Ar}-\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- \xrightarrow{\text{Cu}_2\text{Br}_2 / \text{HBr}} \text{Ar}-\text{Br} + \text{N}_2 \uparrow
$$

* **Gattermann Reaction:**
  

$$
\text{Ar}-\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- \xrightarrow{\text{Cu powder / HCl}} \text{Ar}-\text{Cl} + \text{N}_2 \uparrow + \text{CuCl}
$$

* **Wurtz Reaction:**
  

$$
2\text{R}-\text{X} + 2\text{Na} \xrightarrow{dry ether} \text{R}-\text{R} + 2\text{NaX}
$$

* **Fittig Reaction:**
  

$$
2\text{Ar}-\text{X} + 2\text{Na} \xrightarrow{dry ether} \text{Ar}-\text{Ar} \text{ (Diphenyl)} + 2\text{NaX}
$$

* **Wurtz-Fittig Reaction:**
  

$$
\text{Ar}-\text{X} + \text{R}-\text{X} + 2\text{Na} \xrightarrow{dry ether} \text{Ar}-\text{R} + 2\text{NaX}
$$

* **Dow's Process (Drastic conditions proving unreactivity of Chlorobenzene):**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} + \text{NaOH} \xrightarrow[300\text{ atm}]{623\text{ K}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{O}^-\text{Na}^+ \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH}
$$

  *(Rate accelerates sharply when electron-withdrawing groups like $-\text{NO}_2$ are present at $o$- and $p$-positions).*

* **Hunsdiecker Reaction:**
  

$$
\text{R}-\text{COOAg} + \text{Br}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{CCl}_4, \, \Delta} \text{R}-\text{Br} + \text{CO}_2 \uparrow + \text{AgBr} \downarrow
$$
