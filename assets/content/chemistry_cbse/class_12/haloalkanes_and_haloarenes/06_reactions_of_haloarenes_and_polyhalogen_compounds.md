## 1. Pedagogical Theory & Concepts

### 1.1 Dual Electronic Nature of Halogens on the Benzene Ring

$$
\text{Priority: } \bigstar\bigstar\bigstar\bigstar\bigstar
$$

Halogens attached directly to an $sp^2$-hybridized aromatic ring exhibit two competing electronic effects:

1. **Strong Inductive Effect ($-I$ Effect):**
   - Halogens ($\text{F}, \text{Cl}, \text{Br}, \text{I}$) possess higher electronegativities than $sp^2$-hybridized aromatic carbon ($\chi_{\text{Cl}} \approx 3.16$, $\chi_{\text{C}(sp^2)} \approx 2.75$).
   - The $-I$ effect pulls $\sigma$-electron density away from the aromatic nucleus, permanently decreasing electron density across all ring carbons.
   - **Consequence:** Deactivation of the ring toward electrophiles relative to benzene.

2. **Resonance Effect ($+R$ / $+M$ Effect):**
   - The unshared non-bonding electron pairs ($p$-orbital lone pairs) on the halogen atom undergo delocalization with the adjacent $\pi$-system of the benzene ring.
   - Overlap between carbon $2p$ and halogen $np$ orbitals donates $\pi$-electron density into the ring.

```
       :Cl:                   :Cl:+                  :Cl:+                  :Cl:+                  :Cl:
      .. /                   .. /                   .. /                   .. /                   .. /
    //    \\               //    \\               /      \\              //    \               //    \\
   |        |   <--->     |        |-  <--->     |        |   <--->     |-      |   <--->     |        |
    \\    //               \\    //               \\    //-              \\    //              \\    //
      \--/                   \--/                   \--/                   \--/                   \--/
       (I)                   (II)                   (III)                  (IV)                    (V)
```

- **Resonance Canonical Analysis:**
  - Structure **II** places a formal negative charge on the **ortho** position (C-2).
  - Structure **III** places a formal negative charge on the **para** position (C-4).
  - Structure **IV** places a formal negative charge on the other **ortho** position (C-6).
  - **Zero** resonance structures place a formal negative charge on the **meta** positions (C-3 and C-5).

#### Resolution of the Paradox (Orientation vs. Reactivity)
- **Net Reactivity (Controlled by $-I$):** The $-I$ effect of halogens is stronger than the $+R$ effect. Hence, there is a net withdrawal of electron density from the ring, making haloarenes **less reactive (deactivated)** toward electrophilic attack than unsubstituted benzene.
- **Orientation (Controlled by $+R$):** The $+R$ effect selectively increases the electron density at the *ortho* and *para* positions relative to the *meta* positions. Therefore, incoming electrophiles ($E^+$) preferentially attack at **ortho** and **para** sites.
- **Thermodynamic vs. Steric Outcome:** Between the two isomers, the **para-isomer is the major product** due to minimal steric hindrance between the incoming bulky electrophile and the existing halogen atom, combined with higher molecular symmetry allowing better crystal packing.

## 2. Chemical Reactions & Step-by-Step Mechanisms

### 2.1 Halogenation of Haloarenes

$$
\text{Priority: } \bigstar\bigstar\bigstar\bigstar
$$

Electrophilic chlorination of chlorobenzene requires a Lewis acid catalyst ($\text{FeCl}_3$ or anhydrous $\text{Fe}$) to polarize the halogen molecule.

#### Generation of the Electrophile (Chloronium Ion, $\text{Cl}^+$)

$$
\text{Cl}-\text{Cl} + \text{FeCl}_3 \rightleftharpoons \left[ \text{Cl}^{\delta+}\cdots\text{Cl}\cdots\text{FeCl}_3^{\delta-} \right] \rightleftharpoons \text{Cl}^+ + [\text{FeCl}_4]^-
$$

#### Stoichiometric Equation

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} + \text{Cl}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Anhydrous }\text{FeCl}_3,\;\Delta} \underset{\text{1,2-Dichlorobenzene (Minor)}}{\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{Cl}_2} + \underset{\text{1,4-Dichlorobenzene (Major)}}{\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{Cl}_2} + \text{HCl}
$$

```
        Cl                       Cl                      Cl
         |                        |                       |
       // \                     // \   Cl               // \
      |    |  +  Cl2  ----->   |    |--     +          |    |
       \\ /                     \\ /                    \\ /
                             (Minor, ~20%)                |
                                                          Cl
                                                     (Major, ~80%)
```

### 2.2 Nitration of Haloarenes

$$
\text{Priority: } \bigstar\bigstar\bigstar\bigstar\bigstar
$$

Nitration is carried out using nitrating mixture: concentrated nitric acid ($\text{HNO}_3$, $68\%\text{ w/w}$) and concentrated sulfuric acid ($\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$, $98\%\text{ w/w}$).

#### Mechanism of Electrophile Generation ($\text{NO}_2^+$)
Sulfuric acid acts as a Brønsted acid (proton donor) and nitric acid acts as a Brønsted base (proton acceptor):

$$
\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightleftharpoons \text{H}^+ + \text{HSO}_4^-
$$

$$
\text{HNO}_3 + \text{H}^+ \rightleftharpoons \text{H}_2\text{O}^+-\text{NO}_2
$$

$$
\text{H}_2\text{O}^+-\text{NO}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{slow}} \text{H}_2\text{O} + \underset{\text{Nitronium Ion (Electrophile)}}{\text{NO}_2^+}
$$

$$
\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightleftharpoons \text{H}_3\text{O}^+ + \text{HSO}_4^-
$$

$$
\text{Overall: } \text{HNO}_3 + 2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightleftharpoons \text{NO}_2^+ + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+ + 2\text{HSO}_4^-
$$

#### Stoichiometric Equation

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} + \text{HNO}_3\text{ (conc.)} \xrightarrow{\text{conc. }\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4,\; \Delta} \underset{\text{1-Chloro-2-nitrobenzene (Minor)}}{\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{Cl}(\text{NO}_2)} + \underset{\text{1-Chloro-4-nitrobenzene (Major)}}{\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{Cl}(\text{NO}_2)} + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

### 2.3 Sulfonation of Haloarenes

$$
\text{Priority: } \bigstar\bigstar\bigstar
$$

Sulfonation uses fuming sulfuric acid (oleum, $\text{H}_2\text{S}_2\text{O}_7$) or concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ under prolonged heating.

#### Active Electrophile: Neutral Sulfur Trioxide ($\text{SO}_3$)

$$
2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightleftharpoons \text{SO}_3 + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+ + \text{HSO}_4^-
$$

#### Stoichiometric Equation

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4\text{ (conc.)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \underset{\text{2-Chlorobenzenesulfonic acid (Minor)}}{\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{Cl}(\text{SO}_3\text{H})} + \underset{\text{4-Chlorobenzenesulfonic acid (Major)}}{\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{Cl}(\text{SO}_3\text{H})} + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

> [!IMPORTANT]
> IUPAC nomenclature rule: The $-\text{SO}_3\text{H}$ group has higher priority than the halogen. The ring is numbered starting from the carbon bearing the sulfonic acid group ($\text{C-}1$), making the halogen a substituent (**2-chloro...** and **4-chloro...**).

### 2.4 Friedel-Crafts Alkylation and Acylation

$$
\text{Priority: } \bigstar\bigstar\bigstar\bigstar\bigstar
$$

#### A. Friedel-Crafts Alkylation
Reaction with an alkyl halide in the presence of anhydrous aluminium chloride ($\text{AlCl}_3$):

1. **Electrophile Generation:**
   

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{Cl} + \text{AlCl}_3 \rightleftharpoons \left[\text{CH}_3^{\delta+}\cdots\text{Cl}\cdots\text{AlCl}_3^{\delta-}\right] \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3^+ + [\text{AlCl}_4]^-
$$

2. **Stoichiometric Equation:**
   

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} + \text{CH}_3\text{Cl} \xrightarrow{\text{Anhydrous }\text{AlCl}_3} \underset{\text{1-Chloro-2-methylbenzene (Minor)}}{\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{Cl}(\text{CH}_3)} + \underset{\text{1-Chloro-4-methylbenzene (Major)}}{\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{Cl}(\text{CH}_3)} + \text{HCl}
$$

#### B. Friedel-Crafts Acylation
Reaction with an acyl halide (or acid anhydride) in the presence of anhydrous aluminium chloride:

1. **Electrophile Generation (Acylium Ion):**
   

$$
\text{CH}_3-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{Cl} + \text{AlCl}_3 \rightleftharpoons \left[\text{CH}_3-\overset{+}{\text{C}}=\text{O} \longleftrightarrow \text{CH}_3-\text{C}\equiv\overset{+}{\text{O}}\right] + [\text{AlCl}_4]^-
$$

2. **Stoichiometric Equation:**
   

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} + \text{CH}_3\text{COCl} \xrightarrow{\text{Anhydrous }\text{AlCl}_3} \underset{\text{2-Chloroacetophenone (Minor)}}{\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{Cl}(\text{COCH}_3)} + \underset{\text{4-Chloroacetophenone (Major)}}{\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{Cl}(\text{COCH}_3)} + \text{HCl}
$$

> [!NOTE]
> The product is named as a derivative of acetophenone because $-\text{COCH}_3$ takes principal functional group priority over the chloro substituent.

### 2.5 Arenium Ion (Wheland Intermediate) Stability Derivation

To prove why *ortho/para* attack is favored over *meta* attack:

#### Attack at Ortho-Position:
```
           Cl                      Cl                     Cl                     :Cl:+
         // \                    // \                   // \                   //  \
        |    | + E+  ----->     |    |-- E   <--->     |    |-- E   <--->     |     |-- E
         \\ /                  + \\ /                   \--/+                  \\  /
                                  H                       H                      H
                              (Carbocation)          (Carbocation)         (EXTRA STABLE:
                                                                          Octet complete on all atoms)
```

#### Attack at Para-Position:
```
           Cl                         :Cl:+                      Cl                       Cl
         // \                        //  \                     // \                     // \
        |    | + E+  ----->         |     |          <--->    |    |          <--->    +|   |
         \\ /                      + \\  /                     \\ /+                    \\ /
                                      |                          |                        |
                                    H-C-E                      H-C-E                    H-C-E
                                (EXTRA STABLE)
```

#### Attack at Meta-Position:
```
           Cl                    Cl                    Cl
         // \                  // \                  // \
        |    | + E+  ----->  +|    |-- E   <--->    |    |-- E   <--->   (No resonance contributor
         \\ /                  \\ /                  \\ /+                allows chlorine lone pair
                                  H                     H                 to share positive charge)
```

**Conclusion:** For *ortho* and *para* attacks, an especially stable resonance contributor forms where the positive charge is directly delocalized onto the halogen atom, giving **all atoms (including carbon and halogen) complete octets**. For *meta* attack, the positive charge never lands on the carbon bearing the halogen, preventing the halogen's lone pair from providing direct $+R$ stabilization.

### 3.1 Dichloromethane (Methylene Chloride, $\text{CH}_2\text{Cl}_2$)

$$
\text{Priority: } \bigstar\bigstar\bigstar
$$

- **Industrial Applications:**
  - Widely used as an industrial solvent and paint stripper/remover.
  - Used as a propellant in aerosols and process solvent in pharmaceutical manufacturing.
  - Solvent in food chemistry (decaffeination of coffee and tea, extraction of flavourings).
- **Human Toxicity and Physiological Impact:**
  - **Central Nervous System (CNS):** Exposure to lower levels impairs hearing and vision. High air levels cause dizziness, nausea, tingling, numbness in fingers and toes.
  - **Metabolic Pathway:** Inhaled $\text{CH}_2\text{Cl}_2$ is metabolized by hepatic cytochrome P450 to carbon monoxide ($\text{CO}$), forming **carboxyhemoglobin ($\text{COHb}$)**, leading to impaired oxygen-carrying capacity.
  - **Direct Contact:** Direct skin contact causes intense burning and mild erythema; cornea damage occurs upon direct splash exposure.

***

### 3.2 Trichloromethane (Chloroform, $\text{CHCl}_3$)

$$
\text{Priority: } \bigstar\bigstar\bigstar\bigstar\bigstar
$$

- **Primary Uses:**
  - Precursor to fluorocarbon refrigerant **R-22 (chlorodifluoromethane)**:
    

$$
\text{CHCl}_3 + 2\text{HF} \xrightarrow{\text{SbCl}_5} \underset{\text{Freon-22}}{\text{CHClF}_2} + 2\text{HCl}
$$

  - Historically used as an inhalation anesthetic; replaced by safer alternatives like halothane ($\text{CF}_3\text{CHBrCl}$).
- **The Phosgene Degradation Trap:**
  Chloroform is oxidized slowly by air in the presence of light to form an extremely poisonous gas, **carbonyl chloride (phosgene, $\text{COCl}_2$)**:

  

$$
\boxed{2\text{CHCl}_3 + \text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{h\nu\text{ (light)}} 2\text{COCl}_2\text{ (phosgene)} + 2\text{HCl}}
$$

- **NCERT Storage Mandate:**
  1. Stored in **closed, dark amber-colored bottles** to exclude light.
  2. Filled completely **up to the brim** to exclude air (oxygen).
  3. **Preservation Agent:** $\approx 1\%$ **ethanol ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$)** is added. Ethanol converts any trace phosgene formed into non-toxic **diethyl carbonate**:
     

$$
\text{COCl}_2 + 2\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Diethyl carbonate (non-toxic)}}{(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{O})_2\text{C}=\text{O}} + 2\text{HCl}
$$

***

### 3.3 Triiodomethane (Iodoform, $\text{CHI}_3$)

$$
\text{Priority: } \bigstar\bigstar\bigstar\bigstar
$$

- **Physical Properties:** Bright yellow crystalline solid, melting point $119^\circ\text{C}$, characteristic pungent/medicinal odor.
- **Antiseptic Action Mechanism:**
  

$$
\boxed{\text{CHI}_3\text{ does NOT possess intrinsic antiseptic activity.}}
$$

  Its antiseptic properties are due entirely to the **slow liberation of free molecular iodine ($\text{I}_2$)** upon contact with skin/organic tissue:
  

$$
\text{CHI}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{tissue contact}} \text{I}_2\text{ (free iodine, the active bactericide)}
$$

- Due to its objectionable odor, it has been replaced by modern formulations containing povidone-iodine.

***

### 3.4 Tetrachloromethane (Carbon Tetrachloride, $\text{CCl}_4$)

$$
\text{Priority: } \bigstar\bigstar\bigstar\bigstar
$$

- **Industrial Applications:**
  - Manufacture of chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) and aerosol propellants.
  - Solvent for oils, fats, resins, and iodine.
  - Used commercially as a fire extinguisher under the trade name **Pyrene** (its heavy, non-combustible vapors blanket the fire, cutting off oxygen).
- **Environmental & Toxicological Degradation:**
  - When released into the troposphere, $\text{CCl}_4$ does not readily degrade; it diffuses into the stratosphere, where UV photolysis generates chlorine radicals ($\text{Cl}^\bullet$) that catalyze **ozone layer depletion**:
    

$$
\text{CCl}_4 \xrightarrow{h\nu} \text{CCl}_3^\bullet + \text{Cl}^\bullet
$$

    

$$
\text{Cl}^\bullet + \text{O}_3 \longrightarrow \text{ClO}^\bullet + \text{O}_2
$$

    

$$
\text{ClO}^\bullet + \text{O}^\bullet \longrightarrow \text{Cl}^\bullet + \text{O}_2
$$

  - Causes liver cancer (hepatotoxic), nerve damage, and kidney failure in humans.

***

### 3.5 Freons (Chlorofluorocarbons, CFCs)

$$
\text{Priority: } \bigstar\bigstar\bigstar\bigstar
$$

- **Definition:** The chlorofluorocarbon compounds of methane and ethane are collectively known as **freons**.
- **Key Example — Freon-12 ($\text{CCl}_2\text{F}_2$, Dichlorodifluoromethane):**
  Synthesized industrially via the **Swarts reaction**:
  

$$
\text{CCl}_4 + 2\text{HF} \xrightarrow{\text{SbF}_5 / \text{SbCl}_5} \text{CCl}_2\text{F}_2 + 2\text{HCl}
$$

- **Characteristics:** Extremely stable, unreactive, non-corrosive, non-toxic, easily liquefiable gas.
- **Ozone Depletion Mechanism in Stratosphere:**
  Freon-12 diffuses unchanged into the stratosphere, where high-energy UV-C photolyzes the $\text{C}-\text{Cl}$ bond:
  

$$
\text{CF}_2\text{Cl}_2 \xrightarrow{h\nu\text{ (UV)}} \text{CF}_2\text{Cl}^\bullet + \text{Cl}^\bullet
$$

  A single chlorine free radical ($\text{Cl}^\bullet$) can catalytically destroy over $100{,}000$ ozone molecules via chain propagation before termination.

### 3.6 $p,p^\prime$-Dichlorodiphenyltrichloroethane (DDT)

$$
\text{Priority: } \bigstar\bigstar\bigstar\bigstar\bigstar
$$

```
               Cl                                 Cl
                \                                 /
                 \---\                       /---/
                 | O |                      | O |
                 /---/                       \---\
                   \                           /
                    \                         /
                     \------- CH ------- C -- Cl
                               |         |
                                         Cl
```

#### Complete Synthesis
Synthesized by condensing chlorobenzene with **chloral (trichloroethanal)** in a $2:1$ molar ratio in the presence of concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ as a dehydrating agent:

$$
\text{CCl}_3-\text{CHO} + 2\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} \xrightarrow{\text{conc. }\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4} \underset{\text{DDT}}{\left(p\text{-Cl}-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\right)_2\text{CH}-\text{CCl}_3} + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

#### IUPAC Name

$$
\boxed{\text{2,2-bis(4-chlorophenyl)-1,1,1-trichloroethane}}
$$

#### Environmental Profile
- **Biomagnification:** DDT is highly lipophilic (fat-soluble) and practically insoluble in water. It is not metabolized or excreted efficiently by organisms; instead, it deposits and concentrates in adipose (fatty) tissues.
- **Ecological Impact:** In birds, high DDT concentrations inhibit the enzyme **calcium carbonic anhydrase**, disrupting calcium transport and causing eggshells to thin prematurely, leading to severe population collapses (e.g., bald eagles, peregrine falcons).
- **Regulatory Status:** Banned in the USA in 1972 and internationally under the Stockholm Convention, with limited exemptions for malaria vector control.

## 4. High-Yield Examples & Solved 5-Year Board PYQs (2020–2025)

### Example 1: The Reactivity-Orientation Paradox
**[CBSE 2024, 2 Marks]**
> **Question:** Although chlorine is an electron-withdrawing group via its inductive effect, it directs incoming electrophiles to the *ortho*- and *para*-positions in electrophilic aromatic substitution reactions. Explain why.

**Model Answer:**
1. **Inductive vs. Resonance Competition:** Chlorine exerts two opposing effects:
   - A strong $-I$ (inductive) effect through the $\sigma$-bond, withdrawing electron density from the aromatic ring and deactivating it.
   - A $+R$ (resonance) effect through the delocalization of its lone pair electrons into the ring $\pi$-system.
2. **Positional Electronic Stabilization:** The $+R$ effect increases electron density specifically at the *ortho* and *para* positions relative to the *meta* positions.
3. **Resonance Stabilization of Intermediate:** When an electrophile attacks at the *ortho* or *para* position, the resulting arenium ion intermediate has four resonance structures, including one particularly stable structure where every atom has a complete octet:
   

$$
\left( -\overset{+}{\text{C}}-\overset{..}{\text{Cl}}: \longleftrightarrow -\text{C}=\overset{+}{\text{Cl}}: \right)
$$

   Attack at the *meta* position does not yield this octet-complete contributor (only 3 structures).
4. **Conclusion:** The $-I$ effect controls **reactivity** (deactivating the ring), whereas the $+R$ effect controls **orientation** (*ortho/para*-directing).

### Example 2: Mechanism of Chloroform Inactivation
**[CBSE 2023, 2 Marks]**
> **Question:** 
> (a) Write the balanced chemical equation for the reaction that occurs when chloroform is exposed to air and sunlight.  
> (b) Why is $1\%$ ethanol added to commercial chloroform bottles?

**Model Answer:**
(a) Chloroform is oxidized by oxygen in the presence of sunlight to form toxic phosgene:

$$
2\text{CHCl}_3 + \text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{h\nu} 2\text{COCl}_2\text{ (carbonyl chloride)} + 2\text{HCl}
$$

(b) Ethanol serves two purposes:
1. It acts as an inhibitor, retarding the oxidation of chloroform.
2. It converts any toxic phosgene formed into non-toxic diethyl carbonate:

$$
\text{COCl}_2 + 2\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Diethyl carbonate}}{(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{O})_2\text{C}=\text{O}} + 2\text{HCl}
$$

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{Cl}(\text{NO}_2)\text{ at position 1,4}

### Example 4: Physical Properties of Dichlorobenzene Isomers
**[CBSE 2021 Compartment / Sample Question Bank, 2 Marks]**
> **Question:** Out of *o*-dichlorobenzene, *m*-dichlorobenzene, and *p*-dichlorobenzene, which isomer has the highest melting point and why?

**Model Answer:**
- **Highest Melting Point:** **$p$-Dichlorobenzene** (Melting point: $p$-isomer $\approx 325\text{ K} > o$-isomer $\approx 256\text{ K} > m$-isomer $\approx 248\text{ K}$).
- **Reasoning:**
  1. The $p$-dichlorobenzene molecule possesses a center of inversion, making it **symmetrical**.
  2. Due to this symmetry, molecules of the *para*-isomer pack more closely and uniformly into the crystal lattice.
  3. Greater packing efficiency maximizes intermolecular lattice forces, requiring more thermal energy to break the lattice during melting.

### Example 5: Environmental and Antiseptic Action of Polyhalogens
**[CBSE 2020, 3 Marks]**
> **Question:** Give reasons:
> 1. Iodoform exhibits antiseptic properties, but pure $\text{CHI}_3$ itself is not the active species.
> 2. DDT is banned in several countries.
> 3. Dichloromethane reduces coordination in human operators.

**Model Answer:**
1. $\text{CHI}_3$ has no inherent antiseptic action; its antiseptic effect is due to **slow decomposition to release elemental iodine ($\text{I}_2$)**, which oxidizes bacterial proteins.
2. DDT is chemically stable and fat-soluble, causing it to undergo **biomagnification** across trophic chains. It thins eggshells in birds and disrupts reproductive physiology, leading to environmental bans.
3. Dichloromethane depresses the **central nervous system (CNS)** and is metabolized in the liver to **carbon monoxide ($\text{CO}$)**, which binds with hemoglobin to form carboxyhemoglobin, impairing oxygen delivery to brain tissues.

## 5. Examiner Traps & Common Student Errors

| S. No. | Reaction / Area | Common Student Mistake | Accurate NCERT Standard |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | **Sulfonation Nomenclature** | Naming the product *4-chlorosulfonic benzene* or *p-chlorosulfonic acid*. | Sulfonic acid is the senior principal group: **4-Chlorobenzenesulfonic acid**. |
| 2 | **Catalyst in Friedel-Crafts** | Writing $\text{AlCl}_3$ without specifying **"Anhydrous"**. | Must explicitly write **"Anhydrous $\text{AlCl}_3$"**; traces of moisture hydrolyze it to $\text{Al(OH)}_3$ and $\text{HCl}$. |
| 3 | **Antiseptic Action of $\text{CHI}_3$** | Claiming $\text{CHI}_3$ itself kills bacteria. | $\text{CHI}_3$ is an antiseptic solely due to the **liberation of free iodine ($\text{I}_2$)**. |
| 4 | **Major vs. Minor Rationale** | Attributing the dominance of the *para*-isomer to "resonance stability." | Resonance contributes equally to *ortho* and *para* electron densities; the *para*-isomer dominates due to **less steric hindrance**. |
| 5 | **DDT Structure** | Drawing a methyl carbon instead of a single $\text{C}-\text{H}$ bridge. | The central bridge is a methine group: $-\text{CH}-$, bonded to $-\text{CCl}_3$ and two chlorophenyl rings. |
