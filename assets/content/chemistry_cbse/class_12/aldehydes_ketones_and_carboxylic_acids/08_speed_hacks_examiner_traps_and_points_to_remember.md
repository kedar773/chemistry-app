## 1. Speed Hacks & Fast Solving Shortcuts

### Reaction Mechanism & Product Identification Shortcuts
1. **The Carbonyl Cleavage & Condensation Rule (Nucleophilic Addition-Elimination):**
   * Whenever reacting with ammonia derivatives (

$$
\text{H}_2\text{N}-\text{G}
$$

):
     

$$
\text{C}=\text{O} + \text{H}_2\text{N}-\text{G} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+} \text{C}=\text{N}-\text{G} + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

   * **Hack:** Align oxygen of carbonyl facing the two hydrogen atoms of the amine derivative and cleanly eliminate 

$$
\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

. Do not waste time drawing tetrahedral intermediates unless explicitly asked for mechanism.
2. **Iodoform (

$$
\text{CHI}_3
$$

) Screening (The "3-Second Rule"):**
   * Look **strictly** for:
     * 

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{C}=\text{O}
$$

 (methyl carbonyl) OR
     * 

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}(\text{OH})-
$$

 (methyl carbinol oxidized *in situ* by hypoiodite).
   * *Trap bypass:* Pentan-2-one gives yellow ppt of 

$$
\text{CHI}_3
$$

; Pentan-3-one does **not**. Acetophenone gives it; Benzophenone does **not**.
3. **Aldol vs. Cannizzaro Split:**
   * Look at 

$$
\alpha\text{-H}
$$

 count:
     * 

$$
\ge 1\ \alpha\text{-H} \implies
$$

 **Aldol Condensation** (Reagent: dilute alkali, e.g., 

$$
\text{dil. NaOH, } \text{Ba(OH)}_2
$$

).
     * 

$$
0\ \alpha\text{-H} \implies
$$

 **Cannizzaro Reaction** (Reagent: concentrated alkali, e.g., 

$$
50\%\ \text{KOH / NaOH}
$$

).
     * *Shortcut for self-Aldol product:* Double the molecule, make an 

$$
\alpha\text{-}\beta
$$

 unsaturated bond by pulling 

$$
=\text{O}
$$

 from molecule 1 and two 

$$
\alpha\text{-H}
$$

 from molecule 2:
       

$$
2\,\text{CH}_3\text{CHO} \xrightarrow{-\text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{CH}_3-\text{CH}=\text{CH}-\text{CHO}
$$

 (Crotonaldehyde / But-2-en-1-al).
4. **Ozonolysis Inverse Hack:**
   * To identify starting alkene from ozonolysis products: Place the two carbonyl compounds facing each other at their 

$$
=\text{O}
$$

 ends, delete both oxygens, and fuse the carbons with a double bond.

### Rapid Calculation & Numerical Tricks (Physical/Physical-Organic Interplay)
1. **Logarithmic Mental Approximations:**
   * Memorize 4 values: 

$$
\log 2 \approx 0.3010
$$

, 

$$
\log 3 \approx 0.4771
$$

, 

$$
\log 5 \approx 0.6990
$$

, 

$$
\log 7 \approx 0.8451
$$

.
   * 

$$
\log (1.8 \times 10^{-5}) = \log\left(\frac{9}{5} \times 10^{-5}\right) = 2\log 3 - \log 5 - 5 = 2(0.4771) - 0.6990 - 5 = 0.9542 - 0.6990 - 5 = -4.7448 \approx -4.74
$$

.
2. **

$$
\text{p}K_a
$$

 & Acid Strength Instant Resolver:**
   * 

$$
\text{p}K_a = -\log K_a
$$

. A lower 

$$
\text{p}K_a
$$

 indicates higher acid strength.
   * Shortcut ranking: **

$$
-I / -M
$$

 groups decrease 

$$
\text{p}K_a
$$

** (stabilize carboxylate ion); **

$$
+I / +M
$$

 groups increase 

$$
\text{p}K_a
$$

**.
   * Distance-Number-Strength (DNS) Rule for inductive effect:
     

$$
\text{Distance} > \text{Number} > \text{Strength}
$$

     * *Example:* 2-Chlorobutanoic acid is stronger than 3-chlorobutanoic acid (Distance wins over substituent electronegativity).
3. **Nernst Factor Shortcut (

$$
298\text{ K}
$$

):**
   * 

$$
\frac{2.303 R T}{F} \equiv 0.0591\text{ V}
$$

.
   * Always write: 

$$
E_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cell}} - \frac{0.0591}{n}\log Q
$$

. Do not round to 

$$
0.06
$$

 unless the question explicitly specifies *"Take 

$$
\frac{2.303 RT}{F} = 0.06\text{ V}
$$

"*.

### Examiner Step-Marking Protocols for Chemistry
1. **Conversions (Usually 2 or 3 steps):**
   * If a 2-step conversion is evaluated: Step 1 holds 

$$
\frac{1}{2}
$$

 mark, Step 2 holds 

$$
\frac{1}{2}
$$

 mark.
   * If reagent over arrow is wrong, subsequent product gets **zero** even if product structure is magically correct.
2. **Distinction Tests (2 Marks):**
   * Structure of ideal answer:
     1. **Name of Reagent** (

$$
\frac{1}{2}
$$

 mark).
     2. **Observable Physical Change / Observation** for both compounds (

$$
\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}
$$

 mark).
     3. **Chemical Equation involved** (

$$
\frac{1}{2}
$$

 mark).
   * *Example:* Distinguish between Acetaldehyde and Benzaldehyde:
     * *Test:* Fehling's Test.
     * *Observation:* Acetaldehyde gives a red precipitate of 

$$
\text{Cu}_2\text{O}
$$

; Benzaldehyde does not give a red precipitate.
3. **Mechanisms (3 Marks — only 3 are in the syllabus for this chapter):**
   * *Nucleophilic addition of HCN to aldehyde/ketone* OR *Nucleophilic addition of alcohol (

$$
\text{H}^+
$$

 catalyst)* OR *Acid-catalyzed esterification mechanism*.
   * Arrows must show electron pair movement: from nucleophile/bond **to** electrophilic center. Reversible arrows (

$$
\rightleftharpoons
$$

) are strictly checked in proton transfers.

## 3. Golden Points to Remember (NCERT In-Text Lines & High-Yield Truths)

1. **Boiling Point Hierarchy:**
   

$$
\text{Carboxylic Acids} > \text{Alcohols} > \text{Aldehydes} \approx \text{Ketones} > \text{Ethers} > \text{Hydrocarbons}
$$

   * *Reason:* Carboxylic acids form **stable hydrogen-bonded cyclic dimers** even in the vapour phase and non-polar solvents (benzene). Alcohols form extensive linear intermolecular H-bonding.
2. **Nucleophilic Addition Reactivity:**
   

$$
\text{HCHO} > \text{CH}_3\text{CHO} > \text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_3 > \text{ArCHO} > \text{ArCOCH}_3 > \text{ArCOAr}
$$

   * *Driving factors:*
     1. **Steric hindrance:** Bulkier groups crowd the transition state from 

$$
sp^2
$$

 (

$$
120^\circ
$$

) to 

$$
sp^3
$$

 (

$$
109.5^\circ
$$

).
     2. **Electronic factor:** Alkyl groups release electron density via 

$$
+I
$$

, neutralizing the partial positive charge (

$$
\delta+
$$

) on carbonyl carbon. Aryl groups decrease electrophilicity via resonance (

$$
+R
$$

 from the ring).
3. **Carboxylic Acids do NOT give Carbonyl Reactions:**
   * Carboxylic acids do not form oximes with hydroxylamine, nor do they give 2,4-DNP or NaHSO3 addition products.
   * *NCERT Reason:* The carbonyl carbon in 

$$
-\text{COOH}
$$

 is involved in resonance with the adjacent lone pair of the hydroxyl oxygen (

$$
-\ddot{\text{O}}\text{H}
$$

), significantly reducing its electrophilic character.
4. **Formic Acid (

$$
\text{HCOOH}
$$

) Anomaly:**
   * Formic acid contains both an aldehyde group and a carboxyl group: 

$$
\text{H}-\mathbf{C}(=\mathbf{O})-\text{OH}
$$

.
   * Hence, **formic acid reduces Tollen’s and Fehling’s reagents**, unlike all other carboxylic acids!
5. **Ortho Effect in Benzoic Acids:**
   * Any ortho-substituent (

$$
-I
$$

, 

$$
+I
$$

, 

$$
-M
$$

, or 

$$
+M
$$

; e.g., 

$$
-\text{NO}_2, -\text{CH}_3, -\text{Cl}
$$

) increases the acidity of benzoic acid relative to benzoic acid itself due to steric inhibition of resonance (SIR), tilting the carboxyl group out of plane and increasing the carboxylate's independent resonance stabilization.
6. **Clemmensen vs. Wolff-Kishner Compatibility:**
   * **Clemmensen Reduction** (

$$
\text{Zn-Hg} / \text{conc. HCl}
$$

): Acid-sensitive groups (like acetals, basic groups, or tertiary alcohols) will be attacked or dehydrated by 

$$
\text{HCl}
$$

.
   * **Wolff-Kishner Reduction** (

$$
\text{NH}_2\text{NH}_2 / \text{KOH, ethylene glycol, }\Delta
$$

): Base-sensitive groups (like alkyl halides, esters, or phenolic esters) will undergo hydrolysis or elimination.

### Master Named Reactions Checklist
All equations formatted to current CBSE board requirements:

1. **Rosenmund Reduction:**
   

$$
\text{R}-\text{C}(=\text{O})\text{Cl} + \text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Pd}/\text{BaSO}_4,\ \text{quinoline/S}} \text{R}-\text{CHO} + \text{HCl}
$$

2. **Stephen Reaction:**
   

$$
\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{N} + \text{SnCl}_2 + 2\text{HCl} \xrightarrow{} \text{R}-\text{CH}=\text{NH}\cdot\text{HCl} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{R}-\text{CHO} + \text{NH}_4\text{Cl}
$$

3. **Etard Reaction:**
   

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_3 + 2\text{CrO}_2\text{Cl}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{CS}_2\text{ or }\text{CCl}_4} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}(\text{OCrOHCl}_2)_2 \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CHO}
$$

4. **Gattermann-Koch Reaction:**
   

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{CO} + \text{HCl} \xrightarrow{\text{Anhyd. }\text{AlCl}_3 / \text{CuCl}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CHO} + \text{HCl}
$$

5. **Clemmensen Reduction:**
   

$$
\text{R}-\text{C}(=\text{O})-\text{R}' + 4[\text{H}] \xrightarrow{\text{Zn}-\text{Hg}\ /\ \text{conc. HCl}} \text{R}-\text{CH}_2-\text{R}' + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

6. **Wolff-Kishner Reduction:**
   

$$
\text{R}-\text{C}(=\text{O})-\text{R}' \xrightarrow{\text{NH}_2\text{NH}_2,\ -\text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{R}-\text{C}(=\text{N}-\text{NH}_2)-\text{R}' \xrightarrow{\text{KOH / ethylene glycol},\ \Delta} \text{R}-\text{CH}_2-\text{R}' + \text{N}_2\uparrow
$$

7. **Tollen’s Test (Silver Mirror Test):**
   

$$
\text{R}-\text{CHO} + 2[\text{Ag}(\text{NH}_3)_2]^+ + 3\text{OH}^- \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{R}-\text{COO}^- + 2\text{Ag}\downarrow\ (\text{silver mirror}) + 4\text{NH}_3 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

8. **Fehling’s Test:**
   

$$
\text{R}-\text{CHO} + 2\text{Cu}^{2+} + 5\text{OH}^- \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{R}-\text{COO}^- + \text{Cu}_2\text{O}\downarrow\ (\text{red-brown ppt}) + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

9. **Haloform (Iodoform) Reaction:**
   

$$
\text{R}-\text{COCH}_3 + 3\text{I}_2 + 4\text{NaOH} \xrightarrow{} \text{R}-\text{COONa} + \text{CHI}_3\downarrow\ (\text{canary yellow ppt}) + 3\text{NaI} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

10. **Aldol Condensation:**
    

$$
2\,\text{CH}_3\text{CHO} \xrightarrow{\text{dil. NaOH}} \text{CH}_3-\text{CH}(\text{OH})-\text{CH}_2-\text{CHO} \xrightarrow{\Delta, -\text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{CH}_3-\text{CH}=\text{CH}-\text{CHO}
$$

11. **Cannizzaro Reaction (Disproportionation):**
    

$$
2\,\text{HCHO} \xrightarrow{\text{conc. NaOH (50\%)}} \text{CH}_3\text{OH} + \text{HCOONa}
$$

    

$$
2\,\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CHO} \xrightarrow{\text{conc. KOH}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_2\text{OH} + \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOK}
$$

12. **Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) Reaction:**
    

$$
\text{R}-\text{CH}_2-\text{COOH} \xrightarrow{\text{(i) }\text{X}_2/\text{Red P}\ \ (\text{X}=\text{Cl, Br})}{\text{(ii) }\text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{R}-\text{CH}(\text{X})-\text{COOH} + \text{HX}
$$

13. **Kolbe's Electrolysis (Hydrocarbon synthesis from carboxylate salts):**
    

$$
2\,\text{RCOOK} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{Electrolysis}} \text{R}-\text{R} + 2\text{CO}_2\uparrow\ (\text{at Anode}) + \text{H}_2\uparrow + 2\text{KOH}\ (\text{at Cathode})
$$
