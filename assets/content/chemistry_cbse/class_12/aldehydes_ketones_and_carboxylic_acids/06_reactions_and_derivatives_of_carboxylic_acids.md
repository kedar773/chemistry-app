## 1. Reactions Involving Cleavage of the $\mathrm{C-OH}$ Bond

In these reactions, the hydroxyl group ($-\mathrm{OH}$) attached to the acyl carbon is replaced by a nucleophile or another leaving group, yielding acyl derivatives:

$$
\mathrm{R-CO-OH} + \mathrm{Nu}^- \longrightarrow \mathrm{R-CO-Nu} + \mathrm{OH}^-
$$

***

### 1.1 Formation of Anhydrides (Intermolecular Dehydration)
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**NCERT Reference:** Section 12.8.2  

Carboxylic acids, when heated with mineral acids such as concentrated sulfuric acid ($\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$) or with phosphorus pentoxide ($\mathrm{P_4O_{10}} / \mathrm{P_2O_5}$), undergo intermolecular dehydration to yield acid anhydrides.

#### Complete Chemical Equation:

$$
2\,\mathrm{RCOOH} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\mathrm{H}^+\text{ or }\mathrm{P_2O_5}} \mathrm{R-CO-O-CO-R} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

**Example (Ethanoic Anhydride Synthesis):**

$$
2\,\mathrm{CH_3COOH} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\mathrm{P_2O_5}} \mathrm{(CH_3CO)_2O} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

#### Mechanistic Pathway:
1. **Protonation of Carbonyl Oxygen:**
   

$$
\mathrm{R-COOH} + \mathrm{H}^+ \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{R-C(\!=\!\overset{+}{O}H)OH}
$$

2. **Nucleophilic Attack:** A second molecule of unprotonated carboxylic acid attacks the highly electrophilic carbonyl carbon via its acyl oxygen.
3. **Proton Transfer:** An internal proton shift converts one $-\mathrm{OH}$ group into a superior leaving group ($-\overset{+}{\mathrm{O}}\mathrm{H_2}$).
4. **Water Elimination & Deprotonation:**
   

$$
\mathrm{R-C(OH)(-\overset{+}{O}H_2)-O-C(=O)R} \xrightarrow{-\mathrm{H_2O}} \mathrm{R-C(\!=\!\overset{+}{O}H)-O-C(=O)R} \xrightarrow{-\mathrm{H}^+} \mathrm{(RCO)_2O}
$$

***

### 1.2 Esterification (Fischer-Speier Esterification)
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**NCERT Reference:** Section 12.8.2  

Carboxylic acids react reversibly with primary and secondary alcohols (or phenols) in the presence of an inorganic acid catalyst (concentrated $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ or dry $\mathrm{HCl}$ gas) to form esters.

#### General Reversible Equation:

$$
\mathrm{RCOOH} + \mathrm{R'OH} \xrightleftharpoons{\mathrm{H}^+} \mathrm{RCOOR'} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

#### Detailed Step-by-Step Mechanism:
The entire sequence is an equilibrium reaction governed by Le Chatelier's Principle. To drive the reaction forward, either water is removed continuously using a Dean-Stark apparatus, or an excess of alcohol is used as solvent.

```
Step 1: Protonation of the carbonyl oxygen of carboxylic acid
        O                           +OH
        ||                           ||
    R - C - OH  +  H+   <=====>  R - C - OH

Step 2: Nucleophilic attack of alcohol oxygen on tetrahedral intermediate
        +OH                          OH
         ||                          |
    R - C - OH  +  R'-OH  <=====>  R - C - OH
                                     |
                                    +O - H
                                     |
                                     R'

Step 3: Proton transfer (PT) to an -OH oxygen to generate a good leaving group
        OH                           OH
        |                            |
    R - C - OH  <==============>  R - C - +OH2
        |                            |
       +O - H                        O - R'
        |
        R'

Step 4: Elimination of neutral H2O molecule
        OH
        |                               +OH
    R - C - +OH2  <=====>  - H2O  +  R - C - OR'
        |
        O - R'

Step 5: Deprotonation yielding the neutral ester
        +OH                          O
         ||                          ||
    R - C - OR'   <=====>  - H+  +  R - C - OR'
```

#### Stereochemical & Isotopic Tracer Evidence:
When an alcohol enriched with isotopic oxygen-18 ($\mathrm{R'-{}^{18}OH}$) reacts with benzoic acid:

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH} + \mathrm{CH_3{}^{18}OH} \xrightleftharpoons{\mathrm{H}^+} \mathrm{C_6H_5CO{}^{18}OCH_3} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

*Crucial Inference:* The oxygen in the expelled $\mathrm{H_2O}$ originates exclusively from the carboxylic acid ($-\mathrm{OH}$ cleavage), **not** from the alcohol. The alcohol undergoes $\mathrm{O-H}$ bond cleavage.

#### Steric Hindrance & Reactivity Orders:
Because the rate-determining step involves nucleophilic addition onto a planar $sp^2$ carbon to produce a crowded $sp^3$ tetrahedral intermediate:
- **Order of reactivity of carboxylic acids:**
  

$$
\mathrm{HCOOH} > \mathrm{CH_3COOH} > \mathrm{(CH_3)_2CHCOOH} > \mathrm{(CH_3)_3CCOOH}
$$

- **Order of reactivity of alcohols:**
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3OH} > 1^\circ \text{ alcohol} > 2^\circ \text{ alcohol} \gg 3^\circ \text{ alcohol}
$$

  *(Note: $3^\circ$ alcohols predominantly undergo dehydration to alkenes under strong acid catalysis via carbocation pathways).*

### 1.3 Reactions with $\mathrm{PCl_5}$, $\mathrm{PCl_3}$, and $\mathrm{SOCl_2}$
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**NCERT Reference:** Section 12.8.2  

The $-\mathrm{OH}$ group is readily replaced by a chlorine atom ($-\mathrm{Cl}$) upon treatment with inorganic acid chlorides.

#### Stoichiometric Equations:

1. **Reaction with Phosphorus Pentachloride ($\mathrm{PCl_5}$):**
   

$$
\mathrm{RCOOH} + \mathrm{PCl_5} \longrightarrow \mathrm{RCOCl} + \mathrm{POCl_3} + \mathrm{HCl}\uparrow
$$

2. **Reaction with Phosphorus Trichloride ($\mathrm{PCl_3}$):**
   

$$
3\,\mathrm{RCOOH} + \mathrm{PCl_3} \longrightarrow 3\,\mathrm{RCOCl} + \mathrm{H_3PO_3}
$$

   *(By-product: Phosphorous acid / orthophosphorous acid, $\mathrm{H_3PO_3}$).*

3. **Reaction with Thionyl Chloride ($\mathrm{SOCl_2}$):**
   

$$
\mathrm{RCOOH} + \mathrm{SOCl_2} \xrightarrow{\text{reflux (often with pyridine)}} \mathrm{RCOCl} + \mathrm{SO_2}\uparrow + \mathrm{HCl}\uparrow
$$

> **Why Thionyl Chloride ($\mathrm{SOCl_2}$) is Universally Preferred:**  
> The by-products ($\mathrm{SO_2}$ and $\mathrm{HCl}$) are non-condensable gases under reaction conditions. They escape into the atmosphere (or scrubber), driving the equilibrium forward and leaving behind the pure acyl chloride without requiring tedious solvent fractional distillation.

***

### 1.4 Reaction with Ammonia ($\mathrm{NH_3}$) and Subsequent Thermolysis
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**NCERT Reference:** Section 12.8.2  

Carboxylic acids react with ammonia via an acid-base neutralization to form ammonium carboxylate salts. Upon strong heating ($\Delta$), these salts eliminate water to yield primary acid amides.

#### Generalized Scheme:

$$
\mathrm{RCOOH} + \mathrm{NH_3} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{RCOO^-\overset{+}{N}H_4} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{-\mathrm{H_2O}} \mathrm{RCONH_2}
$$

#### Canonical High-Yield Transformations:

1. **Acetamide Formation:**
   

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COOH} + \mathrm{NH_3} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{CH_3COONH_4} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{-\mathrm{H_2O}} \mathrm{CH_3CONH_2}
$$

2. **Benzamide Formation:**
   

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH} + \mathrm{NH_3} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{C_6H_5COONH_4} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{-\mathrm{H_2O}} \mathrm{C_6H_5CONH_2}
$$

3. **Phthalimide Synthesis from Phthalic Acid (Three-Stage Reaction):**
   - **Stage 1 (Salt Formation):**
     

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_4(COOH)_2} + 2\,\mathrm{NH_3} \longrightarrow \mathrm{C_6H_4(COO^-\overset{+}{N}H_4)_2}\quad\text{(Ammonium phthalate)}
$$

   - **Stage 2 (Mild Heating / Intermolecular Dehydration):**
     

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_4(COO^-\overset{+}{N}H_4)_2} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{-2\,\mathrm{H_2O}} \mathrm{C_6H_4(CONH_2)_2}\quad\text{(Phthalamide)}
$$

   - **Stage 3 (Strong Heating / Intramolecular Deammoniation):**
     

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_4(CONH_2)_2} \xrightarrow[\text{Strong }\Delta]{-\mathrm{NH_3}} \mathrm{C_6H_4(CO)_2NH}\quad\text{(Phthalimide)}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Phthalic Acid} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{NH_3}} \text{Ammonium Phthalate} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{-2\,\mathrm{H_2O}} \text{Phthalamide} \xrightarrow[\text{Strong }\Delta]{-\mathrm{NH_3}} \text{Phthalimide}}
$$

### 2.1 Decarboxylation Reactions
Decarboxylation involves the permanent expulsion of the carboxyl carbon as carbon dioxide ($\mathrm{CO_2}$).

#### A. Soda-Lime Decarboxylation (Oakwood Reaction)
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**NCERT Reference:** Section 12.8.3  

Sodium salts of carboxylic acids, when intimately ground and heated with **soda-lime** (a dry mixture of solid $\mathrm{NaOH}$ and $\mathrm{CaO}$ in a $3:1$ mass ratio), eliminate sodium carbonate to yield an alkane containing **one less carbon atom** than the precursor parent carboxylic acid.

$$
\mathrm{R-COONa} + \mathrm{NaOH} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\mathrm{CaO}} \mathrm{R-H} + \mathrm{Na_2CO_3}
$$

**Example:**

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CH_2COONa} + \mathrm{NaOH} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\mathrm{CaO}} \mathrm{CH_3-CH_3} + \mathrm{Na_2CO_3}
$$

> **Role of Calcium Oxide ($\mathrm{CaO}$):**
> 1. $\mathrm{NaOH}$ is extremely hygroscopic. $\mathrm{CaO}$ acts as a dehydrating/desiccating agent, keeping the reaction mixture completely anhydrous.
> 2. $\mathrm{CaO}$ elevates the fusion temperature, prevents the glass boiling tubes from melting/softening, and avoids thermal cracking by glass etching.

#### B. Kolbe’s Electrolytic Decarboxylation
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐  
An aqueous solution of sodium or potassium carboxylate undergoes electrolysis to yield symmetrical alkanes containing an **even number of carbon atoms** at the anode.

- **Overall Reaction:**
  

$$
2\,\mathrm{RCOOK} + 2\,\mathrm{H_2O} \xrightarrow{\text{Electrolysis}} \mathrm{R-R} + 2\,\mathrm{CO_2}\uparrow + \mathrm{H_2}\uparrow + 2\,\mathrm{KOH}
$$

- **Electrode Half-Reactions:**
  - **At Anode (Oxidation):**
    

$$
2\,\mathrm{RCOO}^- \xrightarrow{-2e^-} 2\,\mathrm{RCOO^\bullet} \xrightarrow{-2\,\mathrm{CO_2}} 2\,\mathrm{R^\bullet} \longrightarrow \mathrm{R-R}
$$

  - **At Cathode (Reduction):**
    

$$
2\,\mathrm{H_2O} + 2e^- \longrightarrow 2\,\mathrm{OH}^- + \mathrm{H_2}\uparrow
$$

- *Limitation:* Methane ($\mathrm{CH_4}$) **cannot** be prepared by Kolbe's electrolysis because the radical coupling requires at least one carbon per alkyl radical.

### 2.2 Reduction of Carboxylic Acids
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**NCERT Reference:** Section 12.8.3  

Carboxylic acids are resistant to mild reducing agents (such as $\mathrm{NaBH_4}$ or catalytic $\mathrm{H_2}/\mathrm{Ni}$ at ambient pressure) due to resonance stabilization of the carboxylate-like carbonyl system.

#### Reagents & Specificity:
1. **Lithium Aluminium Hydride ($\mathrm{LiAlH_4}$ in dry ether, followed by $\mathrm{H_3O^+}$):**  
   Smoothly reduces carboxylic acids to primary ($1^\circ$) alcohols in high yields.
   

$$
\mathrm{RCOOH} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) }\mathrm{H_3O^+}]{\text{(i) }\mathrm{LiAlH_4 / dry\ ether}} \mathrm{RCH_2OH}
$$

2. **Diborane ($\mathrm{B_2H_6}$ in THF):**  
   Diborane reduces carboxylic acids rapidly without reducing other reducible functionalities such as esters, nitro ($-\mathrm{NO_2}$), and halogen ($-\mathrm{X}$) groups.
   

$$
\mathrm{RCOOH} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) }\mathrm{H_2O}]{\text{(i) }\mathrm{B_2H_6 / THF}} \mathrm{RCH_2OH}
$$

> **Critical Selectivity Alert:**  
> $\mathrm{NaBH_4}$ **does not** reduce carboxylic acids or esters. It selectively reduces only aldehydes, ketones, and acid halides.

#### Industrial Conversion to Primary Alcohols:
Because $\mathrm{LiAlH_4}$ is expensive, commercial processes first esterify the carboxylic acid, followed by catalytic hydrogenation:

$$
\mathrm{RCOOH} + \mathrm{R'OH} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{H}^+} \mathrm{RCOOR'} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

$$
\mathrm{RCOOR'} + 2\,\mathrm{H_2} \xrightarrow[\text{Heat, Pressure}]{\text{Cu-Cr (Copper Chromite catalyst)}} \mathrm{RCH_2OH} + \mathrm{R'OH}
$$

### 2.3 Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (H.V.Z.) Reaction
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**NCERT Reference:** Section 12.8.4  

#### Structural Criterion:
The carboxylic acid **must contain at least one $\alpha$-hydrogen atom**. Carboxylic acids lacking $\alpha$-hydrogens (e.g., $\mathrm{HCOOH}$, $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3CCOOH}$, $\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH}$) do not undergo this reaction.

#### Reaction Scheme:
When treated with chlorine ($\mathrm{Cl_2}$) or bromine ($\mathrm{Br_2}$) in the presence of a catalytic amount of red phosphorus, aliphatic carboxylic acids undergo selective halogenation at the $\alpha$-carbon to yield $\alpha$-halocarboxylic acids.

$$
\mathrm{R-CH_2-COOH} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) }\mathrm{H_2O}]{\text{(i) }\mathrm{X_2 / \text{Red P}}} \mathrm{R-CH(X)-COOH} \quad (\mathrm{X} = \mathrm{Cl, Br})
$$

**Example 1:**

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COOH} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) }\mathrm{H_2O}]{\text{(i) }\mathrm{Br_2 / \text{Red P}}} \mathrm{BrCH_2COOH} \quad\text{($\alpha$-Bromoethanoic acid)}
$$

**Example 2 (Excess Reagent):**

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COOH} \xrightarrow[\text{excess }\mathrm{Cl_2 / \text{Red P}}]{} \mathrm{CH_2ClCOOH} \xrightarrow{} \mathrm{CHCl_2COOH} \xrightarrow{} \mathrm{CCl_3COOH}
$$

#### Mechanistic Rationale:
1. Red phosphorus reacts *in situ* with the halogen: $2\,\mathrm{P} + 3\,\mathrm{X_2} \longrightarrow 2\,\mathrm{PX_3}$.
2. $\mathrm{PX_3}$ transforms a small equilibrium fraction of carboxylic acid into the acid halide: $\mathrm{RCH_2COOH} + \mathrm{PX_3} \longrightarrow \mathrm{RCH_2COX}$.
3. The acid halide undergoes rapid keto-enol tautomerism to its enol form: $\mathrm{R-CH=C(X)OH}$.
4. The enol double bond electrophilically attacks $\mathrm{X_2}$, yielding the $\alpha$-halo acyl halide: $\mathrm{R-CH(X)-COX}$.
5. Rapid exchange with unreacted carboxylic acid regenerates the acid halide and yields the final product:
   

$$
\mathrm{R-CH(X)-COX} + \mathrm{R-CH_2-COOH} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{R-CH(X)-COOH} + \mathrm{R-CH_2-COX}
$$

## 3. Ring Substitution Reactions of Aromatic Carboxylic Acids

**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**NCERT Reference:** Section 12.8.4  

The carboxyl group ($-\mathrm{COOH}$) attached to an aromatic ring acts as an **electron-withdrawing group ($-M$ resonance effect and $-I$ inductive effect)**.

```
Resonance structures of Benzoic Acid:
    :O:                :O:-               :O:-               :O:-               :O:
    ||                 |                  |                  |                  ||
    C - OH             C - OH             C - OH             C - OH             C - OH
   /                  //                 //                 //                 /
 // \               //  \+              /   \              /   \+            // \
|    |   <=====>   |     |   <=====>   |     |+  <=====>  |     |   <=====> |    |
 \\ /               \\  /               \\  /              \\  /             \\ /
```

#### Key Consequences:
1. **Deactivation:** The $-\mathrm{COOH}$ group withdraws $\pi$-electron density from the aromatic core, making the ring less nucleophilic than benzene toward electrophiles.
2. **Meta-Direction:** The resonance structures show that the ortho- and para-positions acquire partial positive charges ($\delta^+$). The electrophile ($\mathrm{E}^+$) attacks the meta-position, where electron deficiency is comparatively lower.

***

### 3.1 Nitration of Benzoic Acid
Benzoic acid reacts with a nitrating mixture (concentrated $\mathrm{HNO_3} +$ concentrated $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$) at elevated temperatures to yield $m$-nitrobenzoic acid.

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH} + \mathrm{HNO_3} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{conc. }\mathrm{H_2SO_4}} \underset{m\text{-Nitrobenzoic acid}}{\mathrm{C_6H_4(NO_2)(COOH)}} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

- **Electrophile:** Nitronium ion ($\mathrm{NO_2^+}$).
- **Position of attack:** Meta ($C_3$ or $C_5$).

***

### 3.2 Bromination of Benzoic Acid
Benzoic acid reacts with liquid bromine in the presence of anhydrous iron(III) bromide ($\mathrm{FeBr_3}$) to yield $m$-bromobenzoic acid.

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH} + \mathrm{Br_2} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{FeBr_3}} \underset{m\text{-Bromobenzoic acid}}{\mathrm{C_6H_4(Br)(COOH)}} + \mathrm{HBr}
$$

- **Electrophile:** Bromonium complex ($\mathrm{Br^\delta+ \cdots FeBr_4^\delta-}$).

***

### 3.3 The Anomaly: Why Aromatic Acids Do NOT Undergo Friedel-Crafts Reactions
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  
*(Consistently tested in CBSE reasoning questions).*

**Question:** Benzoic acid does not undergo Friedel-Crafts Alkylation or Friedel-Crafts Acylation. Why?

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH} + \mathrm{CH_3Cl} \xrightarrow{\text{anhyd. }\mathrm{AlCl_3}} \mathbf{\text{NO REACTION}}
$$

**Examiner-Required Two-Point Answer:**
1. **Severe Ring Deactivation:** The carboxyl group ($-\mathrm{COOH}$) exerts strong electron-withdrawing resonance ($-M$) and inductive ($-I$) effects, deactivating the benzene ring toward electrophilic attack by relatively weak Friedel-Crafts carbocations ($\mathrm{R^+}$ or $\mathrm{R-\overset{+}{C}=O}$).
2. **Lewis Acid Deactivation via Catalyst Poisoning:** The catalyst, anhydrous $\mathrm{AlCl_3}$, is a strong Lewis acid. It coordinates with the lone pair of electrons on the basic carboxyl oxygen atom:
   

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH} + \mathrm{AlCl_3} \longrightarrow \mathrm{C_6H_5-\overset{+}{C}(OH)=O\!: \rightarrow AlCl_3^-}
$$

   This coordination introduces a formal positive charge adjacent to the ring, deactivating the aromatic ring toward any electrophilic attack.

## 4. Industrial and Laboratory Uses of Carboxylic Acids

**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐  
**NCERT Reference:** Section 12.9  

| Carboxylic Acid | Systematic IUPAC Name | Key Commercial / Laboratory Applications |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Methanoic Acid** ($\mathrm{HCOOH}$) | Methanoic acid | Leather tanning, coagulating agent in rubber manufacturing, textile dyeing and finishing processes. |
| **Ethanoic Acid** ($\mathrm{CH_3COOH}$) | Ethanoic acid | Industrial solvent, manufacture of cellulose acetate (rayon fibers, safety films), formation of vinegar ($5\text{--}8\%$ aqueous solution). |
| **Hexanedioic Acid** ($\mathrm{HOOC(CH_2)_4COOH}$) | Adipic acid | Monomer for the industrial polycondensation synthesis of **Nylon-6,6** with hexamethylenediamine. |
| **Benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic Acid** | Terephthalic acid | Monomer condensation with ethylene glycol to manufacture **Dacron (Terylene)** polyester fibers. |
| **Benzoic Acid** ($\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH}$) | Benzoic acid | Perfumery, synthesis of aniline/dyes; its sodium salt (**Sodium Benzoate**, $\mathrm{C_6H_5COONa}$) is a food preservative. |
| **Higher Fatty Acids** ($\mathrm{C_{15}\text{--}C_{17}}$) | Palmitic, Stearic acids | Manufacture of soaps (alkali metal salts) and industrial synthetic detergents. |

### [CBSE 2024, 3 Marks]
**Question:** An organic compound $[\mathrm{A}]$ with molecular formula $\mathrm{C_8H_8O}$ forms an orange-red precipitate with 2,4-DNP reagent and gives a yellow precipitate on heating with $\mathrm{I_2}$ and $\mathrm{NaOH}$. It neither reduces Tollens' reagent nor Fehling's solution. Compound $[\mathrm{A}]$ on vigorous oxidation with alkaline $\mathrm{KMnO_4}$ followed by acidification yields compound $[\mathrm{B}]$ of molecular formula $\mathrm{C_7H_6O_2}$. Compound $[\mathrm{B}]$ on reaction with $\mathrm{SOCl_2}$ gives $[\mathrm{C}]$. Identify compounds $[\mathrm{A}]$, $[\mathrm{B}]$, and $[\mathrm{C}]$. Write the reaction of $[\mathrm{B}]$ with bromine in the presence of $\mathrm{FeBr_3}$.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. **Identification of $[\mathrm{A}]$:**
   - Forms orange-red precipitate with 2,4-DNP $\Longrightarrow$ Contains a carbonyl group ($>\!\mathrm{C=O}$).
   - Does not reduce Tollens' or Fehling's reagent $\Longrightarrow$ It is a **ketone**, not an aldehyde.
   - Gives yellow precipitate of iodoform ($\mathrm{CHI_3}$) with $\mathrm{I_2}/\mathrm{NaOH}$ $\Longrightarrow$ It is a **methyl ketone** ($-\mathrm{COCH_3}$).
   - Molecular formula is $\mathrm{C_8H_8O}$. Accounting for $-\mathrm{COCH_3}$ ($2$ carbons), the remaining moiety is $-\mathrm{C_6H_5}$ (phenyl ring).
   - Thus, $[\mathrm{A}]$ is **Acetophenone** ($\mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3}$).

2. **Identification of $[\mathrm{B}]$:**
   - Acetophenone on vigorous oxidation with alkaline $\mathrm{KMnO_4}$ yields benzoic acid:
     

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) }\mathrm{H_3O^+}]{\text{(i) }\mathrm{KMnO_4 / KOH, }\Delta} \mathrm{C_6H_5COOH}\quad\text{($[\mathrm{B}]$, Benzoic acid, }\mathrm{C_7H_6O_2}\text{)}
$$

3. **Identification of $[\mathrm{C}]$:**
   - Benzoic acid on treatment with $\mathrm{SOCl_2}$ replaces $-\mathrm{OH}$ with $-\mathrm{Cl}$:
     

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH} + \mathrm{SOCl_2} \longrightarrow \mathrm{C_6H_5COCl}\quad\text{($[\mathrm{C}]$, Benzoyl chloride)} + \mathrm{SO_2}\uparrow + \mathrm{HCl}\uparrow
$$

4. **Bromination of $[\mathrm{B}]$:**
   

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH} + \mathrm{Br_2} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{FeBr_3}} m\text{-}\mathrm{Br-C_6H_4-COOH} + \mathrm{HBr}
$$

### [CBSE 2023, 2 Marks]
**Question:** Write the chemical equations and conditions for the following conversions:
1. Propanoic acid to 2-Bromopropanoic acid.
2. Phthalic acid to Phthalimide.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**

1. **Propanoic acid $\longrightarrow$ 2-Bromopropanoic acid (Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky Reaction):**
   

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CH_2COOH} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) }\mathrm{H_2O}]{\text{(i) }\mathrm{Br_2 / \text{Red P}}} \underset{\text{2-Bromopropanoic acid}}{\mathrm{CH_3CH(Br)COOH}} + \mathrm{HBr}
$$

2. **Phthalic acid $\longrightarrow$ Phthalimide:**
   

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_4(COOH)_2} + 2\,\mathrm{NH_3} \longrightarrow \mathrm{C_6H_4(COONH_4)_2} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{-2\,\mathrm{H_2O}} \mathrm{C_6H_4(CONH_2)_2} \xrightarrow[\text{Strong }\Delta]{-\mathrm{NH_3}} \mathrm{C_6H_4(CO)_2NH}
$$

### [CBSE 2022, 2 Marks]
**Question:** Give plausible explanations for the following:
1. Benzoic acid does not undergo Friedel-Crafts reaction.
2. Formic acid ($\mathrm{HCOOH}$) reduces Tollens' reagent, but acetic acid ($\mathrm{CH_3COOH}$) does not.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. The $-\mathrm{COOH}$ group is strongly electron-withdrawing ($-M$ and $-I$ effects) and deactivates the benzene ring. Furthermore, the Lewis acid catalyst ($\mathrm{AlCl_3}$) coordinates with the basic carboxyl group, forming a complex that places a positive charge adjacent to the ring, preventing electrophilic substitution.
2. The molecular structure of formic acid contains both an aldehyde functionality and a carboxylic acid functionality:
   ```
       O
     //
   H - C - OH  <-- Contains a formyl (aldehyde) H-C=O group
   ```
   Because of this free formyl hydrogen, formic acid readily acts as a reducing agent and oxidizes to $\mathrm{CO_2}$ and $\mathrm{H_2O}$, reducing Tollens' reagent to metallic silver. In acetic acid ($\mathrm{CH_3-COOH}$), this oxidizable formyl hydrogen is absent.

### [CBSE 2021, Compartment, 3 Marks]
**Question:** How will you carry out the following multi-step conversions?
1. Benzoic acid to $m$-Nitrobenzyl alcohol.
2. Ethanoic acid to 2-Hydroxyethanoic acid (Glycolic acid).

**Step-by-Step Solution:**

1. **Benzoic acid to $m$-Nitrobenzyl alcohol:**
   - **Step 1 (Nitration):**
     

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH} + \text{conc. }\mathrm{HNO_3} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{conc. }\mathrm{H_2SO_4}} m\text{-}\mathrm{NO_2-C_6H_4-COOH}
$$

   - **Step 2 (Selective Reduction):**  
     Reduce the carboxyl group without reducing the nitro group using **Diborane** ($\mathrm{B_2H_6}$) (or $\mathrm{LiAlH_4}$ under controlled conditions):
     

$$
m\text{-}\mathrm{NO_2-C_6H_4-COOH} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) }\mathrm{H_3O^+}]{\text{(i) }\mathrm{B_2H_6 / THF}} m\text{-}\mathrm{NO_2-C_6H_4-CH_2OH}
$$

2. **Ethanoic acid to 2-Hydroxyethanoic acid:**
   - **Step 1 (HVZ Halogenation):**
     

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COOH} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) }\mathrm{H_2O}]{\text{(i) }\mathrm{Cl_2 / \text{Red P}}} \underset{\text{Chloroacetic acid}}{\mathrm{Cl-CH_2-COOH}}
$$

   - **Step 2 (Nucleophilic Substitution):**
     

$$
\mathrm{Cl-CH_2-COOH} + 2\,\mathrm{KOH(aq)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathrm{HO-CH_2-COOK} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{H}^+} \underset{\text{Glycolic acid}}{\mathrm{HO-CH_2-COOH}}
$$

### [CBSE 2020, 1 Mark]
**Question:** Arrange the following in decreasing order of their reactivity toward esterification with ethanol:

$$
\mathrm{(CH_3)_3C-COOH}, \quad \mathrm{HCOOH}, \quad \mathrm{CH_3COOH}, \quad \mathrm{(CH_3)_2CH-COOH}
$$

**Solution:**
Esterification rate is governed by **steric hindrance** at the carbonyl carbon during the formation of the tetrahedral $sp^3$ intermediate. Less sterically hindered acids react faster:

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{HCOOH} > \mathrm{CH_3COOH} > \mathrm{(CH_3)_2CHCOOH} > \mathrm{(CH_3)_3CCOOH}}
$$

## 6. Examiner Traps & Common Student Mistakes

| Trap / Exam Error Point | Common Erroneous Student Conception | Correct Chemical Reality / Marking Criterion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **HVZ Reaction Reagents** | Writing "$\mathrm{Br_2} / \mathrm{FeBr_3}$" for $\alpha$-bromination of aliphatic acids. | The correct reagent is **$\mathrm{Br_2} / \text{Red P}$ followed by $\mathrm{H_2O}$**. $\mathrm{FeBr_3}$ triggers aromatic ring halogenation, not aliphatic $\alpha$-enolization. |
| **HVZ Substrate Criteria** | Attempting HVZ on Trimethylacetic acid, Formic acid, or Benzoic acid. | **Must have $\alpha$-hydrogen atoms.** $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3C-COOH}$, $\mathrm{HCOOH}$, and $\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH}$ have zero $\alpha$-hydrogens and yield **No Reaction**. |
| **Esterification Oxygen Source** | Claiming water's oxygen comes from the alcohol. | As proven by ${}^{18}\mathrm{O}$ isotopic labeling, the $-\mathrm{OH}$ group is excised from the **carboxylic acid**, and the $-\mathrm{H}$ from the **alcohol**. |
| **Decarboxylation Carbon Count** | Expecting an alkane with the same number of carbons from soda-lime. | Soda-lime decarboxylation produces an alkane with **one fewer carbon atom** ($n-1$) than the starting carboxylate salt. |
| **Carboxylic Acid Reduction** | Writing $\mathrm{NaBH_4}$ to reduce $-\mathrm{COOH}$ to $-\mathrm{CH_2OH}$. | $\mathrm{NaBH_4}$ **cannot** reduce carboxylic acids. You must specify **$\mathrm{LiAlH_4}$ in ether** or **$\mathrm{B_2H_6}$ in THF**. |
| **Friedel-Crafts on Benzoic Acid** | Predicting ortho- or para-substituted alkylated benzoic acid. | Benzoic acid **does not undergo** Friedel-Crafts alkylation or acylation due to strong ring deactivation and $\mathrm{AlCl_3}$ coordination. |

## 7. Speed Hacks & Golden Revision Rules

### 1. The Carbonyl Cleavage Decider
```
Reagent has Nucleophile (Nu:)?  ===> Cleaves C-OH bond  (Yields Acyl Deriv: R-CO-Nu)
Reagent is Soda-Lime (NaOH/CaO)? ===> Cleaves C-COOH bond (Loss of CO2; n-1 Alkane)
Reagent is X2 / Red P?           ===> Cleaves alpha C-H bond (HVZ Substitution)
Reagent is Electrophile (E+)?    ===> Attacks Benzene Ring at Meta position
```

### 2. Conversions Cheat Matrix
- **Up one carbon step ($n \to n+1$):**
  

$$
\mathrm{R-X} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{KCN}} \mathrm{R-CN} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{H_3O^+}} \mathrm{R-COOH}
$$

- **Down one carbon step ($n \to n-1$):**
  

$$
\mathrm{R-COOH} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{SOCl_2}} \mathrm{R-COCl} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{NH_3}} \mathrm{R-CONH_2} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Br_2 / KOH}} \mathrm{R-NH_2} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{HNO_2}} \mathrm{R-OH}
$$

  *Alternatively:*
  

$$
\mathrm{R-COOH} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{NaOH}} \mathrm{R-COONa} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\mathrm{NaOH / CaO}} \mathrm{R-H}
$$
