## 1. In-Depth Pedagogical Theory & Concepts

### 1.1 The Carbonyl Group: Electronic Structure & Geometry
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(Crucial for Board reasoning questions & mechanistic foundation)*

#### Definition & Core Orbital Concept
The carbonyl group consists of a carbon atom doubly bonded to an oxygen atom:

$$
\mathrm{>C=O}
$$

* **Hybridisation:** 
  The carbonyl carbon atom utilizes three $\mathrm{sp^2}$ hybrid orbitals and possesses one unhybridised $2\mathrm{p}_z$ orbital. The carbonyl oxygen atom is also regarded as $\mathrm{sp^2}$ hybridised in valence bond theory, holding two lone pairs in two $\mathrm{sp^2}$ hybrid orbitals.
* **$\sigma$-Bond Framework:** 
  The carbonyl carbon forms three coplanar $\sigma$-bonds using its three $\mathrm{sp^2}$ hybrid orbitals:
  1. One $\sigma$-bond with oxygen via $\mathrm{sp^2(C)}-\mathrm{sp^2(O)}$ axial overlap.
  2. Two $\sigma$-bonds with two other attached atoms/groups (carbon or hydrogen) via $\mathrm{sp^2-s}$ or $\mathrm{sp^2-sp^3}$ overlap.
* **$\pi$-Bond Framework:** 
  The fourth valence electron of carbon remains in an unhybridised $2\mathrm{p}_z$ atomic orbital oriented perpendicular to the nodal plane of the $\sigma$-bonds. Lateral (sideways) overlap between this carbon $2\mathrm{p}_z$ orbital and an oxygen $2\mathrm{p}_z$ orbital yields a $\pi$ molecular orbital residing above and below the molecular plane.

```
       R                  :O:
        \                //
         C(sp²) === C === O(sp²)
        /                \\
       R'                 : 
   [Trigonal Planar, ~120° Bond Angles]
```

* **Geometry & Bond Angles:** 
  The three atoms attached to the carbonyl carbon lie in a single plane. The molecular geometry around the carbonyl carbon is **trigonal planar**, with bond angles approaching:
  

$$
\angle \mathrm{R-C-R'} \approx \angle \mathrm{R-C=O} \approx 120^\circ
$$

  *(Note: In methanal $\mathrm{HCHO}$, $\angle \mathrm{H-C-H} = 116.5^\circ$ and $\angle \mathrm{H-C=O} = 121.8^\circ$ due to steric and electronic repulsion between the $\pi$ electron cloud and the $\mathrm{C-H}$ $\sigma$-bonds).*

### 1.2 Nature of the Carbonyl Dipole & Resonance
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(Repeatedly asked: "Explain why carbonyl compounds are more polar than ethers or alkenes.")*

Oxygen possesses an electronegativity of $3.44$ on the Pauling scale, while carbon has an electronegativity of $2.55$ ($\Delta \chi = 0.89$). Consequently:
1. The $\pi$-electron cloud is displaced strongly toward the electronegative oxygen atom.
2. The carbonyl bond is polar, best described by two principal resonance canonical structures:

$$
\mathrm{\underset{(Neutral/Covalent)}{>C=O} \longleftrightarrow \underset{(Dipolar/Zwitterionic)}{>\overset{\oplus}{C}-\overset{\ominus}{\ddot{O}}:}}
$$

#### Dipole Moment Comparison
Carbonyl compounds have large permanent dipole moments ($\mu = 2.3 - 2.8\ \mathrm{D}$), which are substantially higher than those of ethers ($\mu \approx 1.15\ \mathrm{D}$) and haloalkanes of comparable molar mass.

$$
\boxed{\mu(\text{Aldehydes/Ketones}) \approx 2.3\text{ to }2.8\ \mathrm{D} \quad \gg \quad \mu(\text{Ethers}) \approx 1.15\ \mathrm{D}}
$$

#### Dual Reactivity Center (Electrophile vs. Nucleophile)
* The carbonyl carbon is electron-deficient ($\delta^+$) and functions as an **electrophilic center (Lewis acid)**.
* The carbonyl oxygen is electron-rich ($\delta^-$) and functions as a **nucleophilic/basic center (Lewis base)**.

$$
\begin{matrix}
\delta^+\quad \delta^-\\
\mathrm{>C = O}
\end{matrix}
$$

### 1.3 Strict NCERT Nomenclature Rules
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(Guaranteed 1 to 2 marks in CBSE Board Paper)*

#### A. Aldehydes ($\mathrm{-CHO}$)
1. **Acyclic systems:** The longest carbon chain containing the $-\mathrm{CHO}$ group provides the parent alkane name. The suffix **"-al"** replaces the ending "-e" of the alkane:
   

$$
\text{Alkane} - \text{"e"} + \text{"al"} \longrightarrow \text{Alkanal}
$$

   * The carbonyl carbon of $-\mathrm{CHO}$ is **always assigned position 1** ($\mathrm{C\text{-}1}$). Hence, the locant $1$ is usually omitted unless required to prevent ambiguity.
2. **Special IUPAC Rule for 3 or more $-\mathrm{CHO}$ groups:**
   If three or more aldehyde groups are attached directly to an unbranched carbon chain, none of the $-\mathrm{CHO}$ carbons are included in the parent chain numbering. The suffix **"-carbaldehyde"** is appended to the parent alkane:
   

$$
\mathrm{OHC-CH_2-CH(CHO)-CH_2-CHO} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \text{Propane-1,2,3-tricarbaldehyde}
$$

3. **Ring systems:** When $-\mathrm{CHO}$ is attached directly to a carbocyclic or heterocyclic ring, the suffix **"-carbaldehyde"** is used:
   * $\mathrm{C_6H_{11}-CHO}$ $\Longrightarrow$ **Cyclohexanecarbaldehyde**
   * $\mathrm{C_6H_5-CHO}$ $\Longrightarrow$ **Benzaldehyde** *(IUPAC accepted)* or **Benzenecarbaldehyde**

#### B. Ketones ($\mathrm{>C=O}$)
1. Suffix **"-one"** replaces the ending "-e" of the parent alkane:
   

$$
\text{Alkane} - \text{"e"} + \text{"one"} \longrightarrow \text{Alkanone}
$$

2. The parent chain is numbered from the terminal carbon that gives the carbonyl carbon the **lowest possible locant**.
3. Ketones are classified as:
   * **Simple/Symmetrical:** Two identical alkyl/aryl groups attached to carbonyl carbon (e.g., $\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3}$, Propan-2-one).
   * **Mixed/Unsymmetrical:** Two distinct groups attached to carbonyl carbon (e.g., $\mathrm{CH_3COCH_2CH_3}$, Butan-2-one).
4. When carbonyl groups act as a substituent (when a higher-priority group such as $-\mathrm{COOH}$ or $-\mathrm{CHO}$ is present):
   * Prefix used: **"oxo-"** (or rarely "keto-").
   * Example: $\mathrm{CH_3-CO-CH_2-CHO} \Longrightarrow$ **3-Oxobutanal**.

### 1.5 Isomerism in Aldehydes and Ketones
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐

Aldehydes and ketones exhibit structural and stereoisomerism:

1. **Chain Isomerism:**
   Molecules with the same functional group and molecular formula differ in the carbon skeleton:
   * $\mathrm{C_4H_8O}$:
     

$$
\mathrm{\underset{Butanal}{CH_3-CH_2-CH_2-CHO}} \quad \text{and} \quad \mathrm{\underset{2-Methylpropanal}{(CH_3)_2CH-CHO}}
$$

2. **Position Isomerism:**
   Observed in ketones with $\ge 5$ carbons due to the differing locant of the carbonyl group:
   * $\mathrm{C_5H_{10}O}$:
     

$$
\mathrm{\underset{Pentan-2-one}{CH_3-CO-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3}} \quad \text{and} \quad \mathrm{\underset{Pentan-3-one}{CH_3-CH_2-CO-CH_2-CH_3}}
$$

   *(Aldehydes cannot exhibit position isomerism for $-\mathrm{CHO}$ in an open chain because $-\mathrm{CHO}$ is always terminal at $\mathrm{C\text{-}1}$).*

3. **Functional Group Isomerism:**
   Aldehydes and ketones with the general molecular formula $\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n}\mathrm{O}$ ($n \ge 3$) are functional isomers of each other:
   * $\mathrm{C_3H_6O}$:
     

$$
\mathrm{\underset{(Aldehyde)}{CH_3-CH_2-CHO}} \quad \text{and} \quad \mathrm{\underset{(Ketone)}{CH_3-CO-CH_3}}
$$

   * Also isomeric with unsaturated alcohols (e.g., $\mathrm{CH_2=CH-CH_2OH}$) and cyclic ethers (oxiranes/oxetanes).

4. **Metamerism:**
   Observed in ketones ($n \ge 5$) having unequal distribution of alkyl chains on either side of the carbonyl carbon:
   * $\mathrm{C_5H_{10}O}$:
     

$$
\mathrm{\underset{Methyl\ propyl\ ketone}{CH_3-CO-CH_2CH_2CH_3}} \quad \text{and} \quad \mathrm{\underset{Diethyl\ ketone}{CH_3CH_2-CO-CH_2CH_3}}
$$

## 2. Derivations, Structural Comparisons & Mechanisms

### 2.1 Rigorous Comparison: Carbonyl Group vs. Carbon-Carbon Double Bond ($\mathrm{C=C}$)
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(Standard 3-Mark Comparative Theory Question)*

| Characteristic Property | Alkene Double Bond ($\mathrm{>C=C<}$) | Carbonyl Double Bond ($\mathrm{>C=O}$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bond Nature** | $1\ \sigma\ (\mathrm{sp^2-sp^2}) + 1\ \pi\ (\mathrm{2p-2p})$ | $1\ \sigma\ (\mathrm{sp^2-sp^2}) + 1\ \pi\ (\mathrm{2p-2p})$ |
| **Electronegativity Difference** | $\Delta \chi = 0$ (Non-polar covalent) | $\Delta \chi = 3.44 - 2.55 = 0.89$ (Highly Polar) |
| **Electron Distribution** | Symmetrical $\pi$-electron cloud | Unsymmetrical; skewed toward oxygen atom |
| **Dipole Moment ($\mu$)** | $\mu \approx 0\ \mathrm{D}$ (e.g., trans-alkenes = 0) | High permanent dipole ($\mu \approx 2.3 - 2.8\ \mathrm{D}$) |
| **Characteristic Reaction** | **Electrophilic Addition ($\mathrm{Ad_E}$)** | **Nucleophilic Addition ($\mathrm{Ad_N}$)** |
| **Susceptibility to Attack** | Attacked initially by electrophiles ($\mathrm{E^+}$) | Attacked initially by nucleophiles ($\mathrm{Nu^-}$) at $\mathrm{C}$ |
| **Bond Energy** | $E(\mathrm{C=C}) \approx 611\ \mathrm{kJ\ mol^{-1}}$ | $E(\mathrm{C=O}) \approx 745\ \mathrm{kJ\ mol^{-1}}$ (Stronger due to electrostatic contribution) |
| **Bond Length** | $d(\mathrm{C=C}) \approx 134\ \mathrm{pm}$ | $d(\mathrm{C=O}) \approx 122\ \mathrm{pm}$ (Shorter due to polar contraction) |

### 2.2 Mechanism: Nucleophilic Addition to the Carbonyl Group
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(Fundamental mechanism of the entire chapter)*

Because the carbonyl carbon is $\mathrm{sp^2}$ hybridised and planar, it offers minimal steric resistance from either side of the plane. 

#### Step 1: Nucleophilic Attack on Electrophilic Carbon (Slow, Rate-Determining Step)
The nucleophile approaches the planar $\mathrm{sp^2}$ carbonyl carbon from a trajectory roughly perpendicular to the plane of the carbonyl group (Bürgi-Dunitz angle $\approx 107^\circ$). The hybridisation of carbon changes from $\mathrm{sp^2}$ to $\mathrm{sp^3}$:

$$
\mathrm{Nu}^{\ominus} + \begin{matrix}
\mathrm{R_1}\\
\\
\mathrm{R_2}
\end{matrix}>\mathrm{C=O} \xrightarrow[\text{slow}]{\text{r.d.s.}} \left[ \begin{matrix}
\mathrm{R_1}\\
\\
\mathrm{R_2}
\end{matrix}>\overset{\mathrm{Nu}}{\mathrm{C}}-\mathrm{O}^{\ominus} \right]
$$

* *Substrate geometry:* Planar (trigonal, $120^\circ$)
* *Intermediate geometry:* Tetrahedral alkoxide intermediate ($109.5^\circ$)

#### Step 2: Protonation of Tetrahedral Intermediate (Fast Step)
The negatively charged alkoxide oxygen abstracts a proton from the reaction medium (or acid catalyst) to yield the electrically neutral addition product:

$$
\left[ \begin{matrix}
\mathrm{R_1}\\
\\
\mathrm{R_2}
\end{matrix}>\overset{\mathrm{Nu}}{\mathrm{C}}-\mathrm{O}^{\ominus} \right] + \mathrm{H^{\oplus}} \xrightarrow[\text{fast}]{} \begin{matrix}
\mathrm{R_1}\\
\\
\mathrm{R_2}
\end{matrix}>\overset{\mathrm{Nu}}{\mathrm{C}}-\mathrm{OH}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Overall: } \mathrm{>C=O} + \mathrm{H-Nu} \longrightarrow \mathrm{>C(OH)(Nu)}}
$$

### 2.3 Derivation of Reactivity Orders in Nucleophilic Addition
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(Asked almost every year in Board Exams)*

The relative reactivity of carbonyl compounds towards nucleophilic attack follows:

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{HCHO} > \mathrm{CH_3CHO} > \mathrm{CH_3COCH_3} > \mathrm{C_6H_5CHO} > \mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3} > \mathrm{C_6H_5COC_6H_5}}
$$

This order is dictated by two synergistic effects:

#### 1. Inductive / Electronic Factor
* Alkyl groups are electron-donating ($+\mathrm{I}$ effect).
* An increase in the number of alkyl groups attached to the carbonyl carbon disperses the partial positive charge ($\delta^+$), reducing its electrophilicity.

$$
\text{Electrophilicity of Carbon: } \mathrm{HCHO} (\text{no } +\mathrm{I}) > \mathrm{RCHO} (\text{one } +\mathrm{I}) > \mathrm{R_2CO} (\text{two } +\mathrm{I})
$$

#### 2. Steric (Crowding) Factor
* In the transition state, bond angles compress from $120^\circ$ (planar reactant) to $109.5^\circ$ (tetrahedral intermediate).
* Bulky alkyl groups increase steric crowding around the carbon atom in the transition state, raising the activation energy ($\Delta G^\ddagger$) and decreasing the reaction rate.

#### 3. Resonance Effect in Aromatic Aldehydes & Ketones
In benzaldehyde, the carbonyl group is conjugated with the benzene ring. The electron-donating resonance effect ($+\mathrm{R}$) of the phenyl ring increases electron density at the carbonyl carbon:

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5-CHO} \longleftrightarrow \text{[Resonance structures transferring } \pi\text{-electrons from ring to carbonyl]}
$$

This resonance stabilization delocalizes the positive charge into the aromatic ring, making the carbonyl carbon significantly less electrophilic than in aliphatic aldehydes:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CHO} > \mathrm{C_6H_5CHO}
$$

**Question:** Write the IUPAC name of the following compound:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH(CH_3)-CH_2-CO-CH_3}

**Solution:**
1. **Identify Principal Functional Group:** Ketone ($>\mathrm{C=O}$), suffix = "-one".

3. **Numbering:** Must give the lowest locant to the carbonyl carbon:
   * Left-to-right: $\mathrm{C\text{-}4}$ for carbonyl.
   * Right-to-left: $\mathrm{C\text{-}2}$ for carbonyl, $\mathrm{C\text{-}4}$ for methyl. (Correct)
4. **Assemble IUPAC Name:**
   

$$
\boxed{\text{4-Methylpentan-2-one}}
$$

### Example 2 [CBSE Board 2023, 2 Marks]
**Question:** 
(a) Give the IUPAC name of $\mathrm{CH_3-CH=CH-CHO}$.  
(b) Arrange the following compounds in increasing order of their reactivity towards nucleophilic addition reactions:

$$
\mathrm{Ethanal,\ Propanal,\ Propanone,\ Butanone}
$$

**Solution:**
**(a)**
* Longest chain containing $-\mathrm{CHO}$ and double bond = 4 carbons ("but").
* Carbonyl carbon is $\mathrm{C\text{-}1}$: $\mathrm{\overset{4}{C}H_3-\overset{3}{C}H=\overset{2}{C}H-\overset{1}{C}HO}$.

$$
\boxed{\text{But-2-en-1-al (or But-2-enal)}}
$$

**(b)**
* Aldehydes are more reactive than ketones due to lesser steric crowding and smaller $+\mathrm{I}$ effect.

* **Increasing Order of Reactivity:**

$$
\boxed{\text{Butanone} < \text{Propanone} < \text{Propanal} < \text{Ethanal}}

### Example 5 [CBSE Board 2020, 3 Marks]
**Question:** 
An organic compound (A) with molecular formula $\mathrm{C_5H_{10}O}$ forms a phenylhydrazone, gives a negative Tollens' test, but gives a positive iodoform test. On vigorous oxidation, it yields ethanoic acid and propanoic acid.  
(i) Identify compound (A).  
(ii) Write its IUPAC name.  
(iii) Write the chemical equation for the iodoform reaction of compound (A).

**Solution:**
1. **Deductions:**
   * Molecular formula $\mathrm{C_5H_{10}O}$ corresponds to general formula $\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n}\mathrm{O}$ $\rightarrow$ Aldehyde or Ketone.

   * Positive Iodoform test $\rightarrow$ must contain a methyl carbonyl group:
     

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-C(=O)-}
$$

   * The remaining fragment is $\mathrm{C_5H_{10}O} - \mathrm{C_2H_3O} = -\mathrm{CH_2-CH_2-CH_3}$ (propyl group).

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CO-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3}

2. **Answers:**
   * **(i) Structure of (A):** $\mathrm{CH_3-CO-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3}$
   * **(ii) IUPAC Name:** $\boxed{\text{Pentan-2-one}}$
   * **(iii) Iodoform Reaction:**
     

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COCH_2CH_2CH_3 + 3I_2 + 4NaOH \xrightarrow{\Delta} \underset{\substack{\text{Iodoform}\\\text{(Yellow ppt.)}}}{CHI_3\downarrow} + \underset{\text{Sodium butanoate}}{CH_3CH_2CH_2COONa} + 3NaI + 3H_2O}
$$

## 4. Examiner Traps & Common Board Mistakes

### Trap 1: The Numbering of Carbonyl Carbon in Aldehydes
* **Incorrect:** Numbering $\mathrm{CH_3-CH(CH_3)-CHO}$ starting from the left as $\mathrm{\overset{1}{C}H_3-\overset{2}{C}H(CH_3)-\overset{3}{C}HO}$, naming it "3-Methylpropanal".
* **NCERT Rule:** The $-\mathrm{CHO}$ carbon is **always carbon-1 ($\mathrm{C\text{-}1}$)** in an open chain.
* **Correct:** $\mathrm{\overset{3}{C}H_3-\overset{2}{C}H(CH_3)-\overset{1}{C}HO} \Longrightarrow$ **2-Methylpropanal**.

### Trap 2: Suffix Confusion with Multiple Formyl Groups
* **Frequent Error:** Calling $\mathrm{OHC-CH_2-CH(CHO)-CH_2-CHO}$ "3-Formylpentanedial".
* **NCERT Rule:** When three or more identical principal functional groups ($-\mathrm{CHO}$, $-\mathrm{COOH}$, $-\mathrm{CN}$) are directly attached to an acyclic chain, the parent chain is chosen excluding all their carbons, and the suffix **"-carbaldehyde"** is used.
* **Correct:** **Propane-1,2,3-tricarbaldehyde**.

### Trap 3: Dropping the "e" in Saturated vs. Unsaturated Names
* When writing names for unsaturated aldehydes:
  * Incorrect: "But-2-enal" written as "Butan-2-enal".
  * *Distinction:*
    * Saturated: $\text{Alkane} - \text{"e"} + \text{"al"} \rightarrow$ **Alkanal** (e.g., Butanal).
    * Unsaturated: $\text{Alkene} - \text{"e"} + \text{"al"} \rightarrow$ **Alkenal** (e.g., But-2-enal). Adding "an" implies carbon-carbon saturation!

### Trap 4: Dialdehydes Retain the "e"
* For dialdehydes: $\mathrm{OHC-CH_2-CH_2-CHO}$
  * Incorrect: "Butandial"
  * Correct: **Butanedial** *(The terminal "e" of the parent alkane is retained because the suffix begins with a consonant "d")*.

### Trap 5: Misunderstanding Benzaldehyde vs. Aliphatic Aldehydes in Reactivity
* **Examiner Question:** "Why does benzaldehyde undergo nucleophilic addition slower than propanal?"
* **Common Student Mistake:** Answering with *"because of steric hindrance of the bulky phenyl ring"* alone.
* **NCERT Key Phrase for Full Marks:**
  > *"Due to resonance in benzaldehyde, the $+\mathrm{R}$ effect of the benzene ring delocalizes $\pi$-electron density toward the carbonyl carbon, decreasing its electrophilicity ($\delta^+$ character). Resonance stabilization of the unreacted aldehyde raises the activation energy barrier for nucleophilic attack."*

***

## 5. Speed Hacks & Golden Points for Quick Recall

### 1. The IUPAC Priority Ladder (Memory Hook)
To determine whether an oxygen-containing group is named as a suffix or prefix:

$$
\boxed{\text{Acid } (-\mathrm{COOH}) > \text{Ester } (-\mathrm{COOR}) > \text{Amide } (-\mathrm{CONH_2}) > \text{Nitrile } (-\mathrm{CN}) > \mathbf{Aldehyde\ (-CHO)} > \mathbf{Ketone\ (>C=O)} > \text{Alcohol\ (-OH)} > \text{Amine\ (-NH_2)}}
$$

* *Memory Mnemonic:* **C**an **E**very **A**spiring **N**erd **A**lways **K**now **A**ll **A**cid-derivatives?
  * **C**arboxylic acid > **E**ster > **A**mide > **N**itrile > **A**ldehyde > **K**etone > **A**lcohol > **A**mine.

### 2. Isomer Counting Quick-Check ($C_n$ Formulas)
For monocarbonyl compounds with molecular formula $\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n}\mathrm{O}$:
* **$n = 1$ ($\mathrm{CH_2O}$):** Only 1 isomer (Methanal).
* **$n = 2$ ($\mathrm{C_2H_4O}$):** Only 1 carbonyl isomer (Ethanal).
* **$n = 3$ ($\mathrm{C_3H_6O}$):** Exactly **2** carbonyl isomers:
  * 1 Aldehyde: Propanal
  * 1 Ketone: Propan-2-one
* **$n = 4$ ($\mathrm{C_4H_8O}$):** Exactly **3** carbonyl isomers:
  * 2 Aldehydes: Butanal, 2-Methylpropanal
  * 1 Ketone: Butan-2-one
* **$n = 5$ ($\mathrm{C_5H_{10}O}$):** Exactly **7** carbonyl isomers:
  * 4 Aldehydes: Pentanal, 2-Methylbutanal, 3-Methylbutanal, 2,2-Dimethylpropanal
  * 3 Ketones: Pentan-2-one, Pentan-3-one, 3-Methylbutan-2-one

### 3. Reactivity Decider Rule-of-Thumb
To arrange any given set of carbonyls for nucleophilic addition in under 5 seconds:
1. Count the number of attached hydrogen atoms directly on the carbonyl carbon:
   

$$
\text{Two H's (Formaldehyde)} > \text{One H (Aldehyde)} > \text{Zero H's (Ketone)}
$$

2. If comparing within aldehydes:
   

$$
\text{Aliphatic Aldehyde} > \text{Aromatic Aldehyde}
$$

3. If both are aliphatic:
   

$$
\text{Smaller alkyl chain} > \text{Bulkier/branched alkyl chain}
$$

4. Final sequence template:
   

$$
\mathrm{HCHO} > \mathrm{RCHO} > \mathrm{ArCHO} > \mathrm{RCOR} > \mathrm{ArCOR} > \mathrm{ArCOAr}
$$

## 2. General Methods of Preparation (Both Aldehydes and Ketones)

### 2.1 Oxidation of Alcohols ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Controlled oxidation of primary and secondary alcohols yields aldehydes and ketones, respectively.

#### A. Primary Alcohols $\longrightarrow$ Aldehydes
* **Reagent Choice & Rationale**: Strong oxidants like acidified $\text{KMnO}_4$ or $\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$ oxidize primary alcohols directly to carboxylic acids because the intermediate aldehyde hydrates readily to form a gem-diol ($R\text{CH(OH)}_2$), which undergoes rapid further oxidation. To stop at the aldehyde stage, anhydrous non-aqueous reagents must be used.
* **Key Reagents**:
  1. **Pyridinium Chlorochromate (PCC)**: Complex of chromium trioxide with pyridine and $\text{HCl}$ ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_5\text{NH}^+\text{CrO}_3\text{Cl}^-$) in anhydrous dichloromethane ($\text{CH}_2\text{Cl}_2$) at $298\text{ K}$.
  2. **Pyridinium Dichromate (PDC)**: $(\text{C}_5\text{H}_5\text{NH})_2^{2+} \text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}$ in $\text{CH}_2\text{Cl}_2$.
  3. **Collin's Reagent**: $\text{CrO}_3 \cdot 2\text{C}_5\text{H}_5\text{N}$ in $\text{CH}_2\text{Cl}_2$.

$$
\text{R}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH} \xrightarrow[\text{CH}_2\text{Cl}_2, \, 298\text{ K}]{\text{PCC / PDC}} \text{R}-\text{CHO}
$$

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{OH} \xrightarrow[\text{CH}_2\text{Cl}_2]{\text{PCC}} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CHO}
$$

#### B. Secondary Alcohols $\longrightarrow$ Ketones
Secondary alcohols are oxidized to ketones using chromic anhydride ($\text{CrO}_3$) in glacial acetic acid, or acidified potassium dichromate ($\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7 / \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$). Over-oxidation does not occur under mild conditions because ketones lack an $\alpha$-hydrogen on the carbonyl carbon and require destructive $\text{C}-\text{C}$ cleavage.

$$
\text{R}-\text{CH}(\text{OH})-\text{R}' \xrightarrow[\text{glacial }\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}]{\text{CrO}_3} \text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{R}' + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}(\text{OH})-\text{CH}_3 \xrightarrow[\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4]{\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7} \text{CH}_3-\text{CO}-\text{CH}_3
$$

***

### 2.2 Catalytic Dehydrogenation of Alcohols ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Industrial, vapour-phase method suitable for heat-stable volatile alcohols.

* **Reagents & Conditions**: Vapours of alcohol passed over heavy metal catalysts ($\text{Cu}$ or $\text{Ag}$) at $573\text{ K}$ ($300^\circ\text{C}$).
* **Reactions**:
  * **$1^\circ$ Alcohol**:
    

$$
\text{R}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{573\text{ K}, \, \text{Cu}} \text{R}-\text{CHO} + \text{H}_2 \uparrow
$$

  * **$2^\circ$ Alcohol**:
    

$$
\text{R}-\text{CH}(\text{OH})-\text{R}' \xrightarrow[\Delta]{573\text{ K}, \, \text{Cu}} \text{R}-\text{CO}-\text{R}' + \text{H}_2 \uparrow
$$

  * **$3^\circ$ Alcohol (Examiner Trap)**: Tertiary alcohols have no $\alpha$-hydrogen. Instead of dehydrogenation, they undergo **catalytic dehydration** to yield alkenes:
    

$$
(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}-\text{OH} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{573\text{ K}, \, \text{Cu}} \text{CH}_3-\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)=\text{CH}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

***

### 2.3 Ozonolysis of Alkenes ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Two-step oxidative cleavage that cleaves both the $\sigma$ and $\pi$ bonds of the $\text{C}=\text{C}$ double bond.

#### Reaction Scheme:

$$
\text{R}_1\text{R}_2\text{C}=\text{CR}_3\text{R}_4 + \text{O}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{CH}_2\text{Cl}_2, \, 195-200\text{ K}} \text{Molozonide} \longrightarrow \underset{\text{(Ozonide)}}{\begin{matrix}\text{R}_1\backslash\quad/\text{R}_3\\ \text{C}-\text{O}-\text{C}\\ /\quad\backslash\\ \text{R}_2\ \text{O}-\text{O}\ \text{R}_4\end{matrix}} \xrightarrow[\text{reductive workup}]{\text{Zn} / \text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{R}_1\text{R}_2\text{C}=\text{O} + \text{R}_3\text{R}_4\text{C}=\text{O} + \text{ZnO} + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

* **Role of $\text{Zn}$ Dust**: $\text{Zn}$ reduces the intermediate $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ to $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ ($\text{Zn} + \text{H}_2\text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{ZnO} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$). In the absence of $\text{Zn}$, $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ oxidizes aldehydes to carboxylic acids.
* **Product Prediction Rule**:
  * $=\text{CH}_2 \longrightarrow \text{HCHO}$
  * $=\text{CHR} \longrightarrow \text{RCHO}$
  * $=\text{CR}_1\text{R}_2 \longrightarrow \text{R}_1\text{COR}_2$

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)=\text{CH}-\text{CH}_3 \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) }\text{Zn}/\text{H}_2\text{O}]{\text{(i) }\text{O}_3} \text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_3 + \text{CH}_3\text{CHO}
$$

***

### 2.4 Hydration of Alkynes (Kucherov Reaction) ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Acid-catalyzed electrophilic addition of water across an alkyne bond.

* **Reagents**: $40\% \ \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 + 1\% \ \text{HgSO}_4$ at $333\text{ K}$.
* **Mechanism**:
  1. $\text{Hg}^{2+}$ acts as a soft Lewis acid, coordinating to the triple bond to form a cyclic mercurinium ion.
  2. Nucleophilic anti-addition of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ yields an organomercury enol.
  3. Demercuration releases the enol.
  4. Keto-enol tautomerism shifts equilibrium completely toward the carbonyl form ($\text{C}=\text{O}$ bond energy $\approx 745\text{ kJ/mol}$ vs $\text{C}=\text{C}$ $\approx 610\text{ kJ/mol}$).

#### A. Ethyne (Acetylene) $\longrightarrow$ Acetaldehyde (Only aldehyde produced)

$$
\text{H}-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-\text{H} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow[333\text{ K}]{\text{Hg}^{2+}/\text{H}^+} \left[ \text{CH}_2=\text{CH}-\text{OH} \right] \overset{\text{tautomerism}}{\rightleftharpoons} \text{CH}_3-\text{CHO}
$$

#### B. Propyne / Higher Alkynes $\longrightarrow$ Ketones (Markovnikov Orientation)

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow[333\text{ K}]{\text{Hg}^{2+}/\text{H}^+} \left[ \text{CH}_3-\text{C(OH)}=\text{CH}_2 \right] \overset{\text{tautomerism}}{\rightleftharpoons} \text{CH}_3-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{CH}_3
$$

***

## 3. Distinct Methods of Preparation: Aldehydes ONLY

### 3.1 Rosenmund Reduction ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Catalytic hydrogenolysis of acyl chlorides (acid chlorides).

$$
\text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{Cl} + \text{H}_2 \xrightarrow[\text{boiling xylene } (410\text{ K})]{\text{Pd} / \text{BaSO}_4, \, \text{S or Quinoline}} \text{R}-\text{CHO} + \text{HCl}
$$

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COCl} + \text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Pd}/\text{BaSO}_4, \, \text{quinoline}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CHO} + \text{HCl}
$$

* **Function of $\text{BaSO}_4$ & Poison ($\text{S}$ / Quinoline)**:
  $\text{Pd}$ has high catalytic activity. $\text{BaSO}_4$ acts as a low-surface-area support that moderates metal dispersion. Sulfur or quinoline acts as a catalytic poison, blocking active sites on $\text{Pd}$ to suppress the further reduction of the aldehyde to a primary alcohol:
  

$$
\text{R}-\text{CHO} + \text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Pd (unpoisoned)}} \text{R}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}
$$

* **Critical Limitation**: Formaldehyde ($\text{HCHO}$) **cannot** be prepared by this method because formyl chloride ($\text{HCOCl}$) is thermally unstable at room temperature and rapidly decomposes to $\text{CO} + \text{HCl}$.

***

### 3.2 Stephen Reduction & DIBAL-H Reductions ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Stephen Reduction
Reduction of nitriles with tin(II) chloride and hydrochloric acid to an aldimine intermediate, followed by aqueous acidic hydrolysis.

$$
\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{N} + \text{SnCl}_2 + 2\text{HCl} \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} \left[ \text{R}-\text{CH}=\text{NH} \cdot \text{HCl} \right] \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+, \, \Delta} \text{R}-\text{CHO} + \text{NH}_4\text{Cl}
$$

* **Underlying Redox Chemistry**:
  

$$
\text{Sn}^{2+} + 2\text{Cl}^- \longrightarrow \text{Sn}^{4+} + 4e^- \quad (\text{SnCl}_2 + 2\text{HCl} \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{SnCl}_4 / \text{SnCl}_4 + 2[\text{H}])
$$

  

$$
\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{N} + 2[\text{H}] + \text{HCl} \longrightarrow \text{R}-\text{CH}=\text{NH} \cdot \text{HCl} \quad \text{(Aldimine hydrochloride)}
$$

#### B. Diisobutylaluminium Hydride (DIBAL-H / $\left[(i\text{-Bu})_2\text{AlH}\right]$)
* **Selectivity Advantage**: A bulky, electrophilic reducing agent. Operates at low temperature ($-78^\circ\text{C} / 195\text{ K}$) via hydride transfer to nitriles and esters without reducing unconjugated carbon–carbon double bonds.
* **From Nitriles**:
  

$$
\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{N} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) }\text{H}_2\text{O}]{ \text{(i) DIBAL-H, } 195\text{ K}} \text{R}-\text{CHO}
$$

* **From Esters**:
  

$$
\text{CH}_3(\text{CH}_2)_9-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{OCH}_2\text{CH}_3 \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) }\text{H}_2\text{O}]{\text{(i) DIBAL-H, } 195\text{ K}} \text{CH}_3(\text{CH}_2)_9-\text{CHO} + \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH}
$$

* **Retention of Olefinic Double Bonds**:
  

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}=\text{CH}-\text{CH}_2\text{CN} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) }\text{H}_2\text{O}]{\text{(i) DIBAL-H}} \text{CH}_3-\text{CH}=\text{CH}-\text{CH}_2\text{CHO}
$$

***

### 3.3 Oxidation of Methylbenzenes (Aromatic Aldehydes) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Etard Reaction (Chromyl Chloride Method)
Partial oxidation of toluene using chromyl chloride ($\text{CrO}_2\text{Cl}_2$) in non-polar solvents ($\text{CS}_2$ or $\text{CCl}_4$).

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_3 + 2\text{CrO}_2\text{Cl}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{CS}_2} \underset{\text{Brown Chromium Complex}}{\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}\left[\text{OCr(OH)Cl}_2\right]_2} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CHO} + 2\text{Cr(OH)}_2\text{Cl}_2
$$

* **Mechanism**: Homolytic/heterolytic insertion of $\text{CrO}_2\text{Cl}_2$ into benzylic $\text{C}-\text{H}$ bonds. The brown complex precipitates out of the non-polar solvent, isolating the intermediate from further oxidation to benzoic acid.

#### B. Chromic Anhydride in Acetic Anhydride
Toluene reacts with $\text{CrO}_3$ in acetic anhydride at $273-283\text{ K}$ ($0-10^\circ\text{C}$).

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_3 + \text{CrO}_3 + (\text{CH}_3\text{CO})_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{273-283\text{ K}} \underset{\text{Benzylidene diacetate}}{\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}(\text{OCOCH}_3)_2} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+, \, \Delta} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CHO} + 2\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}
$$

* **Why oxidation stops**: The gem-diacetate ester (benzylidene diacetate) is stable to $\text{CrO}_3$ oxidation because it lacks a benzylic hydrogen atom. Subsequent acid hydrolysis regenerates the aldehyde.

#### C. Commercial Preparation: Side-Chain Chlorination followed by Hydrolysis

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_3 + 2\text{Cl}_2 \xrightarrow{h\nu} \underset{\text{Benzal chloride}}{\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CHCl}_2} + 2\text{HCl}
$$

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CHCl}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{373\text{ K}} \left[ \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH(OH)}_2 \right] \xrightarrow{-\text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CHO}
$$

***

### 3.4 Gattermann-Koch Reaction ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Formylation of benzene or alkylbenzenes via in situ generation of formyl chloride ($\text{HCOCl}$).

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{CO} + \text{HCl} \xrightarrow[\text{CuCl}]{\text{Anhydrous }\text{AlCl}_3} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CHO} + \text{HCl}
$$

* **Electrophile Generation**:
  

$$
\text{CO} + \text{HCl} \overset{\text{AlCl}_3 / \text{CuCl}}{\rightleftharpoons} \left[ \text{H}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{Cl} \right] \xrightarrow{\text{AlCl}_3} \text{H}-\overset{+}{\text{C}}=\text{O} + \text{AlCl}_4^-
$$

  The formyl cation ($\text{H}-\overset{+}{\text{C}}=\text{O}$) acts as the electrophile in standard electrophilic aromatic substitution ($S_E\text{Ar}$).
* **Limitation**: Cannot be applied to aromatic amines ($\text{ArNH}_2$) or phenols ($\text{ArOH}$) due to Lewis acid-base coordination with $\text{AlCl}_3$.

***

## 4. Distinct Methods of Preparation: Ketones ONLY

### 4.1 From Acyl Chlorides with Dialkylcadmium ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Grignard reagents ($\text{RMgX}$) react vigorously with acid chlorides to yield tertiary alcohols because ketones are more reactive toward nucleophilic addition than acyl chlorides. Dialkylcadmium ($\text{R}_2\text{Cd}$) reagents are less nucleophilic and stop selectively at the ketone stage.

#### A. Synthesis of Dialkylcadmium:

$$
2\text{R}-\text{MgX} + \text{CdCl}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} \text{R}_2\text{Cd} + 2\text{Mg(X)Cl}
$$

#### B. Reaction with Acyl Chloride:

$$
2\text{R}'-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{Cl} + \text{R}_2\text{Cd} \longrightarrow 2\text{R}'-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{R} + \text{CdCl}_2
$$

$$
\text{Example:}\quad 2\text{CH}_3\text{COCl} + (\text{C}_2\text{H}_5)_2\text{Cd} \longrightarrow 2\text{CH}_3\text{COC}_2\text{H}_5 + \text{CdCl}_2
$$

* **Why Organocadmium is Chemoselectively Controlled**: The $\text{C}-\text{Cd}$ bond is significantly more covalent than the $\text{C}-\text{Mg}$ bond ($\Delta\chi_{\text{C}-\text{Cd}} < \Delta\chi_{\text{C}-\text{Mg}}$). Consequently, the carbanionic character of $\text{R}$ in $\text{R}_2\text{Cd}$ is too low to attack the moderately electrophilic carbonyl carbon of the newly formed ketone, preventing over-addition.

***

### 4.2 From Nitriles with Grignard Reagents ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Addition of Grignard reagents to nitriles forms a magnesium-imine complex, which yields a ketone on acidic hydrolysis.

$$
\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{N} + \text{R}'\text{MgX} \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} \left[ \begin{matrix}\text{R}-\text{C}=\text{N}-\text{MgX}\\ \quad\mid\qquad\\ \text{R}'\qquad\end{matrix} \right] \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{R}' + \text{NH}_3 + \text{Mg(OH)X}
$$

#### Example: Synthesis of Propiophenone

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CN} + \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{MgBr} \xrightarrow{\text{ether}} \left[ \text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{C}(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5)=\text{NMgBr} \right] \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COCH}_2\text{CH}_3 + \text{NH}_4^+ + \text{Mg(OH)Br}
$$

* **Stereoelectronic Constraint**: The nucleophile attacks the nitrile carbon ($\delta+$), shifting electron density onto nitrogen. The resulting imine salt resists a second addition of Grignard reagent due to electrostatic repulsion from the negatively charged imino-nitrogen $[\text{C}=\text{N}^-] \text{MgX}^+$.

***

### 4.3 Friedel-Crafts Acylation ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Aromatic ketones are prepared by treating benzene or substituted benzenes with acid chlorides or acid anhydrides in the presence of anhydrous aluminum chloride.

$$
\text{Ar}-\text{H} + \text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{Cl} \xrightarrow{\text{Anhydrous }\text{AlCl}_3} \text{Ar}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{R} + \text{HCl}
$$

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{CH}_3\text{COCl} \xrightarrow{\text{Anhyd. }\text{AlCl}_3} \underset{\text{Acetophenone}}{\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COCH}_3} + \text{HCl}
$$

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + (\text{CH}_3\text{CO})_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{Anhyd. }\text{AlCl}_3} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COCH}_3 + \text{CH}_3\text{COOH}
$$

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COCl} \xrightarrow{\text{Anhyd. }\text{AlCl}_3} \underset{\text{Benzophenone}}{\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COC}_6\text{H}_5} + \text{HCl}
$$

* **Electrophile Generation**:
  

$$
\text{R}-\text{CO}-\text{Cl} + \text{AlCl}_3 \rightleftharpoons \left[ \text{R}-\overset{+}{\text{C}}=\text{O} \longleftrightarrow \text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\overset{+}{\text{O}} \right] + \text{AlCl}_4^- \quad (\text{Acylium ion, resonance stabilized})
$$

* **No Polyacylation Occurs**: Unlike Friedel-Crafts alkylation, the acyl group ($-\text{COR}$) is strongly electron-withdrawing ($-M$ and $-I$ effects), deactivating the benzene ring toward further electrophilic attack.

# Master Lecture Notes: Carbonyl Compounds (Aldehydes & Ketones)

**Target Module:** Nucleophilic Addition Reactions, Reduction, Oxidation, and Chemical Distinguishing Tests

## 1. Pedagogical Theory & Fundamental Concepts

### 1.1 Nature of the Carbonyl Group & Reactivity Core
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

The carbonyl group ($>C=O$) consists of a carbon atom doubly bonded to an oxygen atom through one $\sigma$-bond (formed by $sp^2-2p$ orbital axial overlap) and one $\pi$-bond (formed by sideways overlap of unhybridized $2p$ orbitals). 

```
        :O:  (Electronegative, δ-)
        //
       C     (Electrophilic Carbon, δ+)
      / \
     R   R'  (Planar geometry, bond angles ≈ 120°)
```

* **Polarity & Electrophilic Character:** Oxygen has a Pauling electronegativity of $3.44$ compared to carbon's $2.55$. Consequently, the $\pi$-electron cloud is strongly shifted toward oxygen:
  

$$
\mathrm{>C=O \longleftrightarrow\ >\overset{\oplus}{C}-\overset{\ominus}{O}:}
$$

  The carbonyl carbon is a potent **electrophilic center** ($\delta^+$), making it susceptible to attack by nucleophiles ($\mathrm{Nu^-}$). The carbonyl oxygen is basic/nucleophilic ($\delta^-$) and can be protonated by Lewis/Brønsted acids.

* **Relative Reactivity: Aldehydes vs. Ketones**
  Aldehydes are substantially more reactive toward nucleophilic attack than ketones due to two distinct factors:
  1. **Steric Hindrance:** In aldehydes, the carbonyl carbon bears only one alkyl/aryl group and a tiny hydrogen atom. In ketones, two relatively bulky alkyl/aryl groups hinder the approaching nucleophile ($Nu^-$) and produce severe non-bonding interactions in the tetrahedral transition state (transition from $sp^2$, $120^\circ$ to $sp^3$, $109.5^\circ$).
  2. **Electronic Factor (Inductive $+I$ Effect):** Alkyl groups are electron-donating ($+I$). Ketones have two alkyl groups dispersing the partial positive charge on the carbonyl carbon more effectively than aldehydes (which have only one $+I$ group). Thus, the electrophilicity of carbon is reduced in ketones:
     

$$
\mathrm{HCHO > RCHO > R_2CO > ArCHO > Ar_2CO}
$$

  3. **Aromatic Aldehydes/Ketones vs. Aliphatic Counterparts:** Benzaldehyde is less reactive than aliphatic aldehydes. The carbonyl group conjugates with the benzene ring through resonance:
     

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5-CHO \longleftrightarrow \text{[Resonance structures exhibiting positive charge delocalization on } ortho/para \text{ positions]}}
$$

     Resonance stabilizes the ground state and reduces the partial positive magnitude ($\delta^+$) on the carbonyl carbon.

***

## 2. Nucleophilic Addition Reactions & Mechanisms

### 2.1 General Mechanism of Nucleophilic Addition
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

1. **Step 1 (Rate-Determining Step / Slow):** Attack of nucleophile on planar carbonyl carbon.
   

$$
\mathrm{R_2C=O + Nu^- \xrightarrow{slow} [R_2C(Nu)-O^-] \quad (\text{Trigonal planar } sp^2 \longrightarrow \text{Tetrahedral intermediate } sp^3)}
$$

2. **Step 2 (Fast):** Protonation of the alkoxide intermediate.
   

$$
\mathrm{[R_2C(Nu)-O^-] + H^+ \xrightarrow{fast} R_2C(Nu)(OH)}
$$

***

### 2.2 Addition of Hydrogen Cyanide ($\mathrm{HCN}$)
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Pure $\mathrm{HCN}$ is an extremely weak acid ($K_a \approx 4.9 \times 10^{-10}$) and does not yield sufficient concentration of nucleophile ($CN^-$) on its own. The reaction is therefore catalyzed by a base ($\mathrm{OH^-}$):

$$
\mathrm{HCN + OH^- \rightleftharpoons :C\equiv N:^- + H_2O}
$$

$$
\mathrm{>C=O + :C\equiv N:^- \xrightarrow{slow} >C(CN)-O^-}
$$

$$
\mathrm{>C(CN)-O^- + H_2O \xrightarrow{fast} >C(CN)(OH) + OH^-}
$$

The products are **cyanohydrins**, which are synthetic precursors:
* Hydrolysis of cyanohydrins yields $\alpha$-hydroxy acids:
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CH(OH)CN + 2H_2O + H^+ \xrightarrow{\Delta} CH_3CH(OH)COOH + NH_4^+}
$$

***

### 2.3 Addition of Sodium Hydrogen Sulphite ($\mathrm{NaHSO_3}$)
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

$$
\mathrm{>C=O + NaHSO_3 \rightleftharpoons \left[ >C(OH)(SO_3Na) \right]}
$$

**Mechanism & Nucleophilic Species:** The nucleophilic center in the hydrogen sulphite ion is the unshared pair on **sulfur**, not oxygen, because sulfur is larger and more polarizable:
1. Attack by sulfur:
   

$$
\mathrm{>C=O + :SO_3H^- \longrightarrow >C(O^-)-SO_3H}
$$

2. Proton transfer:
   

$$
\mathrm{>C(O^-)-SO_3H \xrightarrow{\text{Proton Transfer}} >C(OH)-SO_3^- \xrightarrow{Na^+} >C(OH)SO_3Na \downarrow}
$$

* **Equilibrium Position:** Lies predominantly to the right for aldehydes, but shifts to the left for sterically hindered ketones (e.g., acetophenone does not give bisulphite compounds in practical yields).
* **Purification Significance:** The bisulphite adduct is a crystalline solid that can be filtered and regenerated to pure carbonyl compound by treating with dilute mineral acid or aqueous alkali:
  

$$
\mathrm{>C(OH)SO_3Na + HCl \longrightarrow >C=O + NaCl + SO_2 \uparrow + H_2O}
$$

***

### 2.4 Addition of Alcohols (Acetal & Ketal Formation)
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

* **Aldehydes:** React with one equivalent of monohydric alcohol in the presence of dry $\mathrm{HCl}$ gas to yield a hemiacetal (alkoxyalcohol), which reacts with a second equivalent of alcohol to yield a gem-dialkoxy compound known as an **acetal**:

$$
\mathrm{RCHO + R'OH \xrightleftharpoons{dry\ HCl} R-CH(OH)(OR') \quad [\text{Hemiacetal}]}
$$

$$
\mathrm{R-CH(OH)(OR') + R'OH \xrightleftharpoons{dry\ HCl} R-CH(OR')_2 + H_2O \quad [\text{Acetal}]}
$$

* **Ketones:** Do not readily form ketals with monohydric alcohols under normal conditions. They react with 1,2-diols (ethylene glycol) in the presence of dry $\mathrm{HCl}$ gas or $p$-toluenesulfonic acid ($p$-TsOH) to form **cyclic ketals (ethylene glycol ketals)**:

$$
\mathrm{R_2C=O + \begin{matrix} HO-CH_2 \\ | \qquad \\ HO-CH_2 \end{matrix} \xrightleftharpoons{dry\ HCl, -\mathrm{H_2O}} \begin{matrix} R \quad /O-CH_2 \\ \ \ C \qquad | \\ R \quad \backslash O-CH_2 \end{matrix}}
$$

* **Role of Dry $\mathrm{HCl}$ Gas:**
  1. Protonates the carbonyl oxygen, dramatically increasing the electrophilic character of the carbonyl carbon.
  2. Absorbs water produced during the reversible condensation, shifting the chemical equilibrium forward in accordance with Le Chatelier’s principle.

***

### 2.5 Nucleophilic Addition-Elimination: Ammonia Derivatives ($\mathrm{NH_2-Z}$)
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Reactions proceed through nucleophilic attack of the basic nitrogen on the carbonyl carbon followed by loss of a water molecule:

$$
\mathrm{>C=O + H_2N-Z \xrightleftharpoons{H^+} \left[ >C(OH)(NH-Z) \right] \xrightarrow{-H_2O} >C=N-Z}
$$

| Reagent ($\mathrm{NH_2-Z}$) | Group ($\mathrm{Z}$) | Product Name | Product Structure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\mathrm{NH_3}$ (Ammonia) | $-\mathrm{H}$ | Imine (Schiff's base) | $\mathrm{>C=NH}$ |
| $\mathrm{R'NH_2}$ (Primary Amine) | $-\mathrm{R'}$ | Substituted Imine | $\mathrm{>C=N-R'}$ |
| $\mathrm{NH_2OH}$ (Hydroxylamine) | $-\mathrm{OH}$ | **Oxime** | $\mathrm{>C=N-OH}$ |
| $\mathrm{NH_2NH_2}$ (Hydrazine) | $-\mathrm{NH_2}$ | **Hydrazone** | $\mathrm{>C=N-NH_2}$ |
| $\mathrm{C_6H_5NHNH_2}$ (Phenylhydrazine) | $-\mathrm{NHC_6H_5}$ | **Phenylhydrazone** | $\mathrm{>C=N-NHC_6H_5}$ |
| **2,4-DNP** (2,4-Dinitrophenylhydrazine) | $-\mathrm{NH-C_6H_3(NO_2)_2}$ | **2,4-DNP derivative** | $\mathrm{>C=N-NH-C_6H_3(NO_2)_2}$ *(Orange-Yellow ppt)* |
| **Semicarbazide** | $-\mathrm{NH-CO-NH_2}$ | **Semicarbazone** | $\mathrm{>C=N-NH-CO-NH_2}$ |

* **Optimum pH ($\approx 3.5 - 4.5$):**
  * If $\mathrm{pH} < 3.5$ (too acidic): The amine nucleophile is completely protonated to an ammonium salt ($\mathrm{R-\overset{\oplus}{N}H_3}$), losing its lone pair and nucleophilic ability.
  * If $\mathrm{pH} > 4.5$ (not acidic enough): The carbonyl oxygen cannot be protonated, rendering the electrophilic carbon insufficiently active toward weaker nitrogen nucleophiles.

## 3. Reduction Reactions

### 3.1 Reduction to Hydrocarbons
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Clemmensen Reduction
* **Reagent:** Zinc amalgam and concentrated hydrochloric acid ($\mathrm{Zn(Hg) / conc.\ HCl}$).
* **Transformation:** Direct deoxygenation of carbonyl group into a methylene group ($>C=O \longrightarrow >CH_2$).
* **Equation:**
  

$$
\mathrm{R-CHO + 4[H] \xrightarrow{Zn(Hg)/conc.\ HCl} R-CH_3 + H_2O}
$$

  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3 + 4[H] \xrightarrow{Zn(Hg)/conc.\ HCl} CH_3CH_2CH_3 + H_2O}
$$

* **Substrate Restriction:** **Acid-sensitive groups** (e.g., $-\mathrm{OH}$, $-\mathrm{OR}$, $-\mathrm{C=C}-$, $-\mathrm{NH_2}$) cannot be present because concentrated $\mathrm{HCl}$ will cause dehydration, addition, or protonation side-reactions.

#### B. Wolff-Kishner Reduction
* **Reagents:** Hydrazine ($\mathrm{NH_2NH_2}$) followed by heating with base ($\mathrm{KOH}$ or potassium tert-butoxide) in a high-boiling polar solvent such as ethylene glycol ($T \approx 453 - 473\ \mathrm{K}$).
* **Equation:**
  

$$
\mathrm{>C=O + H_2NNH_2 \xrightarrow{-H_2O} >C=NNH_2 \xrightarrow{KOH / \text{Ethylene glycol}, \Delta} >CH_2 + N_2 \uparrow}
$$

* **Substrate Restriction:** **Base-sensitive groups** (e.g., esters, alkyl halides, phenolic $-\mathrm{OH}$) cannot be present because strong base ($\mathrm{OH^-}$) will cause hydrolysis or elimination.

***

### 3.2 Reduction to Alcohols
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

1. **Catalytic Hydrogenation:**
   

$$
\mathrm{RCHO + H_2 \xrightarrow{Ni/Pt/Pd} RCH_2OH \quad (\text{1}^\circ\ \text{Alcohol})}
$$

   

$$
\mathrm{RCOR' + H_2 \xrightarrow{Ni/Pt/Pd} R-CH(OH)-R' \quad (\text{2}^\circ\ \text{Alcohol})}
$$

2. **Complex Hydrides ($\mathrm{NaBH_4}$ and $\mathrm{LiAlH_4}$):**
   * $\mathrm{NaBH_4}$ selectively reduces aldehydes and ketones to alcohols in protic solvents (ethanol/methanol) without reducing esters, amides, or carboxylic acids:
     

$$
\mathrm{4RCHO + NaBH_4 + 3H_2O \longrightarrow 4RCH_2OH + NaBO_2}
$$

   * Both hydrides reduce the polar $C=O$ bond via hydride transfer ($:H^-$) without affecting isolated non-polar $C=C$ double bonds (except in cinnamaldehyde derivatives where conjugation can lead to partial reduction).

***

## 4. Oxidation Reactions & Selective Tests

### 4.1 Differential Oxidation: Aldehydes vs. Ketones
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

* **Aldehydes** oxidize easily to carboxylic acids with the same number of carbon atoms, even with mild oxidizing agents ($\mathrm{Ag^+}$, $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$), due to the presence of a oxidizable aldehydic hydrogen ($\mathrm{C-H}$ bond directly on the carbonyl carbon):
  

$$
\mathrm{R-CHO \xrightarrow{[O]} R-COOH}
$$

* **Ketones** lack this hydrogen atom and are resistant to mild oxidants. They undergo cleavage of $\mathrm{C-C}$ bonds only under vigorous conditions (concentrated $\mathrm{HNO_3}$, $\mathrm{KMnO_4}/\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$, elevated temperature), yielding mixtures of carboxylic acids with fewer carbon atoms.

#### Popoff’s Rule (for unsymmetrical ketones):
During vigorous oxidation of unsymmetrical aliphatic ketones, the carbonyl group remains preferentially with the smaller alkyl group:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CO-CH_2CH_2CH_3 \xrightarrow{[O], \Delta} CH_3COOH + CH_3CH_2COOH}
$$

*(Carbonyl group remains attached to methyl instead of propyl).*

***

### 4.2 Distinguishing Chemical Tests

#### A. Tollens’ Test (Silver Mirror Test)
* **Reagent:** Ammoniacal silver nitrate solution, $[\mathrm{Ag(NH_3)_2}]^+\mathrm{OH^-}$, freshly prepared by adding dilute $\mathrm{NH_4OH}$ to $\mathrm{AgNO_3}$ until the initial $\mathrm{Ag_2O}$ precipitate dissolves:
  

$$
\mathrm{2AgNO_3 + 2NaOH \longrightarrow Ag_2O\downarrow + 2NaNO_3 + H_2O}
$$

  

$$
\mathrm{Ag_2O + 4NH_4OH \longrightarrow 2[Ag(NH_3)_2]OH + 3H_2O}
$$

* **Action:** Both aliphatic and aromatic aldehydes reduce Tollens' reagent to metallic silver (silver mirror on tube wall):
  

$$
\mathrm{R-CHO + 2[Ag(NH_3)_2]^+ + 3OH^- \xrightarrow{\Delta} R-COO^- + 2Ag\downarrow + 4NH_3 + 2H_2O}
$$

* **Observation:** Brilliant silver mirror on glass surface or dark grey precipitate. Ketones do not respond (except $\alpha$-hydroxy ketones like fructose).

#### B. Fehling’s Test
* **Reagents:**
  * **Fehling’s A:** Aqueous copper(II) sulfate ($\mathrm{CuSO_4 \cdot 5H_2O}$).
  * **Fehling’s B:** Alkaline solution of sodium potassium tartrate (Rochelle salt) with $\mathrm{NaOH}$.
* **Mechanism:** Rochelle salt acts as a bidentate chelating agent for $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$ ions, preventing the precipitation of insoluble $\mathrm{Cu(OH)_2}$ in alkaline media.
* **Equation:**
  

$$
\mathrm{R-CHO + 2Cu^{2+} + 5OH^- \xrightarrow{\Delta} R-COO^- + Cu_2O\downarrow + 3H_2O}
$$

* **Observation:** Distinct **red-brown precipitate** of cuprous oxide ($\mathrm{Cu_2O}$).
* **Scope & Limitation:** Positive for **aliphatic aldehydes only**. Aromatic aldehydes ($\mathrm{C_6H_5CHO}$) and ketones do **not** reduce Fehling’s solution.

#### C. Haloform (Iodoform) Reaction
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

* **Structural Criteria:** Compounds containing either:
  1. A **methyl carbonyl** group: $\mathrm{CH_3-C=O}$
  2. A **methyl carbinol** group capable of in-situ oxidation to methyl carbonyl: $\mathrm{CH_3-CH(OH)-}$
* **Reagent:** Sodium hypoiodite ($\mathrm{NaOI}$), prepared in-situ by mixing $\mathrm{I_2 + NaOH}$:
  

$$
\mathrm{2NaOH + I_2 \longrightarrow NaOI + NaI + H_2O}
$$

* **Stepwise Mechanism for Acetone:**
  1. Successive halogenation of $\alpha$-methyl group (halogenation is base-catalyzed, accelerating with each substitution due to electron-withdrawing iodines):
     

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3 + 3NaOI \longrightarrow CI_3COCH_3 + 3NaOH}
$$

  2. Nucleophilic cleavage of triiodo compound by hydroxide ion:
     

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CO-CI_3 + OH^- \longrightarrow CH_3-COOH + :CI_3^-}
$$

  3. Proton transfer:
     

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-COOH + :CI_3^- \longrightarrow CH_3-COO^- + CHI_3 \downarrow}
$$

* **Net Balance Equation:**
  

$$
\mathrm{R-COCH_3 + 3I_2 + 4NaOH \xrightarrow{\Delta} R-COONa + CHI_3\downarrow + 3NaI + 3H_2O}
$$

* **Observation:** Bright **yellow crystalline precipitate** of iodoform ($\mathrm{CHI_3}$) with a characteristic antiseptic smell (Melting Point: $392\ \mathrm{K} / 119^\circ\mathrm{C}$).

***

## 5. High-Yield Solved Examples & CBSE Board PYQs (2020–2025)

### Problem 1 [CBSE 2024, 3 Marks]
**Question:** An organic compound $[\mathrm{A}]$ with molecular formula $\mathrm{C_3H_6O}$ forms an orange-yellow precipitate with 2,4-DNP reagent and gives a yellow precipitate of compound $[\mathrm{B}]$ on treatment with $\mathrm{I_2}$ and $\mathrm{NaOH}$. Compound $[\mathrm{A}]$ does not reduce Tollens’ or Fehling’s reagent.
1. Identify $[\mathrm{A}]$ and $[\mathrm{B}]$.
2. Write the chemical equation for the formation of $[\mathrm{B}]$ from $[\mathrm{A}]$.
3. What happens when $[\mathrm{A}]$ is treated with $\mathrm{Zn(Hg)}$ and conc. $\mathrm{HCl}$?

**Solution:**
* **Step 1: Structural deduction**
  * Formula $\mathrm{C_3H_6O}$ corresponds to degree of unsaturation:
    

$$
\mathrm{DU = C + 1 - \frac{H}{2} = 3 + 1 - 3 = 1}
$$

  * Forms orange-yellow precipitate with 2,4-DNP $\implies$ Carbonyl compound (aldehyde or ketone).
  * Does not reduce Tollens' or Fehling's reagent $\implies$ It is a **ketone**, not an aldehyde.
  * Gives positive iodoform test $\implies$ Contains $\mathrm{CH_3-C=O}$ group.
  * The only 3-carbon ketone is **Propanone (Acetone)**, $\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3}$.
  * Thus, $[\mathrm{A}] = \mathbf{Propan-2-one\ (CH_3COCH_3)}$ and $[\mathrm{B}] = \mathbf{Iodoform\ (CHI_3)}$.
* **Step 2: Chemical equation for iodoform formation**
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3 + 3I_2 + 4NaOH \xrightarrow{\Delta} CH_3COONa + CHI_3\downarrow + 3NaI + 3H_2O}
$$

* **Step 3: Clemmensen Reduction of $[\mathrm{A}]$**
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3 + 4[H] \xrightarrow{Zn(Hg)/conc.\ HCl} CH_3-CH_2-CH_3 + H_2O \quad (\text{Propane})}
$$

***

### Problem 2 [CBSE 2023, 2 Marks]
**Question:** Explain the following observations:
1. Although semicarbazide has two $-\mathrm{NH_2}$ groups, only one of them is involved in the formation of semicarbazones.
2. Benzaldehyde does not reduce Fehling’s solution while acetaldehyde does.

**Solution:**
1. **Semicarbazide structure:** $\mathrm{H_2\overset{1}{N}-CO-\overset{2}{N}H-\overset{3}{N}H_2}$.
   The terminal $-\mathrm{NH_2}$ group attached directly to the carbonyl group ($\mathrm{N^1}$) and the adjacent secondary amine nitrogen ($\mathrm{N^2}$) have their lone pairs delocalized into the carbonyl $\pi$-system via resonance:
   

$$
\mathrm{H_2N-CO-NH-NH_2 \longleftrightarrow H_2\overset{+}{N}=C(O^-)-NH-NH_2}
$$

   Consequently, the lone pairs on $\mathrm{N^1}$ and $\mathrm{N^2}$ are unavailable for nucleophilic attack. Only the hydrazine-terminus nitrogen ($\mathrm{N^3}$), which is not in direct conjugation with the carbonyl, has a localized lone pair and acts as an effective nucleophile.
2. **Fehling's test differentiation:** The electron-donating resonance effect of the benzene ring reduces the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon in benzaldehyde. Cupric ion ($\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$) in Fehling’s solution is a relatively weak oxidizing agent compared to Tollens’ reagent and cannot overcome the combined resonance stabilization and lower oxidation potential of aromatic aldehydes. Acetaldehyde, being aliphatic, has higher electrophilic carbon polarity and a more readily abstractable aldehydic proton.

### Problem 3 [CBSE 2022 Term-2, 3 Marks]
**Question:** Distinguish between the following pairs of compounds by simple chemical tests:
1. Pentan-2-one and Pentan-3-one
2. Benzaldehyde and Acetophenone
3. Ethanal and Propanal

**Solution:**
1. **Pentan-2-one vs. Pentan-3-one (Iodoform Test):**
   * Reagent: Warm with $\mathrm{I_2 / NaOH}$.
   * **Pentan-2-one** ($\mathrm{CH_3COCH_2CH_2CH_3}$) contains a methyl ketone group ($\mathrm{CH_3C=O}$) and yields a **yellow precipitate of $\mathrm{CHI_3}$**.
   * **Pentan-3-one** ($\mathrm{CH_3CH_2COCH_2CH_3}$) has no methyl ketone group and gives **no yellow precipitate**.
2. **Benzaldehyde vs. Acetophenone (Tollens' Test or Iodoform Test):**
   * *Method A (Tollens’ Test):* Benzaldehyde ($\mathrm{C_6H_5CHO}$) forms a **bright silver mirror** when warmed with ammoniacal $\mathrm{AgNO_3}$. Acetophenone ($\mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3}$) gives **no reaction**.
   * *Method B (Iodoform Test):* Acetophenone contains a $\mathrm{CH_3C=O}$ group and gives a **yellow precipitate of $\mathrm{CHI_3}$**. Benzaldehyde gives **no precipitate**.
3. **Ethanal vs. Propanal (Iodoform Test):**
   * Reagent: $\mathrm{I_2 + NaOH}$.
   * **Ethanal** ($\mathrm{CH_3CHO}$) is the *only* aldehyde containing the $\mathrm{CH_3C=O}$ fragment; it gives a **yellow precipitate of $\mathrm{CHI_3}$**.
   * **Propanal** ($\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CHO}$) does not contain the methyl carbonyl group and gives **no precipitate**.

### Problem 4 [CBSE 2021, 2 Marks]
**Question:** Arrange the following compounds in increasing order of their reactivity toward nucleophilic addition:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CHO,\ C_6H_5COCH_3,\ HCHO,\ CH_3COCH_3}
$$

Give reasons for your arrangement.

**Solution:**
* **Order:**
  

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3 < CH_3COCH_3 < CH_3CHO < HCHO}
$$

* **Reasoning:**
  1. **Steric Factor:** Formaldehyde ($\mathrm{HCHO}$) has only two small hydrogens, offering minimal steric hindrance to the approaching nucleophile. Acetophenone ($\mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3}$) has bulky phenyl and methyl groups, causing maximum steric congestion.
  2. **Electronic Factor:** In $\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3}$, two $+I$ methyl groups decrease the positive charge density ($\delta^+$) on the carbonyl carbon more than the single methyl group in $\mathrm{CH_3CHO}$. In $\mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3}$, the $+R$ effect of the phenyl ring additionally neutralizes the electrophilic center via resonance:
     

$$
\mathrm{Ph \overset{+R}{\longrightarrow} C(=O)CH_3}
$$

## 6. Examiner Traps & Common Board Mistakes

1. **The "Aldehydes = All Iodoform Negative" Trap:**
   * *Mistake:* Stating that *all* aldehydes fail the iodoform test.
   * *Correction:* **Acetaldehyde ($\mathrm{CH_3CHO}$)** is the single aldehyde that gives a positive iodoform test because it contains the required $\mathrm{CH_3C=O}$ unit.

2. **Fehling's Test on Benzaldehyde:**
   * *Mistake:* Using Fehling's solution to confirm Benzaldehyde because "it is an aldehyde."
   * *Correction:* Fehling’s test is negative for **aromatic aldehydes**. Use **Tollens' reagent** to test for benzaldehyde.

3. **Reagent Inversion in Hydrocarbon Reductions:**
   * *Mistake:* Using Clemmensen reduction ($\mathrm{Zn(Hg)/HCl}$) on a substrate containing a protected acetal or an $-\mathrm{OH}$ group.
   * *Correction:* The strong acid ($\mathrm{HCl}$) causes premature hydrolysis of acetals or dehydration of alcohols. Use basic **Wolff-Kishner conditions ($\mathrm{NH_2NH_2 / KOH}$)** for acid-sensitive substrates. Conversely, use Clemmensen reduction if base-sensitive esters or halogen atoms are present.

4. **Semicarbazide Reaction Formula Errors:**
   * *Mistake:* Writing the product of semicarbazide condensation using the amide $-\mathrm{NH_2}$ end:
     

$$
\mathrm{R_2C=N-CO-NH-NH_2 \quad (INCORRECT)}
$$

   * *Correction:* Attack occurs strictly via the hydrazine nitrogen:
     

$$
\mathrm{R_2C=N-NH-CO-NH_2 \quad (CORRECT)}
$$

5. **Equilibrium Reversibility with $\mathrm{NaHSO_3}$ and Acetals:**
   * *Mistake:* Omitting the acid/base regeneration step when discussing the purification of carbonyls.
   * *Correction:* Nucleophilic additions of $\mathrm{NaHSO_3}$ and $\mathrm{ROH}$ are reversible equilibria; mention that **dry $\mathrm{HCl}$ gas** is used to drive acetal formation by sequestering water, and mineral acids ($\mathrm{H_2SO_4/HCl}$) regenerate the original carbonyl compound.

## 1. IN-DEPTH PEDAGOGICAL THEORY & CORE CONCEPTS

### 1.1 Acidity of $\alpha$-Hydrogen Atoms in Aldehydes and Ketones
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(Crucial conceptual root for reasoning questions)*

#### NCERT Definition & Fundamental Basis
In aldehydes and ketones, hydrogen atoms attached to the carbon adjacent to the carbonyl group ($\mathrm{C_\alpha}$) are termed **$\alpha$-hydrogens**. These hydrogens exhibit pronounced acidic character ($pK_a \approx 19 - 20$ in water, compared to $pK_a \approx 45 - 50$ for alkanes).

The acidity is governed by two synergistic electronic effects:
1. **Strong Electron-Withdrawing Inductive Effect ($-I$ effect):**  
   The strongly electronegative oxygen atom polarizes the carbonyl $\pi$-bond ($\mathrm{^{\delta+}C = O^{\delta-}}$), creating electron deficiency on the carbonyl carbon. This pulls electron density from the adjacent $\mathrm{C_\alpha - C}$ bond and labilizes the $\mathrm{C_\alpha - H}$ bond through $-I$ transmission.
2. **Resonance Stabilization of the Enolate Anion:**  
   Deprotonation by a base forms a carbanion/enolate ion that is stabilized via delocalization over the conjugated system:
   
   

$$
\mathrm{R{-}\overset{\alpha}{C}H_2{-}\overset{O}{\overset{\parallel}{C}}{-}H + B: \rightleftharpoons \left[ R{-}\overset{\ominus}{\ddot{C}}H{-}\overset{O}{\overset{\parallel}{C}}{-}H \longleftrightarrow R{-}CH{=}\overset{\ominus}{\ddot{O}}{-}H \right] + B{-}H^+}
$$

   Between the two canonical resonance contributors, the **oxyanion form** ($\mathrm{R-CH=CH-O^-}$) contributes more heavily to the resonance hybrid because the negative charge resides on the more electronegative oxygen atom ($EN_{\mathrm{O}} = 3.44$ vs $EN_{\mathrm{C}} = 2.55$ on Pauling scale).

***

### 1.2 The Aldol Condensation & Cross-Aldol Condensation
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(Guaranteed 3M to 5M presence in Board exams)*

#### Mechanistic Principle
Aldehydes and ketones possessing **at least one $\alpha$-hydrogen** undergo self-condensation in the presence of dilute alkali (e.g., dilute $\mathrm{NaOH}$, $\mathrm{Ba(OH)_2}$, $\mathrm{Na_2CO_3}$) at room temperature ($293\text{--}298\text{ K}$) to yield **$\beta$-hydroxy aldehydes (aldols)** or **$\beta$-hydroxy ketones (ketols)**. 

Upon subsequent heating with trace acid or base ($\Delta \ge 373\text{ K}$), they lose a molecule of water via an $\mathrm{E1cB}$ (elimination conjugate base) pathway to form **$\alpha,\beta$-unsaturated carbonyl compounds**.

#### Cross-Aldol Condensation
When aldol condensation is performed between **two different carbonyl compounds**, it is termed a **Cross-Aldol Condensation**.
- If **both** carbonyl compounds possess $\alpha$-hydrogens, a statistical mixture of **four** distinct condensation products is formed (two self-aldol + two cross-aldol).
- If **only one** component possesses an $\alpha$-hydrogen (e.g., Benzaldehyde + Acetaldehyde), it acts exclusively as the electrophile (acceptor), while the enolizable carbonyl acts as the nucleophile, giving a single major cross-aldol product. This specific variant is designated the **Claisen-Schmidt Condensation**.

***

### 1.3 The Cannizzaro Reaction & Crossed Cannizzaro
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(Highest board weightage for aldehydes lacking $\alpha$-H)*

#### Mechanistic Principle
Aldehydes that have **no $\alpha$-hydrogen atoms** (e.g., $\mathrm{HCHO}$, $\mathrm{C_6H_5CHO}$, $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3CCHO}$) undergo self-oxidation-reduction (**disproportionation / redox**) when heated with **concentrated alkali** ($50\%\text{ aqueous or alcoholic }\mathrm{NaOH}/\mathrm{KOH}$, $373\text{ K}$).
- One molecule of the aldehyde is reduced to the corresponding **primary alcohol**.
- The second molecule is oxidized to the **carboxylic acid salt**.

#### Crossed Cannizzaro Reaction
When a mixture of two different aldehydes lacking $\alpha$-hydrogens reacts with concentrated alkali, one is preferentially oxidized and the other is reduced:
- **Formaldehyde ($\mathrm{HCHO}$)** has no steric hindrance and possesses a highly electrophilic carbonyl carbon due to the absence of electron-donating alkyl/aryl groups.
- Therefore, nucleophilic attack by $\mathrm{OH^-}$ occurs preferentially on $\mathrm{HCHO}$. Hence, in any cross-Cannizzaro involving formaldehyde, **$\mathrm{HCHO}$ is always oxidized to sodium formate ($\mathrm{HCOONa}$)**, while the other aldehyde is reduced to the alcohol.

***

### 1.4 Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution of Aromatic Aldehydes & Ketones
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(Mechanistic and isomer-determination questions)*

Aromatic carbonyl compounds (e.g., Benzaldehyde, Acetophenone) undergo electrophilic aromatic substitution (nitration, halogenation, sulfonation):
- **Deactivating and meta-directing nature:** The carbonyl group ($-\mathrm{CHO}$ or $-\mathrm{COR}$) exerts strong $-R$ (resonance withdrawal) and $-I$ effects. It withdraws $\pi$-electron density from the benzene ring, particularly depleting electron density at the *ortho* and *para* positions:
  
  

$$
\text{Electron density: } meta > ortho, para
$$

  
  Thus, incoming electrophiles ($\mathrm{NO_2^+}$, $\mathrm{Br^+}$, $\mathrm{SO_3H^+}$) attack the relatively less deactivated **meta** position.
- **Friedel-Crafts Reaction Anomaly:** Benzaldehyde and acetophenone **do NOT undergo Friedel-Crafts Alkylation or Acylation**. The strongly deactivating carbonyl group deactivates the ring, and the Lewis acid catalyst ($\mathrm{AlCl_3}$) complexes irreversibly with the lone pair of the carbonyl oxygen:
  
  

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5CHO + AlCl_3 \longrightarrow [C_6H_5CH=\overset{+}{O}{-}\overset{-}{A}lCl_3]}
$$

  
  This complexation further increases the positive charge on the carbonyl center and entirely shuts down electrophilic attack on the benzene ring.

***

### 1.5 Industrial Uses of Aldehydes and Ketones
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐ *(1M Objective, Assertion-Reason, and Case-Based items)*

1. **Formaldehyde ($\mathrm{HCHO}$):**
   - Supplied industrially as **Formalin** ($40\%\text{ aqueous solution of formaldehyde}$ containing $8\text{--}12\%\text{ methanol}$ as an anti-polymerization stabilizer). Used for biological specimen preservation and leather tanning.
   - Starting precursor for polymeric resins: **Bakelite** (phenol-formaldehyde resin), **Urea-formaldehyde resin**, and **Melamine-formaldehyde resins**.
2. **Acetaldehyde ($\mathrm{CH_3CHO}$):**
   - Major industrial intermediate in the manufacture of acetic acid, ethyl acetate, vinyl acetate, and chloral.
3. **Acetone ($\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3}$):**
   - Universal polar aprotic industrial solvent for cellulose acetate, nitrocellulose, varnishes, and acetylene storage cylinders.
   - Precursor for methyl methacrylate (Plexiglas monomer) and bisphenol-A.
4. **Aromatic Aldehydes (Benzaldehyde, Vanillin, Cinnamaldehyde):**
   - Benzaldehyde (oil of bitter almonds): perfume, dye synthesis (e.g., malachite green), and flavoring.
   - Vanillin (from vanilla beans), cinnamaldehyde (cinnamon), and camphor: perfumery, odorants, and pharmaceuticals.

## 2. REACTION DERIVATIONS, CONDITIONS & MECHANISMS

***

### 2.1 Mechanism: Aldol Condensation of Ethanal (Acetaldehyde)

#### Reaction Equation

$$
\mathrm{2\,CH_3CHO \xrightarrow[\text{293--298 K}]{\text{dil. } \mathrm{NaOH}} \underset{\text{3-Hydroxybutanal (Aldol)}}{CH_3-CH(OH)-CH_2-CHO} \xrightarrow[\Delta\text{ (}\ge 373\text{ K)}]{H^+\text{ or }OH^-} \underset{\text{But-2-en-1-al (Crotonaldehyde)}}{CH_3-CH=CH-CHO} + H_2O}
$$

#### Step-by-Step Mechanism

**Step 1: Enolate Generation (Acid-Base Equilibrium)**  
Hydroxide ion abstracts an acidic $\alpha$-proton from ethanal to generate a resonance-stabilized enolate ion.

$$
\mathrm{HO^\ominus + H{-}CH_2{-}CHO \xrightleftharpoons[\text{slow}]{K_{eq} \ll 1} H_2O + \left[ \overset{\ominus}{\ddot{C}}H_2{-}CHO \longleftrightarrow CH_2{=}\overset{\ominus}{\ddot{O}}{-}H \right]}
$$

**Step 2: Nucleophilic Addition (C-C Bond Formation - Rate Determining Step)**  
The nucleophilic carbon of the enolate attacks the electrophilic carbonyl carbon of a second, un-ionized ethanal molecule:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3{-}\overset{\delta+}{C}(=O)H + {^\ominus}CH_2{-}CHO \xrightarrow{\text{slow (RDS)}} CH_3{-}\underset{\underset{O^\ominus}{\mid}}{C}H{-}CH_2{-}CHO}
$$

**Step 3: Proton Transfer (Formation of Aldol)**  
The alkoxide intermediate abstracts a proton from the solvent ($\mathrm{H_2O}$), regenerating the hydroxide catalyst:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3{-}\underset{\underset{O^\ominus}{\mid}}{C}H{-}CH_2{-}CHO + H_2O \xrightarrow{\text{fast}} \underset{\text{3-hydroxybutanal}}{CH_3{-}\underset{\underset{OH}{\mid}}{C}H{-}CH_2{-}CHO} + OH^\ominus}
$$

**Step 4: Dehydration via $\mathrm{E1cB}$ Pathway**  
Heating causes base-promoted removal of the remaining $\alpha$-hydrogen, generating a resonance-stabilized carbanion that expels the poor leaving group ($\mathrm{OH^-}$) to yield a conjugated double bond:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3{-}\underset{\underset{OH}{\mid}}{C}H{-}\overset{\ominus}{\ddot{C}}H{-}CHO \xrightarrow{\Delta} \underset{\text{But-2-enal}}{CH_3{-}CH{=}CH{-}CHO} + OH^\ominus}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Overall Stoichiometry: } 2\,\mathrm{CH_3CHO} \xrightarrow{\text{dil. } \mathrm{NaOH},\, \Delta} \mathrm{CH_3-CH=CH-CHO} + \mathrm{H_2O}}
$$

***

### 2.2 Mechanism: Cannizzaro Reaction of Methanal (Formaldehyde)

#### Reaction Equation

$$
\mathrm{2\,HCHO + conc.\,NaOH\,(50\%) \xrightarrow{\Delta} \underset{\text{Methanol}}{CH_3OH} + \underset{\text{Sodium formate}}{HCOONa}}
$$

#### Step-by-Step Mechanism

**Step 1: Reversible Nucleophilic Addition of Hydroxide**  
The nucleophile $\mathrm{OH^-}$ attacks the electrophilic carbonyl carbon of formaldehyde to yield a mono-anion:

$$
\mathrm{H{-}\overset{O}{\overset{\parallel}{C}}{-}H + OH^\ominus \xrightleftharpoons{\text{fast}} H{-}\underset{\underset{OH}{\mid}}{\overset{O^\ominus}{\overset{\mid}{C}}}{-}H}
$$

*(In strongly basic media, this can further deprotonate to a dianion: $\mathrm{H{-}C(O^-)_2{-}H}$, increasing hydride donor potential).*

**Step 2: Hydride Ion ($\mathrm{H}^\ominus$) Transfer (Rate Determining Step)**  
The monoanion collapses its $\mathrm{C-O^-}$ $\pi$-bond, transferring a hydride ion directly to the carbonyl carbon of a second formaldehyde molecule:

$$
\mathrm{H{-}\underset{\underset{OH}{\mid}}{\overset{O^\ominus}{\overset{\mid}{C}}}{-}H \;+\; \overset{\delta+}{C}(=O)H_2 \xrightarrow{\text{slow (RDS)}} \underset{\text{Formic acid}}{H{-}\overset{O}{\overset{\parallel}{C}}{-}OH} \;+\; \underset{\text{Methoxide ion}}{H_3C{-}O^\ominus}}
$$

**Step 3: Rapid Acid-Base Proton Exchange**  
Formic acid ($pK_a = 3.75$) is vastly more acidic than methanol ($pK_a = 15.5$). An instantaneous proton transfer drives the equilibrium irreversibly to the right:

$$
\mathrm{HCOOH + CH_3O^\ominus \xrightarrow{\text{diffusion-controlled}} \underset{\text{Formate ion}}{HCOO^\ominus} + \underset{\text{Methanol}}{CH_3OH}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Result: } \mathrm{2\,HCHO + NaOH} \longrightarrow \mathrm{HCOONa} + \mathrm{CH_3OH}}
$$

***

### 2.3 Cross-Aldol Condensation: Ethanal and Propanal
When an equimolar mixture of $\mathrm{CH_3CHO}$ and $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CHO}$ reacts with dilute $\mathrm{NaOH}$, **four** condensation products are formed:

| Reaction Category | Reactants Involved | Final Condensation Product ($\alpha,\beta$-unsaturated) | IUPAC Name |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Self-Aldol 1** | $\mathrm{CH_3CHO} + \mathrm{CH_3CHO}$ | $\mathrm{CH_3-CH=CH-CHO}$ | But-2-en-1-al |
| **Self-Aldol 2** | $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CHO} + \mathrm{CH_3CH_2CHO}$ | $\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CH=C(CH_3)-CHO}$ | 2-Methylpent-2-en-1-al |
| **Cross-Aldol 1** | $\mathrm{CH_3CHO}\text{ (acceptor)} + \mathrm{CH_3CH_2CHO}\text{ (enolate)}$ | $\mathrm{CH_3-CH=C(CH_3)-CHO}$ | 2-Methylbut-2-en-1-al |
| **Cross-Aldol 2** | $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CHO}\text{ (acceptor)} + \mathrm{CH_3CHO}\text{ (enolate)}$ | $\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CH=CH-CHO}$ | Pent-2-en-1-al |

$$
\boxed{\text{Total Products} = 4\text{ structural isomers (excluding geometrical cis/trans isomers)}}
$$

### 2.4 Crossed Cannizzaro: Benzaldehyde and Formaldehyde

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5CHO + HCHO \xrightarrow{50\%\,NaOH,\, \Delta} \underset{\text{Benzyl alcohol}}{C_6H_5CH_2OH} + \underset{\text{Sodium formate}}{HCOONa}}
$$

**Governing Rationale:**  
Steric factors and electronic factors favor nucleophilic attack by $\mathrm{OH^-}$ on $\mathrm{HCHO}$ rather than $\mathrm{C_6H_5CHO}$. The intermediate formyl alkoxide transfers hydride ($\mathrm{H^-}$) to benzaldehyde:

$$
\mathrm{H{-}\underset{\underset{OH}{\mid}}{\overset{O^\ominus}{\overset{\mid}{C}}}{-}H + C_6H_5{-}CHO \longrightarrow HCOOH + C_6H_5{-}CH_2O^\ominus \longrightarrow HCOONa + C_6H_5CH_2OH}
$$

## 1. Nomenclature & Molecular Structure of the Carboxyl Group

### 1.1 Structural Anatomy of the Carboxyl Group (⭐⭐⭐)
The carboxyl functional group ($-COOH$) derives its name from two constituent subunits: the **carbonyl** group ($>C=O$) and the **hydroxyl** group ($-OH$).

```
        O :
       //
    R-C
       \
        O - H
        ..
```

* **Hybridization:** The carboxyl carbon is bonded to three atoms via three $\sigma$-bonds and possesses no lone pairs; hence, it is strictly $\mathbf{sp^2}$ **hybridized**.
* **Geometry & Bond Angles:** The carboxyl carbon and the three attached atoms lie in a single plane (coplanar). The bond angles around the carboxyl carbon ($\angle O=C-O$, $\angle R-C=O$, and $\angle R-C-OH$) are all approximately $120^\circ$.
* **Resonance Stabilization of the Carboxyl Group:**
  The unshared pairs of electrons on the hydroxyl oxygen interact with the $\pi$-electron system of the carbonyl group:
  
  

$$
\text{R}-\text{C}(=\text{O})-\ddot{\text{O}}-\text{H} \longleftrightarrow \text{R}-\text{C}(\text{O}^-)=\overset{+}{\text{O}}-\text{H}
$$

  > **NCERT Crucial Concept:** The carbonyl carbon in a carboxylic acid is **less electrophilic** (less electron-deficient) than the carbonyl carbon in aldehydes and ketones. This occurs because the partial positive charge on the carbonyl carbon is substantially neutralized by the continuous resonance donation (mesomeric electron release) from the adjacent lone-pair-bearing hydroxyl oxygen atom. Consequently, carboxylic acids **do not** undergo characteristic nucleophilic addition reactions shown by aldehydes and ketones (e.g., they do not form oximes, hydrazones, or bisulfite adducts under standard conditions).

***

According to the IUPAC rules, the longest continuous carbon chain containing the carboxyl group is selected. The suffix **"-oic acid"** replaces the terminal **"-e"** of the parent alkane. When the $-COOH$ carbon cannot be numbered into the principal chain (e.g., directly bonded to rings or when three or more carboxyl groups are present on an unbranched chain), the suffix **"carboxylic acid"** is utilized.

## 2. Complete Synthetic Protocols & Reaction Mechanisms

Carboxylic acids can be synthesized through six major pathways. Every reaction below includes exact reaction conditions, catalysts, and stoichiometry.

***

### Route 1: Direct Oxidation of Primary Alcohols and Aldehydes (⭐⭐⭐⭐)
Primary alcohols are oxidized cleanly to carboxylic acids using strong oxidants in alkaline, neutral, or acidic media.

#### Chemical Equations

$$
\text{R}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH} \xrightarrow[\text{2. } \text{H}_3\text{O}^+]{1.\; \text{alkaline } \text{KMnO}_4\text{ / }\Delta} \text{R}-\text{COOH}
$$

$$
\text{R}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH} \xrightarrow{\text{CrO}_3\text{ / }\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4\text{ (Jones Reagent)}} \text{R}-\text{COOH}
$$

$$
\text{R}-\text{CHO} \xrightarrow[\text{dil. }\text{HNO}_3\text{ or }\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7\text{ / }\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4]{\text{[O]}} \text{R}-\text{COOH}
$$

* **Reaction Conditions:** 
  * Reagent A: Potassium permanganate ($\text{KMnO}_4$) in aqueous $\text{KOH}$, followed by acidification with dilute $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ or $\text{HCl}$.
  * Reagent B: Jones Reagent ($\text{CrO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ in aqueous acetone at $0\text{--}5^\circ\text{C}$).

***

### Route 2: Vigorous Oxidation of Alkylbenzenes (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
Aromatic carboxylic acids are prepared by the vigorous oxidation of alkylbenzenes with alkaline potassium permanganate or acidic potassium dichromate.

#### Chemical Principles & General Equation

$$
\text{Ar}-\text{CH}_2\text{R}' \xrightarrow[\Delta]{1.\; \text{KMnO}_4\text{ / }\text{KOH}} \text{Ar}-\text{COOK} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{Ar}-\text{COOH}
$$

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{CH}_3 \xrightarrow[\Delta]{1.\; \text{KMnO}_4\text{ / }\text{KOH}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOK} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOH}
$$

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3 \xrightarrow[\Delta]{1.\; \text{KMnO}_4\text{ / }\text{KOH}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOK} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOH}
$$

#### Absolute Requirement: The $\alpha$-Hydrogen Rule
* The entire aliphatic side chain, regardless of its length, is degraded to a single carboxyl group ($-COOH$).
* **Critical Limitation:** The alkyl group **must contain at least one benzylic hydrogen** ($\alpha$-hydrogen). 
* Tert-butylbenzene does **not** undergo oxidation under these conditions because the benzylic carbon is quaternary (no $\alpha\text{-H}$ available for radical/hydride abstraction):

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)_3 \xrightarrow[\Delta]{1.\; \text{KMnO}_4\text{ / }\text{KOH}} No Reaction (Resistant to Oxidation)
$$

***

### Route 3: Stepwise Hydrolysis of Nitriles and Amides (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
Nitriles are converted to carboxylic acids via an amide intermediate under acidic or basic catalytic control.

#### Complete Reaction Scheme

$$
\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{N} \xrightarrow[\text{Catalyst: }\text{H}^+\text{ or }\text{OH}^-]{\text{H}_2\text{O}\text{ (Partial Hydrolysis)}} \text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{NH}_2 \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+\text{ or }\text{OH}^-\text{ (Complete Hydrolysis)}} \text{R}-\text{COOH} + \text{NH}_4^+
$$

#### Step-by-Step Acid-Catalyzed Mechanism
1. **Protonation of the Nitrile Nitrogen:**
   

$$
\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{N} + \text{H}^+ \rightleftharpoons \text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\overset{+}{\text{N}}-\text{H}
$$

2. **Nucleophilic Attack of Water on Electrophilic Carbon:**
   

$$
\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\overset{+}{\text{N}}-\text{H} + \text{H}_2\ddot{\text{O}} \rightleftharpoons \text{R}-\overset{\overset{\overset{+}{\text{O}}\text{H}_2}{\mid}}{\text{C}}=\ddot{\text{N}}-\text{H}
$$

3. **Proton Transfer (Tautomeric Rearrangement to Amide):**
   

$$
\text{R}-\overset{\overset{\overset{+}{\text{O}}\text{H}_2}{\mid}}{\text{C}}=\ddot{\text{N}}-\text{H} \rightleftharpoons \text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{OH}}{\mid}}{\text{C}}=\ddot{\text{N}}-\text{H} + \text{H}^+ \rightleftharpoons \text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\ddot{\text{N}}\text{H}_2 + \text{H}^+
$$

4. **Hydrolytic Cleavage of the Amide:**
   

$$
\text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\ddot{\text{N}}\text{H}_2 \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{R}-\text{COOH} + \text{NH}_4^+
$$

***

### Route 4: Carboxylation of Grignard Reagents (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
Grignard reagents react with dry ice (solid carbon dioxide) to form salts of carboxylic acids which, upon acidification with dilute mineral acid, afford carboxylic acids containing **one more carbon atom** than the starting alkyl/aryl halide.

#### Step-by-Step Reaction Equations
1. **Nucleophilic Addition to Carbon Dioxide:**
   The nucleophilic carbanionic center of the Grignard reagent attacks the electrophilic carbon of carbon dioxide:

   

$$
\text{R}^{\delta-}-\text{MgX}^{\delta+} + \text{O}=\text{C}=\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{Dry Ether}} \left[ \text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{O}^- \;\text{Mg}^{2+}\text{X} \right]
$$

2. **Acid Hydrolysis:**
   

$$
\left[ \text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{O}^- \;\text{Mg}^{2+}\text{X} \right] + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+ \longrightarrow \text{R}-\text{COOH} + \text{Mg}(\text{OH})\text{X}
$$

* **Solvent Condition:** Must be strictly anhydrous (dry diethyl ether or dry THF). Any traces of moisture will protonate the Grignard reagent to form an alkane ($\text{R}-\text{MgX} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{R}-\text{H} + \text{Mg}(\text{OH})\text{X}$), destroying the reagent.

***

### Route 5: Hydrolysis of Acyl Derivatives (Esters, Acid Chlorides, and Anhydrides) (⭐⭐⭐⭐)

#### (a) From Acid Halides

$$
\text{R}-\text{COCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{R}-\text{COOH} + \text{HCl}
$$

$$
\text{R}-\text{COCl} + 2\,\text{OH}^- \longrightarrow \text{R}-\text{COO}^- + \text{Cl}^- + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{R}-\text{COOH}
$$

#### (b) From Acid Anhydrides

$$
(\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CO})_2\text{O} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\,\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOH}
$$

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{COOCOCH}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\,\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}
$$

#### (c) From Esters
* **Acidic Hydrolysis (Reversible Equilibrium):**
  

$$
\text{R}-\text{COOR}' + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightleftharpoons{\text{H}^+} \text{R}-\text{COOH} + \text{R}'-\text{OH}
$$

* **Alkaline Hydrolysis (Irreversible Saponification):**
  

$$
\text{R}-\text{COOR}' + \text{NaOH} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{R}-\text{COO}^-\text{Na}^+ + \text{R}'-\text{OH}
$$

  
  

$$
\text{R}-\text{COO}^-\text{Na}^+ + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+ \longrightarrow \text{R}-\text{COOH} + \text{Na}^+
$$

## 3. Physical Properties & Dimerization

### 3.1 Unusually High Boiling Points
Carboxylic acids exhibit higher boiling points than hydrocarbons, haloalkanes, aldehydes, ketones, and even alcohols of comparable molecular weights.

| Compound | Formula | Molar Mass ($\text{g/mol}$) | Boiling Point ($\text{K}$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Propane | $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3$ | $44$ | $231$ |
| Ethanal | $\text{CH}_3\text{CHO}$ | $44$ | $294$ |
| Ethanol | $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$ | $46$ | $351$ |
| Formic Acid | $\text{HCOOH}$ | $46$ | $373.5$ |
| Acetic Acid | $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$ | $60$ | $391$ |

**Physical Explanation:**
Carboxylic acid molecules form extensive **intermolecular hydrogen bonds**. The hydroxyl hydrogen is strongly polarized by the adjacent carbonyl oxygen, facilitating the formation of extremely stable, cyclic, eight-membered **dimers**.

```
         O ········· H - O
        //                \
     R-C                   C-R
        \                //
         O - H ········· O
```

> **Crucial Observation:** In the **vapor state** and in **aprotic non-polar solvents** (e.g., dry benzene, $\text{CCl}_4$), carboxylic acids exist predominantly as **cyclic dimers**. As a result, vapor-density and colligative-property measurements (e.g., freezing-point depression) yield an apparent molecular mass for acetic acid of approximately $\approx 120\text{ g/mol}$ (double its true empirical formula mass of $60\text{ g/mol}$).

## 4. Rigorous Treatment of Carboxylic Acid Acidity

***

### 4.1 Thermodynamic Definitions: $K_a$ and $\text{p}K_a$ (⭐⭐⭐⭐)
When a carboxylic acid dissociates in water:

$$
\text{R}-\text{COOH} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{R}-\text{COO}^- + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+
$$

The equilibrium constant is given by:

$$
K_{eq} = \frac{[\text{R}-\text{COO}^-][\text{H}_3\text{O}^+]}{[\text{R}-\text{COOH}][\text{H}_2\text{O}]}
$$

Since water is present in large excess, $[\text{H}_2\text{O}]$ is constant:

$$
K_a = K_{eq}[\text{H}_2\text{O}] = \frac{[\text{R}-\text{COO}^-][\text{H}_3\text{O}^+]}{[\text{R}-\text{COOH}]}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{p}K_a = -\log_{10} K_a}
$$

* **Thermodynamic Law:** Greater acid strength corresponds to:
  

$$
\uparrow K_a \iff \downarrow \text{p}K_a
$$

  * Strong acids: $\text{p}K_a < 1$
  * Moderately strong acids: $1 < \text{p}K_a < 5$
  * Weak acids: $5 < \text{p}K_a < 15$
  * Extremely weak acids: $\text{p}K_a > 15$

***

### 4.2 Molecular Basis: Carboxylic Acids vs. Phenols vs. Alcohols (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

$$
Acidity Hierarchy:  \text{R}-\text{COOH} > \text{Ar}-\text{OH} > \text{H}_2\text{O} > \text{R}-\text{OH} > \text{HC}\equiv\text{CH}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{lcccc}
Species: & \text{CH}_3\text{COOH} & \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} & \text{H}_2\text{O} & \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH} \\
pK_a: & 4.76 & 9.98 & 15.7 & 15.9
\end{array}
$$

#### Carboxylate Ion vs. Phenoxide Ion: Why Carboxylic Acids are $10^5$ Times More Acidic than Phenol
1. **Carboxylate Ion ($\text{R}-\text{COO}^-$):**
   

$$
\text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}^-}{\mid}}{\text{C}}=\text{O} \longleftrightarrow \text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{O}^-
$$

   * The conjugate base is stabilized by **two completely equivalent resonance structures**.
   * The negative charge is delocalized over **two highly electronegative oxygen atoms** ($\chi_O \approx 3.44$).
   * The carbon-oxygen bond lengths in the carboxylate ion are completely identical ($127\text{ pm}$), intermediate between single ($136\text{ pm}$) and double ($121\text{ pm}$) bonds.

2. **Phenoxide Ion ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{O}^-$):**
   * The negative charge is delocalized over the aromatic ring across five canonical structures.
   * However, in three of the structures, the negative charge resides on **ortho- and para-carbon atoms** ($\chi_C \approx 2.55$), which are far less electronegative than oxygen.
   * Delocalization over carbon atoms is energetically less favorable than delocalization between two equivalent electronegative oxygen atoms.
   * Furthermore, the initial phenol molecule is itself resonance-stabilized (generating charge separation: positive charge on oxygen, negative on the ring), which diminishes the driving force for deprotonation compared to carboxylic acids.

***

### 4.3 Electronic Factors Governing Acidity (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

$$
\boxed{\text{Acidity} \propto \text{Stability of Conjugate Base (RCOO}^-) \propto \frac{\text{Electron-Withdrawing Groups (-I, -M)}}{\text{Electron-Donating Groups (+I, +M)}}}
$$

#### (a) Inductive Effect ($-I$ vs. $+I$)
* **Electron-Withdrawing Groups (EWGs) ($-I$):** Disperse the negative charge of the carboxylate ion electrostatically, stabilizing it and increasing acid strength.
* **Electron-Donating Groups (EDGs) ($+I$):** Push electron density toward the carboxylate center, intensifying the localized negative charge, destabilizing the anion, and decreasing acid strength.

**Decreasing Order of Inductive Electron Withdrawal ($-I$ Series):**

$$
\text{CF}_3 > \text{NO}_2 > \text{CN} > \text{SO}_3\text{H} > \text{CHO} > \text{COOR} > \text{F} > \text{Cl} > \text{Br} > \text{I} > \text{Ph}
$$

#### (b) Position/Distance Rule for Inductive Effects
The inductive effect is distance-dependent and falls off rapidly through the $\sigma$-bond framework, becoming negligible beyond three $\sigma$-bonds:

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\underset{\substack{\mid\\\text{Cl}}}{\text{CH}}-\text{COOH} \;>\; \text{CH}_3-\underset{\substack{\mid\\\text{Cl}}}{\text{CH}}-\text{CH}_2-\text{COOH} \;>\; \underset{\substack{\mid\\\text{Cl}}}{\text{CH}_2}-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\text{COOH}
$$

$$
\text{2-chlorobutanoic acid } (\text{p}K_a = 2.86) > \text{3-chlorobutanoic acid } (\text{p}K_a = 4.05) > \text{4-chlorobutanoic acid } (\text{p}K_a = 4.52)
$$

#### (c) Number of Halogen Atoms

$$
\text{CCl}_3\text{COOH} > \text{CHCl}_2\text{COOH} > \text{CH}_2\text{ClCOOH} > \text{CH}_3\text{COOH}
$$

$$
\text{p}K_a: \quad 0.64 \quad<\quad 1.29 \quad<\quad 2.87 \quad<\quad 4.76
$$

#### (d) Electronegativity of Halogens

$$
\text{F}-\text{CH}_2\text{COOH} > \text{Cl}-\text{CH}_2\text{COOH} > \text{Br}-\text{CH}_2\text{COOH} > \text{I}-\text{CH}_2\text{COOH}
$$

$$
\text{p}K_a: \quad 2.57 \quad<\quad 2.87 \quad<\quad 2.90 \quad<\quad 3.16
$$

***

### 4.4 Acidity of Substituted Benzoic Acids (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

#### The Ortho Effect in Benzoic Acids
> **Fundamental Law:** Almost **all ortho-substituted benzoic acids are stronger acids than benzoic acid**, regardless of whether the ortho substituent is electron-withdrawing ($-I, -M$ like $-\text{NO}_2$) or electron-donating ($+I, +M$ like $-\text{CH}_3$, $-\text{OCH}_3$, $-\text{OH}$, $-\text{Cl}$).

**Steric & Electronic Origin:**
1. A bulky substituent at the *ortho* position creates severe steric hindrance with the planar carboxyl group.
2. To relieve this steric strain, the $-COOH$ group twists out of the plane of the benzene ring.
3. Once out of plane, the $\pi$-orbital of the carboxyl carbon can no longer overlap effectively with the aromatic ring's $\pi$-system.
4. This **Steric Inhibition of Resonance (SIR)** turns off the electron-donating $+R$ mesomeric resonance from the phenyl ring into the carbonyl carbon.
5. Deprotonation yields a carboxylate ion whose negative charge is delocalized between the two oxygen atoms without destabilizing cross-conjugation from the aromatic ring, shifting the equilibrium toward the carboxylate anion.

#### Substituent Effects at Meta and Para Positions

##### Case 1: Nitrobenzoic Acids ($-\text{NO}_2$ shows $-I$ and $-M$)
* **Ortho-nitrobenzoic acid:** Strongest due to the ortho effect and combined $-I$ and $-M$ effects.
* **Para-nitrobenzoic acid:** The $-\text{NO}_2$ group exerts both a strong $-M$ resonance effect and a $-I$ inductive effect.
* **Meta-nitrobenzoic acid:** Resonance effects cannot operate at the meta position; the $-\text{NO}_2$ group acts solely through a strong $-I$ effect.
* **Benzoic acid:** Unsubstituted reference.

$$
Acidity Order:  \textit{ortho}\text{-nitrobenzoic} > \textit{para}\text{-nitrobenzoic} > \textit{meta}\text{-nitrobenzoic} > \text{benzoic acid}
$$

$$
\text{p}K_a: \quad 2.17 \quad<\quad 3.44 \quad<\quad 3.45 \quad<\quad 4.20
$$

##### Case 2: Methoxybenzoic Acids ($-\text{OCH}_3$ shows $-I$ and $+M$)
* **Ortho-methoxybenzoic acid:** Strongest acid due to the ortho effect ($\text{p}K_a = 4.09$).
* **Benzoic acid:** $\text{p}K_a = 4.20$.
* **Meta-methoxybenzoic acid:** Only the $-I$ effect operates (no $+M$ at meta position), withdrawing electron density and strengthening the acid ($\text{p}K_a = 4.09$).
* **Para-methoxybenzoic acid:** At the para position, the strong electron-releasing $+M$ effect dominates over the weaker $-I$ effect, pumping electron density into the carboxylate anion and significantly destabilizing it ($\text{p}K_a = 4.47$).

$$
Acidity Order:  \textit{ortho}\text{-methoxy} \approx \textit{meta}\text{-methoxy} > \text{benzoic acid} > \textit{para}\text{-methoxybenzoic acid}
$$

## 5. Distinction Tests: Carboxylic Acids vs. Phenols

Carboxylic acids and phenols can be distinguished experimentally by their differential reactivity toward weak bases:

```
                     Compound
                        |
       +----------------+----------------+
       |                                 |
  Carboxylic Acid                     Phenol
       |                                 |
+ NaHCO3 (aq)                     + NaHCO3 (aq)
       |                                 |
Brisk Effervescence (CO2 ↑)       No Reaction (No CO2)
```

$$
\text{R}-\text{COOH} + \text{NaHCO}_3 \longrightarrow \text{R}-\text{COONa} + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2 \uparrow \;(\text{Brisk Effervescence})
$$

$$
\text{Ar}-\text{OH} + \text{NaHCO}_3 \longrightarrow No Reaction
$$

* **Thermodynamic Basis:** A stronger acid displaces a weaker acid from its conjugate salt:
  

$$
\text{Acid}_1 + \text{Salt of Acid}_2 \rightleftharpoons \text{Acid}_2 + \text{Salt of Acid}_1
$$

  * For carboxylic acids:
    

$$
\text{p}K_a(\text{RCOOH}) \approx 4\text{--}5 < \text{p}K_a(\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3) \approx 6.35
$$

    Carboxylic acids are stronger acids than carbonic acid ($\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3$) and drive the reaction forward, liberating $\text{CO}_2$ gas.
  * For phenols:
    

$$
\text{p}K_a(\text{ArOH}) \approx 10 > \text{p}K_a(\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3) \approx 6.35
$$

    Phenol is a weaker acid than carbonic acid and cannot protonate the bicarbonate ion ($\text{HCO}_3^-$).

### [CBSE 2024, 3 Marks]
**Problem:** Arrange the following sets of compounds in order of increasing acid strength, providing reasoned justifications:
1. $\text{F}-\text{CH}_2-\text{COOH}$, $\text{Cl}-\text{CH}_2-\text{COOH}$, $\text{Br}-\text{CH}_2-\text{COOH}$, $\text{CH}_3-\text{COOH}$
2. Benzoic acid, 4-Nitrobenzoic acid, 4-Methoxybenzoic acid
3. $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}(\text{Br})\text{COOH}$, $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}(\text{Br})\text{CH}_2\text{COOH}$, $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CHCOOH}$

**Solution:**

#### Sub-part (1)
* **Order:** $\text{CH}_3-\text{COOH} < \text{Br}-\text{CH}_2-\text{COOH} < \text{Cl}-\text{CH}_2-\text{COOH} < \text{F}-\text{CH}_2-\text{COOH}$
* **Justification:** Halogens exert an electron-withdrawing inductive effect ($-I$), which disperses the negative charge of the conjugate carboxylate anion, increasing its stability. The magnitude of the $-I$ effect is directly proportional to electronegativity: $\text{F} (4.0) > \text{Cl} (3.0) > \text{Br} (2.8) > \text{H} (2.1)$. Higher $-I$ correlates with lower $\text{p}K_a$ and higher acidity.

#### Sub-part (2)
* **Order:** 4-Methoxybenzoic acid $<$ Benzoic acid $<$ 4-Nitrobenzoic acid
* **Justification:** 
  * In 4-nitrobenzoic acid, the $-\text{NO}_2$ group exerts strong electron-withdrawing mesomeric ($-M$) and inductive ($-I$) effects, stabilizing the conjugate benzoate anion.
  * In 4-methoxybenzoic acid, the $-\text{OCH}_3$ group has an unshared electron pair on oxygen that donates electron density into the ring through a positive mesomeric effect ($+M$), which outweighs its $-I$ effect. This destabilizes the conjugate base and lowers its acidity relative to benzoic acid.

#### Sub-part (3)
* **Order:** $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CHCOOH} < \text{CH}_3\text{CH}(\text{Br})\text{CH}_2\text{COOH} < \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}(\text{Br})\text{COOH}$
* **Justification:** The $-I$ effect of bromine decreases rapidly with distance from the carboxyl carbon. In 2-bromobutanoic acid, the bromine is attached to the $\alpha$-carbon (2 bonds away), providing greater stabilization to the carboxylate ion than in 3-bromobutanoic acid ($\beta$-carbon, 3 bonds away). Isobutyric acid lacks a halogen and has two electron-donating methyl groups ($+I$), making it the weakest acid of the three.

### [CBSE 2021 Sample Paper, 2 Marks]
**Problem:** Explain why:
1. Carboxylic acids do not give reactions of the carbonyl group (such as with $\text{NaHSO}_3$ or $\text{NH}_2\text{OH}$).
2. Acetic acid has an apparent molecular mass of $120$ when determined in benzene solvent.

\text{R}-\text{C}(=\text{O})-\ddot{\text{O}}-\text{H} \longleftrightarrow \text{R}-\text{C}(\text{O}^-)=\overset{+}{\text{O}}-\text{H}

$$
\text{Apparent Molar Mass} = 2 \times \text{Molar Mass of Monomer} = 2 \times 60\text{ g/mol} = 120\text{ g/mol}
$$

### [CBSE 2020, 3 Marks]
**Problem:** Give reasons:
1. Formic acid ($\text{HCOOH}$) is a stronger acid than acetic acid ($\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$).
2. Trichloroacetic acid is boiled with copper powder to yield chloroform.
3. Carboxylic acids have higher boiling points than alcohols of similar molecular mass.

**Solution:**
1. The methyl group in acetic acid is an electron-donating group ($+I$), which increases electron density on the carboxylate ion and destabilizes the conjugate base ($\text{CH}_3\text{COO}^-$). Formic acid has a hydrogen atom attached to the carboxyl group instead of an alkyl group, so no $+I$ destabilization occurs, making $\text{HCOOH}$ a stronger acid ($\text{p}K_a = 3.75$) than $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$ ($\text{p}K_a = 4.76$).
2. The three strongly electronegative chlorine atoms in trichloroacetic acid pull electron density away from the $\text{C}-\text{C}$ bond through a strong $-I$ effect. This weakens the $\text{C}-\text{C}$ bond connecting the trichloromethyl carbon to the carboxyl carbon, facilitating decarboxylation upon heating with copper to yield chloroform:
   

$$
\text{CCl}_3\text{COOH} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{Cu}} \text{CHCl}_3 + \text{CO}_2 \uparrow
$$

3. Carboxylic acids form stronger and more extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonds than alcohols. While alcohols form linear hydrogen-bonded chains, carboxylic acids form stable, cyclic dimers with two hydrogen bonds per dimer pair. These dimeric structures require more thermal energy to dissociate into the vapor phase, resulting in higher boiling points.

## 7. Examiner Traps & Common Student Pitfalls

```
                          COMMON EXAM TRAPS
                                 |
       +-------------------------+-------------------------+
       |                         |                         |
  Trap 1: KMnO4              Trap 2: pKa             Trap 3: Nitrile
  Side-Chain                 Inversion               Step-Up
  Oxidation                  Slip                    Omission
       |                         |                         |
Tert-butyl benzene         Low pKa = Strong         Must include
yields NO reaction!        High pKa = Weak          nitrile carbon
(Needs alpha-H)            (Opposite of Ka)         in IUPAC chain
```

* **Trap 1: The Tert-Butyl Benzene Oxidation Error:**
  * *Mistake:* Writing that $(CH_3)_3C-C_6H_5$ oxidizes to benzoic acid when treated with alkaline $\text{KMnO}_4$.
  * *Correction:* Oxidation requires at least one benzylic hydrogen ($\alpha\text{-H}$). Tert-butylbenzene has a quaternary benzylic carbon with no $\alpha\text{-H}$, so it is **completely resistant** to oxidation under these conditions.

* **Trap 2: Forgetting the Acidification Step:**
  * *Mistake:* Writing $\text{Ar}-\text{CH}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{KMnO}_4\text{ / }\text{KOH}} \text{Ar}-\text{COOH}$.
  * *Correction:* Alkaline oxidation first yields the potassium salt ($\text{Ar}-\text{COOK}$). Acidification ($\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$) is necessary to isolate the free carboxylic acid. The correct notation is:
    

$$
\text{Ar}-\text{CH}_3 \xrightarrow[\text{2. } \text{H}_3\text{O}^+]{1.\; \text{KMnO}_4\text{ / }\text{KOH}\text{, }\Delta} \text{Ar}-\text{COOH}
$$

* **Trap 3: $\text{p}K_a$ vs. $K_a$ Value Inversion:**
  * *Mistake:* Assuming that an acid with a higher $\text{p}K_a$ is more acidic.
  * *Correction:* $\text{p}K_a = -\log_{10} K_a$. Therefore, **higher acid strength corresponds to a lower $\text{p}K_a$**.

* **Trap 4: Missing the Nitrile Carbon in IUPAC Nomenclature:**
  * *Mistake:* Naming $\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{CN} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{COOH}$ as "ethanoic acid."
  * *Correction:* The carbon atom of the cyano group ($-CN$) becomes part of the carboxylic acid chain. Hydrolysis of propanenitrile yields **propanoic acid** (a 3-carbon chain).

* **Trap 5: Confusion in Acid-Base Extraction Separations:**
  * *Mistake:* Attempting to separate a mixture of benzoic acid and phenol using aqueous $\text{NaOH}$.
  * *Correction:* Both carboxylic acids ($\text{p}K_a \approx 4.2$) and phenols ($\text{p}K_a \approx 10$) react with aqueous $\text{NaOH}$ to form water-soluble sodium salts. To separate them, use **aqueous $\text{NaHCO}_3$**: benzoic acid dissolves as sodium benzoate with effervescence of $\text{CO}_2$, while phenol remains insoluble and can be separated.

### 8.1 Dicarboxylic Acid Chain Length Mnemonic
To recall the common names of aliphatic dicarboxylic acids of formula $\text{HOOC}-(\text{CH}_2)_n-\text{COOH}$:

\mathbf{O}\text{h }\mathbf{M}\text{y }\mathbf{S}\text{uch }\mathbf{G}\text{ood }\mathbf{A}\text{pple }\mathbf{P}\text{ie}

### 8.2 Acidity Rapid-Sort Engine (The "DNP" Rule)
When comparing the acidities of halogenated carboxylic acids, evaluate substituents in this priority order:

$$
\mathbf{D} \longrightarrow \mathbf{N} \longrightarrow \mathbf{P}
$$

1. **$\mathbf{D}$istance:** The substituent closer to the $-COOH$ group exerts the dominant effect. (An $\alpha$-chloro substituent outweighs a $\beta$-dichloro pattern).
2. **$\mathbf{N}$umber:** If distances are identical, the number of electron-withdrawing groups determines strength. ($\text{Trichloro} > \text{Dichloro} > \text{Monochloro}$).
3. **$\mathbf{P}$ower (Electronegativity):** If distances and numbers are identical, rank by electronegativity/pulling power. ($\text{Fluoro} > \text{Chloro} > \text{Bromo} > \text{Iodo}$).

### 8.3 Carbon-Chain Adjustment Matrix

```
       [ Starting Material: R-X ]
                    |
       +------------+------------+
       |                         |
  Same Carbon Count         Add One Carbon (Step-Up)
       |                         |
1. Hydrolysis (via R-OH)    1. Cyanide (KCN) -> Hydrolysis (H3O+)
   R-X -> R-OH -> R-COOH        R-X -> R-CN -> R-COOH
                                 or
                            2. Grignard Reagent + CO2
                                R-MgX + CO2 -> R-COOH
```

* **Preserving Carbon Count:** 
  

$$
\text{R}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH} \xrightarrow{\text{Jones Reagent}} \text{R}-\text{COOH}
$$

* **Ascending the Series (Adding 1 Carbon / Step-Up):**
  * **Option A:** $\text{R}-\text{X} \xrightarrow{\text{KCN (alc.)}} \text{R}-\text{CN} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+\text{, }\Delta} \text{R}-\text{COOH}$

## 1. Reactions Involving Cleavage of the $\mathrm{C-OH}$ Bond

In these reactions, the hydroxyl group ($-\mathrm{OH}$) attached to the acyl carbon is replaced by a nucleophile or another leaving group, yielding acyl derivatives:

$$
\mathrm{R-CO-OH} + \mathrm{Nu}^- \longrightarrow \mathrm{R-CO-Nu} + \mathrm{OH}^-
$$

***

### 1.1 Formation of Anhydrides (Intermolecular Dehydration)
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**NCERT Reference:** Section 12.8.2  

Carboxylic acids, when heated with mineral acids such as concentrated sulfuric acid ($\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$) or with phosphorus pentoxide ($\mathrm{P_4O_{10}} / \mathrm{P_2O_5}$), undergo intermolecular dehydration to yield acid anhydrides.

#### Complete Chemical Equation:

$$
2\,\mathrm{RCOOH} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\mathrm{H}^+\text{ or }\mathrm{P_2O_5}} \mathrm{R-CO-O-CO-R} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

**Example (Ethanoic Anhydride Synthesis):**

$$
2\,\mathrm{CH_3COOH} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\mathrm{P_2O_5}} \mathrm{(CH_3CO)_2O} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

#### Mechanistic Pathway:
1. **Protonation of Carbonyl Oxygen:**
   

$$
\mathrm{R-COOH} + \mathrm{H}^+ \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{R-C(\!=\!\overset{+}{O}H)OH}
$$

2. **Nucleophilic Attack:** A second molecule of unprotonated carboxylic acid attacks the highly electrophilic carbonyl carbon via its acyl oxygen.
3. **Proton Transfer:** An internal proton shift converts one $-\mathrm{OH}$ group into a superior leaving group ($-\overset{+}{\mathrm{O}}\mathrm{H_2}$).
4. **Water Elimination & Deprotonation:**
   

$$
\mathrm{R-C(OH)(-\overset{+}{O}H_2)-O-C(=O)R} \xrightarrow{-\mathrm{H_2O}} \mathrm{R-C(\!=\!\overset{+}{O}H)-O-C(=O)R} \xrightarrow{-\mathrm{H}^+} \mathrm{(RCO)_2O}
$$

***

### 1.2 Esterification (Fischer-Speier Esterification)
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**NCERT Reference:** Section 12.8.2  

Carboxylic acids react reversibly with primary and secondary alcohols (or phenols) in the presence of an inorganic acid catalyst (concentrated $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ or dry $\mathrm{HCl}$ gas) to form esters.

#### General Reversible Equation:

$$
\mathrm{RCOOH} + \mathrm{R'OH} \xrightleftharpoons{\mathrm{H}^+} \mathrm{RCOOR'} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

#### Detailed Step-by-Step Mechanism:
The entire sequence is an equilibrium reaction governed by Le Chatelier's Principle. To drive the reaction forward, either water is removed continuously using a Dean-Stark apparatus, or an excess of alcohol is used as solvent.

```
Step 1: Protonation of the carbonyl oxygen of carboxylic acid
        O                           +OH
        ||                           ||
    R - C - OH  +  H+   <=====>  R - C - OH

Step 2: Nucleophilic attack of alcohol oxygen on tetrahedral intermediate
        +OH                          OH
         ||                          |
    R - C - OH  +  R'-OH  <=====>  R - C - OH
                                     |
                                    +O - H
                                     |
                                     R'

Step 3: Proton transfer (PT) to an -OH oxygen to generate a good leaving group
        OH                           OH
        |                            |
    R - C - OH  <==============>  R - C - +OH2
        |                            |
       +O - H                        O - R'
        |
        R'

Step 4: Elimination of neutral H2O molecule
        OH
        |                               +OH
    R - C - +OH2  <=====>  - H2O  +  R - C - OR'
        |
        O - R'

Step 5: Deprotonation yielding the neutral ester
        +OH                          O
         ||                          ||
    R - C - OR'   <=====>  - H+  +  R - C - OR'
```

#### Stereochemical & Isotopic Tracer Evidence:
When an alcohol enriched with isotopic oxygen-18 ($\mathrm{R'-{}^{18}OH}$) reacts with benzoic acid:

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH} + \mathrm{CH_3{}^{18}OH} \xrightleftharpoons{\mathrm{H}^+} \mathrm{C_6H_5CO{}^{18}OCH_3} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

*Crucial Inference:* The oxygen in the expelled $\mathrm{H_2O}$ originates exclusively from the carboxylic acid ($-\mathrm{OH}$ cleavage), **not** from the alcohol. The alcohol undergoes $\mathrm{O-H}$ bond cleavage.

#### Steric Hindrance & Reactivity Orders:
Because the rate-determining step involves nucleophilic addition onto a planar $sp^2$ carbon to produce a crowded $sp^3$ tetrahedral intermediate:
- **Order of reactivity of carboxylic acids:**
  

$$
\mathrm{HCOOH} > \mathrm{CH_3COOH} > \mathrm{(CH_3)_2CHCOOH} > \mathrm{(CH_3)_3CCOOH}
$$

- **Order of reactivity of alcohols:**
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3OH} > 1^\circ \text{ alcohol} > 2^\circ \text{ alcohol} \gg 3^\circ \text{ alcohol}
$$

  *(Note: $3^\circ$ alcohols predominantly undergo dehydration to alkenes under strong acid catalysis via carbocation pathways).*

***

### 1.3 Reactions with $\mathrm{PCl_5}$, $\mathrm{PCl_3}$, and $\mathrm{SOCl_2}$
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**NCERT Reference:** Section 12.8.2  

The $-\mathrm{OH}$ group is readily replaced by a chlorine atom ($-\mathrm{Cl}$) upon treatment with inorganic acid chlorides.

#### Stoichiometric Equations:

1. **Reaction with Phosphorus Pentachloride ($\mathrm{PCl_5}$):**
   

$$
\mathrm{RCOOH} + \mathrm{PCl_5} \longrightarrow \mathrm{RCOCl} + \mathrm{POCl_3} + \mathrm{HCl}\uparrow
$$

2. **Reaction with Phosphorus Trichloride ($\mathrm{PCl_3}$):**
   

$$
3\,\mathrm{RCOOH} + \mathrm{PCl_3} \longrightarrow 3\,\mathrm{RCOCl} + \mathrm{H_3PO_3}
$$

   *(By-product: Phosphorous acid / orthophosphorous acid, $\mathrm{H_3PO_3}$).*

3. **Reaction with Thionyl Chloride ($\mathrm{SOCl_2}$):**
   

$$
\mathrm{RCOOH} + \mathrm{SOCl_2} \xrightarrow{\text{reflux (often with pyridine)}} \mathrm{RCOCl} + \mathrm{SO_2}\uparrow + \mathrm{HCl}\uparrow
$$

> **Why Thionyl Chloride ($\mathrm{SOCl_2}$) is Universally Preferred:**  
> The by-products ($\mathrm{SO_2}$ and $\mathrm{HCl}$) are non-condensable gases under reaction conditions. They escape into the atmosphere (or scrubber), driving the equilibrium forward and leaving behind the pure acyl chloride without requiring tedious solvent fractional distillation.

***

### 1.4 Reaction with Ammonia ($\mathrm{NH_3}$) and Subsequent Thermolysis
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**NCERT Reference:** Section 12.8.2  

Carboxylic acids react with ammonia via an acid-base neutralization to form ammonium carboxylate salts. Upon strong heating ($\Delta$), these salts eliminate water to yield primary acid amides.

#### Generalized Scheme:

$$
\mathrm{RCOOH} + \mathrm{NH_3} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{RCOO^-\overset{+}{N}H_4} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{-\mathrm{H_2O}} \mathrm{RCONH_2}
$$

#### Canonical High-Yield Transformations:

1. **Acetamide Formation:**
   

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COOH} + \mathrm{NH_3} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{CH_3COONH_4} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{-\mathrm{H_2O}} \mathrm{CH_3CONH_2}
$$

2. **Benzamide Formation:**
   

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH} + \mathrm{NH_3} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{C_6H_5COONH_4} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{-\mathrm{H_2O}} \mathrm{C_6H_5CONH_2}
$$

3. **Phthalimide Synthesis from Phthalic Acid (Three-Stage Reaction):**
   - **Stage 1 (Salt Formation):**
     

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_4(COOH)_2} + 2\,\mathrm{NH_3} \longrightarrow \mathrm{C_6H_4(COO^-\overset{+}{N}H_4)_2}\quad\text{(Ammonium phthalate)}
$$

   - **Stage 2 (Mild Heating / Intermolecular Dehydration):**
     

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_4(COO^-\overset{+}{N}H_4)_2} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{-2\,\mathrm{H_2O}} \mathrm{C_6H_4(CONH_2)_2}\quad\text{(Phthalamide)}
$$

   - **Stage 3 (Strong Heating / Intramolecular Deammoniation):**
     

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_4(CONH_2)_2} \xrightarrow[\text{Strong }\Delta]{-\mathrm{NH_3}} \mathrm{C_6H_4(CO)_2NH}\quad\text{(Phthalimide)}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Phthalic Acid} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{NH_3}} \text{Ammonium Phthalate} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{-2\,\mathrm{H_2O}} \text{Phthalamide} \xrightarrow[\text{Strong }\Delta]{-\mathrm{NH_3}} \text{Phthalimide}}
$$

## 2. Reactions Involving Cleavage of the Entire $-\mathrm{COOH}$ Group

***

### 2.1 Decarboxylation Reactions
Decarboxylation involves the permanent expulsion of the carboxyl carbon as carbon dioxide ($\mathrm{CO_2}$).

#### A. Soda-Lime Decarboxylation (Oakwood Reaction)
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**NCERT Reference:** Section 12.8.3  

Sodium salts of carboxylic acids, when intimately ground and heated with **soda-lime** (a dry mixture of solid $\mathrm{NaOH}$ and $\mathrm{CaO}$ in a $3:1$ mass ratio), eliminate sodium carbonate to yield an alkane containing **one less carbon atom** than the precursor parent carboxylic acid.

$$
\mathrm{R-COONa} + \mathrm{NaOH} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\mathrm{CaO}} \mathrm{R-H} + \mathrm{Na_2CO_3}
$$

**Example:**

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CH_2COONa} + \mathrm{NaOH} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\mathrm{CaO}} \mathrm{CH_3-CH_3} + \mathrm{Na_2CO_3}
$$

> **Role of Calcium Oxide ($\mathrm{CaO}$):**
> 1. $\mathrm{NaOH}$ is extremely hygroscopic. $\mathrm{CaO}$ acts as a dehydrating/desiccating agent, keeping the reaction mixture completely anhydrous.
> 2. $\mathrm{CaO}$ elevates the fusion temperature, prevents the glass boiling tubes from melting/softening, and avoids thermal cracking by glass etching.

#### B. Kolbe’s Electrolytic Decarboxylation
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐  
An aqueous solution of sodium or potassium carboxylate undergoes electrolysis to yield symmetrical alkanes containing an **even number of carbon atoms** at the anode.

- **Overall Reaction:**
  

$$
2\,\mathrm{RCOOK} + 2\,\mathrm{H_2O} \xrightarrow{\text{Electrolysis}} \mathrm{R-R} + 2\,\mathrm{CO_2}\uparrow + \mathrm{H_2}\uparrow + 2\,\mathrm{KOH}
$$

- **Electrode Half-Reactions:**
  - **At Anode (Oxidation):**
    

$$
2\,\mathrm{RCOO}^- \xrightarrow{-2e^-} 2\,\mathrm{RCOO^\bullet} \xrightarrow{-2\,\mathrm{CO_2}} 2\,\mathrm{R^\bullet} \longrightarrow \mathrm{R-R}
$$

  - **At Cathode (Reduction):**
    

$$
2\,\mathrm{H_2O} + 2e^- \longrightarrow 2\,\mathrm{OH}^- + \mathrm{H_2}\uparrow
$$

- *Limitation:* Methane ($\mathrm{CH_4}$) **cannot** be prepared by Kolbe's electrolysis because the radical coupling requires at least one carbon per alkyl radical.

***

### 2.2 Reduction of Carboxylic Acids
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**NCERT Reference:** Section 12.8.3  

Carboxylic acids are resistant to mild reducing agents (such as $\mathrm{NaBH_4}$ or catalytic $\mathrm{H_2}/\mathrm{Ni}$ at ambient pressure) due to resonance stabilization of the carboxylate-like carbonyl system.

#### Reagents & Specificity:
1. **Lithium Aluminium Hydride ($\mathrm{LiAlH_4}$ in dry ether, followed by $\mathrm{H_3O^+}$):**  
   Smoothly reduces carboxylic acids to primary ($1^\circ$) alcohols in high yields.
   

$$
\mathrm{RCOOH} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) }\mathrm{H_3O^+}]{\text{(i) }\mathrm{LiAlH_4 / dry\ ether}} \mathrm{RCH_2OH}
$$

2. **Diborane ($\mathrm{B_2H_6}$ in THF):**  
   Diborane reduces carboxylic acids rapidly without reducing other reducible functionalities such as esters, nitro ($-\mathrm{NO_2}$), and halogen ($-\mathrm{X}$) groups.
   

$$
\mathrm{RCOOH} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) }\mathrm{H_2O}]{\text{(i) }\mathrm{B_2H_6 / THF}} \mathrm{RCH_2OH}
$$

> **Critical Selectivity Alert:**  
> $\mathrm{NaBH_4}$ **does not** reduce carboxylic acids or esters. It selectively reduces only aldehydes, ketones, and acid halides.

#### Industrial Conversion to Primary Alcohols:
Because $\mathrm{LiAlH_4}$ is expensive, commercial processes first esterify the carboxylic acid, followed by catalytic hydrogenation:

$$
\mathrm{RCOOH} + \mathrm{R'OH} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{H}^+} \mathrm{RCOOR'} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

$$
\mathrm{RCOOR'} + 2\,\mathrm{H_2} \xrightarrow[\text{Heat, Pressure}]{\text{Cu-Cr (Copper Chromite catalyst)}} \mathrm{RCH_2OH} + \mathrm{R'OH}
$$

***

### 2.3 Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (H.V.Z.) Reaction
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**NCERT Reference:** Section 12.8.4  

#### Structural Criterion:
The carboxylic acid **must contain at least one $\alpha$-hydrogen atom**. Carboxylic acids lacking $\alpha$-hydrogens (e.g., $\mathrm{HCOOH}$, $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3CCOOH}$, $\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH}$) do not undergo this reaction.

#### Reaction Scheme:
When treated with chlorine ($\mathrm{Cl_2}$) or bromine ($\mathrm{Br_2}$) in the presence of a catalytic amount of red phosphorus, aliphatic carboxylic acids undergo selective halogenation at the $\alpha$-carbon to yield $\alpha$-halocarboxylic acids.

$$
\mathrm{R-CH_2-COOH} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) }\mathrm{H_2O}]{\text{(i) }\mathrm{X_2 / \text{Red P}}} \mathrm{R-CH(X)-COOH} \quad (\mathrm{X} = \mathrm{Cl, Br})
$$

**Example 1:**

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COOH} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) }\mathrm{H_2O}]{\text{(i) }\mathrm{Br_2 / \text{Red P}}} \mathrm{BrCH_2COOH} \quad\text{($\alpha$-Bromoethanoic acid)}
$$

**Example 2 (Excess Reagent):**

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COOH} \xrightarrow[\text{excess }\mathrm{Cl_2 / \text{Red P}}]{} \mathrm{CH_2ClCOOH} \xrightarrow{} \mathrm{CHCl_2COOH} \xrightarrow{} \mathrm{CCl_3COOH}
$$

#### Mechanistic Rationale:
1. Red phosphorus reacts *in situ* with the halogen: $2\,\mathrm{P} + 3\,\mathrm{X_2} \longrightarrow 2\,\mathrm{PX_3}$.
2. $\mathrm{PX_3}$ transforms a small equilibrium fraction of carboxylic acid into the acid halide: $\mathrm{RCH_2COOH} + \mathrm{PX_3} \longrightarrow \mathrm{RCH_2COX}$.
3. The acid halide undergoes rapid keto-enol tautomerism to its enol form: $\mathrm{R-CH=C(X)OH}$.
4. The enol double bond electrophilically attacks $\mathrm{X_2}$, yielding the $\alpha$-halo acyl halide: $\mathrm{R-CH(X)-COX}$.
5. Rapid exchange with unreacted carboxylic acid regenerates the acid halide and yields the final product:
   

$$
\mathrm{R-CH(X)-COX} + \mathrm{R-CH_2-COOH} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{R-CH(X)-COOH} + \mathrm{R-CH_2-COX}
$$

## 3. Ring Substitution Reactions of Aromatic Carboxylic Acids

**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**NCERT Reference:** Section 12.8.4  

The carboxyl group ($-\mathrm{COOH}$) attached to an aromatic ring acts as an **electron-withdrawing group ($-M$ resonance effect and $-I$ inductive effect)**.

```
Resonance structures of Benzoic Acid:
    :O:                :O:-               :O:-               :O:-               :O:
    ||                 |                  |                  |                  ||
    C - OH             C - OH             C - OH             C - OH             C - OH
   /                  //                 //                 //                 /
 // \               //  \+              /   \              /   \+            // \
|    |   <=====>   |     |   <=====>   |     |+  <=====>  |     |   <=====> |    |
 \\ /               \\  /               \\  /              \\  /             \\ /
```

#### Key Consequences:
1. **Deactivation:** The $-\mathrm{COOH}$ group withdraws $\pi$-electron density from the aromatic core, making the ring less nucleophilic than benzene toward electrophiles.
2. **Meta-Direction:** The resonance structures show that the ortho- and para-positions acquire partial positive charges ($\delta^+$). The electrophile ($\mathrm{E}^+$) attacks the meta-position, where electron deficiency is comparatively lower.

***

### 3.1 Nitration of Benzoic Acid
Benzoic acid reacts with a nitrating mixture (concentrated $\mathrm{HNO_3} +$ concentrated $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$) at elevated temperatures to yield $m$-nitrobenzoic acid.

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH} + \mathrm{HNO_3} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{conc. }\mathrm{H_2SO_4}} \underset{m\text{-Nitrobenzoic acid}}{\mathrm{C_6H_4(NO_2)(COOH)}} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

- **Electrophile:** Nitronium ion ($\mathrm{NO_2^+}$).
- **Position of attack:** Meta ($C_3$ or $C_5$).

***

### 3.2 Bromination of Benzoic Acid
Benzoic acid reacts with liquid bromine in the presence of anhydrous iron(III) bromide ($\mathrm{FeBr_3}$) to yield $m$-bromobenzoic acid.

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH} + \mathrm{Br_2} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{FeBr_3}} \underset{m\text{-Bromobenzoic acid}}{\mathrm{C_6H_4(Br)(COOH)}} + \mathrm{HBr}
$$

- **Electrophile:** Bromonium complex ($\mathrm{Br^\delta+ \cdots FeBr_4^\delta-}$).

***

### 3.3 The Anomaly: Why Aromatic Acids Do NOT Undergo Friedel-Crafts Reactions
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  
*(Consistently tested in CBSE reasoning questions).*

**Question:** Benzoic acid does not undergo Friedel-Crafts Alkylation or Friedel-Crafts Acylation. Why?

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH} + \mathrm{CH_3Cl} \xrightarrow{\text{anhyd. }\mathrm{AlCl_3}} \mathbf{\text{NO REACTION}}
$$

**Examiner-Required Two-Point Answer:**
1. **Severe Ring Deactivation:** The carboxyl group ($-\mathrm{COOH}$) exerts strong electron-withdrawing resonance ($-M$) and inductive ($-I$) effects, deactivating the benzene ring toward electrophilic attack by relatively weak Friedel-Crafts carbocations ($\mathrm{R^+}$ or $\mathrm{R-\overset{+}{C}=O}$).
2. **Lewis Acid Deactivation via Catalyst Poisoning:** The catalyst, anhydrous $\mathrm{AlCl_3}$, is a strong Lewis acid. It coordinates with the lone pair of electrons on the basic carboxyl oxygen atom:
   

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH} + \mathrm{AlCl_3} \longrightarrow \mathrm{C_6H_5-\overset{+}{C}(OH)=O\!: \rightarrow AlCl_3^-}
$$

   This coordination introduces a formal positive charge adjacent to the ring, deactivating the aromatic ring toward any electrophilic attack.

### [CBSE 2024, 3 Marks]
**Question:** An organic compound $[\mathrm{A}]$ with molecular formula $\mathrm{C_8H_8O}$ forms an orange-red precipitate with 2,4-DNP reagent and gives a yellow precipitate on heating with $\mathrm{I_2}$ and $\mathrm{NaOH}$. It neither reduces Tollens' reagent nor Fehling's solution. Compound $[\mathrm{A}]$ on vigorous oxidation with alkaline $\mathrm{KMnO_4}$ followed by acidification yields compound $[\mathrm{B}]$ of molecular formula $\mathrm{C_7H_6O_2}$. Compound $[\mathrm{B}]$ on reaction with $\mathrm{SOCl_2}$ gives $[\mathrm{C}]$. Identify compounds $[\mathrm{A}]$, $[\mathrm{B}]$, and $[\mathrm{C}]$. Write the reaction of $[\mathrm{B}]$ with bromine in the presence of $\mathrm{FeBr_3}$.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. **Identification of $[\mathrm{A}]$:**
   - Forms orange-red precipitate with 2,4-DNP $\Longrightarrow$ Contains a carbonyl group ($>\!\mathrm{C=O}$).
   - Does not reduce Tollens' or Fehling's reagent $\Longrightarrow$ It is a **ketone**, not an aldehyde.
   - Gives yellow precipitate of iodoform ($\mathrm{CHI_3}$) with $\mathrm{I_2}/\mathrm{NaOH}$ $\Longrightarrow$ It is a **methyl ketone** ($-\mathrm{COCH_3}$).
   - Molecular formula is $\mathrm{C_8H_8O}$. Accounting for $-\mathrm{COCH_3}$ ($2$ carbons), the remaining moiety is $-\mathrm{C_6H_5}$ (phenyl ring).
   - Thus, $[\mathrm{A}]$ is **Acetophenone** ($\mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3}$).

2. **Identification of $[\mathrm{B}]$:**
   - Acetophenone on vigorous oxidation with alkaline $\mathrm{KMnO_4}$ yields benzoic acid:
     

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) }\mathrm{H_3O^+}]{\text{(i) }\mathrm{KMnO_4 / KOH, }\Delta} \mathrm{C_6H_5COOH}\quad\text{($[\mathrm{B}]$, Benzoic acid, }\mathrm{C_7H_6O_2}\text{)}
$$

3. **Identification of $[\mathrm{C}]$:**
   - Benzoic acid on treatment with $\mathrm{SOCl_2}$ replaces $-\mathrm{OH}$ with $-\mathrm{Cl}$:
     

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH} + \mathrm{SOCl_2} \longrightarrow \mathrm{C_6H_5COCl}\quad\text{($[\mathrm{C}]$, Benzoyl chloride)} + \mathrm{SO_2}\uparrow + \mathrm{HCl}\uparrow
$$

4. **Bromination of $[\mathrm{B}]$:**
   

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH} + \mathrm{Br_2} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{FeBr_3}} m\text{-}\mathrm{Br-C_6H_4-COOH} + \mathrm{HBr}
$$

### [CBSE 2022, 2 Marks]
**Question:** Give plausible explanations for the following:
1. Benzoic acid does not undergo Friedel-Crafts reaction.
2. Formic acid ($\mathrm{HCOOH}$) reduces Tollens' reagent, but acetic acid ($\mathrm{CH_3COOH}$) does not.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. The $-\mathrm{COOH}$ group is strongly electron-withdrawing ($-M$ and $-I$ effects) and deactivates the benzene ring. Furthermore, the Lewis acid catalyst ($\mathrm{AlCl_3}$) coordinates with the basic carboxyl group, forming a complex that places a positive charge adjacent to the ring, preventing electrophilic substitution.
2. The molecular structure of formic acid contains both an aldehyde functionality and a carboxylic acid functionality:
   ```
       O
     //
   H - C - OH  <-- Contains a formyl (aldehyde) H-C=O group
   ```
   Because of this free formyl hydrogen, formic acid readily acts as a reducing agent and oxidizes to $\mathrm{CO_2}$ and $\mathrm{H_2O}$, reducing Tollens' reagent to metallic silver. In acetic acid ($\mathrm{CH_3-COOH}$), this oxidizable formyl hydrogen is absent.

### [CBSE 2021, Compartment, 3 Marks]
**Question:** How will you carry out the following multi-step conversions?
1. Benzoic acid to $m$-Nitrobenzyl alcohol.
2. Ethanoic acid to 2-Hydroxyethanoic acid (Glycolic acid).

**Step-by-Step Solution:**

1. **Benzoic acid to $m$-Nitrobenzyl alcohol:**
   - **Step 1 (Nitration):**
     

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH} + \text{conc. }\mathrm{HNO_3} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{conc. }\mathrm{H_2SO_4}} m\text{-}\mathrm{NO_2-C_6H_4-COOH}
$$

   - **Step 2 (Selective Reduction):**  
     Reduce the carboxyl group without reducing the nitro group using **Diborane** ($\mathrm{B_2H_6}$) (or $\mathrm{LiAlH_4}$ under controlled conditions):
     

$$
m\text{-}\mathrm{NO_2-C_6H_4-COOH} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) }\mathrm{H_3O^+}]{\text{(i) }\mathrm{B_2H_6 / THF}} m\text{-}\mathrm{NO_2-C_6H_4-CH_2OH}
$$

2. **Ethanoic acid to 2-Hydroxyethanoic acid:**
   - **Step 1 (HVZ Halogenation):**
     

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COOH} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) }\mathrm{H_2O}]{\text{(i) }\mathrm{Cl_2 / \text{Red P}}} \underset{\text{Chloroacetic acid}}{\mathrm{Cl-CH_2-COOH}}
$$

   - **Step 2 (Nucleophilic Substitution):**
     

$$
\mathrm{Cl-CH_2-COOH} + 2\,\mathrm{KOH(aq)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathrm{HO-CH_2-COOK} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{H}^+} \underset{\text{Glycolic acid}}{\mathrm{HO-CH_2-COOH}}
$$

### [CBSE 2020, 1 Mark]
**Question:** Arrange the following in decreasing order of their reactivity toward esterification with ethanol:

$$

**Solution:**

## 6. Examiner Traps & Common Student Mistakes

| Trap / Exam Error Point | Common Erroneous Student Conception | Correct Chemical Reality / Marking Criterion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **HVZ Reaction Reagents** | Writing "$\mathrm{Br_2} / \mathrm{FeBr_3}$" for $\alpha$-bromination of aliphatic acids. | The correct reagent is **$\mathrm{Br_2} / \text{Red P}$ followed by $\mathrm{H_2O}$**. $\mathrm{FeBr_3}$ triggers aromatic ring halogenation, not aliphatic $\alpha$-enolization. |
| **HVZ Substrate Criteria** | Attempting HVZ on Trimethylacetic acid, Formic acid, or Benzoic acid. | **Must have $\alpha$-hydrogen atoms.** $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3C-COOH}$, $\mathrm{HCOOH}$, and $\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH}$ have zero $\alpha$-hydrogens and yield **No Reaction**. |
| **Esterification Oxygen Source** | Claiming water's oxygen comes from the alcohol. | As proven by ${}^{18}\mathrm{O}$ isotopic labeling, the $-\mathrm{OH}$ group is excised from the **carboxylic acid**, and the $-\mathrm{H}$ from the **alcohol**. |
| **Decarboxylation Carbon Count** | Expecting an alkane with the same number of carbons from soda-lime. | Soda-lime decarboxylation produces an alkane with **one fewer carbon atom** ($n-1$) than the starting carboxylate salt. |
| **Carboxylic Acid Reduction** | Writing $\mathrm{NaBH_4}$ to reduce $-\mathrm{COOH}$ to $-\mathrm{CH_2OH}$. | $\mathrm{NaBH_4}$ **cannot** reduce carboxylic acids. You must specify **$\mathrm{LiAlH_4}$ in ether** or **$\mathrm{B_2H_6}$ in THF**. |
| **Friedel-Crafts on Benzoic Acid** | Predicting ortho- or para-substituted alkylated benzoic acid. | Benzoic acid **does not undergo** Friedel-Crafts alkylation or acylation due to strong ring deactivation and $\mathrm{AlCl_3}$ coordination. |

### Question 3 [CBSE 2024, 1 Mark] ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Which of the following carboxylic acids has the lowest $\text{p}K_a$ value?
- (A) $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$
- (B) $\text{FCH}_2\text{COOH}$
- (C) $\text{ClCH}_2\text{COOH}$
- (D) $\text{BrCH}_2\text{COOH}$

**Model Solution:**
- **Correct Option:** **(B)**
- **Explanation:** Acidity increases as $\text{p}K_a$ decreases. Stronger electron-withdrawing groups ($-I$ effect) stabilize the carboxylate conjugate base ($\text{RCOO}^-$). Because fluorine is the most electronegative halogen, its $-I$ inductive effect is the strongest:
  

$$
\text{F} > \text{Cl} > \text{Br} > \text{H}
$$

  Fluoroacetic acid ($\text{FCH}_2\text{COOH}$) is the strongest acid and therefore possesses the lowest $\text{p}K_a$.

**Marking Scheme:**
* Option (B): $[1/2 \text{ mark}]$
* Justification based on electronegativity/$-I$ effect and $\text{p}K_a$: $[1/2 \text{ mark}]$

### Question 8 [CBSE 2024 Delhi, 2 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Give plausible chemical explanations for the following:
1. Aldehydes are generally more reactive than ketones towards nucleophilic addition reactions.
2. Carboxylic acids have higher boiling points than alcohols of similar molecular masses.

**Model Solution:**
1. **Reactivity of Aldehydes vs Ketones:**
   - **Steric Factor:** Aldehydes have only one alkyl substituent attached to the carbonyl carbon, whereas ketones have two bulky alkyl groups that sterically hinder the incoming nucleophile.
   - **Electronic Factor:** Two alkyl groups in ketones push electron density toward the carbonyl carbon via $+I$ effect more than the single alkyl group in aldehydes, reducing electrophilicity ($\delta+$ charge) of the carbonyl carbon.
2. **Boiling Point of Carboxylic Acids:**
   - Carboxylic acid molecules are held together by stronger, extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding. In liquid and vapor states, they exist as stable cyclic dimers through two hydrogen bonds per pair of molecules:
     

$$
\begin{matrix} \text{R--C} & = & \text{O} \cdots \text{H--O} \\ & & \vdots \quad\quad\quad \vdots \\ \text{O--H} & \cdots & \text{O} = \text{C--R} \end{matrix}
$$

**CBSE Marking Scheme:**
* (1) Mentioning both steric hindrance and $+I$ electronic effect: $[1 \text{ mark}]$ (or $[1/2 \text{ mark}]$ for either one).
* (2) Mentioning extensive intermolecular H-bonding / cyclic dimer formation: $[1 \text{ mark}]$.

### Question 10 [CBSE 2022 Term-2, 2 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Write chemical equations to illustrate the following named reactions:
1. Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) reaction
2. Clemmensen reduction

**Model Solution:**
1. **Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) reaction:**
   Carboxylic acids having an $\alpha$-hydrogen react with chlorine or bromine in the presence of a catalytic amount of red phosphorus to yield $\alpha$-halocarboxylic acids:
   

$$
\text{R--CH}_2\text{--COOH} \xrightarrow{\text{(i) } \text{X}_2\text{ / Red P},\ \text{(ii) } \text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{R--CH(X)--COOH} \quad (\text{where X} = \text{Cl, Br})

   *Example:*
   

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} \xrightarrow{\text{(i) } \text{Br}_2\text{ / Red P},\ \text{(ii) } \text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{CH}_2\text{BrCOOH}
$$

2. **Clemmensen reduction:**
   The carbonyl group of aldehydes and ketones is reduced directly to a methylene ($-\text{CH}_2-$) group upon treatment with zinc amalgam ($\text{Zn-Hg}$) and concentrated hydrochloric acid:
   

$$
>\!\text{C=O} + 4[\text{H}] \xrightarrow{\text{Zn-Hg / conc. HCl}} >\!\text{CH}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

   *Example:*
   

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{Zn-Hg / conc. HCl}} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

**CBSE Marking Scheme:**
* (1) Complete balanced HVZ reaction with condition (Red P / $\text{X}_2$, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$): $[1 \text{ mark}]$
* (2) Complete balanced Clemmensen reduction equation with reagents ($\text{Zn-Hg} / \text{conc. HCl}$): $[1 \text{ mark}]$

### Question 13 [CBSE 2023 Delhi, 3 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Write the products formed when ethanal ($\text{CH}_3\text{CHO}$) reacts with:
1. Dilute $\text{NaOH}$ followed by heating.
2. $\text{H}_2\text{N--NH--CONH}_2$ (semicarbazide) in a weakly acidic medium.

1. **Reaction with dilute $\text{NaOH}$ followed by heating (Aldol Condensation):**
   Two molecules of ethanal condense in dilute alkali to give 3-hydroxybutanal, which loses water on heating to form but-2-enal (crotonaldehyde):

   The nucleophilic hydrazine-terminal $-\text{NH}_2$ reacts with the carbonyl group to yield an ethanal semicarbazone:
   

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CHO} + \text{H}_2\text{N--NH--CONH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+} \text{CH}_3\text{CH=N--NH--CONH}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

3. **Reaction with $\text{HCN}$ followed by complete acid hydrolysis:**
   - Addition of $\text{HCN}$ yields ethanal cyanohydrin:
     

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CHO} + \text{HCN} \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{--CH(OH)--CN}
$$

### Question 15 [CBSE 2024 Delhi & All India, 4 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
**Read the passage given below and answer the following questions:**

> Carbonyl compounds containing $\alpha$-hydrogens undergo self-condensation in the presence of dilute bases to form $\beta$-hydroxy aldehydes or $\beta$-hydroxy ketones, known as aldols or ketols, respectively. This reaction is designated as the **Aldol reaction**. Dehydration of these products yields $\alpha,\beta$-unsaturated carbonyl compounds. When aldol condensation is performed between two different aldehydes or ketones, it is known as a **Cross-Aldol condensation**. If both carbonyl partners contain $\alpha$-hydrogens, a mixture of four distinct condensation products is obtained, which limits synthetic utility. However, if one aldehyde lacks $\alpha$-hydrogens (e.g., benzaldehyde or formaldehyde), cross-aldol condensation gives predominantly a single condensation product. In contrast, aldehydes lacking $\alpha$-hydrogen atoms undergo self-oxidation and reduction (disproportionation) when treated with concentrated alkali, generating an alcohol and a carboxylate salt (Cannizzaro reaction).

1. Write the IUPAC name and structural formula of the aldol condensation product obtained from propan-2-one. *(1 Mark)*
2. Why does methanal ($\text{HCHO}$) undergo Cannizzaro reaction whereas ethanal ($\text{CH}_3\text{CHO}$) does not? *(1 Mark)*
3. **Answer either sub-part (a) or (b):** *(2 Marks)*
   - **(a)** Predict all possible condensation products formed when an equimolar mixture of ethanal and propanal is warmed in the presence of dilute aqueous $\text{NaOH}$.
   - **(b)** Write the complete chemical equation for the reaction of benzaldehyde with concentrated potassium hydroxide solution ($50\%\ \text{KOH}$) and provide the IUPAC names of both products.

**Model Solution:**
1. **Condensation product of propan-2-one:**
   - Propan-2-one condenses to give 4-hydroxy-4-methylpentan-2-one, which upon elimination of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ yields:
     

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{--C(CH}_3\text{)=CH--CO--CH}_3
$$

   - **IUPAC Name:** **4-Methylpent-3-en-2-one**

2. **Reason:**
   - Methanal ($\text{HCHO}$) has no $\alpha$-carbon and thus completely lacks $\alpha$-hydrogen atoms, enabling hydride transfer upon nucleophilic addition of $\text{OH}^-$.
   - Ethanal ($\text{CH}_3\text{CHO}$) possesses three weakly acidic $\alpha$-hydrogens, so base treatment induces rapid $\alpha$-deprotonation to form a resonance-stabilized enolate ion (aldol route) rather than disproportionation.

3. **(a) Mixture of Ethanal and Propanal:**
   Four products are obtained:
   - **Simple (Self) Aldol Products:**
     1. From 2 molecules of ethanal: **But-2-enal** ($\text{CH}_3\text{--CH=CH--CHO}$)
     2. From 2 molecules of propanal: **2-Methylpent-2-enal** ($\text{CH}_3\text{--CH}_2\text{--CH=C(CH}_3\text{)--CHO}$)
   - **Cross-Aldol Products:**
     3. Ethanal acting as nucleophile + Propanal as electrophile: **Pent-2-enal** ($\text{CH}_3\text{--CH}_2\text{--CH=CH--CHO}$)
     4. Propanal acting as nucleophile + Ethanal as electrophile: **2-Methylbut-2-enal** ($\text{CH}_3\text{--CH=C(CH}_3\text{)--CHO}$)

   *OR*

   **(b) Cannizzaro Reaction of Benzaldehyde:**
   - Reaction Equation:
     

$$
2\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CHO} + \text{KOH (conc.)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_2\text{OH} + \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOK}
$$

   - **Products:**
     - $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$: **Phenylmethanol** (Benzyl alcohol)
     - $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOK}$: **Potassium benzoate**

### Question 16 [CBSE 2024 All India / 2023 Delhi, 5 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
**(a)** An organic compound **[A]** having molecular formula $\text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}\text{O}$ gives a positive 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine (2,4-DNP) test but negative Tollens' and Fehling's tests. Compound **[A]** on oxidation with alkaline $\text{KMnO}_4$ followed by acidification gives a mixture of ethanoic acid and propanoic acid.
1. Identify the compound **[A]**.
2. Write the structural formula and IUPAC name of **[A]**.
3. Write the chemical equation for the oxidation of **[A]** by $\text{KMnO}_4$. *(3 Marks)*

**(b)** Give reasons for the following observations:
1. Chloroacetic acid is a stronger acid than acetic acid.
2. The boiling point of propanal is lower than that of propan-1-ol. *(2 Marks)*

**Model Solution:**
**(a)**
1. **Deduction:**
   - Positive 2,4-DNP test shows **[A]** is a carbonyl compound (aldehyde or ketone).
   - Negative Tollens' and Fehling's tests confirm that **[A]** is a **ketone**.
   - Drastic oxidation gives a mixture of ethanoic acid ($\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$, 2 carbons) and propanoic acid ($\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{COOH}$, 3 carbons).
   - Popoff's rule indicates that cleavage of the carbon chain occurs on either side of the carbonyl group. A five-carbon symmetric ketone, pentan-3-one, cleaves to yield ethanoic acid and propanoic acid:
     

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{--CH}_2\text{--CO--CH}_2\text{--CH}_3 \xrightarrow{[\text{O}]} \text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{COOH}
$$

2. **Identity & IUPAC Name:**
   - **Compound [A]:** Pentan-3-one
   - **Structure:** $\text{CH}_3\text{--CH}_2\text{--CO--CH}_2\text{--CH}_3$
   - **IUPAC Name:** **Pentan-3-one**
3. **Oxidation Equation:**
   

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{COCH}_2\text{CH}_3 + 3[\text{O}] \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) } \text{H}^+]{\text{(i) Alk. KMnO}_4,\ \Delta} \text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{COOH}
$$

**(b)**
1. **Chloroacetic acid vs Acetic acid:**
   - Chlorine is electronegative and exerts a strong electron-withdrawing inductive effect ($-I$ effect).
   - This $-I$ effect disperses the negative charge of the carboxylate anion ($\text{ClCH}_2\text{COO}^-$), stabilizing the conjugate base relative to the acetate ion ($\text{CH}_3\text{COO}^-$), where the methyl group destabilizes the anion via electron-donating $+I$ effect.
   - Hence, chloroacetic acid releases protons more readily.
2. **Boiling Point: Propanal vs Propan-1-ol:**
   - Propan-1-ol molecules form strong intermolecular hydrogen bonds due to the polar $-\text{O--H}$ bond.
   - Propanal exhibits only weaker dipole-dipole interactions between polar carbonyl ($>\!\text{C}=\text{O}$) groups.
   - Weaker intermolecular attractions require less energy to overcome, giving propanal a lower boiling point.

### Question 17 [CBSE 2023 All India / 2022 Term-2, 5 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
**(a)** Describe the following chemical reactions with a suitable balanced chemical equation for each:
1. Rosenmund Reduction
2. Wolff-Kishner Reduction
3. Decarboxylation using Soda-lime *(3 Marks)*

**(b)** Show how each of the following conversions can be carried out in not more than two steps:
1. Bromobenzene to 1-Phenylethanol
2. Ethanal to But-2-enoic acid *(2 Marks)*

**Model Solution:**
**(a)**
1. **Rosenmund Reduction:**
   Acyl chlorides (acid chlorides) are catalytically hydrogenated to aldehydes over palladium supported on barium sulfate, deactivated (poisoned) with sulfur or quinoline to prevent over-reduction to alcohols:
   

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{COCl} + \text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Pd--BaSO}_4\text{ / S / Boiling Xylene}} \text{CH}_3\text{CHO} + \text{HCl}
$$

2. **Wolff-Kishner Reduction:**
   The carbonyl group of aldehydes or ketones is converted to a hydrazone by treatment with hydrazine, which upon heating with potassium hydroxide in a high-boiling solvent like ethylene glycol yields the corresponding alkane:
   

$$
>\!\text{C=O} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{N--NH}_2,\ -\text{H}_2\text{O}} >\!\text{C=N--NH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{KOH / Ethylene glycol},\ \Delta} >\!\text{CH}_2 + \text{N}_2 \uparrow
$$

   *Example:*
   

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_3 \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) KOH / glycol, } \Delta]{\text{(i) } \text{NH}_2\text{NH}_2} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3 + \text{N}_2 \uparrow
$$

3. **Decarboxylation with Soda-lime:**
   Sodium salts of carboxylic acids lose carbon dioxide when heated dry with soda-lime (a mixture of $\text{NaOH}$ and $\text{CaO}$ in a $3:1$ mass ratio) to produce an alkane with one fewer carbon atom:
   

$$
\text{R--COONa} + \text{NaOH} \xrightarrow{\text{CaO},\ \Delta} \text{R--H} + \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3
$$

   *Example:*
   

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{COONa} + \text{NaOH} \xrightarrow{\text{CaO},\ \Delta} \text{CH}_4 + \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3
$$

**(b)**
1. **Bromobenzene to 1-Phenylethanol:**
   - **Step 1:** Prepare phenylmagnesium bromide (Grignard reagent):
     

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Br} + \text{Mg} \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{MgBr}
$$

   - **Step 2:** Nucleophilic addition to ethanal followed by acid hydrolysis:
     

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{MgBr} + \text{CH}_3\text{CHO} \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{--CH(OMgBr)--CH}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{--CH(OH)--CH}_3 + \text{Mg(OH)Br}
$$

2. **Ethanal to But-2-enoic acid:**
   - **Step 1:** Aldol condensation in dilute alkali followed by dehydration to yield but-2-enal:
     

$$
2\text{CH}_3\text{CHO} \xrightarrow{\text{dil. NaOH},\ \Delta} \text{CH}_3\text{--CH=CH--CHO} + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

   - **Step 2:** Selective oxidation of the aldehyde group using Tollens' reagent ($[\text{Ag}(\text{NH}_3)_2]^+$), preserving the carbon-carbon double bond:
     

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{--CH=CH--CHO} + 2[\text{Ag}(\text{NH}_3)_2]^+ + 3\text{OH}^- \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{--CH=CH--COO}^- + 2\text{Ag} \downarrow + 4\text{NH}_3 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

     

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{--CH=CH--COO}^- \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+} \text{CH}_3\text{--CH=CH--COOH} \quad (\text{But-2-enoic acid})
$$

### Question 18 [CBSE 2025 Sample Question Paper / 2020 Delhi, 5 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
**(a)** Complete each of the following chemical reactions by identifying **[A]**, **[B]**, and **[C]**:
1. 

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CHO} + \text{HCHO} \xrightarrow{\text{conc. NaOH},\ \Delta} \mathbf{[A]} + \mathbf{[B]}
$$

2. 

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{--C}\equiv\text{CH} \xrightarrow{\text{dil. } \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4\text{ / }\text{HgSO}_4,\ 333\text{ K}} \mathbf{[C]}
$$

 *(3 Marks)*

**(b)** Provide an organic chemical mechanism for the nucleophilic addition of hydrogen cyanide ($\text{HCN}$) to a carbonyl group in the presence of a base ($\text{OH}^-$). *(2 Marks)*

**Model Solution:**
**(a)**
1. **Crossed Cannizzaro Reaction:**
   - Both benzaldehyde and formaldehyde lack $\alpha$-hydrogens.
   - Formaldehyde is oxidized more readily than benzaldehyde because nucleophilic attack by hydroxide on formaldehyde is sterically and electronically favored.
   - Hence, formaldehyde is oxidized to sodium formate, and benzaldehyde is reduced to benzyl alcohol:
     

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CHO} + \text{HCHO} \xrightarrow{\text{conc. NaOH}} \underbrace{\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_2\text{OH}}_{\mathbf{[A]}\text{ (Benzyl alcohol)}} + \underbrace{\text{HCOONa}}_{\mathbf{[B]}\text{ (Sodium formate)}}
$$

2. **Hydration of Alkyne:**
   - Propyne adds water across the triple bond according to Markovnikov's rule in the presence of mercuric sulfate and sulfuric acid:
     

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{--C}\equiv\text{CH} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{Hg}^{2+}\text{ / }\text{H}^+} [\text{CH}_3\text{--C(OH)=CH}_2] \xrightarrow{\text{tautomerism}} \underbrace{\text{CH}_3\text{--CO--CH}_3}_{\mathbf{[C]}\text{ (Propan-2-one)}}
$$

**(b) Mechanism of Nucleophilic Addition of $\text{HCN}$:**
- **Step 1: Generation of the strong nucleophile (Cyanide ion):**
  Pure $\text{HCN}$ is a weak acid and dissociates slowly. A base catalyzes the reaction by abstracting the proton to generate cyanide ion:
  

$$
\text{HCN} + \text{OH}^- \rightleftharpoons \text{CN}^- + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

- **Step 2: Nucleophilic attack on the planar carbonyl carbon (Slow / Rate-determining step):**
  The nucleophile ($\text{CN}^-$) attacks the electrophilic carbonyl carbon perpendicularly to the $sp^2$ plane, producing an alkoxide tetrahedral intermediate:
  

$$
>\!\text{C}^{\delta+}=\text{O}^{\delta-} + \text{CN}^- \xrightarrow{\text{slow}} >\!\text{C}(\text{CN})\text{--O}^-
$$

- **Step 3: Proton transfer (Fast):**
  The tetrahedral alkoxide abstracts a proton from the solvent to yield the stable cyanohydrin product and regenerates the base catalyst:
  

$$
>\!\text{C}(\text{CN})\text{--O}^- + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{fast}} >\!\text{C}(\text{CN})\text{--OH} + \text{OH}^-
$$

## 3. Golden Points to Remember (NCERT In-Text Lines & High-Yield Truths)

1. **Boiling Point Hierarchy:**
   

$$
\text{Carboxylic Acids} > \text{Alcohols} > \text{Aldehydes} \approx \text{Ketones} > \text{Ethers} > \text{Hydrocarbons}
$$

   * *Reason:* Carboxylic acids form **stable hydrogen-bonded cyclic dimers** even in the vapour phase and non-polar solvents (benzene). Alcohols form extensive linear intermolecular H-bonding.
2. **Nucleophilic Addition Reactivity:**
   

$$
\text{HCHO} > \text{CH}_3\text{CHO} > \text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_3 > \text{ArCHO} > \text{ArCOCH}_3 > \text{ArCOAr}
$$

   * *Driving factors:*
     1. **Steric hindrance:** Bulkier groups crowd the transition state from 

$$
sp^2
$$

 (

$$
120^\circ
$$

) to 

$$
sp^3
$$

 (

$$
109.5^\circ
$$

).
     2. **Electronic factor:** Alkyl groups release electron density via 

$$
+I
$$

, neutralizing the partial positive charge (

$$
\delta+
$$

) on carbonyl carbon. Aryl groups decrease electrophilicity via resonance (

$$
+R
$$

 from the ring).
3. **Carboxylic Acids do NOT give Carbonyl Reactions:**
   * Carboxylic acids do not form oximes with hydroxylamine, nor do they give 2,4-DNP or NaHSO3 addition products.
   * *NCERT Reason:* The carbonyl carbon in 

$$
-\text{COOH}
$$

 is involved in resonance with the adjacent lone pair of the hydroxyl oxygen (

$$
-\ddot{\text{O}}\text{H}
$$

), significantly reducing its electrophilic character.
4. **Formic Acid (

$$
\text{HCOOH}
$$

) Anomaly:**
   * Formic acid contains both an aldehyde group and a carboxyl group: 

$$
\text{H}-\mathbf{C}(=\mathbf{O})-\text{OH}
$$

.
   * Hence, **formic acid reduces Tollen’s and Fehling’s reagents**, unlike all other carboxylic acids!
5. **Ortho Effect in Benzoic Acids:**
   * Any ortho-substituent (

$$
-I
$$

, 

$$
+I
$$

, 

$$
-M
$$

, or 

$$
+M
$$

; e.g., 

$$
-\text{NO}_2, -\text{CH}_3, -\text{Cl}
$$

) increases the acidity of benzoic acid relative to benzoic acid itself due to steric inhibition of resonance (SIR), tilting the carboxyl group out of plane and increasing the carboxylate's independent resonance stabilization.
6. **Clemmensen vs. Wolff-Kishner Compatibility:**
   * **Clemmensen Reduction** (

$$
\text{Zn-Hg} / \text{conc. HCl}
$$

): Acid-sensitive groups (like acetals, basic groups, or tertiary alcohols) will be attacked or dehydrated by 

$$
\text{HCl}
$$

.
   * **Wolff-Kishner Reduction** (

$$
\text{NH}_2\text{NH}_2 / \text{KOH, ethylene glycol, }\Delta
$$

): Base-sensitive groups (like alkyl halides, esters, or phenolic esters) will undergo hydrolysis or elimination.
