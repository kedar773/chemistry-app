## 1. In-Depth Pedagogical Theory & Concepts

### 1.1 The Carbonyl Group: Electronic Structure & Geometry
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(Crucial for Board reasoning questions & mechanistic foundation)*

#### Definition & Core Orbital Concept
The carbonyl group consists of a carbon atom doubly bonded to an oxygen atom:

$$
\mathrm{>C=O}
$$

* **Hybridisation:** 
  The carbonyl carbon atom utilizes three $\mathrm{sp^2}$ hybrid orbitals and possesses one unhybridised $2\mathrm{p}_z$ orbital. The carbonyl oxygen atom is also regarded as $\mathrm{sp^2}$ hybridised in valence bond theory, holding two lone pairs in two $\mathrm{sp^2}$ hybrid orbitals.
* **$\sigma$-Bond Framework:** 
  The carbonyl carbon forms three coplanar $\sigma$-bonds using its three $\mathrm{sp^2}$ hybrid orbitals:
  1. One $\sigma$-bond with oxygen via $\mathrm{sp^2(C)}-\mathrm{sp^2(O)}$ axial overlap.
  2. Two $\sigma$-bonds with two other attached atoms/groups (carbon or hydrogen) via $\mathrm{sp^2-s}$ or $\mathrm{sp^2-sp^3}$ overlap.
* **$\pi$-Bond Framework:** 
  The fourth valence electron of carbon remains in an unhybridised $2\mathrm{p}_z$ atomic orbital oriented perpendicular to the nodal plane of the $\sigma$-bonds. Lateral (sideways) overlap between this carbon $2\mathrm{p}_z$ orbital and an oxygen $2\mathrm{p}_z$ orbital yields a $\pi$ molecular orbital residing above and below the molecular plane.

```
       R                  :O:
        \                //
         C(sp²) === C === O(sp²)
        /                \\
       R'                 : 
   [Trigonal Planar, ~120° Bond Angles]
```

* **Geometry & Bond Angles:** 
  The three atoms attached to the carbonyl carbon lie in a single plane. The molecular geometry around the carbonyl carbon is **trigonal planar**, with bond angles approaching:
  

$$
\angle \mathrm{R-C-R'} \approx \angle \mathrm{R-C=O} \approx 120^\circ
$$

  *(Note: In methanal $\mathrm{HCHO}$, $\angle \mathrm{H-C-H} = 116.5^\circ$ and $\angle \mathrm{H-C=O} = 121.8^\circ$ due to steric and electronic repulsion between the $\pi$ electron cloud and the $\mathrm{C-H}$ $\sigma$-bonds).*

### 1.2 Nature of the Carbonyl Dipole & Resonance
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(Repeatedly asked: "Explain why carbonyl compounds are more polar than ethers or alkenes.")*

Oxygen possesses an electronegativity of $3.44$ on the Pauling scale, while carbon has an electronegativity of $2.55$ ($\Delta \chi = 0.89$). Consequently:
1. The $\pi$-electron cloud is displaced strongly toward the electronegative oxygen atom.
2. The carbonyl bond is polar, best described by two principal resonance canonical structures:

$$
\mathrm{\underset{(Neutral/Covalent)}{>C=O} \longleftrightarrow \underset{(Dipolar/Zwitterionic)}{>\overset{\oplus}{C}-\overset{\ominus}{\ddot{O}}:}}
$$

#### Dipole Moment Comparison
Carbonyl compounds have large permanent dipole moments ($\mu = 2.3 - 2.8\ \mathrm{D}$), which are substantially higher than those of ethers ($\mu \approx 1.15\ \mathrm{D}$) and haloalkanes of comparable molar mass.

$$
\boxed{\mu(\text{Aldehydes/Ketones}) \approx 2.3\text{ to }2.8\ \mathrm{D} \quad \gg \quad \mu(\text{Ethers}) \approx 1.15\ \mathrm{D}}
$$

#### Dual Reactivity Center (Electrophile vs. Nucleophile)
* The carbonyl carbon is electron-deficient ($\delta^+$) and functions as an **electrophilic center (Lewis acid)**.
* The carbonyl oxygen is electron-rich ($\delta^-$) and functions as a **nucleophilic/basic center (Lewis base)**.

$$
\begin{matrix}
\delta^+\quad \delta^-\\
\mathrm{>C = O}
\end{matrix}
$$

### 1.3 Strict NCERT Nomenclature Rules
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(Guaranteed 1 to 2 marks in CBSE Board Paper)*

#### A. Aldehydes ($\mathrm{-CHO}$)
1. **Acyclic systems:** The longest carbon chain containing the $-\mathrm{CHO}$ group provides the parent alkane name. The suffix **"-al"** replaces the ending "-e" of the alkane:
   

$$
\text{Alkane} - \text{"e"} + \text{"al"} \longrightarrow \text{Alkanal}
$$

   * The carbonyl carbon of $-\mathrm{CHO}$ is **always assigned position 1** ($\mathrm{C\text{-}1}$). Hence, the locant $1$ is usually omitted unless required to prevent ambiguity.
2. **Special IUPAC Rule for 3 or more $-\mathrm{CHO}$ groups:**
   If three or more aldehyde groups are attached directly to an unbranched carbon chain, none of the $-\mathrm{CHO}$ carbons are included in the parent chain numbering. The suffix **"-carbaldehyde"** is appended to the parent alkane:
   

$$
\mathrm{OHC-CH_2-CH(CHO)-CH_2-CHO} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \text{Propane-1,2,3-tricarbaldehyde}
$$

3. **Ring systems:** When $-\mathrm{CHO}$ is attached directly to a carbocyclic or heterocyclic ring, the suffix **"-carbaldehyde"** is used:
   * $\mathrm{C_6H_{11}-CHO}$ $\Longrightarrow$ **Cyclohexanecarbaldehyde**
   * $\mathrm{C_6H_5-CHO}$ $\Longrightarrow$ **Benzaldehyde** *(IUPAC accepted)* or **Benzenecarbaldehyde**

#### B. Ketones ($\mathrm{>C=O}$)
1. Suffix **"-one"** replaces the ending "-e" of the parent alkane:
   

$$
\text{Alkane} - \text{"e"} + \text{"one"} \longrightarrow \text{Alkanone}
$$

2. The parent chain is numbered from the terminal carbon that gives the carbonyl carbon the **lowest possible locant**.
3. Ketones are classified as:
   * **Simple/Symmetrical:** Two identical alkyl/aryl groups attached to carbonyl carbon (e.g., $\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3}$, Propan-2-one).
   * **Mixed/Unsymmetrical:** Two distinct groups attached to carbonyl carbon (e.g., $\mathrm{CH_3COCH_2CH_3}$, Butan-2-one).
4. When carbonyl groups act as a substituent (when a higher-priority group such as $-\mathrm{COOH}$ or $-\mathrm{CHO}$ is present):
   * Prefix used: **"oxo-"** (or rarely "keto-").
   * Example: $\mathrm{CH_3-CO-CH_2-CHO} \Longrightarrow$ **3-Oxobutanal**.

### 1.5 Isomerism in Aldehydes and Ketones
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐

Aldehydes and ketones exhibit structural and stereoisomerism:

1. **Chain Isomerism:**
   Molecules with the same functional group and molecular formula differ in the carbon skeleton:
   * $\mathrm{C_4H_8O}$:
     

$$
\mathrm{\underset{Butanal}{CH_3-CH_2-CH_2-CHO}} \quad \text{and} \quad \mathrm{\underset{2-Methylpropanal}{(CH_3)_2CH-CHO}}
$$

2. **Position Isomerism:**
   Observed in ketones with $\ge 5$ carbons due to the differing locant of the carbonyl group:
   * $\mathrm{C_5H_{10}O}$:
     

$$
\mathrm{\underset{Pentan-2-one}{CH_3-CO-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3}} \quad \text{and} \quad \mathrm{\underset{Pentan-3-one}{CH_3-CH_2-CO-CH_2-CH_3}}
$$

   *(Aldehydes cannot exhibit position isomerism for $-\mathrm{CHO}$ in an open chain because $-\mathrm{CHO}$ is always terminal at $\mathrm{C\text{-}1}$).*

3. **Functional Group Isomerism:**
   Aldehydes and ketones with the general molecular formula $\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n}\mathrm{O}$ ($n \ge 3$) are functional isomers of each other:
   * $\mathrm{C_3H_6O}$:
     

$$
\mathrm{\underset{(Aldehyde)}{CH_3-CH_2-CHO}} \quad \text{and} \quad \mathrm{\underset{(Ketone)}{CH_3-CO-CH_3}}
$$

   * Also isomeric with unsaturated alcohols (e.g., $\mathrm{CH_2=CH-CH_2OH}$) and cyclic ethers (oxiranes/oxetanes).

4. **Metamerism:**
   Observed in ketones ($n \ge 5$) having unequal distribution of alkyl chains on either side of the carbonyl carbon:
   * $\mathrm{C_5H_{10}O}$:
     

$$
\mathrm{\underset{Methyl\ propyl\ ketone}{CH_3-CO-CH_2CH_2CH_3}} \quad \text{and} \quad \mathrm{\underset{Diethyl\ ketone}{CH_3CH_2-CO-CH_2CH_3}}
$$

## 2. Derivations, Structural Comparisons & Mechanisms

### 2.1 Rigorous Comparison: Carbonyl Group vs. Carbon-Carbon Double Bond ($\mathrm{C=C}$)
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(Standard 3-Mark Comparative Theory Question)*

| Characteristic Property | Alkene Double Bond ($\mathrm{>C=C<}$) | Carbonyl Double Bond ($\mathrm{>C=O}$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bond Nature** | $1\ \sigma\ (\mathrm{sp^2-sp^2}) + 1\ \pi\ (\mathrm{2p-2p})$ | $1\ \sigma\ (\mathrm{sp^2-sp^2}) + 1\ \pi\ (\mathrm{2p-2p})$ |
| **Electronegativity Difference** | $\Delta \chi = 0$ (Non-polar covalent) | $\Delta \chi = 3.44 - 2.55 = 0.89$ (Highly Polar) |
| **Electron Distribution** | Symmetrical $\pi$-electron cloud | Unsymmetrical; skewed toward oxygen atom |
| **Dipole Moment ($\mu$)** | $\mu \approx 0\ \mathrm{D}$ (e.g., trans-alkenes = 0) | High permanent dipole ($\mu \approx 2.3 - 2.8\ \mathrm{D}$) |
| **Characteristic Reaction** | **Electrophilic Addition ($\mathrm{Ad_E}$)** | **Nucleophilic Addition ($\mathrm{Ad_N}$)** |
| **Susceptibility to Attack** | Attacked initially by electrophiles ($\mathrm{E^+}$) | Attacked initially by nucleophiles ($\mathrm{Nu^-}$) at $\mathrm{C}$ |
| **Bond Energy** | $E(\mathrm{C=C}) \approx 611\ \mathrm{kJ\ mol^{-1}}$ | $E(\mathrm{C=O}) \approx 745\ \mathrm{kJ\ mol^{-1}}$ (Stronger due to electrostatic contribution) |
| **Bond Length** | $d(\mathrm{C=C}) \approx 134\ \mathrm{pm}$ | $d(\mathrm{C=O}) \approx 122\ \mathrm{pm}$ (Shorter due to polar contraction) |

### 2.2 Mechanism: Nucleophilic Addition to the Carbonyl Group
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(Fundamental mechanism of the entire chapter)*

Because the carbonyl carbon is $\mathrm{sp^2}$ hybridised and planar, it offers minimal steric resistance from either side of the plane. 

#### Step 1: Nucleophilic Attack on Electrophilic Carbon (Slow, Rate-Determining Step)
The nucleophile approaches the planar $\mathrm{sp^2}$ carbonyl carbon from a trajectory roughly perpendicular to the plane of the carbonyl group (Bürgi-Dunitz angle $\approx 107^\circ$). The hybridisation of carbon changes from $\mathrm{sp^2}$ to $\mathrm{sp^3}$:

$$
\mathrm{Nu}^{\ominus} + \begin{matrix}
\mathrm{R_1}\\
\\
\mathrm{R_2}
\end{matrix}>\mathrm{C=O} \xrightarrow[\text{slow}]{\text{r.d.s.}} \left[ \begin{matrix}
\mathrm{R_1}\\
\\
\mathrm{R_2}
\end{matrix}>\overset{\mathrm{Nu}}{\mathrm{C}}-\mathrm{O}^{\ominus} \right]
$$

* *Substrate geometry:* Planar (trigonal, $120^\circ$)
* *Intermediate geometry:* Tetrahedral alkoxide intermediate ($109.5^\circ$)

#### Step 2: Protonation of Tetrahedral Intermediate (Fast Step)
The negatively charged alkoxide oxygen abstracts a proton from the reaction medium (or acid catalyst) to yield the electrically neutral addition product:

$$
\left[ \begin{matrix}
\mathrm{R_1}\\
\\
\mathrm{R_2}
\end{matrix}>\overset{\mathrm{Nu}}{\mathrm{C}}-\mathrm{O}^{\ominus} \right] + \mathrm{H^{\oplus}} \xrightarrow[\text{fast}]{} \begin{matrix}
\mathrm{R_1}\\
\\
\mathrm{R_2}
\end{matrix}>\overset{\mathrm{Nu}}{\mathrm{C}}-\mathrm{OH}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Overall: } \mathrm{>C=O} + \mathrm{H-Nu} \longrightarrow \mathrm{>C(OH)(Nu)}}
$$

### 2.3 Derivation of Reactivity Orders in Nucleophilic Addition
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(Asked almost every year in Board Exams)*

The relative reactivity of carbonyl compounds towards nucleophilic attack follows:

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{HCHO} > \mathrm{CH_3CHO} > \mathrm{CH_3COCH_3} > \mathrm{C_6H_5CHO} > \mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3} > \mathrm{C_6H_5COC_6H_5}}
$$

This order is dictated by two synergistic effects:

#### 1. Inductive / Electronic Factor
* Alkyl groups are electron-donating ($+\mathrm{I}$ effect).
* An increase in the number of alkyl groups attached to the carbonyl carbon disperses the partial positive charge ($\delta^+$), reducing its electrophilicity.

$$
\text{Electrophilicity of Carbon: } \mathrm{HCHO} (\text{no } +\mathrm{I}) > \mathrm{RCHO} (\text{one } +\mathrm{I}) > \mathrm{R_2CO} (\text{two } +\mathrm{I})
$$

#### 2. Steric (Crowding) Factor
* In the transition state, bond angles compress from $120^\circ$ (planar reactant) to $109.5^\circ$ (tetrahedral intermediate).
* Bulky alkyl groups increase steric crowding around the carbon atom in the transition state, raising the activation energy ($\Delta G^\ddagger$) and decreasing the reaction rate.

#### 3. Resonance Effect in Aromatic Aldehydes & Ketones
In benzaldehyde, the carbonyl group is conjugated with the benzene ring. The electron-donating resonance effect ($+\mathrm{R}$) of the phenyl ring increases electron density at the carbonyl carbon:

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5-CHO} \longleftrightarrow \text{[Resonance structures transferring } \pi\text{-electrons from ring to carbonyl]}
$$

This resonance stabilization delocalizes the positive charge into the aromatic ring, making the carbonyl carbon significantly less electrophilic than in aliphatic aldehydes:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CHO} > \mathrm{C_6H_5CHO}
$$

**Question:** Write the IUPAC name of the following compound:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH(CH_3)-CH_2-CO-CH_3}
$$

**Solution:**
1. **Identify Principal Functional Group:** Ketone ($>\mathrm{C=O}$), suffix = "-one".
2. **Select Longest Carbon Chain:** A 5-carbon chain containing the carbonyl carbon $\rightarrow$ "pentan".
3. **Numbering:** Must give the lowest locant to the carbonyl carbon:
   * Left-to-right: $\mathrm{C\text{-}4}$ for carbonyl.
   * Right-to-left: $\mathrm{C\text{-}2}$ for carbonyl, $\mathrm{C\text{-}4}$ for methyl. (Correct)
4. **Assemble IUPAC Name:**
   

$$
\boxed{\text{4-Methylpentan-2-one}}
$$

### Example 2 [CBSE Board 2023, 2 Marks]
**Question:** 
(a) Give the IUPAC name of $\mathrm{CH_3-CH=CH-CHO}$.  
(b) Arrange the following compounds in increasing order of their reactivity towards nucleophilic addition reactions:

$$
\mathrm{Ethanal,\ Propanal,\ Propanone,\ Butanone}
$$

**Solution:**
**(a)**
* Longest chain containing $-\mathrm{CHO}$ and double bond = 4 carbons ("but").
* Carbonyl carbon is $\mathrm{C\text{-}1}$: $\mathrm{\overset{4}{C}H_3-\overset{3}{C}H=\overset{2}{C}H-\overset{1}{C}HO}$.
* The double bond begins at $\mathrm{C\text{-}3}$.
* Suffixes: "-en" at 3, "-al" at 1.

$$
\boxed{\text{But-2-en-1-al (or But-2-enal)}}
$$

**(b)**
* Aldehydes are more reactive than ketones due to lesser steric crowding and smaller $+\mathrm{I}$ effect.
* Within aldehydes: Ethanal has one $-\mathrm{CH_3}$ ($+\mathrm{I}$), Propanal has one $-\mathrm{C_2H_5}$ (larger $+\mathrm{I}$, greater steric hindrance).
* Within ketones: Propanone has two $-\mathrm{CH_3}$, Butanone has one $-\mathrm{CH_3}$ and one $-\mathrm{C_2H_5}$.
* **Increasing Order of Reactivity:**

$$
\boxed{\text{Butanone} < \text{Propanone} < \text{Propanal} < \text{Ethanal}}
$$

### Example 5 [CBSE Board 2020, 3 Marks]
**Question:** 
An organic compound (A) with molecular formula $\mathrm{C_5H_{10}O}$ forms a phenylhydrazone, gives a negative Tollens' test, but gives a positive iodoform test. On vigorous oxidation, it yields ethanoic acid and propanoic acid.  
(i) Identify compound (A).  
(ii) Write its IUPAC name.  
(iii) Write the chemical equation for the iodoform reaction of compound (A).

**Solution:**
1. **Deductions:**
   * Molecular formula $\mathrm{C_5H_{10}O}$ corresponds to general formula $\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n}\mathrm{O}$ $\rightarrow$ Aldehyde or Ketone.
   * Forms phenylhydrazone $\rightarrow$ contains a carbonyl group ($>\mathrm{C=O}$).
   * Negative Tollens' test $\rightarrow$ **Ketone** (not an aldehyde).
   * Positive Iodoform test $\rightarrow$ must contain a methyl carbonyl group:
     

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-C(=O)-}
$$

   * The remaining fragment is $\mathrm{C_5H_{10}O} - \mathrm{C_2H_3O} = -\mathrm{CH_2-CH_2-CH_3}$ (propyl group).

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CO-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3}
$$

2. **Answers:**
   * **(i) Structure of (A):** $\mathrm{CH_3-CO-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3}$
   * **(ii) IUPAC Name:** $\boxed{\text{Pentan-2-one}}$
   * **(iii) Iodoform Reaction:**
     

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COCH_2CH_2CH_3 + 3I_2 + 4NaOH \xrightarrow{\Delta} \underset{\substack{\text{Iodoform}\\\text{(Yellow ppt.)}}}{CHI_3\downarrow} + \underset{\text{Sodium butanoate}}{CH_3CH_2CH_2COONa} + 3NaI + 3H_2O}
$$

## 4. Examiner Traps & Common Board Mistakes

### Trap 1: The Numbering of Carbonyl Carbon in Aldehydes
* **Incorrect:** Numbering $\mathrm{CH_3-CH(CH_3)-CHO}$ starting from the left as $\mathrm{\overset{1}{C}H_3-\overset{2}{C}H(CH_3)-\overset{3}{C}HO}$, naming it "3-Methylpropanal".
* **NCERT Rule:** The $-\mathrm{CHO}$ carbon is **always carbon-1 ($\mathrm{C\text{-}1}$)** in an open chain.
* **Correct:** $\mathrm{\overset{3}{C}H_3-\overset{2}{C}H(CH_3)-\overset{1}{C}HO} \Longrightarrow$ **2-Methylpropanal**.

### Trap 2: Suffix Confusion with Multiple Formyl Groups
* **Frequent Error:** Calling $\mathrm{OHC-CH_2-CH(CHO)-CH_2-CHO}$ "3-Formylpentanedial".
* **NCERT Rule:** When three or more identical principal functional groups ($-\mathrm{CHO}$, $-\mathrm{COOH}$, $-\mathrm{CN}$) are directly attached to an acyclic chain, the parent chain is chosen excluding all their carbons, and the suffix **"-carbaldehyde"** is used.
* **Correct:** **Propane-1,2,3-tricarbaldehyde**.

### Trap 3: Dropping the "e" in Saturated vs. Unsaturated Names
* When writing names for unsaturated aldehydes:
  * Incorrect: "But-2-enal" written as "Butan-2-enal".
  * *Distinction:*
    * Saturated: $\text{Alkane} - \text{"e"} + \text{"al"} \rightarrow$ **Alkanal** (e.g., Butanal).
    * Unsaturated: $\text{Alkene} - \text{"e"} + \text{"al"} \rightarrow$ **Alkenal** (e.g., But-2-enal). Adding "an" implies carbon-carbon saturation!

### Trap 4: Dialdehydes Retain the "e"
* For dialdehydes: $\mathrm{OHC-CH_2-CH_2-CHO}$
  * Incorrect: "Butandial"
  * Correct: **Butanedial** *(The terminal "e" of the parent alkane is retained because the suffix begins with a consonant "d")*.

### Trap 5: Misunderstanding Benzaldehyde vs. Aliphatic Aldehydes in Reactivity
* **Examiner Question:** "Why does benzaldehyde undergo nucleophilic addition slower than propanal?"
* **Common Student Mistake:** Answering with *"because of steric hindrance of the bulky phenyl ring"* alone.
* **NCERT Key Phrase for Full Marks:**
  > *"Due to resonance in benzaldehyde, the $+\mathrm{R}$ effect of the benzene ring delocalizes $\pi$-electron density toward the carbonyl carbon, decreasing its electrophilicity ($\delta^+$ character). Resonance stabilization of the unreacted aldehyde raises the activation energy barrier for nucleophilic attack."*

## 5. Speed Hacks & Golden Points for Quick Recall

### 1. The IUPAC Priority Ladder (Memory Hook)
To determine whether an oxygen-containing group is named as a suffix or prefix:

$$
\boxed{\text{Acid } (-\mathrm{COOH}) > \text{Ester } (-\mathrm{COOR}) > \text{Amide } (-\mathrm{CONH_2}) > \text{Nitrile } (-\mathrm{CN}) > \mathbf{Aldehyde\ (-CHO)} > \mathbf{Ketone\ (>C=O)} > \text{Alcohol\ (-OH)} > \text{Amine\ (-NH_2)}}
$$

* *Memory Mnemonic:* **C**an **E**very **A**spiring **N**erd **A**lways **K**now **A**ll **A**cid-derivatives?
  * **C**arboxylic acid > **E**ster > **A**mide > **N**itrile > **A**ldehyde > **K**etone > **A**lcohol > **A**mine.

### 2. Isomer Counting Quick-Check ($C_n$ Formulas)
For monocarbonyl compounds with molecular formula $\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n}\mathrm{O}$:
* **$n = 1$ ($\mathrm{CH_2O}$):** Only 1 isomer (Methanal).
* **$n = 2$ ($\mathrm{C_2H_4O}$):** Only 1 carbonyl isomer (Ethanal).
* **$n = 3$ ($\mathrm{C_3H_6O}$):** Exactly **2** carbonyl isomers:
  * 1 Aldehyde: Propanal
  * 1 Ketone: Propan-2-one
* **$n = 4$ ($\mathrm{C_4H_8O}$):** Exactly **3** carbonyl isomers:
  * 2 Aldehydes: Butanal, 2-Methylpropanal
  * 1 Ketone: Butan-2-one
* **$n = 5$ ($\mathrm{C_5H_{10}O}$):** Exactly **7** carbonyl isomers:
  * 4 Aldehydes: Pentanal, 2-Methylbutanal, 3-Methylbutanal, 2,2-Dimethylpropanal
  * 3 Ketones: Pentan-2-one, Pentan-3-one, 3-Methylbutan-2-one

### 3. Reactivity Decider Rule-of-Thumb
To arrange any given set of carbonyls for nucleophilic addition in under 5 seconds:
1. Count the number of attached hydrogen atoms directly on the carbonyl carbon:
   

$$
\text{Two H's (Formaldehyde)} > \text{One H (Aldehyde)} > \text{Zero H's (Ketone)}
$$

2. If comparing within aldehydes:
   

$$
\text{Aliphatic Aldehyde} > \text{Aromatic Aldehyde}
$$

3. If both are aliphatic:
   

$$
\text{Smaller alkyl chain} > \text{Bulkier/branched alkyl chain}
$$

4. Final sequence template:
   

$$
\mathrm{HCHO} > \mathrm{RCHO} > \mathrm{ArCHO} > \mathrm{RCOR} > \mathrm{ArCOR} > \mathrm{ArCOAr}
$$
