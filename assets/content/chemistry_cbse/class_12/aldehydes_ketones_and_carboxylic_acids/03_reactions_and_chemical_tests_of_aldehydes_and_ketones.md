## 1. Pedagogical Theory & Fundamental Concepts

### 1.1 Nature of the Carbonyl Group & Reactivity Core
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

The carbonyl group ($>C=O$) consists of a carbon atom doubly bonded to an oxygen atom through one $\sigma$-bond (formed by $sp^2-2p$ orbital axial overlap) and one $\pi$-bond (formed by sideways overlap of unhybridized $2p$ orbitals). 

```
        :O:  (Electronegative, δ-)
        //
       C     (Electrophilic Carbon, δ+)
      / \
     R   R'  (Planar geometry, bond angles ≈ 120°)
```

* **Polarity & Electrophilic Character:** Oxygen has a Pauling electronegativity of $3.44$ compared to carbon's $2.55$. Consequently, the $\pi$-electron cloud is strongly shifted toward oxygen:
  

$$
\mathrm{>C=O \longleftrightarrow\ >\overset{\oplus}{C}-\overset{\ominus}{O}:}
$$

  The carbonyl carbon is a potent **electrophilic center** ($\delta^+$), making it susceptible to attack by nucleophiles ($\mathrm{Nu^-}$). The carbonyl oxygen is basic/nucleophilic ($\delta^-$) and can be protonated by Lewis/Brønsted acids.

* **Relative Reactivity: Aldehydes vs. Ketones**
  Aldehydes are substantially more reactive toward nucleophilic attack than ketones due to two distinct factors:
  1. **Steric Hindrance:** In aldehydes, the carbonyl carbon bears only one alkyl/aryl group and a tiny hydrogen atom. In ketones, two relatively bulky alkyl/aryl groups hinder the approaching nucleophile ($Nu^-$) and produce severe non-bonding interactions in the tetrahedral transition state (transition from $sp^2$, $120^\circ$ to $sp^3$, $109.5^\circ$).
  2. **Electronic Factor (Inductive $+I$ Effect):** Alkyl groups are electron-donating ($+I$). Ketones have two alkyl groups dispersing the partial positive charge on the carbonyl carbon more effectively than aldehydes (which have only one $+I$ group). Thus, the electrophilicity of carbon is reduced in ketones:
     

$$
\mathrm{HCHO > RCHO > R_2CO > ArCHO > Ar_2CO}
$$

  3. **Aromatic Aldehydes/Ketones vs. Aliphatic Counterparts:** Benzaldehyde is less reactive than aliphatic aldehydes. The carbonyl group conjugates with the benzene ring through resonance:
     

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5-CHO \longleftrightarrow \text{[Resonance structures exhibiting positive charge delocalization on } ortho/para \text{ positions]}}
$$

     Resonance stabilizes the ground state and reduces the partial positive magnitude ($\delta^+$) on the carbonyl carbon.

## 2. Nucleophilic Addition Reactions & Mechanisms

### 2.1 General Mechanism of Nucleophilic Addition
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

1. **Step 1 (Rate-Determining Step / Slow):** Attack of nucleophile on planar carbonyl carbon.
   

$$
\mathrm{R_2C=O + Nu^- \xrightarrow{slow} [R_2C(Nu)-O^-] \quad (\text{Trigonal planar } sp^2 \longrightarrow \text{Tetrahedral intermediate } sp^3)}
$$

2. **Step 2 (Fast):** Protonation of the alkoxide intermediate.
   

$$
\mathrm{[R_2C(Nu)-O^-] + H^+ \xrightarrow{fast} R_2C(Nu)(OH)}
$$

***

### 2.2 Addition of Hydrogen Cyanide ($\mathrm{HCN}$)
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Pure $\mathrm{HCN}$ is an extremely weak acid ($K_a \approx 4.9 \times 10^{-10}$) and does not yield sufficient concentration of nucleophile ($CN^-$) on its own. The reaction is therefore catalyzed by a base ($\mathrm{OH^-}$):

$$
\mathrm{HCN + OH^- \rightleftharpoons :C\equiv N:^- + H_2O}
$$

$$
\mathrm{>C=O + :C\equiv N:^- \xrightarrow{slow} >C(CN)-O^-}
$$

$$
\mathrm{>C(CN)-O^- + H_2O \xrightarrow{fast} >C(CN)(OH) + OH^-}
$$

The products are **cyanohydrins**, which are synthetic precursors:
* Hydrolysis of cyanohydrins yields $\alpha$-hydroxy acids:
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CH(OH)CN + 2H_2O + H^+ \xrightarrow{\Delta} CH_3CH(OH)COOH + NH_4^+}
$$

***

### 2.3 Addition of Sodium Hydrogen Sulphite ($\mathrm{NaHSO_3}$)
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

$$
\mathrm{>C=O + NaHSO_3 \rightleftharpoons \left[ >C(OH)(SO_3Na) \right]}
$$

**Mechanism & Nucleophilic Species:** The nucleophilic center in the hydrogen sulphite ion is the unshared pair on **sulfur**, not oxygen, because sulfur is larger and more polarizable:
1. Attack by sulfur:
   

$$
\mathrm{>C=O + :SO_3H^- \longrightarrow >C(O^-)-SO_3H}
$$

2. Proton transfer:
   

$$
\mathrm{>C(O^-)-SO_3H \xrightarrow{\text{Proton Transfer}} >C(OH)-SO_3^- \xrightarrow{Na^+} >C(OH)SO_3Na \downarrow}
$$

* **Equilibrium Position:** Lies predominantly to the right for aldehydes, but shifts to the left for sterically hindered ketones (e.g., acetophenone does not give bisulphite compounds in practical yields).
* **Purification Significance:** The bisulphite adduct is a crystalline solid that can be filtered and regenerated to pure carbonyl compound by treating with dilute mineral acid or aqueous alkali:
  

$$
\mathrm{>C(OH)SO_3Na + HCl \longrightarrow >C=O + NaCl + SO_2 \uparrow + H_2O}
$$

***

### 2.4 Addition of Alcohols (Acetal & Ketal Formation)
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

* **Aldehydes:** React with one equivalent of monohydric alcohol in the presence of dry $\mathrm{HCl}$ gas to yield a hemiacetal (alkoxyalcohol), which reacts with a second equivalent of alcohol to yield a gem-dialkoxy compound known as an **acetal**:

$$
\mathrm{RCHO + R'OH \xrightleftharpoons{dry\ HCl} R-CH(OH)(OR') \quad [\text{Hemiacetal}]}
$$

$$
\mathrm{R-CH(OH)(OR') + R'OH \xrightleftharpoons{dry\ HCl} R-CH(OR')_2 + H_2O \quad [\text{Acetal}]}
$$

* **Ketones:** Do not readily form ketals with monohydric alcohols under normal conditions. They react with 1,2-diols (ethylene glycol) in the presence of dry $\mathrm{HCl}$ gas or $p$-toluenesulfonic acid ($p$-TsOH) to form **cyclic ketals (ethylene glycol ketals)**:

$$
\mathrm{R_2C=O + \begin{matrix} HO-CH_2 \\ | \qquad \\ HO-CH_2 \end{matrix} \xrightleftharpoons{dry\ HCl, -\mathrm{H_2O}} \begin{matrix} R \quad /O-CH_2 \\ \ \ C \qquad | \\ R \quad \backslash O-CH_2 \end{matrix}}
$$

* **Role of Dry $\mathrm{HCl}$ Gas:**
  1. Protonates the carbonyl oxygen, dramatically increasing the electrophilic character of the carbonyl carbon.
  2. Absorbs water produced during the reversible condensation, shifting the chemical equilibrium forward in accordance with Le Chatelier’s principle.

***

### 2.5 Nucleophilic Addition-Elimination: Ammonia Derivatives ($\mathrm{NH_2-Z}$)
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Reactions proceed through nucleophilic attack of the basic nitrogen on the carbonyl carbon followed by loss of a water molecule:

$$
\mathrm{>C=O + H_2N-Z \xrightleftharpoons{H^+} \left[ >C(OH)(NH-Z) \right] \xrightarrow{-H_2O} >C=N-Z}
$$

| Reagent ($\mathrm{NH_2-Z}$) | Group ($\mathrm{Z}$) | Product Name | Product Structure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\mathrm{NH_3}$ (Ammonia) | $-\mathrm{H}$ | Imine (Schiff's base) | $\mathrm{>C=NH}$ |
| $\mathrm{R'NH_2}$ (Primary Amine) | $-\mathrm{R'}$ | Substituted Imine | $\mathrm{>C=N-R'}$ |
| $\mathrm{NH_2OH}$ (Hydroxylamine) | $-\mathrm{OH}$ | **Oxime** | $\mathrm{>C=N-OH}$ |
| $\mathrm{NH_2NH_2}$ (Hydrazine) | $-\mathrm{NH_2}$ | **Hydrazone** | $\mathrm{>C=N-NH_2}$ |
| $\mathrm{C_6H_5NHNH_2}$ (Phenylhydrazine) | $-\mathrm{NHC_6H_5}$ | **Phenylhydrazone** | $\mathrm{>C=N-NHC_6H_5}$ |
| **2,4-DNP** (2,4-Dinitrophenylhydrazine) | $-\mathrm{NH-C_6H_3(NO_2)_2}$ | **2,4-DNP derivative** | $\mathrm{>C=N-NH-C_6H_3(NO_2)_2}$ *(Orange-Yellow ppt)* |
| **Semicarbazide** | $-\mathrm{NH-CO-NH_2}$ | **Semicarbazone** | $\mathrm{>C=N-NH-CO-NH_2}$ |

* **Optimum pH ($\approx 3.5 - 4.5$):**
  * If $\mathrm{pH} < 3.5$ (too acidic): The amine nucleophile is completely protonated to an ammonium salt ($\mathrm{R-\overset{\oplus}{N}H_3}$), losing its lone pair and nucleophilic ability.
  * If $\mathrm{pH} > 4.5$ (not acidic enough): The carbonyl oxygen cannot be protonated, rendering the electrophilic carbon insufficiently active toward weaker nitrogen nucleophiles.

## 3. Reduction Reactions

### 3.1 Reduction to Hydrocarbons
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### A. Clemmensen Reduction
* **Reagent:** Zinc amalgam and concentrated hydrochloric acid ($\mathrm{Zn(Hg) / conc.\ HCl}$).
* **Transformation:** Direct deoxygenation of carbonyl group into a methylene group ($>C=O \longrightarrow >CH_2$).
* **Equation:**
  

$$
\mathrm{R-CHO + 4[H] \xrightarrow{Zn(Hg)/conc.\ HCl} R-CH_3 + H_2O}
$$

  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3 + 4[H] \xrightarrow{Zn(Hg)/conc.\ HCl} CH_3CH_2CH_3 + H_2O}
$$

* **Substrate Restriction:** **Acid-sensitive groups** (e.g., $-\mathrm{OH}$, $-\mathrm{OR}$, $-\mathrm{C=C}-$, $-\mathrm{NH_2}$) cannot be present because concentrated $\mathrm{HCl}$ will cause dehydration, addition, or protonation side-reactions.

#### B. Wolff-Kishner Reduction
* **Reagents:** Hydrazine ($\mathrm{NH_2NH_2}$) followed by heating with base ($\mathrm{KOH}$ or potassium tert-butoxide) in a high-boiling polar solvent such as ethylene glycol ($T \approx 453 - 473\ \mathrm{K}$).
* **Equation:**
  

$$
\mathrm{>C=O + H_2NNH_2 \xrightarrow{-H_2O} >C=NNH_2 \xrightarrow{KOH / \text{Ethylene glycol}, \Delta} >CH_2 + N_2 \uparrow}
$$

* **Substrate Restriction:** **Base-sensitive groups** (e.g., esters, alkyl halides, phenolic $-\mathrm{OH}$) cannot be present because strong base ($\mathrm{OH^-}$) will cause hydrolysis or elimination.

***

### 3.2 Reduction to Alcohols
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

1. **Catalytic Hydrogenation:**
   

$$
\mathrm{RCHO + H_2 \xrightarrow{Ni/Pt/Pd} RCH_2OH \quad (\text{1}^\circ\ \text{Alcohol})}
$$

   

$$
\mathrm{RCOR' + H_2 \xrightarrow{Ni/Pt/Pd} R-CH(OH)-R' \quad (\text{2}^\circ\ \text{Alcohol})}
$$

2. **Complex Hydrides ($\mathrm{NaBH_4}$ and $\mathrm{LiAlH_4}$):**
   * $\mathrm{NaBH_4}$ selectively reduces aldehydes and ketones to alcohols in protic solvents (ethanol/methanol) without reducing esters, amides, or carboxylic acids:
     

$$
\mathrm{4RCHO + NaBH_4 + 3H_2O \longrightarrow 4RCH_2OH + NaBO_2}
$$

   * Both hydrides reduce the polar $C=O$ bond via hydride transfer ($:H^-$) without affecting isolated non-polar $C=C$ double bonds (except in cinnamaldehyde derivatives where conjugation can lead to partial reduction).

## 4. Oxidation Reactions & Selective Tests

### 4.1 Differential Oxidation: Aldehydes vs. Ketones
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

* **Aldehydes** oxidize easily to carboxylic acids with the same number of carbon atoms, even with mild oxidizing agents ($\mathrm{Ag^+}$, $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$), due to the presence of a oxidizable aldehydic hydrogen ($\mathrm{C-H}$ bond directly on the carbonyl carbon):
  

$$
\mathrm{R-CHO \xrightarrow{[O]} R-COOH}
$$

* **Ketones** lack this hydrogen atom and are resistant to mild oxidants. They undergo cleavage of $\mathrm{C-C}$ bonds only under vigorous conditions (concentrated $\mathrm{HNO_3}$, $\mathrm{KMnO_4}/\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$, elevated temperature), yielding mixtures of carboxylic acids with fewer carbon atoms.

#### Popoff’s Rule (for unsymmetrical ketones):
During vigorous oxidation of unsymmetrical aliphatic ketones, the carbonyl group remains preferentially with the smaller alkyl group:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CO-CH_2CH_2CH_3 \xrightarrow{[O], \Delta} CH_3COOH + CH_3CH_2COOH}
$$

*(Carbonyl group remains attached to methyl instead of propyl).*

***

### 4.2 Distinguishing Chemical Tests

#### A. Tollens’ Test (Silver Mirror Test)
* **Reagent:** Ammoniacal silver nitrate solution, $[\mathrm{Ag(NH_3)_2}]^+\mathrm{OH^-}$, freshly prepared by adding dilute $\mathrm{NH_4OH}$ to $\mathrm{AgNO_3}$ until the initial $\mathrm{Ag_2O}$ precipitate dissolves:
  

$$
\mathrm{2AgNO_3 + 2NaOH \longrightarrow Ag_2O\downarrow + 2NaNO_3 + H_2O}
$$

  

$$
\mathrm{Ag_2O + 4NH_4OH \longrightarrow 2[Ag(NH_3)_2]OH + 3H_2O}
$$

* **Action:** Both aliphatic and aromatic aldehydes reduce Tollens' reagent to metallic silver (silver mirror on tube wall):
  

$$
\mathrm{R-CHO + 2[Ag(NH_3)_2]^+ + 3OH^- \xrightarrow{\Delta} R-COO^- + 2Ag\downarrow + 4NH_3 + 2H_2O}
$$

* **Observation:** Brilliant silver mirror on glass surface or dark grey precipitate. Ketones do not respond (except $\alpha$-hydroxy ketones like fructose).

#### B. Fehling’s Test
* **Reagents:**
  * **Fehling’s A:** Aqueous copper(II) sulfate ($\mathrm{CuSO_4 \cdot 5H_2O}$).
  * **Fehling’s B:** Alkaline solution of sodium potassium tartrate (Rochelle salt) with $\mathrm{NaOH}$.
* **Mechanism:** Rochelle salt acts as a bidentate chelating agent for $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$ ions, preventing the precipitation of insoluble $\mathrm{Cu(OH)_2}$ in alkaline media.
* **Equation:**
  

$$
\mathrm{R-CHO + 2Cu^{2+} + 5OH^- \xrightarrow{\Delta} R-COO^- + Cu_2O\downarrow + 3H_2O}
$$

* **Observation:** Distinct **red-brown precipitate** of cuprous oxide ($\mathrm{Cu_2O}$).
* **Scope & Limitation:** Positive for **aliphatic aldehydes only**. Aromatic aldehydes ($\mathrm{C_6H_5CHO}$) and ketones do **not** reduce Fehling’s solution.

#### C. Haloform (Iodoform) Reaction
**Star Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

* **Structural Criteria:** Compounds containing either:
  1. A **methyl carbonyl** group: $\mathrm{CH_3-C=O}$
  2. A **methyl carbinol** group capable of in-situ oxidation to methyl carbonyl: $\mathrm{CH_3-CH(OH)-}$
* **Reagent:** Sodium hypoiodite ($\mathrm{NaOI}$), prepared in-situ by mixing $\mathrm{I_2 + NaOH}$:
  

$$
\mathrm{2NaOH + I_2 \longrightarrow NaOI + NaI + H_2O}
$$

* **Stepwise Mechanism for Acetone:**
  1. Successive halogenation of $\alpha$-methyl group (halogenation is base-catalyzed, accelerating with each substitution due to electron-withdrawing iodines):
     

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3 + 3NaOI \longrightarrow CI_3COCH_3 + 3NaOH}
$$

  2. Nucleophilic cleavage of triiodo compound by hydroxide ion:
     

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CO-CI_3 + OH^- \longrightarrow CH_3-COOH + :CI_3^-}
$$

  3. Proton transfer:
     

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-COOH + :CI_3^- \longrightarrow CH_3-COO^- + CHI_3 \downarrow}
$$

* **Net Balance Equation:**
  

$$
\mathrm{R-COCH_3 + 3I_2 + 4NaOH \xrightarrow{\Delta} R-COONa + CHI_3\downarrow + 3NaI + 3H_2O}
$$

* **Observation:** Bright **yellow crystalline precipitate** of iodoform ($\mathrm{CHI_3}$) with a characteristic antiseptic smell (Melting Point: $392\ \mathrm{K} / 119^\circ\mathrm{C}$).

## 5. High-Yield Solved Examples & CBSE Board PYQs (2020–2025)

### Problem 1 [CBSE 2024, 3 Marks]
**Question:** An organic compound $[\mathrm{A}]$ with molecular formula $\mathrm{C_3H_6O}$ forms an orange-yellow precipitate with 2,4-DNP reagent and gives a yellow precipitate of compound $[\mathrm{B}]$ on treatment with $\mathrm{I_2}$ and $\mathrm{NaOH}$. Compound $[\mathrm{A}]$ does not reduce Tollens’ or Fehling’s reagent.
1. Identify $[\mathrm{A}]$ and $[\mathrm{B}]$.
2. Write the chemical equation for the formation of $[\mathrm{B}]$ from $[\mathrm{A}]$.
3. What happens when $[\mathrm{A}]$ is treated with $\mathrm{Zn(Hg)}$ and conc. $\mathrm{HCl}$?

**Solution:**
* **Step 1: Structural deduction**
  * Formula $\mathrm{C_3H_6O}$ corresponds to degree of unsaturation:
    

$$
\mathrm{DU = C + 1 - \frac{H}{2} = 3 + 1 - 3 = 1}
$$

  * Forms orange-yellow precipitate with 2,4-DNP $\implies$ Carbonyl compound (aldehyde or ketone).
  * Does not reduce Tollens' or Fehling's reagent $\implies$ It is a **ketone**, not an aldehyde.
  * Gives positive iodoform test $\implies$ Contains $\mathrm{CH_3-C=O}$ group.
  * The only 3-carbon ketone is **Propanone (Acetone)**, $\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3}$.
  * Thus, $[\mathrm{A}] = \mathbf{Propan-2-one\ (CH_3COCH_3)}$ and $[\mathrm{B}] = \mathbf{Iodoform\ (CHI_3)}$.
* **Step 2: Chemical equation for iodoform formation**
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3 + 3I_2 + 4NaOH \xrightarrow{\Delta} CH_3COONa + CHI_3\downarrow + 3NaI + 3H_2O}
$$

* **Step 3: Clemmensen Reduction of $[\mathrm{A}]$**
  

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3 + 4[H] \xrightarrow{Zn(Hg)/conc.\ HCl} CH_3-CH_2-CH_3 + H_2O \quad (\text{Propane})}
$$

### Problem 2 [CBSE 2023, 2 Marks]
**Question:** Explain the following observations:
1. Although semicarbazide has two $-\mathrm{NH_2}$ groups, only one of them is involved in the formation of semicarbazones.
2. Benzaldehyde does not reduce Fehling’s solution while acetaldehyde does.

**Solution:**
1. **Semicarbazide structure:** $\mathrm{H_2\overset{1}{N}-CO-\overset{2}{N}H-\overset{3}{N}H_2}$.
   The terminal $-\mathrm{NH_2}$ group attached directly to the carbonyl group ($\mathrm{N^1}$) and the adjacent secondary amine nitrogen ($\mathrm{N^2}$) have their lone pairs delocalized into the carbonyl $\pi$-system via resonance:
   

$$
\mathrm{H_2N-CO-NH-NH_2 \longleftrightarrow H_2\overset{+}{N}=C(O^-)-NH-NH_2}
$$

   Consequently, the lone pairs on $\mathrm{N^1}$ and $\mathrm{N^2}$ are unavailable for nucleophilic attack. Only the hydrazine-terminus nitrogen ($\mathrm{N^3}$), which is not in direct conjugation with the carbonyl, has a localized lone pair and acts as an effective nucleophile.
2. **Fehling's test differentiation:** The electron-donating resonance effect of the benzene ring reduces the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon in benzaldehyde. Cupric ion ($\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$) in Fehling’s solution is a relatively weak oxidizing agent compared to Tollens’ reagent and cannot overcome the combined resonance stabilization and lower oxidation potential of aromatic aldehydes. Acetaldehyde, being aliphatic, has higher electrophilic carbon polarity and a more readily abstractable aldehydic proton.

### Problem 3 [CBSE 2022 Term-2, 3 Marks]
**Question:** Distinguish between the following pairs of compounds by simple chemical tests:
1. Pentan-2-one and Pentan-3-one
2. Benzaldehyde and Acetophenone
3. Ethanal and Propanal

**Solution:**
1. **Pentan-2-one vs. Pentan-3-one (Iodoform Test):**
   * Reagent: Warm with $\mathrm{I_2 / NaOH}$.
   * **Pentan-2-one** ($\mathrm{CH_3COCH_2CH_2CH_3}$) contains a methyl ketone group ($\mathrm{CH_3C=O}$) and yields a **yellow precipitate of $\mathrm{CHI_3}$**.
   * **Pentan-3-one** ($\mathrm{CH_3CH_2COCH_2CH_3}$) has no methyl ketone group and gives **no yellow precipitate**.
2. **Benzaldehyde vs. Acetophenone (Tollens' Test or Iodoform Test):**
   * *Method A (Tollens’ Test):* Benzaldehyde ($\mathrm{C_6H_5CHO}$) forms a **bright silver mirror** when warmed with ammoniacal $\mathrm{AgNO_3}$. Acetophenone ($\mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3}$) gives **no reaction**.
   * *Method B (Iodoform Test):* Acetophenone contains a $\mathrm{CH_3C=O}$ group and gives a **yellow precipitate of $\mathrm{CHI_3}$**. Benzaldehyde gives **no precipitate**.
3. **Ethanal vs. Propanal (Iodoform Test):**
   * Reagent: $\mathrm{I_2 + NaOH}$.
   * **Ethanal** ($\mathrm{CH_3CHO}$) is the *only* aldehyde containing the $\mathrm{CH_3C=O}$ fragment; it gives a **yellow precipitate of $\mathrm{CHI_3}$**.
   * **Propanal** ($\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CHO}$) does not contain the methyl carbonyl group and gives **no precipitate**.

### Problem 4 [CBSE 2021, 2 Marks]
**Question:** Arrange the following compounds in increasing order of their reactivity toward nucleophilic addition:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CHO,\ C_6H_5COCH_3,\ HCHO,\ CH_3COCH_3}
$$

Give reasons for your arrangement.

**Solution:**
* **Order:**
  

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3 < CH_3COCH_3 < CH_3CHO < HCHO}
$$

* **Reasoning:**
  1. **Steric Factor:** Formaldehyde ($\mathrm{HCHO}$) has only two small hydrogens, offering minimal steric hindrance to the approaching nucleophile. Acetophenone ($\mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3}$) has bulky phenyl and methyl groups, causing maximum steric congestion.
  2. **Electronic Factor:** In $\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3}$, two $+I$ methyl groups decrease the positive charge density ($\delta^+$) on the carbonyl carbon more than the single methyl group in $\mathrm{CH_3CHO}$. In $\mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3}$, the $+R$ effect of the phenyl ring additionally neutralizes the electrophilic center via resonance:
     

$$
\mathrm{Ph \overset{+R}{\longrightarrow} C(=O)CH_3}
$$

### Problem 5 [CBSE 2020, 3 Marks]
**Question:** Complete the following reactions:
1. $\mathrm{CH_3-CH=CH-CHO \xrightarrow{NaBH_4} ?}$
2. $\mathrm{C_6H_5CHO + CH_3CH_2NH_2 \xrightarrow{H^+} ?}$
3. $\mathrm{(CH_3)_2C=O + H_2N-OH \xrightarrow{H^+} ?}$

**Solution:**
1. $\mathrm{NaBH_4}$ selectively reduces the carbonyl group without affecting the conjugated carbon-carbon double bond under standard conditions:
   

$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH=CH-CHO \xrightarrow{NaBH_4} CH_3-CH=CH-CH_2OH \quad (\text{But-2-en-1-ol})}
$$

2. Benzaldehyde reacts with ethylamine to form a Schiff's base:
   

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5CHO + H_2N-CH_2CH_3 \xrightarrow{H^+} C_6H_5CH=N-CH_2CH_3 + H_2O \quad (\text{N-Benzylideneethanamine})}
$$

3. Propan-2-one condenses with hydroxylamine to yield an oxime:
   

$$
\mathrm{(CH_3)_2C=O + H_2N-OH \xrightarrow{H^+} (CH_3)_2C=N-OH + H_2O \quad (\text{Propan-2-one oxime / Acetoxime})}
$$

## 6. Examiner Traps & Common Board Mistakes

1. **The "Aldehydes = All Iodoform Negative" Trap:**
   * *Mistake:* Stating that *all* aldehydes fail the iodoform test.
   * *Correction:* **Acetaldehyde ($\mathrm{CH_3CHO}$)** is the single aldehyde that gives a positive iodoform test because it contains the required $\mathrm{CH_3C=O}$ unit.

2. **Fehling's Test on Benzaldehyde:**
   * *Mistake:* Using Fehling's solution to confirm Benzaldehyde because "it is an aldehyde."
   * *Correction:* Fehling’s test is negative for **aromatic aldehydes**. Use **Tollens' reagent** to test for benzaldehyde.

3. **Reagent Inversion in Hydrocarbon Reductions:**
   * *Mistake:* Using Clemmensen reduction ($\mathrm{Zn(Hg)/HCl}$) on a substrate containing a protected acetal or an $-\mathrm{OH}$ group.
   * *Correction:* The strong acid ($\mathrm{HCl}$) causes premature hydrolysis of acetals or dehydration of alcohols. Use basic **Wolff-Kishner conditions ($\mathrm{NH_2NH_2 / KOH}$)** for acid-sensitive substrates. Conversely, use Clemmensen reduction if base-sensitive esters or halogen atoms are present.

4. **Semicarbazide Reaction Formula Errors:**
   * *Mistake:* Writing the product of semicarbazide condensation using the amide $-\mathrm{NH_2}$ end:
     

$$
\mathrm{R_2C=N-CO-NH-NH_2 \quad (INCORRECT)}
$$

   * *Correction:* Attack occurs strictly via the hydrazine nitrogen:
     

$$
\mathrm{R_2C=N-NH-CO-NH_2 \quad (CORRECT)}
$$

5. **Equilibrium Reversibility with $\mathrm{NaHSO_3}$ and Acetals:**
   * *Mistake:* Omitting the acid/base regeneration step when discussing the purification of carbonyls.
   * *Correction:* Nucleophilic additions of $\mathrm{NaHSO_3}$ and $\mathrm{ROH}$ are reversible equilibria; mention that **dry $\mathrm{HCl}$ gas** is used to drive acetal formation by sequestering water, and mineral acids ($\mathrm{H_2SO_4/HCl}$) regenerate the original carbonyl compound.

## 7. Speed Hacks, Mnemonics & Golden Revision Table

### Rapid Mnemonic: Reaction Selectivity
* **"T-B-F" Rule (Tollens-Benzaldehyde-Fehling):**
  * **T**ollens’ oxidizes **B**oth (Aliphatic + Aromatic).
  * **F**ehling’s oxidizes **F**atty/Aliphatic only (Fails for Aromatic).

* **"Acid vs. Base Reduction Selection":**

  * **W**olff-Kishner uses **B**ase ($\mathrm{KOH}$) $\longrightarrow$ **W**ill destroy **B**ase-sensitive groups.
