## 1. Nomenclature & Molecular Structure of the Carboxyl Group

### 1.1 Structural Anatomy of the Carboxyl Group (⭐⭐⭐)
The carboxyl functional group ($-COOH$) derives its name from two constituent subunits: the **carbonyl** group ($>C=O$) and the **hydroxyl** group ($-OH$).

```
        O :
       //
    R-C
       \
        O - H
        ..
```

* **Hybridization:** The carboxyl carbon is bonded to three atoms via three $\sigma$-bonds and possesses no lone pairs; hence, it is strictly $\mathbf{sp^2}$ **hybridized**.
* **Geometry & Bond Angles:** The carboxyl carbon and the three attached atoms lie in a single plane (coplanar). The bond angles around the carboxyl carbon ($\angle O=C-O$, $\angle R-C=O$, and $\angle R-C-OH$) are all approximately $120^\circ$.
* **Resonance Stabilization of the Carboxyl Group:**
  The unshared pairs of electrons on the hydroxyl oxygen interact with the $\pi$-electron system of the carbonyl group:
  
  

$$
\text{R}-\text{C}(=\text{O})-\ddot{\text{O}}-\text{H} \longleftrightarrow \text{R}-\text{C}(\text{O}^-)=\overset{+}{\text{O}}-\text{H}
$$

  > **NCERT Crucial Concept:** The carbonyl carbon in a carboxylic acid is **less electrophilic** (less electron-deficient) than the carbonyl carbon in aldehydes and ketones. This occurs because the partial positive charge on the carbonyl carbon is substantially neutralized by the continuous resonance donation (mesomeric electron release) from the adjacent lone-pair-bearing hydroxyl oxygen atom. Consequently, carboxylic acids **do not** undergo characteristic nucleophilic addition reactions shown by aldehydes and ketones (e.g., they do not form oximes, hydrazones, or bisulfite adducts under standard conditions).

***

## 2. Complete Synthetic Protocols & Reaction Mechanisms

Carboxylic acids can be synthesized through six major pathways. Every reaction below includes exact reaction conditions, catalysts, and stoichiometry.

***

### Route 1: Direct Oxidation of Primary Alcohols and Aldehydes (⭐⭐⭐⭐)
Primary alcohols are oxidized cleanly to carboxylic acids using strong oxidants in alkaline, neutral, or acidic media.

#### Chemical Equations

$$
\text{R}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH} \xrightarrow[\text{2. } \text{H}_3\text{O}^+]{1.\; \text{alkaline } \text{KMnO}_4\text{ / }\Delta} \text{R}-\text{COOH}
$$

$$
\text{R}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH} \xrightarrow{\text{CrO}_3\text{ / }\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4\text{ (Jones Reagent)}} \text{R}-\text{COOH}
$$

$$
\text{R}-\text{CHO} \xrightarrow[\text{dil. }\text{HNO}_3\text{ or }\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7\text{ / }\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4]{\text{[O]}} \text{R}-\text{COOH}
$$

* **Reaction Conditions:** 
  * Reagent A: Potassium permanganate ($\text{KMnO}_4$) in aqueous $\text{KOH}$, followed by acidification with dilute $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ or $\text{HCl}$.
  * Reagent B: Jones Reagent ($\text{CrO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ in aqueous acetone at $0\text{--}5^\circ\text{C}$).

***

### Route 2: Vigorous Oxidation of Alkylbenzenes (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
Aromatic carboxylic acids are prepared by the vigorous oxidation of alkylbenzenes with alkaline potassium permanganate or acidic potassium dichromate.

#### Chemical Principles & General Equation

$$
\text{Ar}-\text{CH}_2\text{R}' \xrightarrow[\Delta]{1.\; \text{KMnO}_4\text{ / }\text{KOH}} \text{Ar}-\text{COOK} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{Ar}-\text{COOH}
$$

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{CH}_3 \xrightarrow[\Delta]{1.\; \text{KMnO}_4\text{ / }\text{KOH}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOK} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOH}
$$

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3 \xrightarrow[\Delta]{1.\; \text{KMnO}_4\text{ / }\text{KOH}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOK} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOH}
$$

#### Absolute Requirement: The $\alpha$-Hydrogen Rule
* The entire aliphatic side chain, regardless of its length, is degraded to a single carboxyl group ($-COOH$).
* **Critical Limitation:** The alkyl group **must contain at least one benzylic hydrogen** ($\alpha$-hydrogen). 
* Tert-butylbenzene does **not** undergo oxidation under these conditions because the benzylic carbon is quaternary (no $\alpha\text{-H}$ available for radical/hydride abstraction):

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)_3 \xrightarrow[\Delta]{1.\; \text{KMnO}_4\text{ / }\text{KOH}} No Reaction (Resistant to Oxidation)
$$

***

### Route 3: Stepwise Hydrolysis of Nitriles and Amides (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
Nitriles are converted to carboxylic acids via an amide intermediate under acidic or basic catalytic control.

#### Complete Reaction Scheme

$$
\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{N} \xrightarrow[\text{Catalyst: }\text{H}^+\text{ or }\text{OH}^-]{\text{H}_2\text{O}\text{ (Partial Hydrolysis)}} \text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{NH}_2 \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+\text{ or }\text{OH}^-\text{ (Complete Hydrolysis)}} \text{R}-\text{COOH} + \text{NH}_4^+
$$

#### Step-by-Step Acid-Catalyzed Mechanism
1. **Protonation of the Nitrile Nitrogen:**
   

$$
\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{N} + \text{H}^+ \rightleftharpoons \text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\overset{+}{\text{N}}-\text{H}
$$

2. **Nucleophilic Attack of Water on Electrophilic Carbon:**
   

$$
\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\overset{+}{\text{N}}-\text{H} + \text{H}_2\ddot{\text{O}} \rightleftharpoons \text{R}-\overset{\overset{\overset{+}{\text{O}}\text{H}_2}{\mid}}{\text{C}}=\ddot{\text{N}}-\text{H}
$$

3. **Proton Transfer (Tautomeric Rearrangement to Amide):**
   

$$
\text{R}-\overset{\overset{\overset{+}{\text{O}}\text{H}_2}{\mid}}{\text{C}}=\ddot{\text{N}}-\text{H} \rightleftharpoons \text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{OH}}{\mid}}{\text{C}}=\ddot{\text{N}}-\text{H} + \text{H}^+ \rightleftharpoons \text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\ddot{\text{N}}\text{H}_2 + \text{H}^+
$$

4. **Hydrolytic Cleavage of the Amide:**
   

$$
\text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\ddot{\text{N}}\text{H}_2 \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{R}-\text{COOH} + \text{NH}_4^+
$$

***

### Route 4: Carboxylation of Grignard Reagents (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
Grignard reagents react with dry ice (solid carbon dioxide) to form salts of carboxylic acids which, upon acidification with dilute mineral acid, afford carboxylic acids containing **one more carbon atom** than the starting alkyl/aryl halide.

#### Step-by-Step Reaction Equations
1. **Nucleophilic Addition to Carbon Dioxide:**
   The nucleophilic carbanionic center of the Grignard reagent attacks the electrophilic carbon of carbon dioxide:

   

$$
\text{R}^{\delta-}-\text{MgX}^{\delta+} + \text{O}=\text{C}=\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{Dry Ether}} \left[ \text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{O}^- \;\text{Mg}^{2+}\text{X} \right]
$$

2. **Acid Hydrolysis:**
   

$$
\left[ \text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{O}^- \;\text{Mg}^{2+}\text{X} \right] + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+ \longrightarrow \text{R}-\text{COOH} + \text{Mg}(\text{OH})\text{X}
$$

* **Solvent Condition:** Must be strictly anhydrous (dry diethyl ether or dry THF). Any traces of moisture will protonate the Grignard reagent to form an alkane ($\text{R}-\text{MgX} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{R}-\text{H} + \text{Mg}(\text{OH})\text{X}$), destroying the reagent.

***

### Route 5: Hydrolysis of Acyl Derivatives (Esters, Acid Chlorides, and Anhydrides) (⭐⭐⭐⭐)

#### (a) From Acid Halides

$$
\text{R}-\text{COCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{R}-\text{COOH} + \text{HCl}
$$

$$
\text{R}-\text{COCl} + 2\,\text{OH}^- \longrightarrow \text{R}-\text{COO}^- + \text{Cl}^- + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{R}-\text{COOH}
$$

#### (b) From Acid Anhydrides

$$
(\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CO})_2\text{O} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\,\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOH}
$$

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{COOCOCH}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\,\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}
$$

#### (c) From Esters
* **Acidic Hydrolysis (Reversible Equilibrium):**
  

$$
\text{R}-\text{COOR}' + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightleftharpoons{\text{H}^+} \text{R}-\text{COOH} + \text{R}'-\text{OH}
$$

* **Alkaline Hydrolysis (Irreversible Saponification):**
  

$$
\text{R}-\text{COOR}' + \text{NaOH} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{R}-\text{COO}^-\text{Na}^+ + \text{R}'-\text{OH}
$$

  
  

$$
\text{R}-\text{COO}^-\text{Na}^+ + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+ \longrightarrow \text{R}-\text{COOH} + \text{Na}^+
$$

## 3. Physical Properties & Dimerization

### 3.1 Unusually High Boiling Points
Carboxylic acids exhibit higher boiling points than hydrocarbons, haloalkanes, aldehydes, ketones, and even alcohols of comparable molecular weights.

| Compound | Formula | Molar Mass ($\text{g/mol}$) | Boiling Point ($\text{K}$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Propane | $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3$ | $44$ | $231$ |
| Ethanal | $\text{CH}_3\text{CHO}$ | $44$ | $294$ |
| Ethanol | $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$ | $46$ | $351$ |
| Formic Acid | $\text{HCOOH}$ | $46$ | $373.5$ |
| Acetic Acid | $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$ | $60$ | $391$ |

**Physical Explanation:**
Carboxylic acid molecules form extensive **intermolecular hydrogen bonds**. The hydroxyl hydrogen is strongly polarized by the adjacent carbonyl oxygen, facilitating the formation of extremely stable, cyclic, eight-membered **dimers**.

```
         O ········· H - O
        //                \
     R-C                   C-R
        \                //
         O - H ········· O
```

> **Crucial Observation:** In the **vapor state** and in **aprotic non-polar solvents** (e.g., dry benzene, $\text{CCl}_4$), carboxylic acids exist predominantly as **cyclic dimers**. As a result, vapor-density and colligative-property measurements (e.g., freezing-point depression) yield an apparent molecular mass for acetic acid of approximately $\approx 120\text{ g/mol}$ (double its true empirical formula mass of $60\text{ g/mol}$).

## 4. Rigorous Treatment of Carboxylic Acid Acidity

***

### 4.1 Thermodynamic Definitions: $K_a$ and $\text{p}K_a$ (⭐⭐⭐⭐)
When a carboxylic acid dissociates in water:

$$
\text{R}-\text{COOH} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{R}-\text{COO}^- + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+
$$

The equilibrium constant is given by:

$$
K_{eq} = \frac{[\text{R}-\text{COO}^-][\text{H}_3\text{O}^+]}{[\text{R}-\text{COOH}][\text{H}_2\text{O}]}
$$

Since water is present in large excess, $[\text{H}_2\text{O}]$ is constant:

$$
K_a = K_{eq}[\text{H}_2\text{O}] = \frac{[\text{R}-\text{COO}^-][\text{H}_3\text{O}^+]}{[\text{R}-\text{COOH}]}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{p}K_a = -\log_{10} K_a}
$$

* **Thermodynamic Law:** Greater acid strength corresponds to:
  

$$
\uparrow K_a \iff \downarrow \text{p}K_a
$$

  * Strong acids: $\text{p}K_a < 1$
  * Moderately strong acids: $1 < \text{p}K_a < 5$
  * Weak acids: $5 < \text{p}K_a < 15$
  * Extremely weak acids: $\text{p}K_a > 15$

***

### 4.2 Molecular Basis: Carboxylic Acids vs. Phenols vs. Alcohols (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

$$
Acidity Hierarchy:  \text{R}-\text{COOH} > \text{Ar}-\text{OH} > \text{H}_2\text{O} > \text{R}-\text{OH} > \text{HC}\equiv\text{CH}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{lcccc}
Species: & \text{CH}_3\text{COOH} & \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} & \text{H}_2\text{O} & \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH} \\
pK_a: & 4.76 & 9.98 & 15.7 & 15.9
\end{array}
$$

#### Carboxylate Ion vs. Phenoxide Ion: Why Carboxylic Acids are $10^5$ Times More Acidic than Phenol
1. **Carboxylate Ion ($\text{R}-\text{COO}^-$):**
   

$$
\text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}^-}{\mid}}{\text{C}}=\text{O} \longleftrightarrow \text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{O}^-
$$

   * The conjugate base is stabilized by **two completely equivalent resonance structures**.
   * The negative charge is delocalized over **two highly electronegative oxygen atoms** ($\chi_O \approx 3.44$).
   * The carbon-oxygen bond lengths in the carboxylate ion are completely identical ($127\text{ pm}$), intermediate between single ($136\text{ pm}$) and double ($121\text{ pm}$) bonds.

2. **Phenoxide Ion ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{O}^-$):**
   * The negative charge is delocalized over the aromatic ring across five canonical structures.
   * However, in three of the structures, the negative charge resides on **ortho- and para-carbon atoms** ($\chi_C \approx 2.55$), which are far less electronegative than oxygen.
   * Delocalization over carbon atoms is energetically less favorable than delocalization between two equivalent electronegative oxygen atoms.
   * Furthermore, the initial phenol molecule is itself resonance-stabilized (generating charge separation: positive charge on oxygen, negative on the ring), which diminishes the driving force for deprotonation compared to carboxylic acids.

***

### 4.3 Electronic Factors Governing Acidity (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

$$
\boxed{\text{Acidity} \propto \text{Stability of Conjugate Base (RCOO}^-) \propto \frac{\text{Electron-Withdrawing Groups (-I, -M)}}{\text{Electron-Donating Groups (+I, +M)}}}
$$

#### (a) Inductive Effect ($-I$ vs. $+I$)
* **Electron-Withdrawing Groups (EWGs) ($-I$):** Disperse the negative charge of the carboxylate ion electrostatically, stabilizing it and increasing acid strength.
* **Electron-Donating Groups (EDGs) ($+I$):** Push electron density toward the carboxylate center, intensifying the localized negative charge, destabilizing the anion, and decreasing acid strength.

**Decreasing Order of Inductive Electron Withdrawal ($-I$ Series):**

$$
\text{CF}_3 > \text{NO}_2 > \text{CN} > \text{SO}_3\text{H} > \text{CHO} > \text{COOR} > \text{F} > \text{Cl} > \text{Br} > \text{I} > \text{Ph}
$$

#### (b) Position/Distance Rule for Inductive Effects
The inductive effect is distance-dependent and falls off rapidly through the $\sigma$-bond framework, becoming negligible beyond three $\sigma$-bonds:

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\underset{\substack{\mid\\\text{Cl}}}{\text{CH}}-\text{COOH} \;>\; \text{CH}_3-\underset{\substack{\mid\\\text{Cl}}}{\text{CH}}-\text{CH}_2-\text{COOH} \;>\; \underset{\substack{\mid\\\text{Cl}}}{\text{CH}_2}-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\text{COOH}
$$

$$
\text{2-chlorobutanoic acid } (\text{p}K_a = 2.86) > \text{3-chlorobutanoic acid } (\text{p}K_a = 4.05) > \text{4-chlorobutanoic acid } (\text{p}K_a = 4.52)
$$

#### (c) Number of Halogen Atoms

$$
\text{CCl}_3\text{COOH} > \text{CHCl}_2\text{COOH} > \text{CH}_2\text{ClCOOH} > \text{CH}_3\text{COOH}
$$

$$
\text{p}K_a: \quad 0.64 \quad<\quad 1.29 \quad<\quad 2.87 \quad<\quad 4.76
$$

#### (d) Electronegativity of Halogens

$$
\text{F}-\text{CH}_2\text{COOH} > \text{Cl}-\text{CH}_2\text{COOH} > \text{Br}-\text{CH}_2\text{COOH} > \text{I}-\text{CH}_2\text{COOH}
$$

$$
\text{p}K_a: \quad 2.57 \quad<\quad 2.87 \quad<\quad 2.90 \quad<\quad 3.16
$$

### 4.4 Acidity of Substituted Benzoic Acids (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

#### The Ortho Effect in Benzoic Acids
> **Fundamental Law:** Almost **all ortho-substituted benzoic acids are stronger acids than benzoic acid**, regardless of whether the ortho substituent is electron-withdrawing ($-I, -M$ like $-\text{NO}_2$) or electron-donating ($+I, +M$ like $-\text{CH}_3$, $-\text{OCH}_3$, $-\text{OH}$, $-\text{Cl}$).

**Steric & Electronic Origin:**
1. A bulky substituent at the *ortho* position creates severe steric hindrance with the planar carboxyl group.
2. To relieve this steric strain, the $-COOH$ group twists out of the plane of the benzene ring.
3. Once out of plane, the $\pi$-orbital of the carboxyl carbon can no longer overlap effectively with the aromatic ring's $\pi$-system.
4. This **Steric Inhibition of Resonance (SIR)** turns off the electron-donating $+R$ mesomeric resonance from the phenyl ring into the carbonyl carbon.
5. Deprotonation yields a carboxylate ion whose negative charge is delocalized between the two oxygen atoms without destabilizing cross-conjugation from the aromatic ring, shifting the equilibrium toward the carboxylate anion.

#### Substituent Effects at Meta and Para Positions

##### Case 1: Nitrobenzoic Acids ($-\text{NO}_2$ shows $-I$ and $-M$)
* **Ortho-nitrobenzoic acid:** Strongest due to the ortho effect and combined $-I$ and $-M$ effects.
* **Para-nitrobenzoic acid:** The $-\text{NO}_2$ group exerts both a strong $-M$ resonance effect and a $-I$ inductive effect.
* **Meta-nitrobenzoic acid:** Resonance effects cannot operate at the meta position; the $-\text{NO}_2$ group acts solely through a strong $-I$ effect.
* **Benzoic acid:** Unsubstituted reference.

$$
Acidity Order:  \textit{ortho}\text{-nitrobenzoic} > \textit{para}\text{-nitrobenzoic} > \textit{meta}\text{-nitrobenzoic} > \text{benzoic acid}
$$

$$
\text{p}K_a: \quad 2.17 \quad<\quad 3.44 \quad<\quad 3.45 \quad<\quad 4.20
$$

##### Case 2: Methoxybenzoic Acids ($-\text{OCH}_3$ shows $-I$ and $+M$)
* **Ortho-methoxybenzoic acid:** Strongest acid due to the ortho effect ($\text{p}K_a = 4.09$).
* **Benzoic acid:** $\text{p}K_a = 4.20$.
* **Meta-methoxybenzoic acid:** Only the $-I$ effect operates (no $+M$ at meta position), withdrawing electron density and strengthening the acid ($\text{p}K_a = 4.09$).
* **Para-methoxybenzoic acid:** At the para position, the strong electron-releasing $+M$ effect dominates over the weaker $-I$ effect, pumping electron density into the carboxylate anion and significantly destabilizing it ($\text{p}K_a = 4.47$).

$$
Acidity Order:  \textit{ortho}\text{-methoxy} \approx \textit{meta}\text{-methoxy} > \text{benzoic acid} > \textit{para}\text{-methoxybenzoic acid}
$$

## 5. Distinction Tests: Carboxylic Acids vs. Phenols

Carboxylic acids and phenols can be distinguished experimentally by their differential reactivity toward weak bases:

```
                     Compound
                        |
       +----------------+----------------+
       |                                 |
  Carboxylic Acid                     Phenol
       |                                 |
+ NaHCO3 (aq)                     + NaHCO3 (aq)
       |                                 |
Brisk Effervescence (CO2 ↑)       No Reaction (No CO2)
```

$$
\text{R}-\text{COOH} + \text{NaHCO}_3 \longrightarrow \text{R}-\text{COONa} + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2 \uparrow \;(\text{Brisk Effervescence})
$$

$$
\text{Ar}-\text{OH} + \text{NaHCO}_3 \longrightarrow No Reaction
$$

* **Thermodynamic Basis:** A stronger acid displaces a weaker acid from its conjugate salt:
  

$$
\text{Acid}_1 + \text{Salt of Acid}_2 \rightleftharpoons \text{Acid}_2 + \text{Salt of Acid}_1
$$

  * For carboxylic acids:
    

$$
\text{p}K_a(\text{RCOOH}) \approx 4\text{--}5 < \text{p}K_a(\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3) \approx 6.35
$$

    Carboxylic acids are stronger acids than carbonic acid ($\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3$) and drive the reaction forward, liberating $\text{CO}_2$ gas.
  * For phenols:
    

$$
\text{p}K_a(\text{ArOH}) \approx 10 > \text{p}K_a(\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3) \approx 6.35
$$

    Phenol is a weaker acid than carbonic acid and cannot protonate the bicarbonate ion ($\text{HCO}_3^-$).

### [CBSE 2024, 3 Marks]
**Problem:** Arrange the following sets of compounds in order of increasing acid strength, providing reasoned justifications:
1. $\text{F}-\text{CH}_2-\text{COOH}$, $\text{Cl}-\text{CH}_2-\text{COOH}$, $\text{Br}-\text{CH}_2-\text{COOH}$, $\text{CH}_3-\text{COOH}$
2. Benzoic acid, 4-Nitrobenzoic acid, 4-Methoxybenzoic acid
3. $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}(\text{Br})\text{COOH}$, $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}(\text{Br})\text{CH}_2\text{COOH}$, $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CHCOOH}$

**Solution:**

#### Sub-part (1)
* **Order:** $\text{CH}_3-\text{COOH} < \text{Br}-\text{CH}_2-\text{COOH} < \text{Cl}-\text{CH}_2-\text{COOH} < \text{F}-\text{CH}_2-\text{COOH}$
* **Justification:** Halogens exert an electron-withdrawing inductive effect ($-I$), which disperses the negative charge of the conjugate carboxylate anion, increasing its stability. The magnitude of the $-I$ effect is directly proportional to electronegativity: $\text{F} (4.0) > \text{Cl} (3.0) > \text{Br} (2.8) > \text{H} (2.1)$. Higher $-I$ correlates with lower $\text{p}K_a$ and higher acidity.

#### Sub-part (2)
* **Order:** 4-Methoxybenzoic acid $<$ Benzoic acid $<$ 4-Nitrobenzoic acid
* **Justification:** 
  * In 4-nitrobenzoic acid, the $-\text{NO}_2$ group exerts strong electron-withdrawing mesomeric ($-M$) and inductive ($-I$) effects, stabilizing the conjugate benzoate anion.
  * In 4-methoxybenzoic acid, the $-\text{OCH}_3$ group has an unshared electron pair on oxygen that donates electron density into the ring through a positive mesomeric effect ($+M$), which outweighs its $-I$ effect. This destabilizes the conjugate base and lowers its acidity relative to benzoic acid.

#### Sub-part (3)
* **Order:** $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CHCOOH} < \text{CH}_3\text{CH}(\text{Br})\text{CH}_2\text{COOH} < \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}(\text{Br})\text{COOH}$
* **Justification:** The $-I$ effect of bromine decreases rapidly with distance from the carboxyl carbon. In 2-bromobutanoic acid, the bromine is attached to the $\alpha$-carbon (2 bonds away), providing greater stabilization to the carboxylate ion than in 3-bromobutanoic acid ($\beta$-carbon, 3 bonds away). Isobutyric acid lacks a halogen and has two electron-donating methyl groups ($+I$), making it the weakest acid of the three.

### [CBSE 2023, 2 Marks]
**Problem:** An organic compound $[\text{A}]$ with molecular formula $\text{C}_8\text{H}_8\text{O}$ gives a positive 2,4-DNP test and gives yellow precipitate of iodoform on treatment with $\text{I}_2$ and $\text{NaOH}$. Compound $[\text{A}]$ on drastic oxidation with alkaline $\text{KMnO}_4$ followed by acidification yields compound $[\text{B}]$ of molecular formula $\text{C}_7\text{H}_6\text{O}_2$.
1. Identify compounds $[\text{A}]$ and $[\text{B}]$.
2. Write the chemical equation for the conversion of $[\text{A}] \rightarrow [\text{B}]$.

**Solution:**
1. **Identification:**
   * Compound $[\text{A}]$ ($\text{C}_8\text{H}_8\text{O}$) gives a positive 2,4-DNP test, indicating it is an aldehyde or ketone.
   * It forms a yellow precipitate of iodoform ($\text{CHI}_3$) with $\text{I}_2/\text{NaOH}$, confirming the presence of a methyl carbonyl group ($\text{CH}_3-\text{C}=\text{O}$).
   * Subtracting $\text{CH}_3\text{CO}-$ ($43\text{ u}$) from $\text{C}_8\text{H}_8\text{O}$ leaves $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-$ (a phenyl ring).
   * Therefore, compound $[\mathbf{A}]$ is **Acetophenone** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COCH}_3$).
   * Drastic oxidation of acetophenone degrades the side chain to a carboxyl group on the benzene ring, forming compound $[\mathbf{B}]$, which is **Benzoic acid** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOH}$, formula $\text{C}_7\text{H}_6\text{O}_2$).

2. **Chemical Equation:**
   

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COCH}_3 \xrightarrow[\Delta]{1.\; \text{KMnO}_4\text{ / }\text{KOH}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOK} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOH} + \text{CH}_3\text{COOH}
$$

### [CBSE 2021 Sample Paper, 2 Marks]
**Problem:** Explain why:
1. Carboxylic acids do not give reactions of the carbonyl group (such as with $\text{NaHSO}_3$ or $\text{NH}_2\text{OH}$).
2. Acetic acid has an apparent molecular mass of $120$ when determined in benzene solvent.

**Solution:**
1. In carboxylic acids, the lone pair on the hydroxyl oxygen atom participates in resonance with the carbonyl $\pi$-bond:
   

$$
\text{R}-\text{C}(=\text{O})-\ddot{\text{O}}-\text{H} \longleftrightarrow \text{R}-\text{C}(\text{O}^-)=\overset{+}{\text{O}}-\text{H}
$$

   This electron donation reduces the electrophilic character of the carbonyl carbon compared to aldehydes and ketones, so it does not readily undergo nucleophilic additions with weak nucleophiles like sodium bisulfite ($\text{NaHSO}_3$) or hydroxylamine ($\text{NH}_2\text{OH}$).
2. In non-polar aprotic solvents like benzene, acetic acid forms stable, cyclic, eight-membered dimers through two intermolecular hydrogen bonds. Because the molecules associate as dimers:
   

$$
\text{Apparent Molar Mass} = 2 \times \text{Molar Mass of Monomer} = 2 \times 60\text{ g/mol} = 120\text{ g/mol}
$$

### [CBSE 2020, 3 Marks]
**Problem:** Give reasons:
1. Formic acid ($\text{HCOOH}$) is a stronger acid than acetic acid ($\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$).
2. Trichloroacetic acid is boiled with copper powder to yield chloroform.
3. Carboxylic acids have higher boiling points than alcohols of similar molecular mass.

**Solution:**
1. The methyl group in acetic acid is an electron-donating group ($+I$), which increases electron density on the carboxylate ion and destabilizes the conjugate base ($\text{CH}_3\text{COO}^-$). Formic acid has a hydrogen atom attached to the carboxyl group instead of an alkyl group, so no $+I$ destabilization occurs, making $\text{HCOOH}$ a stronger acid ($\text{p}K_a = 3.75$) than $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$ ($\text{p}K_a = 4.76$).
2. The three strongly electronegative chlorine atoms in trichloroacetic acid pull electron density away from the $\text{C}-\text{C}$ bond through a strong $-I$ effect. This weakens the $\text{C}-\text{C}$ bond connecting the trichloromethyl carbon to the carboxyl carbon, facilitating decarboxylation upon heating with copper to yield chloroform:
   

$$
\text{CCl}_3\text{COOH} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{Cu}} \text{CHCl}_3 + \text{CO}_2 \uparrow
$$

3. Carboxylic acids form stronger and more extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonds than alcohols. While alcohols form linear hydrogen-bonded chains, carboxylic acids form stable, cyclic dimers with two hydrogen bonds per dimer pair. These dimeric structures require more thermal energy to dissociate into the vapor phase, resulting in higher boiling points.

## 7. Examiner Traps & Common Student Pitfalls

```
                          COMMON EXAM TRAPS
                                 |
       +-------------------------+-------------------------+
       |                         |                         |
  Trap 1: KMnO4              Trap 2: pKa             Trap 3: Nitrile
  Side-Chain                 Inversion               Step-Up
  Oxidation                  Slip                    Omission
       |                         |                         |
Tert-butyl benzene         Low pKa = Strong         Must include
yields NO reaction!        High pKa = Weak          nitrile carbon
(Needs alpha-H)            (Opposite of Ka)         in IUPAC chain
```

* **Trap 1: The Tert-Butyl Benzene Oxidation Error:**
  * *Mistake:* Writing that $(CH_3)_3C-C_6H_5$ oxidizes to benzoic acid when treated with alkaline $\text{KMnO}_4$.
  * *Correction:* Oxidation requires at least one benzylic hydrogen ($\alpha\text{-H}$). Tert-butylbenzene has a quaternary benzylic carbon with no $\alpha\text{-H}$, so it is **completely resistant** to oxidation under these conditions.

* **Trap 2: Forgetting the Acidification Step:**
  * *Mistake:* Writing $\text{Ar}-\text{CH}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{KMnO}_4\text{ / }\text{KOH}} \text{Ar}-\text{COOH}$.
  * *Correction:* Alkaline oxidation first yields the potassium salt ($\text{Ar}-\text{COOK}$). Acidification ($\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$) is necessary to isolate the free carboxylic acid. The correct notation is:
    

$$
\text{Ar}-\text{CH}_3 \xrightarrow[\text{2. } \text{H}_3\text{O}^+]{1.\; \text{KMnO}_4\text{ / }\text{KOH}\text{, }\Delta} \text{Ar}-\text{COOH}
$$

* **Trap 3: $\text{p}K_a$ vs. $K_a$ Value Inversion:**
  * *Mistake:* Assuming that an acid with a higher $\text{p}K_a$ is more acidic.
  * *Correction:* $\text{p}K_a = -\log_{10} K_a$. Therefore, **higher acid strength corresponds to a lower $\text{p}K_a$**.

* **Trap 4: Missing the Nitrile Carbon in IUPAC Nomenclature:**
  * *Mistake:* Naming $\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{CN} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{COOH}$ as "ethanoic acid."
  * *Correction:* The carbon atom of the cyano group ($-CN$) becomes part of the carboxylic acid chain. Hydrolysis of propanenitrile yields **propanoic acid** (a 3-carbon chain).

* **Trap 5: Confusion in Acid-Base Extraction Separations:**
  * *Mistake:* Attempting to separate a mixture of benzoic acid and phenol using aqueous $\text{NaOH}$.
  * *Correction:* Both carboxylic acids ($\text{p}K_a \approx 4.2$) and phenols ($\text{p}K_a \approx 10$) react with aqueous $\text{NaOH}$ to form water-soluble sodium salts. To separate them, use **aqueous $\text{NaHCO}_3$**: benzoic acid dissolves as sodium benzoate with effervescence of $\text{CO}_2$, while phenol remains insoluble and can be separated.

### 8.1 Dicarboxylic Acid Chain Length Mnemonic
To recall the common names of aliphatic dicarboxylic acids of formula $\text{HOOC}-(\text{CH}_2)_n-\text{COOH}$:

$$
\mathbf{O}\text{h }\mathbf{M}\text{y }\mathbf{S}\text{uch }\mathbf{G}\text{ood }\mathbf{A}\text{pple }\mathbf{P}\text{ie}
$$

* $n = 0$: **O**xalic acid ($\text{HOOC}-\text{COOH}$)
* $n = 1$: **M**alonic acid ($\text{HOOC}-\text{CH}_2-\text{COOH}$)
* $n = 2$: **S**uccinic acid ($\text{HOOC}-(\text{CH}_2)_2-\text{COOH}$)
* $n = 3$: **G**lutaric acid ($\text{HOOC}-(\text{CH}_2)_3-\text{COOH}$)
* $n = 4$: **A**dipic acid ($\text{HOOC}-(\text{CH}_2)_4-\text{COOH}$)
* $n = 5$: **P**imelic acid ($\text{HOOC}-(\text{CH}_2)_5-\text{COOH}$)

### 8.2 Acidity Rapid-Sort Engine (The "DNP" Rule)
When comparing the acidities of halogenated carboxylic acids, evaluate substituents in this priority order:

$$
\mathbf{D} \longrightarrow \mathbf{N} \longrightarrow \mathbf{P}
$$

1. **$\mathbf{D}$istance:** The substituent closer to the $-COOH$ group exerts the dominant effect. (An $\alpha$-chloro substituent outweighs a $\beta$-dichloro pattern).
2. **$\mathbf{N}$umber:** If distances are identical, the number of electron-withdrawing groups determines strength. ($\text{Trichloro} > \text{Dichloro} > \text{Monochloro}$).
3. **$\mathbf{P}$ower (Electronegativity):** If distances and numbers are identical, rank by electronegativity/pulling power. ($\text{Fluoro} > \text{Chloro} > \text{Bromo} > \text{Iodo}$).

### 8.3 Carbon-Chain Adjustment Matrix

```
       [ Starting Material: R-X ]
                    |
       +------------+------------+
       |                         |
  Same Carbon Count         Add One Carbon (Step-Up)
       |                         |
1. Hydrolysis (via R-OH)    1. Cyanide (KCN) -> Hydrolysis (H3O+)
   R-X -> R-OH -> R-COOH        R-X -> R-CN -> R-COOH
                                 or
                            2. Grignard Reagent + CO2
                                R-MgX + CO2 -> R-COOH
```

* **Preserving Carbon Count:** 
  

$$
\text{R}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH} \xrightarrow{\text{Jones Reagent}} \text{R}-\text{COOH}
$$

* **Ascending the Series (Adding 1 Carbon / Step-Up):**
  * **Option A:** $\text{R}-\text{X} \xrightarrow{\text{KCN (alc.)}} \text{R}-\text{CN} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+\text{, }\Delta} \text{R}-\text{COOH}$
  * **Option B:** $\text{R}-\text{X} \xrightarrow{\text{Mg/dry ether}} \text{R}-\text{MgX} \xrightarrow{1.\;\text{CO}_2,\; 2.\;\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{R}-\text{COOH}$
