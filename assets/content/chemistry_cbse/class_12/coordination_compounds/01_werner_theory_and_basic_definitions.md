## 1. Pedagogical Theory & Foundational Concepts

### 1.1 Werner's Coordination Theory (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
Proposed by Alfred Werner in 1893 (Nobel Prize in 1913), this theory was the first successful attempt to explain the bonding and structure of complex compounds that violated traditional valence concepts.

#### Postulates of Werner's Theory:
1. **Dual Valence Concept:** In coordination compounds, central metal atoms/ions exhibit two distinct types of valencies:
   - **Primary Valency (Principal / Ionisable Valency):**
     - Corresponds to the **oxidation state** of the central metal atom/ion.
     - It is **ionisable** in aqueous solution.
     - It is non-directional in space.
     - It is satisfied exclusively by **negative ions** (anions).
   - **Secondary Valency (Auxiliary / Non-Ionisable Valency):**
     - Corresponds to the **coordination number** ($\text{CN}$) of the central metal.
     - It is **non-ionisable** in solution.
     - It is strictly **directional** in three-dimensional space, imparting a definite geometry (stereochemistry) to the complex.
     - It is satisfied by **neutral molecules** or **negative ions** (and rarely cations).

2. **Fixed Secondary Valency:** Every metal ion has a fixed number of secondary valencies (e.g., $\text{Co}^{3+}$ and $\text{Pt}^{4+}$ universally have a coordination number of 6; $\text{Pt}^{2+}$ and $\text{Pd}^{2+}$ have 4).

3. **Dual Role of Anions:** Certain negative ligands can satisfy **both primary and secondary valencies simultaneously**. Such ions are placed inside the coordination sphere. When written in equations, they do not ionise as free anions, yet they contribute to the oxidation state of the central metal ion.

### 1.3 Essential Terminology (NCERT Definitions) (⭐⭐⭐⭐)

1. **Coordination Entity:** An electrically charged or neutral species comprising a central metal atom/ion bonded to a fixed number of ions or molecules. 
   - *Example:* $[\text{Ni}(\text{CO})_4]$, $[\text{PtCl}_2(\text{NH}_3)_2]$, $[\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6]^{4-}$.
2. **Central Atom / Ion:** The metal atom or cation to which a fixed number of anions or neutral donor molecules are bound in a definite geometrical arrangement. It acts as a **Lewis acid** (electron pair acceptor).
3. **Coordination Sphere vs. Ionisation Sphere:**
   - **Coordination Sphere:** Enclosed within square brackets $[ \dots ]$. Contains the central metal ion and directly coordinated ligands. It does **not** dissociate in solution under normal conditions.
   - **Ionisation Sphere:** The portion written outside the brackets. Contains counter-ions that dissociate completely in water.
4. **Coordination Polyhedron:** The spatial arrangement of the ligand donor atoms directly attached to the central atom/ion. Common geometries: Octahedral, Tetrahedral, Square planar, Trigonal bipyramidal.
5. **Oxidation State (OS):** The residual charge a central metal atom would carry if all coordinate ligands were pulled away along with the shared electron pairs.
6. **Homoleptic vs. Heteroleptic Complexes:**
   - **Homoleptic:** Metal is bound to only one kind of donor group (e.g., $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_6]^{3+}$).
   - **Heteroleptic:** Metal is bound to more than one kind of donor group (e.g., $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_4\text{Cl}_2]^{+}$).
7. **Double Salt vs. Coordination Complex:**
   - **Double Salt:** A stoichiometric addition compound that dissociates completely into its constituent simple ions in aqueous solution (loses its identity).
     - *Example:* Mohr's Salt:
       

$$
\text{FeSO}_4 \cdot (\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4 \cdot 6\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{Fe}^{2+}_{(\text{aq})} + 2\text{NH}_{4(\text{aq})}^+ + 2\text{SO}_{4(\text{aq})}^{2-} + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

     - *Example:* Potash Alum:
       

$$
\text{KAl}(\text{SO}_4)_2 \cdot 12\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{K}^+_{(\text{aq})} + \text{Al}^{3+}_{(\text{aq})} + 2\text{SO}_{4(\text{aq})}^{2-} + 12\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

   - **Complex Compound:** Retains its chemical identity in aqueous solution; the complex ion does not dissociate into simple constituent ions.
     - *Example:* Potassium ferrocyanide:
       

$$
\text{K}_4[\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6]_{(\text{s})} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{O}} 4\text{K}^+_{(\text{aq})} + [\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6]^{4-}_{(\text{aq})}
$$

       *(Does not give individual test for $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ or $\text{CN}^-$ ions).*

### 1.4 Detailed Classification of Ligands (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

A **ligand** is an ion or molecule capable of donating at least one electron pair to the central metal atom/ion to form a coordinate covalent (dative) bond (Lewis Base).

#### A. Based on Denticity (Number of Donor Atoms):
1. **Unidentate (Monodentate):** Binds via a single donor atom.
   - Neutral: $\text{H}_2\ddot{\text{O}}$ (aqua), $:\!\text{NH}_3$ (ammine), $:\!\text{CO}$ (carbonyl), $:\!\text{NO}$ (nitrosyl), $\text{C}_5\text{H}_5\text{N}$ (pyridine, $\text{py}$).
   - Anionic: $\text{F}^-$ (fluorido), $\text{Cl}^-$ (chlorido), $\text{Br}^-$ (bromido), $\text{OH}^-$ (hydroxido), $\text{CN}^-$ (cyanido).
2. **Didentate (Bidentate):** Possesses two donor atoms positioned to bind simultaneously to the same metal ion:
   - **Ethane-1,2-diamine ($\text{en}$):** Neutral, symmetric. Donor atoms: two nitrogen atoms ($-\ddot{\text{N}}\text{H}_2$).
     

$$
\text{H}_2\ddot{\text{N}}-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\ddot{\text{N}}\text{H}_2
$$

   - **Oxalate ion ($\text{ox}^{2-}$ or $\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}$):** Dianionic, symmetric. Donor atoms: two carboxylate oxygens ($-\text{O}^-$).
     

$$
\begin{bmatrix} \text{O}=\text{C}-\text{O}^- \\ \quad\ \ \vert \\ \text{O}=\text{C}-\text{O}^- \end{bmatrix}
$$

   - **Glycinate ion ($\text{gly}^-$):** Monoanionic, unsymmetric. Donor atoms: $\text{N}$ of $-\text{NH}_2$ and $\text{O}$ of $-\text{COO}^-$.
     

$$
\text{H}_2\ddot{\text{N}}-\text{CH}_2-\text{COO}^-
$$

3. **Polydentate:**
   - **Tridentate:** Diethylenetriamine ($\text{dien}$) [3 $\text{N}$ donor atoms].
   - **Tetradentate:** Triethylenetetramine ($\text{trien}$) [4 $\text{N}$ donor atoms].
   - **Hexadentate:** Ethylenediaminetetraacetate ion ($\text{EDTA}^{4-}$):
     

$$
\begin{pmatrix} ^{-}\text{OOC}-\text{CH}_2 \\ ^{-}\text{OOC}-\text{CH}_2 \end{pmatrix} \!\ddot{\text{N}}-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\ddot{\text{N}}\! \begin{pmatrix} \text{CH}_2-\text{COO}^- \\ \text{CH}_2-\text{COO}^- \end{pmatrix}
$$

     Donor atoms: $2$ tertiary nitrogen atoms and $4$ carboxylate oxygen atoms.

#### B. Ambidentate Ligands (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):
Unidentate ligands containing **two different potential donor atoms**, but coordinating through **only one** donor atom at a time to a given metal ion:
- **Nitrito ligand:**
  - Coordinates through $\text{N}$: $-\text{NO}_2$ $\rightarrow$ **nitrito-N** (nitro)
  - Coordinates through $\text{O}$: $-\text{ONO}$ $\rightarrow$ **nitrito-O**
- **Thiocyanato ligand:**
  - Coordinates through $\text{S}$: $-\text{SCN}$ $\rightarrow$ **thiocyanato-S** (thiocyanato)
  - Coordinates through $\text{N}$: $-\text{NCS}$ $\rightarrow$ **thiocyanato-N** (isothiocyanato)
- **Cyanido ligand:**
  - Coordinates through $\text{C}$: $-\text{CN}$ $\rightarrow$ **cyanido-C**
  - Coordinates through $\text{N}$: $-\text{NC}$ $\rightarrow$ **cyanido-N** (isocyanido)

> **Linkage Isomerism:** The presence of ambidentate ligands gives rise to linkage isomerism in coordination complexes (e.g., $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_5(\text{NO}_2)]\text{Cl}_2$ [yellow-brown] and $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_5(\text{ONO})]\text{Cl}_2$ [red]).

#### C. Chelate Ligands & The Chelate Effect (⭐⭐⭐⭐):
When a di- or polydentate ligand binds to a single metal ion forming a 5- or 6-membered heterocyclic ring, it is termed a **chelating ligand**, and the resulting complex is a **chelate**.
- **The Chelate Effect:** Chelate complexes exhibit markedly higher thermodynamic stability compared to analogous complexes with unidentate ligands.
- **Thermodynamic Basis:**
  

$$
\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ
$$

  When a hexadentate ligand like $\text{EDTA}^{4-}$ displaces 6 unidentate water molecules:
  

$$
[\text{Ni}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+} + \text{EDTA}^{4-} \rightleftharpoons [\text{Ni}(\text{EDTA})]^{2-} + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

  Number of independent particles in solution increases from $2 \rightarrow 7$.
  Hence, $\Delta S^\circ \gg 0$, making $-T\Delta S^\circ$ large and negative, which drives $\Delta G^\circ < 0$.

## 2. Quantitative Relationships & Formulations

### 2.1 Coordination Number ($\text{CN}$) Formulation

$$
\boxed{\text{Coordination Number (CN)} = \sum_{i} \left( n_i \times d_i \right)}
$$

Where:
- $n_i$ = number of ligands of type $i$
- $d_i$ = denticity of ligand $i$

*(Note: $\pi$-bonding interactions are ignored when assigning the formal coordination number).*

### 2.2 Oxidation State Formulation

$$
\boxed{\text{Charge on Coordination Sphere} = \text{OS of Central Metal} + \sum (\text{Charge on each Ligand})}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{OS of Metal} = q_{\text{sphere}} - \sum_{i} (n_i \cdot z_i)}
$$

Where:
- $q_{\text{sphere}}$ = net charge on the complex ion
- $z_i$ = formal electrostatic charge carried by ligand $i$

### 2.3 Precipitation & Conductance Stoichiometry (Derivation)

Consider the general Werner formulation of a cobalt ammine complex:

$$
\text{CoCl}_3 \cdot x\text{NH}_3 \equiv [\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_x\text{Cl}_{3-y}]\text{Cl}_y
$$

Upon dissolution in pure water at $298\text{ K}$:

$$
[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_x\text{Cl}_{3-y}]\text{Cl}_y\ (\text{s}) \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{O}} [\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_x\text{Cl}_{3-y}]^{y+}_{(\text{aq})} + y\text{Cl}^-_{(\text{aq})}
$$

Reaction with excess silver nitrate ($\text{AgNO}_3$):

$$
y\text{Cl}^-_{(\text{aq})} + y\text{Ag}^+_{(\text{aq})} \xrightarrow{\text{excess }\text{AgNO}_3} y\text{AgCl}\ (\text{s, white})\downarrow
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Moles of AgCl ppt per mole of complex} = y = \text{Number of ionisable counter-anions}}
$$

#### Electrical Molar Conductivity ($\Lambda_m$):

$$
\Lambda_m = \frac{\kappa \times 1000}{M} \quad \left(\text{in }\text{S}\cdot\text{cm}^2\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}\right)
$$

- For non-electrolyte ($y=0$): $\Lambda_m \approx 0\ \text{S}\cdot\text{cm}^2\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$
- For $1:1$ electrolyte ($2\text{ ions}$, $y=1$): $\Lambda_m \approx 90 - 130\ \text{S}\cdot\text{cm}^2\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$
- For $1:2$ electrolyte ($3\text{ ions}$, $y=2$): $\Lambda_m \approx 220 - 260\ \text{S}\cdot\text{cm}^2\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$
- For $1:3$ electrolyte ($4\text{ ions}$, $y=3$): $\Lambda_m \approx 380 - 435\ \text{S}\cdot\text{cm}^2\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$

## 3. High-Yield Examples & Solved 5-Year PYQs (2020–2025)

### Example 1 [CBSE 2024, 3 Marks]
**Problem:** 
When a coordination compound $\text{CrCl}_3 \cdot 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$ is mixed with excess $\text{AgNO}_3$, $2\text{ moles}$ of $\text{AgCl}$ are precipitated per mole of the compound. Write:
1. The structural formula of the complex.
2. The IUPAC name of the complex.
3. The secondary valency and primary valency of chromium in this complex.

**Step-by-step Solution:**
- **Step 1:** Moles of $\text{AgCl}$ precipitated per mole of complex $= 2$.
  Therefore, there are $2$ ionisable chloride ions outside the coordination sphere: $y = 2$.
- **Step 2:** The total chlorine atoms $= 3$. Thus, $3 - 2 = 1$ chloride ion is non-ionisable and present inside the coordination sphere, acting as a ligand.
- **Step 3:** $\text{Cr}^{3+}$ has a characteristic coordination number of $6$. Since $1$ chloride is inside, $5$ water molecules must be inside the coordination sphere to complete $\text{CN} = 6$. The 6th water molecule exists as water of crystallization (hydrate).
  

$$
\text{Structural Formula: } \boxed{[\text{Cr}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_5\text{Cl}]\text{Cl}_2 \cdot \text{H}_2\text{O}}
$$

- **Step 4:** IUPAC Name: **Pentaaquachloridochromium(III) chloride monohydrate**.
- **Step 5:** 
  - $\text{Secondary Valency} = \text{Coordination Number} = 5\ (\text{from }\text{H}_2\text{O}) + 1\ (\text{from }\text{Cl}^-) = \mathbf{6}$.
  - $\text{Primary Valency} = \text{Oxidation State of Cr}$:
    

$$
x + 5(0) + 1(-1) + 2(-1) = 0 \implies x = +3 \implies \mathbf{3}
$$

.

### Example 2 [CBSE 2023, 2 Marks]
**Problem:** 
Give reasons for the following:
1. $[\text{Ni}(\text{en})_3]^{2+}$ is thermodynamically more stable than $[\text{Ni}(\text{NH}_3)_6]^{2+}$.
2. Nitrito ($-\text{NO}_2^-$) is referred to as an ambidentate ligand.

**Step-by-step Solution:**
1. **Chelate Effect:** In $[\text{Ni}(\text{en})_3]^{2+}$, ethane-1,2-diamine ($\text{en}$) is a bidentate chelating ligand. It forms three stable five-membered chelate rings around $\text{Ni}^{2+}$. In contrast, $\text{NH}_3$ in $[\text{Ni}(\text{NH}_3)_6]^{2+}$ is a unidentate ligand forming no rings. Chelation results in an increase in entropy ($\Delta S^\circ > 0$) during ligand displacement, making $[\text{Ni}(\text{en})_3]^{2+}$ thermodynamically more stable.
2. **Ambidentate Nature:** The $\text{NO}_2^-$ ion possesses two distinct donor atoms with lone pairs (nitrogen and oxygen). It can coordinate to the central metal ion either through the nitrogen atom ($-\text{NO}_2$, nitrito-N) or through the oxygen atom ($-\text{ONO}$, nitrito-O). Hence, it is an ambidentate ligand.

### Example 3 [CBSE 2022 Term-II, 2 Marks]
**Problem:** 
Calculate the coordination number and oxidation number of the central metal atom in the following complexes:
1. $[\text{Fe}(\text{C}_2\text{O}_4)_3]^{3-}$
2. $[\text{Co}(\text{en})_2\text{Cl}_2]^+$

**Step-by-step Solution:**
1. For $[\text{Fe}(\text{C}_2\text{O}_4)_3]^{3-}$:
   - Ligand: Oxalate ion ($\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}$), didentate ($d = 2$).
   - $\text{CN} = \text{number of ligands} \times \text{denticity} = 3 \times 2 = \mathbf{6}$.
   - Oxidation State ($x$):
     

$$
x + 3(-2) = -3 \implies x - 6 = -3 \implies x = \mathbf{+3}
$$

.
2. For $[\text{Co}(\text{en})_2\text{Cl}_2]^+$:
   - Ligands: Two $\text{en}$ ($d = 2$) and two $\text{Cl}^-$ ($d = 1$).
   - $\text{CN} = (2 \times 2) + (2 \times 1) = 4 + 2 = \mathbf{6}$.
   - Oxidation State ($x$):
     

$$
x + 2(0) + 2(-1) = +1 \implies x - 2 = +1 \implies x = \mathbf{+3}
$$

.

### Example 4 [CBSE 2021 (Compartment/Model), 2 Marks]
**Problem:** 
A coordination compound with empirical formula $\text{PtCl}_4 \cdot 2\text{KCl}$ does not precipitate any $\text{AgCl}$ upon treatment with cold aqueous $\text{AgNO}_3$. Determine the coordination entity, the coordination number of $\text{Pt}$, and the primary valency of platinum.

**Step-by-step Solution:**
- Since no precipitate of $\text{AgCl}$ is formed, **no chloride ions reside in the ionisation sphere**.
- Potassium ions ($\text{K}^+$) act as counter-cations outside the coordination sphere: $2\text{K}^+$.
- All four chloride ions must be inside the coordination sphere along with the central platinum atom.
- Formula: $\mathbf{K_2[\text{PtCl}_6]}$? *Notice stoichiometry: $\text{PtCl}_4 \cdot 2\text{KCl} \implies \text{K}_2[\text{PtCl}_6]$.*
  - Total chlorine atoms $= 4 + 2 = 6$.
  - All $6$ chlorides are inside the coordination sphere: $[\text{PtCl}_6]^{2-}$.
  - Coordination number of $\text{Pt} = \mathbf{6}$.
  - Primary valency (oxidation state of $\text{Pt}$):
    

$$
x + 6(-1) = -2 \implies x = \mathbf{+4}
$$

.

### Example 5 [CBSE 2020, 3 Marks]
**Problem:** 
$0.01\text{ mol}$ of a complex $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_5(\text{SO}_4)]\text{Br}$ and $0.01\text{ mol}$ of $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_5\text{Br}]\text{SO}_4$ are dissolved separately in water to prepare $1\text{ L}$ solutions (Solution A and Solution B, respectively).
1. What will happen when aqueous $\text{BaCl}_2$ is added to both solutions?
2. What will happen when aqueous $\text{AgNO}_3$ is added to both solutions?
3. Write the chemical equation for the positive reaction in each case.

**Step-by-step Solution:**
1. **Addition of $\text{BaCl}_2$:**
   - Solution A ($[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_5(\text{SO}_4)]\text{Br}$): Counter-ion is $\text{Br}^-$. No reaction / no precipitate with $\text{Ba}^{2+}$.
   - Solution B ($[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_5\text{Br}]\text{SO}_4$): Counter-ion is $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$. Yields a dense **white precipitate** of barium sulphate ($\text{BaSO}_4$).
2. **Addition of $\text{AgNO}_3$:**
   - Solution A ($[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_5(\text{SO}_4)]\text{Br}$): Counter-ion is $\text{Br}^-$. Yields a **pale yellow precipitate** of silver bromide ($\text{AgBr}$) sparingly soluble in $\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$.
   - Solution B ($[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_5\text{Br}]\text{SO}_4$): Counter-ion is $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$. No precipitate with $\text{Ag}^+$.
3. **Chemical Equations:**
   - For Solution B with $\text{BaCl}_2$:
     

$$
\text{SO}_{4(\text{aq})}^{2-} + \text{Ba}^{2+}_{(\text{aq})} \longrightarrow \text{BaSO}_{4(\text{s})}\downarrow\ (\text{white precipitate})
$$

   - For Solution A with $\text{AgNO}_3$:
     

$$
\text{Br}^-_{(\text{aq})} + \text{Ag}^+_{(\text{aq})} \longrightarrow \text{AgBr}_{(\text{s})}\downarrow\ (\text{pale yellow precipitate})
$$

## 4. Examiner Traps & Common Student Errors

| Error Category | Mistake | Correct Pedagogical Correction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Denticity Confusion** | Counting $\text{CN} = 3$ in $[\text{Fe}(\text{ox})_3]^{3-}$ because there are 3 ligands. | Oxalate ($\text{ox}^{2-}$) is **bidentate** ($d=2$). $\text{CN} = 3 \times 2 = 6$. Always check denticity before stating $\text{CN}$. |
| **Ambidentate vs. Polydentate** | Stating that $\text{NO}_2^-$ or $\text{SCN}^-$ is bidentate because it has 2 donor atoms. | Ambidentate ligands are **strictly monodentate**. They have 2 different donor atoms, but coordinate via **only one donor atom at a time** due to geometric ring strain. |
| **Primary vs. Secondary Valency** | Treating primary valency as secondary valency when anions satisfy both. | If $\text{Cl}^-$ is inside the coordination sphere, it satisfies **both** primary and secondary valency. It counts toward coordination number **and** oxidation state, but it **does not ionise**. |
| **Oxidation State Sign** | Writing oxidation state as $3$ or $3+$ instead of Roman numeral in IUPAC, or omitting the positive sign in valency. | Write Roman numerals in parentheses for IUPAC names: $\text{Chromium(III)}$. For formal oxidation states, write $+3$ (not just $3$). |
| **Double Salt vs. Complex** | Confusing Mohr's salt with a complex ion in aqueous conductivity questions. | Mohr's salt ($\text{FeSO}_4 \cdot (\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4 \cdot 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$) completely dissociates to give independent tests for $\text{Fe}^{2+}$, $\text{NH}_4^+$, and $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$. Complexes do not dissociate into individual constituent ions. |

## 5. Speed Hacks, Mnemonics & Golden Revision Points

### 1. The Common Denticity Cheat-Sheet
- **Neutral Monodentate:** $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (aqua), $\text{NH}_3$ (ammine), $\text{CO}$ (carbonyl), $\text{NO}$ (nitrosyl), $\text{py}$ (pyridine).
- **Anionic Monodentate (Charge $-1$):** $\text{X}^-$ ($\text{F}^-, \text{Cl}^-, \text{Br}^-, \text{I}^-$), $\text{OH}^-$, $\text{CN}^-$, $\text{NC}^-$, $\text{NO}_2^-$, $\text{ONO}^-$, $\text{SCN}^-$, $\text{NCS}^-$, $\text{CH}_3\text{COO}^-$.
- **Symmetric Bidentate (Charge $-2$):** $\text{ox}^{2-}$ ($\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}$).
- **Symmetric Bidentate (Charge $0$):** $\text{en}$ ($\text{H}_2\text{NCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2$).
- **Unsymmetric Bidentate (Charge $-1$):** $\text{gly}^-$ ($\text{NH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{COO}^-$).
- **Hexadentate (Charge $-4$):** $\text{EDTA}^{4-}$.

### 2. Coordination Number Fingerprints of Common Metals
- **Always $\text{CN} = 6$:** $\text{Co}^{3+}, \text{Cr}^{3+}, \text{Fe}^{3+}, \text{Pt}^{4+}$.
- **Always $\text{CN} = 4$ (Square Planar):** $\text{Pt}^{2+}, \text{Pd}^{2+}$.
- **$\text{CN} = 4$ (Tetrahedral or Square Planar) or $6$:** $\text{Ni}^{2+}, \text{Cu}^{2+}, \text{Zn}^{2+}$.
- **$\text{CN} = 2$:** $\text{Ag}^+, \text{Au}^+, \text{Cu}^+$.

### 3. Werner's Electrolyte Ratio Hack
Let formula be $[\text{Complex}]\text{X}_n$ or $\text{M}_m[\text{Complex}]$:
- Total ions $= (n + 1)$ or $(m + 1)$.
- Moles of $\text{AgCl}$ precipitated per mole of complex = number of chloride ions located **outside** the square brackets.
- Conductivity order directly follows the **total number of ions per formula unit**:
  

$$
\Lambda_m \propto \text{Number of ions in solution}
$$

  

$$
[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_6]\text{Cl}_3\ (4\text{ ions}) > [\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_5\text{Cl}]\text{Cl}_2\ (3\text{ ions}) > [\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_4\text{Cl}_2]\text{Cl}\ (2\text{ ions}) > [\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_3\text{Cl}_3]\ (0\text{ ions, non-electrolyte})
$$
