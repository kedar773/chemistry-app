### Question 1 [CBSE 2024, Set-1, 1 Mark] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Question:**  
The coordination number and oxidation state of $\text{Cr}$ in the complex $[\text{Cr}(\text{C}_2\text{O}_4)_3]^{3-}$ are, respectively:  
(a) $3$ and $+3$  
(b) $6$ and $+3$  
(c) $6$ and $+6$  
(d) $4$ and $+2$  

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
* **Correct Option:** **(b)** $[6 \text{ and } +3]$  
* **Step-by-step Justification:**  
  * Oxalate ion $(\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-})$ is a bidentate (didentate) chelating ligand.  
    

$$
\text{Coordination Number} = 3 \times 2 = 6
$$

  
  * Let the oxidation state of $\text{Cr}$ be $x$:  
    

$$
x + 3(-2) = -3 \implies x - 6 = -3 \implies x = +3
$$

  
* **Marking Breakdown:**  
  * [½ Mark] Identifying coordination number as $6$ due to didentate nature of $\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}$.  
  * [½ Mark] Correct calculation yielding oxidation state $+3$ and choosing option (b).

### Question 2 [CBSE 2023, Set-2, 1 Mark] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Question:**  
Which of the following complexes is homoleptic and paramagnetic?  
(a) $[\text{Ni}(\text{CO})_4]$  
(b) $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_6]^{3+}$  
(c) $[\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6]^{3-}$  
(d) $[\text{Ni}(\text{CN})_4]^{2-}$  

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
* **Correct Option:** **(c)** $[[\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6]^{3-}]$  
* **Step-by-step Justification:**  
  * A **homoleptic** complex contains only one kind of donor group/ligand. All four options are homoleptic.  
  * In $[\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6]^{3-}$:  
    

$$
\text{Fe}^{3+} \implies [\text{Ar}]\,3d^5
$$

  
    $\text{CN}^-$ is a strong field ligand, causing pairing: $t_{2g}^5\,e_g^0$.  
    Number of unpaired electrons ($n$) = $1$. Hence, it is **paramagnetic**.  
  * In $[\text{Ni}(\text{CO})_4]$, $\text{Ni}^0 (3d^8 4s^2 \to 3d^{10})$ has $n=0$ (diamagnetic).  
  * In $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_6]^{3+}$, $\text{Co}^{3+} (3d^6)$ undergoes complete pairing into $t_{2g}^6$ (diamagnetic).  
  * In $[\text{Ni}(\text{CN})_4]^{2-}$, $\text{Ni}^{2+} (3d^8)$ pairs up to form square planar $dsp^2$ (diamagnetic).  
* **Marking Breakdown:**  
  * [½ Mark] Electronic configuration analysis of $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ ($d^5$).  
  * [½ Mark] Stating presence of 1 unpaired electron under strong field ligand $\implies$ paramagnetic.

### Question 3 [CBSE 2024, Set-3, 1 Mark] ⭐⭐⭐⭐
**Question:**  
When $1\text{ mol of } \text{CoCl}_3 \cdot 5\text{NH}_3$ is treated with excess $\text{AgNO}_3$ solution, $2\text{ mol of } \text{AgCl}$ precipitate is obtained. The secondary valence of cobalt in this complex is:  
(a) $3$  
(b) $5$  
(c) $6$  
(d) $4$  

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
* **Correct Option:** **(c)** $[6]$  
* **Step-by-step Justification:**  
  * Precipitation of $2\text{ mol of } \text{AgCl}$ indicates $2$ ionisable $\text{Cl}^-$ ions present outside the coordination sphere.  
  * The Werner coordination formula is therefore:  
    

$$
[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_5\text{Cl}]\text{Cl}_2
$$

  
  * Secondary valence corresponds to the coordination number of the central metal ion:  
    

$$
\text{Secondary Valence} = 5\,(\text{from NH}_3) + 1\,(\text{from Cl}^-) = 6
$$

  
* **Marking Breakdown:**  
  * [½ Mark] Deducing formula as $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_5\text{Cl}]\text{Cl}_2$.  
  * [½ Mark] Correctly assigning secondary valence as $6$.

### Question 5 [CBSE 2020 Delhi, 1 Mark] ⭐⭐⭐
**Question:**  
Which of the following ligands forms a chelate complex?  
(a) Acetate  
(b) Oxalate  
(c) Cyanide  
(d) Ammonia  

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
* **Correct Option:** **(b)** [Oxalate]  
* **Step-by-step Justification:**  
  * A **chelating ligand** is a di- or polydentate ligand that binds via two or more donor atoms to the same central metal ion, forming a stable 5- or 6-membered ring.  
  * Oxalate ion $(\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-})$ has two carboxylate donor oxygen atoms capable of forming a 5-membered chelate ring. Acetate, cyanide, and ammonia are monodentate.  
* **Marking Breakdown:**  
  * [1 Mark] Choosing option (b) with proper identification of didentate chelating ring-former.

### Question 6 [CBSE 2024, Set-2, 1 Mark] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Question (Assertion-Reason):**  
Given below are two statements labeled as Assertion (A) and Reason (R):  
* **Assertion (A):** $[\text{Fe}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{3+}$ is strongly paramagnetic whereas $[\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6]^{3-}$ is weakly paramagnetic.  
* **Reason (R):** $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ is a weak field ligand causing no pairing of electrons, while $\text{CN}^-$ is a strong field ligand causing pairing of electrons.  
Select the correct answer from the codes below:  
(a) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).  
(b) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).  
(c) (A) is true, but (R) is false.  
(d) (A) is false, but (R) is true.  

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
* **Correct Option:** **(a)**  
* **Step-by-step Justification:**  
  * Electronic configuration of $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ is $3d^5$.  
  * With $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (weak field, $\Delta_o < P$): Configuration is $t_{2g}^3\,e_g^2$. Number of unpaired electrons $n = 5$ ($\mu = \sqrt{35} \approx 5.92\text{ BM}$), which is **strongly paramagnetic**.  
  * With $\text{CN}^-$ (strong field, $\Delta_o > P$): Configuration is $t_{2g}^5\,e_g^0$. Number of unpaired electrons $n = 1$ ($\mu = \sqrt{3} \approx 1.73\text{ BM}$), which is **weakly paramagnetic**.  
  * Reason (R) correctly explains the variation in spin states via spectrochemical field strength.  
* **Marking Breakdown:**  
  * [½ Mark] Correct determination of truth values of (A) and (R).  
  * [½ Mark] Correct recognition of causal relationship leading to option (a).

### Question 9 [CBSE 2023, Set-1, 2 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Question:**  
Giving a suitable example for each, explain the following:  
(i) Ambidentate ligand  
(ii) Crystal Field Splitting Energy ($\Delta_o$)  

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
* **(i) Ambidentate ligand:**  
  * A monodentate ligand that can coordinate to the central metal atom/ion through either of two different donor atoms.  
  * **Example:** Nitrito ligand: $-\text{NO}_2^-$ binds through Nitrogen ($-\text{NO}_2$, nitro) or Oxygen ($-\text{ONO}$, nitrito-O); or thiocyanate ($-\text{SCN}^-$ vs $-\text{NCS}^-$).  
  * *[Marking: ½ Mark for definition + ½ Mark for valid example].*  

* **(ii) Crystal Field Splitting Energy ($\Delta_o$):**  
  * When ligands approach a central metal ion in an octahedral field, the degeneracy of the five $d$-orbitals is broken, splitting them into lower energy $t_{2g}$ and higher energy $e_g$ orbitals. The difference in energy between these two split sets of orbitals is defined as the Crystal Field Splitting Energy ($\Delta_o$).  
  * *[Marking: 1 Mark for clear definition / energy difference between $t_{2g}$ and $e_g$].*

### Question 10 [CBSE 2022 Term-2, 2 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐
**Question:**  
Using Valence Bond Theory (VBT), predict the hybridization, geometry, and magnetic behavior of $[\text{NiCl}_4]^{2-}$. [Atomic number of $\text{Ni} = 28$]  

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
* **Step 1: Oxidation state and ground state configuration:**  
  

$$
\text{Ni} (Z = 28): [\text{Ar}]\,3d^8 4s^2
$$

  
  

$$
\text{Ni}^{2+}: [\text{Ar}]\,3d^8 4s^0
$$

  
  Orbital diagram: $3d$ has 3 paired orbitals and 2 half-filled orbitals ($\uparrow\downarrow$ $\uparrow\downarrow$ $\uparrow\downarrow$ $\uparrow$ $\uparrow$).  
* **Step 2: Effect of ligand:**  
  $\text{Cl}^-$ is a **weak field ligand** ($\Delta_o < P$); it cannot cause pairing of electrons in the $3d$ subshell.  
* **Step 3: Hybridization and geometry:**  
  One $4s$ and three $4p$ empty orbitals hybridize to give **$sp^3$ hybridization**.  
  * Geometry: **Tetrahedral**  
  * Magnetic property: Since there are $2$ unpaired electrons ($n=2$), the complex is **Paramagnetic**.  
* **Marking Breakdown:**  
  * [½ Mark] Correct valence electron distribution of $\text{Ni}^{2+}$.  
  * [½ Mark] Hybridization identified as $sp^3$.  
  * [½ Mark] Geometry stated as Tetrahedral.  
  * [½ Mark] Paramagnetic nature with $n=2$ unpaired electrons specified.

### Question 11 [CBSE 2020 AI, 2 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐
**Question:**  
What is the difference between a double salt and a complex compound? Explain with one example of each.  

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
* **Double Salt:**  
  * An addition compound that exists only in the crystalline state and completely dissociates into its constituent simple ions when dissolved in water.  
  * **Example:** Mohr's salt, $\text{FeSO}_4 \cdot (\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4 \cdot 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (gives tests for $\text{Fe}^{2+}$, $\text{NH}_4^+$, and $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$).  
  * *[Marking: ½ Mark definition + ½ Mark example].*  
* **Complex Compound (Coordination Entity):**  
  * An addition compound that retains its identity in solid state as well as in aqueous solution and does not completely dissociate into its constituent individual ions.  
  * **Example:** Potassium ferrocyanide, $\text{K}_4[\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6]$ (does not give test for free $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ or $\text{CN}^-$).  
  * *[Marking: ½ Mark definition + ½ Mark example].*

### Question 12 [CBSE 2024, Set-2, 3 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Question:**  
Account for the following observations:  
(a) $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_6]^{3+}$ is an inner orbital diamagnetic complex, whereas $[\text{Ni}(\text{NH}_3)_6]^{2+}$ is an outer orbital paramagnetic complex.  
(b) Low-spin tetrahedral complexes are rarely observed.  
(c) $[\text{Ti}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{3+}$ is colored, but on heating it becomes colorless.  

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
* **(a)**  
  * In $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_6]^{3+}$, $\text{Co}$ is in $+3$ oxidation state: $\text{Co}^{3+} = 3d^6$.  
    $\text{NH}_3$ acts as a strong field ligand for $\text{Co}^{3+}$, forcing 6 electrons to pair up in three $3d$ orbitals ($t_{2g}^6$). Two empty inner $3d$ orbitals participate in **$d^2sp^3$** hybridization (**inner orbital**). No unpaired electrons $\implies$ **diamagnetic**.  
  * In $[\text{Ni}(\text{NH}_3)_6]^{2+}$, $\text{Ni}^{2+} = 3d^8$.  
    The $3d$ subshell has configurations: $\uparrow\downarrow$ $\uparrow\downarrow$ $\uparrow\downarrow$ $\uparrow$ $\uparrow$. Even if pairing were forced, only one $d$-orbital could be vacated, but two are required for octahedral hybridization. Thus, it utilizes outer $4d$ orbitals forming **$sp^3d^2$** (**outer orbital**). It contains $2$ unpaired electrons $\implies$ **paramagnetic**.  
  * *[Marking: 1 Mark total: ½ mark for $\text{Co}^{3+}$ configuration/inner orbital explanation + ½ mark for $\text{Ni}^{2+}$ outer orbital $sp^3d^2$].*  

* **(b)**  
  * In tetrahedral geometry, the crystal field splitting energy ($\Delta_t$) is relatively small:  
    

$$
\Delta_t = \frac{4}{9}\Delta_o
$$

  
  * This splitting energy is always less than the pairing energy ($\Delta_t < P$). Hence, electrons prefer entering higher energy orbitals rather than pairing up, preventing the formation of low-spin complexes.  
  * *[Marking: 1 Mark for mentioning $\Delta_t = \frac{4}{9}\Delta_o$ and $\Delta_t < P$ condition].*  

* **(c)**  
  * $[\text{Ti}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{3+}$ is colored due to $d-d$ transition of a single electron from $t_{2g}$ to $e_g$ ($\text{Ti}^{3+} = 3d^1$).  
  * On heating, water ligands are lost ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$ molecules evaporate), destroying the coordination sphere and the crystal field splitting. Without splitting, no $d-d$ transition occurs; hence, it becomes colorless.  
  * *[Marking: 1 Mark: ½ mark for $d-d$ transition + ½ mark for loss of ligand / removal of crystal field].*

### Question 13 [CBSE 2023, Set-3, 3 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Question:**  
Write the formula, draw the structures of geometrical isomers, and state the optical activity of the isomers of:  

$$
[\text{Co}(\text{en})_2\text{Cl}_2]^+
$$

  

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
* **Geometrical Isomerism:**  
  The complex exhibits two geometrical isomers: *cis* and *trans*.  

```
        cis-isomer                          trans-isomer
           Cl                                   Cl
           |                                    |
     en---Co---Cl                         en---Co---en
         / \                                    |
       en   (en)                                Cl
```
  * In the **cis-isomer**, the two chlorido ligands occupy adjacent (90°) positions.  
  * In the **trans-isomer**, the two chlorido ligands occupy opposite (180°) positions across the central cobalt.  
  * *[Marking: 1 Mark for correctly sketching/representing cis- and trans-structures].*  

* **Optical Activity:**  
  * **trans-isomer:** Contains a center of symmetry / plane of symmetry; it is achiral and therefore **optically inactive**.  
  * **cis-isomer:** Lacks a plane of symmetry; it is chiral and nonsuperimposable on its mirror image, resolving into dextro ($d$) and laevo ($l$) enantiomers. Thus, it is **optically active**.  
  * *[Marking: 1 Mark for stating optical inactivity of trans due to symmetry + 1 Mark for stating optical activity and enantiomeric pair of cis].*

### Question 15 [CBSE 2024 Board Pattern, 4 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Read the passage given below and answer the following questions:**

> The interaction between ligands and transition metal ions is explained by Crystal Field Theory (CFT). Rather than treating metal-ligand bonds as covalent sharing, CFT considers the interaction as purely electrostatic: point charges for anions or dipoles for neutral molecules. In an isolated gaseous transition metal atom or ion, all five $d$-orbitals have identical energies (degenerate). When ligands approach in an octahedral arrangement, the electrostatic field created splits the five $d$-orbitals into two sets: three lower-energy orbitals designated as $t_{2g}$ ($d_{xy}, d_{yz}, d_{zx}$) and two higher-energy orbitals designated as $e_g$ ($d_{x^2-y^2}, d_{z^2}$).  
> The magnitude of this separation is denoted by $\Delta_o$. Ligands causing large crystal field splitting are classified as strong field ligands, whereas those producing small splitting are weak field ligands. This orbital bifurcation dictates spectral absorption, color, and magnetic moments of coordination complexes.

**Questions:**  
(a) Why do $e_g$ orbitals experience greater repulsion than $t_{2g}$ orbitals in an octahedral crystal field? **[1 Mark]**  
(b) Calculate the spin-only magnetic moment ($\mu_s$) of $[\text{Mn}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+}$. [Atomic number: $\text{Mn} = 25$] **[1 Mark]**  
(c) A coordination compound with the formula $\text{CoCl}_3 \cdot 4\text{NH}_3$ gives $1\text{ mole}$ of $\text{AgCl}$ precipitate per mole of the compound with excess $\text{AgNO}_3$.  
(i) Write the structural formula and IUPAC name of the complex.  
(ii) How many geometrical isomers does this complex exhibit? Draw them. **[2 Marks]**  
*OR*  
(c) $[\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6]^{4-}$ and $[\text{Fe}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+}$ differ in color and magnetic behavior despite having the same central metal ion in the same oxidation state. Explain using CFT. **[2 Marks]**

***

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**

* **(a)**  
  * The lobes of the two $e_g$ orbitals ($d_{x^2-y^2}$ and $d_{z^2}$) lie directly along the coordinate Cartesian axes ($x, y, z$), which is the precise direction along which the six octahedral ligands approach the central metal ion. Thus, they experience maximum electrostatic repulsion.  
  * *[Marking: 1 Mark for pointing out that $e_g$ lobes point directly along the axes of ligand approach].*  

* **(b)**  
  * $\text{Mn}$ has $Z=25$: $[\text{Ar}]\,3d^5 4s^2 \implies \text{Mn}^{2+} = 3d^5$.  
  * $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ is a weak field ligand ($\Delta_o < P$). Configuration: $t_{2g}^3\,e_g^2$.  
  * Number of unpaired electrons $n = 5$.  
    

$$
\mu_s = \sqrt{n(n+2)} = \sqrt{5(5+2)} = \sqrt{35} \approx 5.92\text{ BM}
$$

  
  * *[Marking: ½ Mark for formula & $n=5$, ½ Mark for final numerical value with units $\text{BM}$].*  

* **(c)**  
  * **(i)** Precipitation of $1\text{ mol } \text{AgCl}$ indicates $1\text{ Cl}^-$ ion in the ionisation sphere.  
    * Structural formula: $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_4\text{Cl}_2]\text{Cl}$  
    * IUPAC Name: **Tetraamminedichloridocobalt(III) chloride**  
    *[Marking: 1 Mark: ½ mark for formula, ½ mark for IUPAC name].*  
  * **(ii)** It exhibits $2$ geometrical isomers: *cis* and *trans*.  
    * *cis* (adjacent $\text{Cl}$ ligands at $90^\circ$) and *trans* (opposite $\text{Cl}$ ligands at $180^\circ$).  
    *[Marking: 1 Mark for specifying 2 isomers and drawing/describing configurations].*  

* **[OR Alternative for (c)]**  
  * In both complexes, iron is in $+2$ oxidation state: $\text{Fe}^{2+} \implies 3d^6$.  
  * In $[\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6]^{4-}$: $\text{CN}^-$ is a strong field ligand ($\Delta_o > P$). Electrons pair up: $t_{2g}^6\,e_g^0$. Unpaired electrons $n = 0 \implies$ **diamagnetic**. The large $\Delta_o$ causes absorption of high energy (UV/violet), reflecting pale yellow.  
  * In $[\text{Fe}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+}$: $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ is a weak field ligand ($\Delta_o < P$). Configuration: $t_{2g}^4\,e_g^2$. Unpaired electrons $n = 4 \implies$ **paramagnetic**. A smaller $\Delta_o$ enables absorption of lower energy red light, transmitting a pale green color.  
  * *[Marking: 1 Mark for magnetic behavior justification via $t_{2g}^6$ vs $t_{2g}^4 e_g^2$ + 1 Mark for color origin based on magnitude of $\Delta_o$ and $d-d$ transition absorption].*

### Question 16 [CBSE 2023, Set-1, 5 Marks] ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]
**Question:**  
(a) A metal complex having composition $\text{Co(en)}_2\text{Cl}_2\text{Br}$ was found to exist in two different isomeric forms, 'A' and 'B'. Form 'A' reacts with $\text{AgNO}_3$ to give a pale yellow precipitate soluble with difficulty in concentrated ammonia, whereas Form 'B' reacts with $\text{AgNO}_3$ to give a curdy white precipitate soluble readily in dilute ammonia.  
(i) Identify the type of isomerism exhibited by 'A' and 'B'.  
(ii) Write the coordination formula and IUPAC name of both isomers 'A' and 'B'.  
(iii) Which of these isomers can display optical isomerism? Give reasons. **[3 Marks]**  

(b) Compare the magnetic properties and geometries of $[\text{CoF}_6]^{3-}$ and $[\text{Co}(\text{C}_2\text{O}_4)_3]^{3-}$ using Crystal Field Theory. [Atomic number: $\text{Co} = 27$] **[2 Marks]**  

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**

* **(a)**  
  * **(i)** **Ionisation Isomerism** (gives different ions in aqueous solution).  
    *[Marking: ½ Mark].*  
  * **(ii) Identification:**

* **(b) Synergistic Bonding in Metal Carbonyls:**  

```
        M <========:C≡O:   (σ-bond: Carbon lone pair donation to vacant metal d-orbital)
        M =========> C≡O   (π-backbond: Filled metal d-orbital to vacant π* of CO)
```

  1. **$\sigma$-Bond Formation:**  
     The carbonyl ligand donates its lone pair of electrons on carbon into a vacant hybridized orbital of the central transition metal atom along the internuclear axis ($\text{M} \leftarrow \text{C}$).  
  2. **$\pi$-Backbonding:**  
     A filled non-bonding $d$-orbital of the transition metal overlaps sideways with the empty antibonding $\pi^*$ ($2p$) orbital of the carbon monoxide molecule ($\text{M} \to \text{C}$).  
  3. **Synergistic Effect:**  
     The back-donation ($\text{M} \to \text{C}$) relieves excess electron density on the electropositive metal, strengthening the $\sigma$-bond. Concurrently, the $\sigma$-donation strengthens the $\pi$-bond. This cooperative mutual reinforcement is termed the **synergic effect**, rendering metal carbonyl complexes exceptionally stable.  
  * *[Marking: 3 Marks: 1 mark for $\sigma$-bond description, 1 mark for $\pi$-backbonding explanation, 1 mark for defining the synergic effect and resulting stability].*
