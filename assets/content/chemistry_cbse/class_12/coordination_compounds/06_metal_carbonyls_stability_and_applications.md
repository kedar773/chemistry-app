### 1.1 Classification and Structure of Representative Homoleptic Carbonyls

Homoleptic metal carbonyls contain exclusively carbonyl ($\text{CO}$) ligands coordinated to a transition metal center. In these complexes, transition metals generally reside in the formal oxidation state of **zero** ($0$).

```
1. Tetracarbonylnickel(0)         [Ni(CO)4]      -> Tetrahedral (sp3)
2. Pentacarbonyliron(0)           [Fe(CO)5]      -> Trigonal bipyramidal (dsp3)
3. Hexacarbonylchromium(0)        [Cr(CO)6]      -> Octahedral (d2sp3)
4. Decacarbonyldimanganese(0)     [Mn2(CO)10]    -> Two square-pyramidal [Mn(CO)5] units joined by a direct Mn-Mn single bond (no bridging CO)
5. Octacarbonyldicobalt(0)        [Co2(CO)8]     -> Two bridging CO ligands + six terminal CO ligands + one Co-Co bond (in solid state)
```

$$
\begin{array}{c}
\mathbf{[Ni(CO)_4]} \\
\text{Tetrahedral} \\
\mu = 0\text{ D}
\end{array}
\qquad
\begin{array}{c}
\mathbf{[Fe(CO)_5]} \\
\text{Trigonal Bipyramidal} \\
(3\text{ equatorial } \text{CO} + 2\text{ axial } \text{CO})
\end{array}
\qquad
\begin{array}{c}
\mathbf{[Cr(CO)_6]} \\
\text{Octahedral} \\
O_h\text{ symmetry}
\end{array}
$$

### 1.2 Nature of $\text{M}-\text{C}$ Bonding: Synergic Bonding Mechanism

The metal-carbon bond in metal carbonyls possesses both **$\sigma$** and **$\pi$** character. This bonding interaction reinforces itself and is referred to as **Synergic Bonding**.

```
[Metal d_pi / t2g orbital]  <=========== pi-backdonation ===========  [Carbon monoxide pi* antibonding orbital]
                                (d_pi -> pi* back-bonding)

[Metal empty d_sigma / hybrid orbital] <==== sigma donation ========= [Carbon lone pair (sp hybrid / 3sigma orbital)]
```

#### Component 1: $\sigma$-Dative Bond ($\text{C} \to \text{M}$)
* The carbon monoxide molecule possesses a lone pair of electrons localized primarily on the carbon atom (in its highest occupied molecular orbital, non-bonding $3\sigma$ / $2\sigma^*$ with strong carbon character).
* **Electron Transfer:** The carbon atom donates this lone pair into a vacant hybrid orbital of the transition metal atom directed along the internuclear axis:

$$
\text{M}(\text{vacant hybrid orbital}) \leftarrow :\text{C}\equiv\text{O}: \implies \mathbf{M} \xleftarrow{\sigma} \mathbf{CO}
$$

#### Component 2: $\pi$-Backbonding ($\text{M} \to \text{C}$)
* Transition metals in low or zero oxidation states have electron-rich filled $d$-orbitals of appropriate symmetry ($d_{xy}, d_{yz}, d_{zx}$ or $t_{2g}$ set).
* The $\text{CO}$ ligand has vacant, low-lying degenerate **antibonding $\pi^*$ molecular orbitals** ($\pi^* 2p_x, \pi^* 2p_y$).
* **Electron Transfer:** Filled metal $d_\pi$ orbitals overlap laterally with the empty $\pi^*$ orbitals of the carbon atom:

$$
\text{M}(\text{filled } d_\pi) \longrightarrow \pi^*_{\text{CO}}(\text{empty}) \implies \mathbf{M} \xrightarrow{\pi} \mathbf{CO}
$$

#### Synergic Reinforcement (Feedback Effect)
1. $\sigma$-donation transfers negative charge density from $\text{CO}$ to the metal, making the metal center more electron-rich.
2. The increased electron density on the metal facilitates stronger $\pi$-backdonation into the $\pi^*$ orbitals of $\text{CO}$.
3. $\pi$-backdonation alleviates excess negative charge accumulation on the metal atom, which in turn enhances the ability of the metal to accept further $\sigma$-donation.
4. **Thermodynamic Result:** The two components operate cooperatively:

$$
\boxed{\text{Overall Effect: Strengthens the } \mathbf{M-C} \text{ bond, shortens } r_{\text{M-C}}, \text{ weakens the } \mathbf{C-O} \text{ bond, and lengthens } r_{\text{C-O}}.}
$$

### 1.3 Molecular Orbital & Spectroscopic Manifestations (IR Stretching Frequency)

By Hooke’s Law for a diatomic harmonic oscillator, the vibrational stretching frequency $\tilde{\nu}$ is:

$$
\tilde{\nu} = \frac{1}{2\pi c} \sqrt{\frac{k}{\mu}}
$$

Where:
* $k$ = force constant of the bond (proportional to bond order and bond strength, in $\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^{-1}$ or $\text{dyn}\cdot\text{cm}^{-1}$)
* $\mu$ = reduced mass of the vibrating system: $\mu = \frac{m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2}$ (in $\text{kg}$)
* $c$ = speed of light ($2.998 \times 10^{10} \text{ cm}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$)

#### Derivation of Consequences of $\pi$-Backdonation:

$$
\pi\text{-backdonation} \implies \text{Populates the } \pi^*_{\text{CO}} \text{ antibonding orbital}
$$

$$
\text{Bond Order of C-O} = \frac{N_b - N_a}{2}
$$

Because electrons enter the antibonding manifold ($N_a$ increases):
1. **$\text{C}-\text{O}$ Bond Order decreases** (from $3.0$ toward $2.0$).
2. **Force constant $k_{\text{C-O}}$ decreases**.
3. **$\text{C}-\text{O}$ stretching frequency $\tilde{\nu}_{\text{C-O}}$ shifts to lower wavenumbers** (red-shift).
4. Simultaneously, **$\text{M}-\text{C}$ bond order increases** (from $1.0$ toward $2.0$), shortening the $\text{M}-\text{C}$ bond and increasing $\tilde{\nu}_{\text{M-C}}$.

#### Isoelectronic Series Comparison (Crucial Board & Entrance Favorite):
Consider the isoelectronic hexacarbonyl series: $[\text{V}(\text{CO})_6]^{-}$, $[\text{Cr}(\text{CO})_6]$, and $[\text{Mn}(\text{CO})_6]^{+}$.

```
Complex:                   [V(CO)6]-       [Cr(CO)6]       [Mn(CO)6]+
Central Metal Ox. State:     -1                0               +1
Electron density on Metal: Highest         Moderate         Lowest
Extensive pi-backbonding:  Maximum         Moderate         Minimum
M-C Bond Strength:         Highest         Moderate         Lowest
C-O Bond Order:            Lowest          Moderate         Highest
C-O Stretching Frequency:  Lowest          Intermediate     Highest
```

$$
\tilde{\nu}_{\text{CO}} \text{ order: } \mathbf{[V(CO)_6]^- < [Cr(CO)_6] < [Mn(CO)_6]^+ < \text{Free CO } (2143\text{ cm}^{-1})}
$$

$$
\text{M-C bond length order: } \mathbf{[V(CO)_6]^- < [Cr(CO)_6] < [Mn(CO)_6]^+ }
$$

$$
\text{C-O bond length order: } \mathbf{[Mn(CO)_6]^+ < [Cr(CO)_6] < [V(CO)_6]^- }
$$

### 2.1 Thermodynamic vs. Kinetic Stability

* **Thermodynamic Stability:** Measures the extent to which a complex forms or is transformed into another species at chemical equilibrium under a given set of conditions. It is governed by the standard Gibbs free energy change ($\Delta G^\circ$) and equilibrium formation constants ($\beta$).
* **Kinetic Stability:** Refers to the speed (reaction rate) at which ligand substitution or dissociation reactions occur. It is governed by the activation energy ($E_a$).
  * **Labile Complexes:** Undergo ligand exchange rapidly ($t_{1/2} < 1\text{ minute}$).
  * **Inert Complexes:** Undergo ligand exchange extremely slowly ($t_{1/2} > 1\text{ minute}$), despite favorable thermodynamic driving forces.
  * *Classic Demonstration:* $\left[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_6\right]^{3+} + 6\text{H}_3\text{O}^+ \rightleftharpoons \left[\text{Co}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6\right]^{3+} + 6\text{NH}_4^+$ has $\beta \approx 10^{25}$ (thermodynamically unstable in acid), yet it persists in concentrated acid for days at room temperature because it is **kinetically inert**.

***

### 2.2 Stepwise ($K_n$) vs. Overall ($\beta_n$) Stability Constants

Consider the step-by-step formation of a complex $\text{ML}_n$ from a central metal ion $\text{M}$ and monodentate ligands $\text{L}$ in aqueous solution (ignoring coordinated water molecules for clarity):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{M} + \text{L} &\xrightleftharpoons{K_1} \text{ML} && K_1 = \frac{[\text{ML}]}{[\text{M}][\text{L}]} \\
\text{ML} + \text{L} &\xrightleftharpoons{K_2} \text{ML}_2 && K_2 = \frac{[\text{ML}_2]}{[\text{ML}][\text{L}]} \\
\text{ML}_2 + \text{L} &\xrightleftharpoons{K_3} \text{ML}_3 && K_3 = \frac{[\text{ML}_3]}{[\text{ML}_2][\text{L}]} \\
&\;\;\vdots && \;\;\vdots \\
\text{ML}_{n-1} + \text{L} &\xrightleftharpoons{K_n} \text{ML}_n && K_n = \frac{[\text{ML}_n]}{[\text{ML}_{n-1}][\text{L}]}
\end{aligned}
$$

Here, $K_1, K_2, \dots, K_n$ are the **Stepwise Stability Constants**.

#### Overall Formation Reaction:

$$
\text{M} + n\text{L} \xrightleftharpoons{\beta_n} \text{ML}_n \qquad \beta_n = \frac{[\text{ML}_n]}{[\text{M}][\text{L}]^n}
$$

Here, $\beta_n$ is the **Overall Stability Constant** (or cumulative formation constant).

#### Mathematical Relationship Between $\beta_n$ and $K_n$:
Multiply the stepwise equilibria constants together:

$$
K_1 \times K_2 \times K_3 \times \dots \times K_n = \left(\frac{[\text{ML}]}{[\text{M}][\text{L}]}\right) \times \left(\frac{[\text{ML}_2]}{[\text{ML}][\text{L}]}\right) \times \dots \times \left(\frac{[\text{ML}_n]}{[\text{ML}_{n-1}][\text{L}]}\right)
$$

All intermediate complex concentrations cancel out:

$$
K_1 \times K_2 \times \dots \times K_n = \frac{[\text{ML}_n]}{[\text{M}][\text{L}]^n} = \beta_n
$$

Taking the logarithm ($\log_{10}$) on both sides:

$$
\boxed{\log_{10} \beta_n = \log_{10} K_1 + \log_{10} K_2 + \dots + \log_{10} K_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \log_{10} K_i}
$$

#### General Rule for Stepwise Constants:
Under normal conditions:

$$
\mathbf{K_1 > K_2 > K_3 > \dots > K_n}
$$

**Underlying Physical & Chemical Reasons:**
1. **Statistical Effect:** As more coordination sites are occupied, fewer vacant sites remain on $\text{M}$ for incoming ligands, while the probability of dissociation of attached ligands increases.
2. **Steric Hindrance:** As coordination number increases, mutual steric crowding of ligands increases.
3. **Electrostatic Factors:** If $\text{M}^{m+}$ is positive and $\text{L}^-$ is anionic, every successive addition of $\text{L}^-$ reduces the net positive charge on the central species, weakening electrostatic attraction toward the next incoming ligand.

#### Instability / Dissociation Constant ($K_d$):
The instability constant is the reciprocal of the overall formation constant:

$$
\boxed{K_d = \frac{1}{\beta_n} = \frac{[\text{M}][\text{L}]^n}{[\text{ML}_n]}}
$$

***

### 2.3 Thermodynamic Derivations ($\Delta G^\circ, \Delta H^\circ, \Delta S^\circ$)

The relationship between the standard Gibbs free energy change and the overall stability constant is given by the van 't Hoff isotherm:

$$
\Delta G^\circ = -RT \ln \beta_n = -2.303 RT \log_{10} \beta_n
$$

Applying the fundamental thermodynamic identity $\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ$:

$$
-2.303 RT \log_{10} \beta_n = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ
$$

$$
\boxed{\log_{10} \beta_n = -\frac{\Delta H^\circ}{2.303 RT} + \frac{\Delta S^\circ}{2.303 R}}
$$

* High stability ($\beta_n \gg 1$) requires a large negative $\Delta G^\circ$, which is achieved when:
  1. $\Delta H^\circ$ is strongly negative (exothermic coordinate bond formation due to strong $\sigma$-donation and ligand field stabilization).
  2. $\Delta S^\circ$ is positive (release of solvated water molecules causing an increase in system disorder).

***

### 2.4 Factors Affecting the Stability of Coordination Compounds

#### Factor 1: Charge Density of the Central Metal Ion

$$
\text{Charge Density} = \frac{\text{Ionic Charge}}{\text{Ionic Radius}} = \frac{z}{r}
$$

Higher charge density polarizes the ligand lone pairs more effectively, resulting in shorter, stronger, and more covalent coordinate bonds.
* **Effect of Oxidation State:** For the same metal, higher oxidation state produces higher stability:
  

$$
\text{Stability: } [\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6]^{3-} (\text{Fe}^{3+}) > [\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6]^{4-} (\text{Fe}^{2+})
$$

* **Effect of Ionic Radius:** Across a transition series, as radius decreases ($r \downarrow$), stability increases.
* **Irving-Williams Order of Stability:** For divalent high-spin ions of the first transition series:
  

$$
\boxed{\text{Ba}^{2+} < \text{Sr}^{2+} < \text{Ca}^{2+} < \text{Mg}^{2+} < \text{Mn}^{2+} < \text{Fe}^{2+} < \text{Co}^{2+} < \text{Ni}^{2+} < \mathbf{Cu^{2+}} > \mathbf{Zn^{2+}}}
$$

  *(Note: $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ ($d^9$) exhibits maximum stability due to Jahn-Teller stabilization energy over $\text{Ni}^{2+}$, whereas $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ ($d^{10}$) lacks LFSE and experiences a drop).*

#### Factor 2: Basic Nature and Polarizability of Ligands
* According to Lewis acid-base theory, ligands are Lewis bases. Stronger Lewis bases (higher $pK_a$ of conjugate acid) donate lone pairs more readily and form more stable complexes:
  

$$
\text{Stability: } \text{NH}_3 > \text{H}_2\text{O} > \text{F}^-
$$

* Strong field ligands that induce back-bonding ($\text{CN}^-$, $\text{CO}$) yield large values of $\Delta_o$, conferring substantial **Crystal Field Stabilization Energy (CFSE)**, which contributes favorably to $\Delta H^\circ$.

#### Factor 3: Chelate Effect (Thermodynamics of Ring Formation)
Complexes containing **polydentate (chelating) ligands** are significantly more stable than analogous complexes containing unidentate ligands. This is known as the **Chelate Effect**.

$$
\left[\text{Ni}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6\right]^{2+}_{(\text{aq})} + 3\,\text{en}_{(\text{aq})} \rightleftharpoons \left[\text{Ni}(\text{en})_3\right]^{2+}_{(\text{aq})} + 6\,\text{H}_2\text{O}_{(\text{l})} \qquad (\log \beta \approx 18.28)
$$

$$
\left[\text{Ni}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6\right]^{2+}_{(\text{aq})} + 6\,\text{NH}_{3(\text{aq})} \rightleftharpoons \left[\text{Ni}(\text{NH}_3)_6\right]^{2+}_{(\text{aq})} + 6\,\text{H}_2\text{O}_{(\text{l})} \qquad (\log \beta \approx 8.61)
$$

```
Reactant Species: 1 complex ion + 3 en molecules = 4 particles in solution
Product Species:  1 complex ion + 6 H2O molecules = 7 particles in solution
Net Change in Independent Particles: Delta n = 7 - 4 = +3 particles
```

**Thermodynamic Proof:**
* $\Delta n > 0 \implies$ Translational degrees of freedom and disorder increase drastically.
* $\Delta S^\circ_{\text{chelate}} \gg 0$ (large positive entropy change).
* Because $\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ$, a large positive $T\Delta S^\circ$ makes $\Delta G^\circ$ substantially more negative, drastically increasing $\beta$.
* **Ring Size Rule:** Five- and six-membered chelate rings exhibit minimal steric strain and possess the greatest thermodynamic stability.

#### Factor 4: Macrocyclic Effect
Polydentate ligands that are pre-organized into cyclic structures (e.g., porphyrin, crown ethers, phthalocyanines) form complexes that are even more stable than those with open-chain polydentate ligands. This is driven by favorable entropy and reduced reorganization enthalpy.

### 3.1 Analytical Chemistry

#### 1. Qualitative Inorganic Analysis (Salt Analysis):
* **Confirmatory Test for $\text{Fe}^{3+}$:** Formation of Prussian blue (iron(III) hexacyanoferrate(II)):
  

$$
\text{Fe}^{3+}_{(\text{aq})} + [\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6]^{4-}_{(\text{aq})} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Fe_4[Fe(CN)_6]_3 \cdot xH_2O} \downarrow \text{ (Intense Prussian Blue)}
$$

* **Confirmatory Test for $\text{Cu}^{2+}$:** Reaction with excess aqueous ammonia:
  

$$
\text{Cu}^{2+}_{(\text{aq})} + 4\text{NH}_{3(\text{aq})} \rightleftharpoons \mathbf{[Cu(NH_3)_4]^{2+}}_{(\text{aq})} \text{ (Deep Azure Blue)}
$$

* **Confirmatory Test for $\text{Ni}^{2+}$:** Precipitation with Dimethylglyoxime ($\text{DMG}$) in basic medium:
  

$$
\text{Ni}^{2+} + 2\,\text{DMGH} + 2\text{OH}^- \longrightarrow \mathbf{[Ni(dmg)_2]} \downarrow + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \text{ (Brilliant Rosy-Red Precipitate)}
$$

  *(Note: The bis(dimethylglyoximato)nickel(II) square planar complex is stabilized by intramolecular $\text{O}-\text{H}\cdots\text{O}$ hydrogen bonds forming two 5-membered and two 6-membered chelate rings).*
* **Separation of Group I Cations ($\text{AgCl}$ dissolution):**
  

$$
\text{AgCl}_{(\text{s})} + 2\text{NH}_{3(\text{aq})} \rightleftharpoons \mathbf{[Ag(NH_3)_2]^+}_{(\text{aq})} + \text{Cl}^-_{(\text{aq})} \text{ (Soluble Diamminesilver(I) Complex)}
$$

#### 2. Quantitative Volumetric Analysis:
* **Hardness of Water Estimation:** Hardness caused by $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ and $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ ions is estimated by complexometric titration with disodium salt of $\text{EDTA}$ ($\text{Na}_2\text{H}_2\text{EDTA}$) using Eriochrome Black-T ($\text{EBT}$) indicator at $\text{pH} \approx 10$ (buffered with $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + \text{NH}_4\text{OH}$):
  

$$
\text{M}^{2+} + [\text{H}_2\text{EDTA}]^{2-} \xrightleftharpoons{\text{pH } 10} \mathbf{[M(EDTA)]^{2-}} + 2\text{H}^+ \qquad (\text{M} = \text{Ca, Mg})
$$

  *(The $\text{Ca}-\text{EDTA}$ chelate complex has a higher formation constant than the $\text{Mg}-\text{EDTA}$ chelate).*

### 3.2 Metallurgy and Extraction of Metals

#### 1. Cyanide Leaching of Gold and Silver (MacArthur-Forrest Cyanide Process):
Extraction involves air oxidation followed by reductive precipitation with zinc dust:
* **Oxidative Leaching:**
  

$$
4\text{Au}_{(\text{s})} + 8\text{CN}^-_{(\text{aq})} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}_{(\text{l})} + \text{O}_{2(\text{g})} \longrightarrow 4\mathbf{[Au(CN)_2]^-}_{(\text{aq})} + 4\text{OH}^-_{(\text{aq})}
$$

  

$$
4\text{Ag}_{(\text{s})} + 8\text{CN}^-_{(\text{aq})} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}_{(\text{l})} + \text{O}_{2(\text{g})} \longrightarrow 4\mathbf{[Ag(CN)_2]^-}_{(\text{aq})} + 4\text{OH}^-_{(\text{aq})}
$$

* **Displacement / Reduction with Zinc:**
  

$$
2\mathbf{[Au(CN)_2]^-}_{(\text{aq})} + \text{Zn}_{(\text{s})} \longrightarrow \mathbf{[Zn(CN)_4]^{2-}}_{(\text{aq})} + 2\text{Au}_{(\text{s})} \downarrow
$$

  

$$
2\mathbf{[Ag(CN)_2]^-}_{(\text{aq})} + \text{Zn}_{(\text{s})} \longrightarrow \mathbf{[Zn(CN)_4]^{2-}}_{(\text{aq})} + 2\text{Ag}_{(\text{s})} \downarrow
$$

#### 2. Purification of Impure Nickel by Mond's Carbonyl Process:
Based on the principle of reversible formation and thermal decomposition of a volatile metal carbonyl:
* **Step 1 (Volatilization):** Heating impure nickel in a stream of carbon monoxide at $330 - 350\text{ K}$:
  

$$
\text{Ni}_{(\text{impure, s})} + 4\text{CO}_{(\text{g})} \xrightarrow{330 - 350\text{ K}} \mathbf{[Ni(CO)_4]_{(\text{g})}} \quad (\text{Volatile tetracarbonylnickel(0)})
$$

* **Step 2 (Thermal Decomposition):** Passing the vapor into a decomposition chamber heated to $450 - 470\text{ K}$:
  

$$
\mathbf{[Ni(CO)_4]_{(\text{g})}} \xrightarrow{450 - 470\text{ K}} \mathbf{Ni}_{(\text{pure, s})} \downarrow + 4\text{CO}_{(\text{g})}
$$

### 3.4 Industrial Homogeneous Catalysis

#### 1. Wilkinson’s Catalyst:
* **Chemical Formula:** $\mathbf{[RhCl(PPh_3)_3]}$ (Chloridotris(triphenylphosphine)rhodium(I))
* **Application:** Homogeneous hydrogenation of alkenes to alkanes under mild conditions:
  

$$
\text{R}-\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 + \text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{[\text{RhCl}(\text{PPh}_3)_3]} \text{R}-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_3

#### 2. Ziegler-Natta Polymerization:
* **Composition:** $\text{TiCl}_4 + \text{Al}(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5)_3$ (Titanium tetrachloride + Triethylaluminium)
* **Application:** Stereospecific polymerization of ethene into high-density polyethene (HDPE) under low pressures and temperatures.

#### 3. Wacker Process:
* **Catalyst:** $\text{PdCl}_2 / \text{CuCl}_2$
* **Application:** Industrial conversion of ethene to ethanal:
  

$$
\text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + \frac{1}{2}\text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{PdCl}_2 / \text{CuCl}_2} \text{CH}_3\text{CHO}
$$

### 3.5 Medicinal Applications & Chelation Therapy

#### 1. Platinum Complexes in Cancer Chemotherapy:
* **$\mathbf{cis}\text{-Platin}$:** $\mathbf{\textit{cis}\text{-[Pt(NH}_3\text{)}_2\text{Cl}_2\text{]}}$ ($\textit{cis}$-diamminedichloridoplatinum(II)).
  * **Mechanism:** Diffuses into cancer cells, hydrolyzes, and coordinates to the nitrogen atoms of guanine bases in $\text{DNA}$, creating intrastrand cross-links that inhibit $\text{DNA}$ replication and trigger apoptosis.
  * **Critical Distinction:** $\mathbf{\textit{trans}\text{-Platin}}$ is **biologically inactive** as an antitumor agent because its geometry prevents correct cross-linking with adjacent purine bases.

#### 2. Chelation Therapy for Toxic Heavy Metals:
* **Lead ($\text{Pb}^{2+}$) Poisoning:** Treated with **$\text{CaNa}_2\text{EDTA}$** (Calcium disodium ethylenediaminetetraacetate).
  * *Why not pure $\text{Na}_4\text{EDTA}$?* Free $\text{EDTA}^{4-}$ would chelate and strip essential $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ from blood serum, causing hypocalcemic tetany and death. $\text{CaNa}_2\text{EDTA}$ exchanges $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ safely for $\text{Pb}^{2+}$ because $\beta(\text{Pb-EDTA}) \gg \beta(\text{Ca-EDTA})$:
  

$$
\mathbf{[Ca(EDTA)]^{2-}} + \text{Pb}^{2+}_{(\text{toxic, in body})} \longrightarrow \mathbf{[Pb(EDTA)]^{2-}}_{(\text{excreted via urine})} + \text{Ca}^{2+}
$$

* **Copper Overload (Wilson's Disease):** Treated with chelating agents like **D-penicillamine** or **trientine**.
* **Iron Overload (Hemochromatosis / Post-transfusion):** Treated using **Desferrioxamine B**, which forms a hexadentate octahedral complex with $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ excreted via renal pathways.

### Question 1 [CBSE Board 2024, 3 Marks]
**(a)** Draw a diagram showing the synergic bonding mechanism in metal carbonyls.  
**(b)** Out of $\left[\text{Fe}(\text{CO})_5\right]$ and $\left[\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6\right]^{4-}$, which complex has a stronger carbon-to-metal $\pi$-bond? Explain.  
**(c)** What is the geometry and magnetic behavior of $\left[\text{Ni}(\text{CO})_4\right]$?

#### Comprehensive Step-by-Step Marking Solution:
**(a)** Diagrammatic representation of synergic bonding:
* Show $\sigma$-donation: Metal $\leftarrow$ Carbon lone pair via axial overlap ($\sigma_{\text{M}\leftarrow\text{C}}$).
* Show $\pi$-backdonation: Metal filled $d$-orbital $\to$ Carbon empty $\pi^*$ orbital via lateral overlap ($\pi_{\text{M}\to\text{C}}$).

$$
\text{M}(\text{empty } d_\sigma) \xleftarrow{\sigma \text{ (dative)}} \text{C}\equiv\text{O} \qquad\text{and}\qquad \text{M}(\text{filled } d_\pi) \xrightarrow{\pi \text{ (back-bond)}} \pi^*_{\text{CO}}
$$

**(b)** The metal ion in $\left[\text{Fe}(\text{CO})_5\right]$ has oxidation state **$0$**, whereas in $\left[\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6\right]^{4-}$ it has oxidation state **$+2$**.
* The $\text{CO}$ ligand has significantly lower-lying vacant $\pi^*$ antibonding orbitals than $\text{CN}^-$, making it a stronger $\pi$-acceptor.
* Moreover, in $\left[\text{Fe}(\text{CO})_5\right]$, the neutral iron atom ($\text{Fe}^0, 3d^8$) retains higher polarizable $d$-electron density than $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ ($3d^6$), facilitating stronger $d_\pi \to \pi^*$ back-donation into the $\text{CO}$ ligand.
* Therefore, the $\mathbf{\text{M}-\text{C}}$ $\pi$-bond is stronger in $\mathbf{[Fe(CO)_5]}$.

**(c)** In $\left[\text{Ni}(\text{CO})_4\right]$:
* Ground state $\text{Ni}^0 = [Ar]\,3d^8\,4s^2$.
* Carbon monoxide is a strong field ligand. The two $4s$ electrons pair up into the $3d$ subshell: $\text{Ni}^0 \to [Ar]\,3d^{10}\,4s^0\,4p^0$.
* The empty $4s$ and three $4p$ orbitals hybridize to form four **$sp^3$** hybrid orbitals.
* **Geometry:** **Tetrahedral**.
* **Magnetic Behavior:** All electrons are paired ($n = 0 \implies \mu_s = 0\text{ BM}$); it is **Diamagnetic**.

### Question 2 [CBSE Board 2023, 2 Marks]
**(a)** Write the IUPAC name of the complex used in cancer chemotherapy.  
**(b)** What is the chelate effect? Why is a chelated complex generally more stable than an unchelated complex having identical donor atoms?

#### Comprehensive Step-by-Step Marking Solution:
**(a)** The complex used in cancer chemotherapy is $\textit{cis}\text{-[Pt(NH}_3\text{)}_2\text{Cl}_2\text{]}$.  
**IUPAC Name:** **$\textit{cis}$-diamminedichloridoplatinum(II)**.  
*(Examiner Alert: Must include the oxidation state `(II)` in Roman numerals and alphabetical order `ammine` before `chlorido`).*

**(b)** **Definition:** When a di- or polydentate ligand binds to a central metal ion to form a closed cyclic ring structure, the phenomenon is known as chelation, and the resulting complex is termed a chelate. The enhanced thermodynamic stability of such a chelated complex compared to a similar complex containing unidentate ligands is known as the **Chelate Effect**.

**Physical Reason:**
During chelation, the coordination of one donor atom brings subsequent donor atoms into close spatial proximity with the metal ion, increasing the probability of coordinate bond formation. Thermodynamically:

$$
\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ
$$

The displacement of several unidentate molecules by fewer polydentate ligand molecules results in a net increase in the total number of free particles in solution. This causes a **large positive increase in entropy ($\Delta S^\circ > 0$)**, making $\Delta G^\circ$ more negative and maximizing the overall stability constant $\beta$.

### Question 3 [CBSE Board 2022 Term-II, 3 Marks]
**(a)** Calculate the overall stability constant ($\beta_4$) of a complex $[\text{Cu}(\text{NH}_3)_4]^{2+}$ if the stepwise stability constants are:

$$
\log_{10} K_1 = 4.0, \quad \log_{10} K_2 = 3.2, \quad \log_{10} K_3 = 2.7, \quad \log_{10} K_4 = 2.0
$$

**(b)** State the reason for the progressive decrease in values from $K_1$ to $K_4$.  
**(c)** What is the instability constant ($K_{\text{inst}}$) of this complex?

#### Comprehensive Step-by-Step Marking Solution:
**(a)** The relationship between overall stability constant and stepwise stability constants is:

$$
\beta_4 = K_1 \cdot K_2 \cdot K_3 \cdot K_4
$$

Taking $\log_{10}$ on both sides:

$$
\log_{10} \beta_4 = \log_{10} K_1 + \log_{10} K_2 + \log_{10} K_3 + \log_{10} K_4
$$

$$
\log_{10} \beta_4 = 4.0 + 3.2 + 2.7 + 2.0 = 11.9
$$

Taking the antilogarithm:

$$
\beta_4 = 10^{11.9} = 10^{0.9} \times 10^{11} \approx \mathbf{7.94 \times 10^{11}}
$$

**(b)** The progressive decrease in stepwise stability constants ($K_1 > K_2 > K_3 > K_4$) is due to:
1. **Statistical Factors:** As more coordination sites are occupied, fewer open coordination positions remain available for an incoming $\text{NH}_3$ molecule, while the probability of dissociation increases.
2. **Steric Hindrance:** Successive accommodation of bulky $\text{NH}_3$ molecules around $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ increases mutual spatial repulsion.

**(c)** The instability constant ($K_{\text{inst}}$ or $K_d$) is the reciprocal of the overall stability constant ($\beta_4$):

$$
K_{\text{inst}} = \frac{1}{\beta_4} = \frac{1}{7.94 \times 10^{11}} = 10^{-11.9} = 10^{0.1} \times 10^{-12} \approx \mathbf{1.26 \times 10^{-12}}
$$

### Question 4 [CBSE Board 2021, 2 Marks]
Explain the chemical principles involved in:
**(a)** Mond's process for refining nickel.  
**(b)** Estimation of hardness of water using $\text{EDTA}$.

#### Comprehensive Step-by-Step Marking Solution:
**(a)** **Principle of Mond's Process:**  
Mond's process is based on the formation of a volatile coordination complex with a low boiling point at lower temperatures, followed by its thermal decomposition at elevated temperatures to yield pure metal:
1. Impure nickel reacts with carbon monoxide at $330 - 350\text{ K}$ to yield volatile tetracarbonylnickel(0):
   

$$
\text{Ni}_{(\text{impure})} + 4\text{CO} \xrightarrow{330 - 350\text{ K}} [\text{Ni}(\text{CO})_4]_{(\text{g})}
$$

2. The vapors are decomposed by heating to $450 - 470\text{ K}$, leaving pure nickel:
   

$$
[\text{Ni}(\text{CO})_4]_{(\text{g})} \xrightarrow{450 - 470\text{ K}} \text{Ni}_{(\text{pure})} + 4\text{CO}_{(\text{g})}
$$

**(b)** **Principle of Water Hardness Estimation:**  
$\text{Ca}^{2+}$ and $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ ions form stable, hexadentate chelate complexes with the ethylenediaminetetraacetate ion ($[\text{EDTA}]^{4-}$).  
* When Eriochrome Black-T ($\text{EBT}$) is added to hard water at $\text{pH} \approx 10$, it forms weak, wine-red complexes with $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ and $\text{Mg}^{2+}$.  
* Upon titration with standard $\text{Na}_2\text{H}_2\text{EDTA}$, the $[\text{EDTA}]^{4-}$ ligand displaces the indicator from the metal ions because the metal-EDTA chelate is thermodynamically more stable:
  

$$
\text{M-EBT complex (Wine Red)} + \text{EDTA}^{4-} \longrightarrow \text{[M(EDTA)]}^{2-} + \text{Free EBT (Steel Blue)}
$$

* The end-point is signaled by a sharp color transition from **wine-red to steel blue**.

### Question 5 [CBSE Board 2020, 3 Marks]
**(a)** Arrange the following complexes in increasing order of their $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond length:

$$
\left[\text{Mn}(\text{CO})_6\right]^{+}, \quad \left[\text{V}(\text{CO})_6\right]^{-}, \quad \left[\text{Cr}(\text{CO})_6\right]
$$

Justify your answer on the basis of synergic bonding.  
**(b)** What is the role of coordination compounds in the extraction of gold? Write the equations involved.

#### Comprehensive Step-by-Step Marking Solution:
**(a)** **Increasing Order of $\text{C}-\text{O}$ Bond Length:**

$$
\mathbf{[\text{Mn}(\text{CO})_6]^+ < [\text{Cr}(\text{CO})_6] < [\text{V}(\text{CO})_6]^-}
$$

**Justification:**
* All three are octahedral, isoelectronic species ($d^6$).
* The central metal ions have oxidation states: $\text{V} (-1)$, $\text{Cr} (0)$, and $\text{Mn} (+1)$.
* As the negative charge on the metal center increases (from $+1$ to $-1$), the electron density on the metal increases:
  

$$
\text{V}^{(-1)} > \text{Cr}^{(0)} > \text{Mn}^{(+1)}
$$

* Higher electron density enhances $d_\pi \to \pi^*$ back-donation into the antibonding molecular orbital of $\text{CO}$.
* Greater occupation of the $\pi^*$ antibonding orbital of $\text{CO}$ decreases the $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond order, leading to elongation of the $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond length. Hence, $[\text{V}(\text{CO})_6]^{-}$ has the longest $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond and $[\text{Mn}(\text{CO})_6]^{+}$ has the shortest.

**(b)** **Role in Gold Extraction:**  
Coordination compounds enable the selective dissolution and recovery of metallic gold from its low-grade ores using a cyanide-leaching process:
1. **Oxidative Complexation (Leaching):** Atmospheric oxygen oxidizes native gold in the presence of $\text{CN}^-$ to form a soluble, linear dicyanidoaurate(I) coordination complex:
   

$$
4\text{Au}_{(\text{s})} + 8\text{CN}^-_{(\text{aq})} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}_{(\text{l})} + \text{O}_{2(\text{g})} \longrightarrow 4[\text{Au}(\text{CN})_2]^-_{(\text{aq})} + 4\text{OH}^-_{(\text{aq})}
$$

2. **Reductive Precipitation (Recovery):** The soluble complex is treated with electropositive zinc dust, which displaces gold by forming a stable tetracyanidozincate(II) chelate:
   

$$
2[\text{Au}(\text{CN})_2]^-_{(\text{aq})} + \text{Zn}_{(\text{s})} \longrightarrow [\text{Zn}(\text{CN})_4]^{2-}_{(\text{aq})} + 2\text{Au}_{(\text{s})} \downarrow
$$

### 1. The Synergic See-Saw Rule (Mental Anchor)
Whenever evaluating metal carbonyls, visualize a see-saw between the $\text{M}-\text{C}$ bond and the $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond:

$$
\boxed{\uparrow \pi\text{-Backdonation} \implies \uparrow \text{Bond Order}(\text{M}-\text{C}) \implies \downarrow \text{Length}(\text{M}-\text{C}) \implies \uparrow \tilde{\nu}(\text{M}-\text{C})}
$$

$$
\boxed{\downarrow \text{Bond Order}(\text{C}-\text{O}) \implies \uparrow \text{Length}(\text{C}-\text{O}) \implies \downarrow \tilde{\nu}(\text{C}-\text{O})}
$$

* **More negative charge on metal** $\implies$ Maximum backdonation $\implies$ **Lowest $\tilde{\nu}_{\text{C-O}}$**, **Longest $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond**.
* **More positive charge on metal** $\implies$ Minimum backdonation $\implies$ **Highest $\tilde{\nu}_{\text{C-O}}$**, **Shortest $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond**.

### 3. Stability Constant Conversion Formula
To quickly calculate $\beta_n$ or $K_d$ during calculations:

$$
\log_{10} \beta_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \log_{10} K_i
$$

$$
K_d = 10^{-\log_{10} \beta_n}
$$

* *Example:* If $\log_{10} \beta_4 = 12$, then:
  

$$
\beta_4 = 1.0 \times 10^{12}, \qquad K_d = 1.0 \times 10^{-12}
$$

### 4. Ring Size Rule for Chelates
* **Ligands with NO resonance (e.g., en, EDTA):** **5-membered rings** are the most stable.
* **Ligands with conjugated $\pi$-systems (e.g., acetylacetonate $\text{acac}^-$):** **6-membered rings** achieve superior stability due to extended aromatic-like delocalization.
