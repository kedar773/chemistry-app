***

## 1. Pedagogical Theory & Fundamental Concepts

### 1.1 Definition and General Introduction (⭐⭐⭐)
Amines are organic nitrogenous bases regarded as alkyl or aryl derivatives of ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$), formed by the progressive replacement of one, two, or all three hydrogen atoms with hydrocarbon groups (alkyl or aryl radicals).

$$
\text{NH}_3 \xrightarrow{-\text{H},\, +\text{R}} \text{R}-\text{NH}_2 \xrightarrow{-\text{H},\, +\text{R}} \text{R}_2\text{NH} \xrightarrow{-\text{H},\, +\text{R}} \text{R}_3\text{N}
$$

They occur widely in biological macromolecules, vitamins, alkaloids (such as quinine, nicotine, morphine), hormones (adrenaline, ephedrine), and synthetic polymers (e.g., Nylon-6,6).

***

### 1.2 Electronic Structure and Geometry of the Amine Nitrogen (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

#### Hybridization & Orbital Geometry
- The valence shell electronic configuration of nitrogen in ground state is:
  

$$
\text{N (ground state)}: [\text{He}]\, 2s^2\, 2p_x^1\, 2p_y^1\, 2p_z^1
$$

- In amines, the central nitrogen atom undergoes **$\text{sp}^3$ hybridization**.
- The four $\text{sp}^3$ hybrid orbitals are directed toward the vertices of a regular tetrahedron.
  - Three $\text{sp}^3$ orbitals overlap axially with orbitals of carbon (alkyl/aryl groups) or hydrogen ($\sigma$-bonds).
  - The fourth $\text{sp}^3$ orbital houses a **non-bonding electron pair (lone pair)**.

```
          ..  <-- Lone pair in sp³ orbital
          : N
        /  |  \
       /   |   \
      R    H    H  (Trigonal Pyramidal geometry)
```

#### Molecular Geometry and Bond Angle Deviation
- **Molecular Geometry:** **Trigonal Pyramidal** (molecular shape accounts only for nuclear positions, ignoring the lone pair).
- **Ideal Tetrahedral Angle:** $109^\circ 28'$ ($109.5^\circ$).
- **Observed Bond Angle in Trimethylamine ($(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{N}$):** Approximately **$108^\circ$** (NCERT Standard: $108^\circ$).
- **Observed Bond Angle in Ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$):** Approximately **$107^\circ$**.

$$
\text{Repulsion Hierarchy (VSEPR Theory):} \quad \text{LP}-\text{BP} > \text{BP}-\text{BP}
$$

#### Driving Forces for the Angle Deviation:
1. **$\text{LP}-\text{BP}$ Repulsion:** The unshared pair on nitrogen exerts greater repulsive force on the bonding electron pairs than bonding pairs exert upon one another, compressing the $\text{C}-\text{N}-\text{H}$ or $\text{H}-\text{N}-\text{H}$ angles below $109.5^\circ$.
2. **Steric Crowding (Alkyl Repulsion):** In trimethylamine ($(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{N}$), bulky methyl groups repel one another electrostatically and sterically, counteracting lone-pair compression and opening the angle back up from $107^\circ$ to nearly $108^\circ$.

$$
\boxed{\text{Geometry: Trigonal Pyramidal} \quad \big| \quad \text{Hybridization: } \text{sp}^3 \quad \big| \quad \angle \text{C}-\text{N}-\text{C (in Me}_3\text{N)} \approx 108^\circ}
$$

***

### 1.3 Amine Inversion (Walden / Umbrella Inversion) (⭐⭐⭐⭐)

Trivalent nitrogen compounds with three distinct substituents ($\text{N}R^1 R^2 R^3$) are nonsuperimposable on their mirror images, possessing a stereogenic nitrogen centre. However, acyclic chiral amines **cannot be resolved into optically active enantiomers at room temperature**.

```
    R¹              R¹
     \               /
    ..N ----> [  =N=  ] ----> N..
     / \        / | \        / \
   R²   R³     R² H  R³    R³   R²
 (sp³ Pyramidal)  (sp² Planar TS)  (sp³ Inverted)
```

1. The molecule undergoes rapid intramolecular inversion analogous to an umbrella turning inside out in a gale.
2. During inversion, the nitrogen atom transitions through a planar transition state with **$\text{sp}^2$ hybridization**, where the lone pair resides temporarily in an unhybridized pure $p$ orbital:
   

$$
\text{Ground State } (\text{sp}^3 \text{ pyramidal}) \rightleftharpoons [\text{Transition State } (\text{sp}^2 \text{ planar})]^\ddagger \rightleftharpoons \text{Inverted State } (\text{sp}^3 \text{ pyramidal})
$$

3. **Activation Energy ($\Delta G^\ddagger$):** Very low (roughly $25 \text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$ or $\approx 6 \text{ kcal}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$).
4. Because ambient thermal energy ($RT$) at room temperature ($298\text{ K} \implies RT \approx 2.48\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$) easily surmounts this low activation barrier, interconversion occurs billions of times per second ($10^{10} - 10^{11} \text{ s}^{-1}$), yielding an equimolar, non-separable **racemic mixture**.

> [!NOTE]
> Quaternary ammonium salts of the type $[\text{N}R^1 R^2 R^3 R^4]^+\text{X}^-$ lack an unshared lone pair to mediate inversion. Thus, chiral quaternary ammonium salts **can** be resolved into stable enantiomers.

## 2. Classification of Amines (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

### 2.1 Classification Based on Degree of Substitution at Nitrogen
Unlike alkyl halides or alcohols (where classification is governed by the degree of the *carbon* atom bearing the functional group), amines are classified strictly according to the **number of carbon groups attached directly to the nitrogen atom**.

| Class | Functional Group | General Formula | Characteristic NCERT Name |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary ($1^\circ$)** | Amino group ($-\text{NH}_2$) | $\text{R}-\text{NH}_2$ | Primary amine |
| **Secondary ($2^\circ$)** | Imino group ($>\text{NH}$) | $\text{R}-\text{NH}-\text{R}'$ | Secondary amine |
| **Tertiary ($3^\circ$)** | Tertiary nitrogen (${\ni}\text{N}$) | $\text{R}-\text{N}(\text{R}')-\text{R}''$ | Tertiary amine |
| **Quaternary ($4^\circ$)** | Quaternary ammonium ion | $[\text{R}_4\text{N}]^+\text{X}^-$ | Quaternary ammonium salt |

```
Primary (1°):      Secondary (2°):      Tertiary (3°):
     H                   R'                   R'
     |                   |                    |
 R - N - H           R - N - H            R - N - R''
```

***

### 2.2 Classification Based on Nature of Hydrocarbon Residue

#### Aliphatic Amines
All hydrocarbon groups directly linked to nitrogen are alkyl groups (open-chain or cycloalkyl).  
*Examples:*

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{NH}_2 \quad (\text{Ethylamine}); \quad (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{NH} \quad (\text{Dimethylamine})
$$

#### Aromatic Amines
Subdivided into two distinct structural classes:
1. **Aryl Amines (Aromatic Amines Proper):** The nitrogen atom is directly bonded to one or more aromatic rings (benzene rings).
   *Example:* $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2$ (Aniline / Benzenamine).
2. **Arylalkyl Amines (Side-Chain Substituted):** Nitrogen is attached to an alkyl carbon that in turn bears an aromatic ring.
   *Example:* $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2$ (Benzylamine / Phenylmethanamine).
   *(Note: Benzylamine behaves chemically like a primary aliphatic amine, not an arylamine).*

#### Simple (Symmetrical) vs. Mixed (Unsymmetrical) Amines
- **Simple ($2^\circ$ or $3^\circ$):** All attached hydrocarbon groups are identical.
  - Symmetrical $2^\circ$: Diethylamine, $\text{C}_2\text{H}_5-\text{NH}-\text{C}_2\text{H}_5$
  - Symmetrical $3^\circ$: Trimethylamine, $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{N}$
- **Mixed ($2^\circ$ or $3^\circ$):** Attached groups differ from one another.
  - Unsymmetrical $2^\circ$: Ethylmethanamine, $\text{CH}_3-\text{NH}-\text{C}_2\text{H}_5$
  - Unsymmetrical $3^\circ$: Ethyldimethylamine, $\text{CH}_3-\text{N}(\text{CH}_3)-\text{C}_2\text{H}_5$

## 3. Comprehensive IUPAC Nomenclature (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

### 3.1 Systematic IUPAC Rules

#### 1. Primary Amines ($\text{R}-\text{NH}_2$)
- Identify the longest continuous chain of carbon atoms containing the carbon bonded to the $-\text{NH}_2$ group.
- Replace the terminal **"-e"** of the parent alkane name with the suffix **"-amine"**:
  

$$
\text{Alkane} - \text{e} + \text{amine} = \mathbf{\text{Alkanamine}}
$$

- Number the principal carbon chain from the end that gives the carbon carrying the $-\text{NH}_2$ group the lowest locant number.
- For polyamines containing multiple amino groups, retain the terminal "-e" of the alkane name and append prefixes like *diamine*, *triamine* along with their locants (e.g., $\text{Ethane-1,2-diamine}$).

#### 2. Secondary Amines ($\text{R}-\text{NH}-\text{R}'$)
- Choose the longest alkyl group as the parent alkane.
- The smaller alkyl group attached to nitrogen is designated as an **$N$-alkyl** substituent.
  

$$
\text{Prefix Formula:} \quad \mathbf{N\text{-Alkylalkanamine}}
$$

#### 3. Tertiary Amines ($\text{R}-\text{N}(\text{R}')-\text{R}''$)
- Select the largest alkyl group as the parent chain.
- The other two alkyl groups are treated as substituents and given the prefix **$N,N$-dialkyl** (or $N\text{-alkyl-}N\text{-alkyl}'$ alphabetically if different).
  

$$
\text{Prefix Formula:} \quad \mathbf{N,N\text{-Dialkylalkanamine}}
$$

## 4. Isomerism in Amines (⭐⭐⭐⭐)

Amines exhibit four major types of structural isomerism:

### 4.1 Chain Isomerism
Amines having the same molecular formula and same functional group differ in the carbon skeleton:
- **Formula:** $\text{C}_4\text{H}_{11}\text{N}$
  - $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2$ (Butan-1-amine; unbranched chain)
  - $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}(\text{CH}_3)\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2$ (2-Methylpropan-1-amine; branched chain)

### 4.2 Position Isomerism
Differ in the position of the amino group on the same carbon skeleton:
- **Formula:** $\text{C}_3\text{H}_9\text{N}$
  - $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2$ (Propan-1-amine)
  - $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}(\text{NH}_2)\text{CH}_3$ (Propan-2-amine)

### 4.3 Functional Group Isomerism
Primary, secondary, and tertiary amines having the same molecular formula are **functional isomers** of one another because they possess different functional groups ($-\text{NH}_2$, $>\text{NH}$, and ${\ni}\text{N}$):
- **Formula:** $\text{C}_3\text{H}_9\text{N}$
  - Primary: $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2$ (Propan-1-amine)
  - Secondary: $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{NH}\text{CH}_3$ ($N$-Methylethanamine)
  - Tertiary: $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{N}$ ($N,N$-Dimethylmethanamine)

### 4.4 Metamerism
Secondary and tertiary amines with the same functional group exhibit metamerism due to unequal distribution of alkyl carbons on either side of the nitrogen atom:
- **Formula:** $\text{C}_4\text{H}_{11}\text{N}$ ($2^\circ$ amines)
  - $\text{CH}_3-\text{NH}-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3$ ($N$-Methylpropan-1-amine)
  - $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2-\text{NH}-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3$ ($N$-Ethylethanamine)

## 5. Examiner Traps & Common Board Hallucinations

> [!WARNING]
> ### 1. The Carbon-Degree Trap ($1^\circ$ vs $2^\circ$ vs $3^\circ$)
> - **In Alcohols/Alkyl Halides:** $(CH_3)_3C-OH$ is a **$3^\circ$ alcohol** because the $-OH$ is linked to a tertiary carbon.
> - **In Amines:** $(CH_3)_3C-NH_2$ (tert-butylamine) is a **$1^\circ$ amine**, NOT a $3^\circ$ amine, because the nitrogen atom is bonded to **only one carbon** and retains two protons ($-\text{NH}_2$).
> - *Examiner Deduction Rule:* Always count the number of C atoms attached directly to the **Nitrogen**, not the nature of the carbon attached!

> [!CAUTION]
> ### 2. The Dropped "e" Rule in Polyamines
> - When naming monoamines: drop the final **"-e"** of alkane: $\text{Propane} \to \text{Propan-1-amine}$.
> - When naming polyamines: **RETAIN** the final **"-e"** of alkane:
>   - Correct: **$\text{Ethane-1,2-diamine}$**
>   - Incorrect: *$\text{Ethan-1,2-diamine}$* (Marks lost in CBSE board moderation!).

> [!NOTE]
> ### 3. Benzylamine vs Aniline Classification
> - $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2$ (Aniline) is an **aryl amine** (lone pair delocalized by resonance into the $\pi$-system of the benzene ring).
> - $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2$ (Benzylamine) is an **aromatic-aliphatic amine** (arylalkyl amine). The $-\text{NH}_2$ group is attached to an $\text{sp}^3$ carbon; its lone pair is **not** delocalized into the ring. It is more basic than aniline and yields an unstable aliphatic diazonium salt at $0-5^\circ\text{C}$.

## 6. High-Yield Worked Examples & Previous Years Board Questions (PYQs 2020–2025)

### Example 1: Total Structural Isomers Calculation
**Problem:** Write down the structures and IUPAC names of all eight structural isomeric amines corresponding to the molecular formula $\text{C}_4\text{H}_{11}\text{N}$. Categorize them into $1^\circ$, $2^\circ$, and $3^\circ$ amines.  
*[CBSE Board 2020, 2023 3-Marks]*

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
Calculate Degree of Unsaturation (Double Bond Equivalent, DBE):

$$
\text{DBE} = C + 1 - \frac{H - N}{2} = 4 + 1 - \frac{11 - 1}{2} = 5 - 5 = 0 \quad (\text{Fully saturated})
$$

#### Four Primary ($1^\circ$) Amines:
1. $\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\text{NH}_2$  
   **IUPAC:** Butan-1-amine
2. $\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}(\text{NH}_2)-\text{CH}_3$  
   **IUPAC:** Butan-2-amine
3. $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CH}-\text{CH}_2-\text{NH}_2$  
   **IUPAC:** 2-Methylpropan-1-amine
4. $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}-\text{NH}_2$  
   **IUPAC:** 2-Methylpropan-2-amine

#### Three Secondary ($2^\circ$) Amines:
5. $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2-\text{NH}-\text{CH}_3$  
   **IUPAC:** $N$-Methylpropan-1-amine
6. $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CH}-\text{NH}-\text{CH}_3$  
   **IUPAC:** $N$-Methylpropan-2-amine
7. $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2-\text{NH}-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3$  
   **IUPAC:** $N$-Ethylethanamine

#### One Tertiary ($3^\circ$) Amine:
8. $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2-\text{N}(\text{CH}_3)_2$  
   **IUPAC:** $N,N$-Dimethylethanamine

$$
\boxed{\text{Total Isomers: } 8 \quad \big(4 \times 1^\circ, \; 3 \times 2^\circ, \; 1 \times 3^\circ\big)}
$$

### Example 2: CBSE 2024 Actual Board Question
**Problem:** Give the correct IUPAC nomenclature for the following compounds:
1. $\text{H}_2\text{N}-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2$
2. $(\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2)_2\text{N}-\text{CH}_3$
3. $p\text{-Toluidine}$  
*[CBSE 2024, 3M]*

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. **$\text{H}_2\text{N}-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2$**
   - The principal chain has 3 carbon atoms containing a double bond and an amine group.
   - Priority order for numbering: Principal Functional Group ($-\text{NH}_2$) > Double bond ($=$).
   - Numbering: $\overset{3}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2 = \overset{2}{\text{C}}\text{H} - \overset{1}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2-\text{NH}_2$.
   - **IUPAC Name:** **Prop-2-en-1-amine**

2. **$(\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2)_2\text{N}-\text{CH}_3$**
   - Longest chain attached to nitrogen: 2 carbons (Ethanamine).
   - Substituents on nitrogen: One ethyl group and one methyl group.
   - Alphabetical arrangement of prefixes: *Ethyl* precedes *Methyl*.
   - **IUPAC Name:** **$N$-Ethyl-$N$-methylethanamine**

3. **$p$-Toluidine**
   - Structure: A benzene ring with $-\text{NH}_2$ at position 1 and $-\text{CH}_3$ at position 4.
   - Parent base: Benzenamine (Aniline).
   - **IUPAC Name:** **4-Methylbenzenamine** (or 4-Methylaniline)

***

### Example 3: CBSE 2022 Term-II Board Problem
**Problem:** Write the IUPAC name of the compound given below:

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}(\text{CH}_3)-\text{NH}-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_3
$$

Identify the class ($1^\circ, 2^\circ, 3^\circ$) to which it belongs.  
*[CBSE 2022, 2M]*

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. Determine the longest carbon chain linked to nitrogen:
   - Chain 1: $-\text{CH}(\text{CH}_3)_2 \implies$ 3 carbon chain (Propane derivative).
   - Chain 2: $-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3 \implies$ 2 carbon chain (Ethyl group).
2. The longer chain is the parent: **Propan-2-amine**.
3. The substituent on nitrogen is an ethyl group: **$N$-Ethyl**.
4. **IUPAC Name:** **$N$-Ethylpropan-2-amine**
5. **Class:** It contains the $>\text{NH}$ group attached directly to two carbon atoms; hence, it is a **Secondary ($2^\circ$) amine**.

## 7. Speed Hacks & Memory Mnemonics

```
                       AMINE QUICK-AUDIT CHECKLIST
                                  │
          ┌───────────────────────┴───────────────────────┐
   Degree by Nitrogen                              Parent Chain
  (Count bonded C atoms)                      (Pick longest chain)
   • 1 Carbon  → 1°                            • Name as "alkanamine"
   • 2 Carbons → 2°                            • Other chains → "N-alkyl"
   • 3 Carbons → 3°
```

### 1. The "N-Count" Rule
> Never determine amine classification by looking at the $\alpha$-carbon.  
> **Count the Carbons directly bonded to Nitrogen:**  
> - $1\text{ C-N bond} \implies 1^\circ$  
> - $2\text{ C-N bonds} \implies 2^\circ$  
> - $3\text{ C-N bonds} \implies 3^\circ$

### 2. Suffix Mnemonic: "Single drops 'e', Double keeps 'e'"
- Single $-\text{NH}_2$ group: $\text{Drop the '-e'}$ $\to$ **$\text{Alkanamine}$**  
- Multiple $-\text{NH}_2$ groups (diamines, triamines): $\text{Keep the '-e'}$ $\to$ **$\text{Alkane-X,Y-diamine}$**

### 3. Rapid Isomer Count Formula for Saturated Aliphatic Amines ($\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+3}\text{N}$)
For quick checks in objective/competitive questions:
- $\text{C}_2\text{H}_7\text{N} \implies \mathbf{2}$ isomers ($1 \times 1^\circ, 1 \times 2^\circ$)
- $\text{C}_3\text{H}_9\text{N} \implies \mathbf{4}$ isomers ($2 \times 1^\circ, 1 \times 2^\circ, 1 \times 3^\circ$)
- $\text{C}_4\text{H}_{11}\text{N} \implies \mathbf{8}$ isomers ($4 \times 1^\circ, 3 \times 2^\circ, 1 \times 3^\circ$)

## 2. In-Depth Chemical Transformations & Reaction Mechanisms

***

### Method A: Reduction Processes

#### 1. Reduction of Nitro Compounds ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Nitroalkanes and nitroarenes are reduced to their corresponding primary amines using catalytic hydrogenation or active metal-acid reduction.

##### Catalytic Hydrogenation

$$
\text{Ar-NO}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \xrightarrow[\text{or Pt, Ni in Ethanol}]{\text{Pd / C, } 298\text{ K}} \text{Ar-NH}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

##### Chemical Reduction (Metal + Acid)

$$
\text{Ar-NO}_2 + 6[\text{H}] \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{Fe} + \text{conc. HCl}} \text{Ar-NH}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

$$
\text{Ar-NO}_2 + 6[\text{H}] \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{Sn} + \text{conc. HCl}} \text{Ar-NH}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

> **Crucial NCERT Key Point (Board Examiner Favorite):**
> **Why is reduction with $\text{Fe} + \text{HCl}$ preferred over $\text{Sn} + \text{HCl}$?**
> 

$$
\text{FeCl}_2 \text{ formed during the reaction undergoes hydrolysis to regenerate } \text{HCl}:
$$

> 

$$
> \text{FeCl}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{Fe(OH)}_2 \downarrow + 2\text{HCl}
>
$$

> Hence, only a **small initial aliquot of hydrochloric acid** is required to initiate and sustain the catalytic cycle, making the process cost-efficient and environmentally favorable.

***

#### 2. Reduction of Nitriles (Cyanides) & Isonitriles (Isocyanides) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

##### A. Reduction of Nitriles (Mendius Reaction)
Nitriles yield primary amines containing **one more carbon atom** than the parent haloalkane from which the nitrile was derived.

* **Reagents:** 
  1. Catalytic: $\text{H}_2 / \text{Ni}$ or $\text{H}_2 / \text{Pd-C}$
  2. Chemical: Sodium amalgam and ethanol ($\text{Na(Hg)} / \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$)
  3. Hydride Donor: $\text{LiAlH}_4$ in anhydrous ether, followed by acidic workup ($\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$)

$$
\text{R-C}\equiv\text{N} + 4[\text{H}] \xrightarrow{\text{LiAlH}_4 \text{ / ether or } \text{H}_2/\text{Ni}} \text{R-CH}_2\text{-NH}_2
$$

* **Ascent of Series Principle:**
  Haloalkane $\xrightarrow{\text{alc. KCN}} \text{R-CN} \xrightarrow{\text{reduction}} \text{R-CH}_2\text{-NH}_2$ adds exactly one $-\text{CH}_2-$ unit to the parent carbon skeleton.

##### B. Reduction of Isonitriles
Reduction of isonitriles (carbylamines) gives **secondary amines** containing a methyl substituent on the nitrogen:

$$
\text{R-N}\equiv\overset{\ominus}{\text{C}} + 4[\text{H}] \xrightarrow{\text{LiAlH}_4 \text{ or } \text{H}_2/\text{Ni}} \text{R-NH-CH}_3
$$

***

#### 3. Reduction of Amides ⭐⭐⭐
Amides undergo reductive deoxygenation on treatment with Lithium Aluminium Hydride ($\text{LiAlH}_4$), retaining the exact original carbon count.

$$
\text{R-CONH}_2 \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) } \text{H}_2\text{O}]{\text{(i) } \text{LiAlH}_4 \text{ / dry ether}} \text{R-CH}_2\text{-NH}_2 + [\text{Al and Li salts}]
$$

* **Contrast Example:**
  - $\text{CH}_3\text{CONH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{LiAlH}_4} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2$ (Ethanamide $\to$ Ethanamine: **Carbon count preserved = 2**)
  - $\text{CH}_3\text{CONH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Br}_2 / \text{NaOH}} \text{CH}_3\text{NH}_2$ (Ethanamide $\to$ Methanamine: **Carbon count degraded = 1**)

***

### Method B: Ammonolysis of Alkyl Halides (Hoffmann's Ammonolysis) ⭐⭐⭐

Ammonolysis is a nucleophilic substitution reaction ($\text{S}_\text{N}2$) where the carbon–halogen ($\text{C}-\text{X}$) bond is cleaved by an ammonia molecule acting as an uncharged nucleophile.

#### Stepwise Substitution Mechanism

1. **Formation of primary substituted ammonium salt:**

$$
\text{H}_3\text{N}: + \text{R}-\text{X} \longrightarrow \left[ \text{R}-\overset{\oplus}{\text{NH}}_3 \right]\text{X}^\ominus
$$

2. **Liberation of primary amine:**

$$
\left[ \text{R}-\overset{\oplus}{\text{NH}}_3 \right]\text{X}^\ominus + \text{NH}_3 \rightleftharpoons \text{R}-\text{NH}_2 + \text{NH}_4^\oplus\text{X}^\ominus
$$

3. **Subsequent alkylations:** Because the resulting primary amine ($\text{R-NH}_2$) has a lone pair and the alkyl group ($\text{R}-$) has an electron-donating inductive effect ($+I$), $\text{R-NH}_2$ is **more nucleophilic** than ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$). Consequently, it attacks remaining $\text{R-X}$:

$$
\text{R-NH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{R-X}} \text{R}_2\text{NH} \cdot \text{HX} \xrightarrow{\text{NH}_3} \underset{\text{Secondary amine}}{\text{R}_2\text{NH}}
$$

$$
\text{R}_2\text{NH} \xrightarrow{\text{R-X}} \text{R}_3\text{N} \cdot \text{HX} \xrightarrow{\text{NH}_3} \underset{\text{Tertiary amine}}{\text{R}_3\text{N}}
$$

$$
\text{R}_3\text{N} + \text{R-X} \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Quaternary ammonium salt}}{\left[ \text{R}_4\overset{\oplus}{\text{N}} \right]\text{X}^\ominus}
$$

#### Reactivity Trends & Reaction Conditions
- **Order of reactivity of halides:**
  

$$
\text{R-I} > \text{R-Br} > \text{R-Cl} \quad (\text{governed by C-X bond dissociation enthalpy})
$$

- **Reaction Conditions:** Ethanolic solution of ammonia heated in a sealed glass tube at $373\text{ K}$.
- **Controlling Product Distribution:**
  - **Excess $\text{NH}_3$:** Shifts equilibrium toward **primary amine** ($\text{R-NH}_2$) as the major product.
  - **Excess $\text{R-X}$:** Yields predominantly **quaternary ammonium salt** ($\text{R}_4\text{N}^+\text{X}^-$).
- **Limitation:** Aryl halides ($\text{Ar-X}$) and vinyl halides do **not** undergo ammonolysis under normal conditions due to partial double-bond character from resonance and $sp^2$-hybridization of the halogen-bearing carbon.

***

### Method C: Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

This method is dedicated to the preparation of **pure aliphatic primary amines**. It completely eliminates the formation of secondary or tertiary amines and quaternary ammonium salts.

#### Stepwise Reaction Pathway

```
      O                      O                          O
      ||                     ||                         ||
    C                      C                          C
   / \                    / \                        / \
Ar    N-H  + KOH(alc) -> Ar   N:⁻ K⁺  + R-X (S_N2) -> Ar   N-R + KX
   \ /                    \ /                        \ /
    C                      C                          C
    ||                     ||                         ||
    O                      O                          O
Phthalimide         Potassium phthalimide       N-Alkylphthalimide
                                                      |
                                                      | + 2 NaOH (aq), heat
                                                      v
                                            O
                                            ||
                                          C-O⁻ Na⁺
                                         /
                                       Ar           +  R-NH₂
                                         \             (Pure 1° Amine)
                                          C-O⁻ Na⁺
                                            ||
                                            O
                                       Sodium phthalate
```

#### Detailed Equations

1. **Deprotonation of Phthalimide:**
   The hydrogen attached to nitrogen in phthalimide is acidic ($\text{p}K_a \approx 8.3$) because the conjugate base anion is resonance-stabilized by two strongly electron-withdrawing carbonyl ($-\text{C}=\text{O}$) groups.

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_4(\text{CO})_2\text{NH} + \text{KOH (alc.)} \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_4(\text{CO})_2\text{N}^\ominus \text{K}^\oplus + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

2. **Nucleophilic Substitution ($\text{S}_\text{N}2$ Attack):**
   The nucleophilic phthalimide anion attacks a primary alkyl halide:

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_4(\text{CO})_2\text{N}^\ominus \text{K}^\oplus + \text{R-X} \xrightarrow{\text{S}_\text{N}2} \text{C}_6\text{H}_4(\text{CO})_2\text{N-R} + \text{KX}
$$

3. **Alkaline Hydrolysis (or Hydrazinolysis):**
   The $N$-alkylphthalimide is cleaved under basic aqueous conditions to yield the carboxylate salt and liberate pure primary amine:

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_4(\text{CO})_2\text{N-R} + 2\text{NaOH (aq)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{C}_6\text{H}_4(\text{COONa})_2 + \text{R-NH}_2
$$

#### Major Conceptual Exceptions & Board Traps
1. **Aromatic primary amines (e.g., Aniline) CANNOT be synthesized by this method:**
   - **Reason:** Aryl halides ($\text{Ar-X}$) do not undergo nucleophilic substitution ($\text{S}_\text{N}2$) with the phthalimide anion due to:
     1. Partial double bond character of the $\text{C}-\text{X}$ bond via resonance.
     2. Steric hindrance preventing backside attack.
     3. Instability of the hypothetical phenyl cation.
2. **Tertiary alkyl halides ($3^\circ$ R-X) CANNOT be used:**
   - The phthalimide anion acts as a base rather than a nucleophile, causing **dehydrohalogenation (elimination)** to yield an **alkene** instead of substitution.

***

### Method D: Hoffmann Bromamide Degradation Reaction ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

This degradation reaction converts a primary acid amide into an aliphatic or aromatic primary amine containing **one carbon atom fewer** than the starting amide.

#### Overall Balanced Stoichiometric Equation

$$
\text{R-CONH}_2 + \text{Br}_2 + 4\text{NaOH} \longrightarrow \text{R-NH}_2 + \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 + 2\text{NaBr} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

$$
\text{Alternative with Potassium Hydroxide:}
$$

$$
\text{R-CONH}_2 + \text{Br}_2 + 4\text{KOH} \longrightarrow \text{R-NH}_2 + \text{K}_2\text{CO}_3 + 2\text{KBr} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

#### Detailed Step-by-Step Reaction Mechanism

##### Step 1: Base-catalyzed Deprotonation & Bromination
The base abstracts an acidic amide proton to generate an amidate ion, which then attacks molecular bromine:

$$
\text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{NH}_2 + \text{OH}^\ominus \rightleftharpoons \left[\text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\overset{\ominus}{\text{N}}\text{H}\right] + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

$$
\left[\text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\overset{\ominus}{\text{N}}\text{H}\right] + \text{Br}-\text{Br} \longrightarrow \underset{\text{N-bromoamide}}{\text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{NH-Br}} + \text{Br}^\ominus
$$

##### Step 2: Second Deprotonation to form Bromoamidate Anion

$$
\text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{NH-Br} + \text{OH}^\ominus \longrightarrow \left[\text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\overset{\ominus}{\text{N}}-\text{Br}\right] + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

##### Step 3: Concerted Intramolecular Rearrangement (Rate Determining Step - RDS)
The alkyl/aryl group $\text{R}$ migrates from the carbonyl carbon to the electron-deficient nitrogen atom with simultaneous loss of the bromide leaving group, generating an **alkyl isocyanate**:

$$
\left[\text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\overset{\ominus}{\text{N}}-\text{Br}\right] \xrightarrow[\text{Rearrangement}]{\text{Slow (r.d.s.)}} \underset{\text{Alkyl isocyanate}}{\text{O}=\text{C}=\text{N}-\text{R}} + \text{Br}^\ominus
$$

> **Stereochemical Note:** If the migrating alkyl group is chiral, it migrates with **complete retention of configuration** because the migration is strictly concerted (intramolecular 1,2-shift).

##### Step 4: Alkaline Hydrolysis of Isocyanate
The isocyanate reacts with hydroxide ion to form an unstable carbamate intermediate, which decarboxylates to yield the primary amine:

$$
\text{O}=\text{C}=\text{N}-\text{R} + \text{OH}^\ominus \longrightarrow \left[ \text{R}-\text{NH}-\text{COO} \right]^\ominus
$$

$$
\left[ \text{R}-\text{NH}-\text{COO} \right]^\ominus + \text{OH}^\ominus \longrightarrow \text{R}-\text{NH}_2 + \text{CO}_3^{2\ominus}
$$

Overall, with $\text{Na}^+$ counterions:

$$
\text{CO}_3^{2\ominus} + 2\text{Na}^\oplus \longrightarrow \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3
$$

**(a)** Write the chemical equation for the preparation of ethanamine from:

#### Solution

\text{CH}_3\text{CONH}_2 \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) } \text{H}_2\text{O}]{\text{(i) } \text{LiAlH}_4 \text{ / ether}} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2
$$

   *(Note: Hoffmann degradation on acetamide would yield methanamine, not ethanamine).*

**(b)**
In Gabriel phthalimide synthesis, the reaction involves an $\text{S}_\text{N}2$ nucleophilic displacement of a halide ion by the potassium phthalimide anion. Haloarenes (e.g., chlorobenzene) do **not** undergo nucleophilic substitution with potassium phthalimide under mild conditions because the carbon-halogen bond possesses partial double bond character due to resonance with the benzene ring, and the ring prevents nucleophilic backside attack.

### Question 2 [CBSE Board 2023, 2 Marks]
An aromatic compound 'A' on treatment with aqueous ammonia and heating forms compound 'B', which on heating with $\text{Br}_2$ and $\text{KOH}$ forms compound 'C' of molecular formula $\text{C}_6\text{H}_7\text{N}$. 
1. Identify compounds 'A', 'B', and 'C'.
2. Write the chemical equation for the conversion of 'B' to 'C'.

#### Solution
1. **Identification:**

   - Compound 'B' is formed by heating compound 'A' with aqueous ammonia. Thus, 'A' is **Benzoic acid** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOH}$).

   

$$
\text{A} = \text{Benzoic acid } (\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOH})
$$

   

$$
\text{B} = \text{Benzamide } (\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CONH}_2)
$$

   

$$
\text{C} = \text{Aniline / Benzenamine } (\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2)
$$

2. **Reaction Equation ($\text{B} \to \text{C}$):**
   

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CONH}_2 + \text{Br}_2 + 4\text{KOH} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{K}_2\text{CO}_3 + 2\text{KBr} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}

### Question 3 [CBSE Board 2022, Term-II, 3 Marks]
Account for the following:
1. Ammonolysis of alkyl halides is not a suitable method for the preparation of pure primary amines.
2. During the preparation of primary amines by reduction of nitro compounds, $\text{Fe} + \text{HCl}$ is preferred over $\text{Sn} + \text{HCl}$.
3. Gabriel phthalimide synthesis is preferred for synthesizing primary aliphatic amines.

#### Solution
1. **Reason:** The primary amine formed initially contains a lone pair of electrons on nitrogen and an electron-donating alkyl group, making it a stronger nucleophile than ammonia. Consequently, it undergoes further alkylation with unreacted alkyl halide, yielding a complex mixture of $1^\circ, 2^\circ, 3^\circ$ amines and quaternary ammonium salts that are difficult to separate by fractional distillation.
2. **Reason:** The reaction of iron with hydrochloric acid produces $\text{FeCl}_2$, which readily undergoes aqueous hydrolysis to regenerate $\text{HCl}$ ($\text{FeCl}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{Fe(OH)}_2 + 2\text{HCl}$). Thus, only a small catalytic amount of $\text{HCl}$ is required.
3. **Reason:** The phthalimide anion undergoes selective monosubstitution via an $\text{S}_\text{N}2$ mechanism to produce exclusively $N$-alkylphthalimide. Because there are no remaining acidic protons on the nitrogen atom of the intermediate, polyalkylation is physically impossible, yielding uncontaminated $1^\circ$ aliphatic amines.

## 4. Examiner Traps & Common Student Pitfalls

### Trap 1: Stoichiometric Negligence in Hoffmann Bromamide Reaction
* **Student Error:** Writing $2\text{NaOH}$ or omitting water/carbonates:
  

$$
\text{RCONH}_2 + \text{Br}_2 + 2\text{NaOH} \to \text{RNH}_2 + 2\text{NaBr} \quad \text{(INCORRECT)}
$$

* **NCERT Standard Requirement:** Must explicitly use a **$1:1:4$ molar ratio** for Amide : $\text{Br}_2$ : Base:
  

$$
\text{Amide} + \text{Br}_2 + 4\text{NaOH} \longrightarrow \text{Amine} + \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 + 2\text{NaBr} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

### Trap 2: Reagent Confusion: $\text{LiAlH}_4$ vs $\text{Br}_2/\text{NaOH}$ on Amides
* **Student Error:** Using Hoffmann bromamide to prepare an amine with the *same* number of carbons.
* **Correction:**
  - Need same number of carbons ($n \to n$)? Use $\text{LiAlH}_4$ in dry ether.
  - Need one less carbon ($n \to n-1$)? Use Hoffmann degradation ($\text{Br}_2 / \text{NaOH}$).

### Trap 3: Substrate Suitability for Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis
* **Student Error:** Choosing chlorobenzene or bromobenzene to synthesize aniline or $p$-toluidine using Gabriel Phthalimide.
* **Examiner Rule:** Zero marks awarded. Gabriel Phthalimide synthesis is strictly limited to **aliphatic primary amines with $1^\circ$ or unhindered $2^\circ$ halides**. It fails with aromatic halides and gives elimination products (alkenes) with $3^\circ$ alkyl halides.

### Trap 4: Workup of Aniline Produced via Acidic Reduction
* **Subtle Point:** When nitrobenzene is reduced by $\text{Fe/HCl}$ or $\text{Sn/HCl}$, aniline is formed initially as its hydrochloride salt:
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_3^\oplus \text{Cl}^\ominus
$$

  It requires treatment with a base ($\text{NaOH}$) to liberate free aniline base ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2$).

## 5. Speed Hacks & Golden Memory Mnemonics

### Carbon Count Decision Tree

```
Need to prepare an amine? Count the target carbons relative to the precursor:

  Target carbons = Precursor carbons + 1  (Ascent / Step-Up)
  └── Convert Precursor to Halide -> R-X + KCN(alc) -> R-CN -> Reduce (LiAlH4 or H2/Ni)

  Target carbons = Precursor carbons      (Chain Preservation)
  ├── Aliphatic: R-X + Potassium Phthalimide -> Hydrolysis  [Gabriel Synthesis]
  ├── Reduction of Amides: R-CONH2 + LiAlH4 -> R-CH2-NH2
  └── Reduction of Nitro: R-NO2 + Fe/HCl -> R-NH2

  Target carbons = Precursor carbons - 1  (Descent / Step-Down)
  └── Carboxylic Acid -> Amide (R-CONH2) -> Br2 + 4NaOH [Hoffmann Bromamide Degradation]
```

### High-Yield Mnemonics

1. **"G-A-P" Rule for Gabriel Synthesis:**
   - **G**abriel **A**lways **P**refers:
     - **A**liphatic $1^\circ$
     - **P**ure (no polyalkylation)
     - **P**recludes Aromatics ($\text{Ar-X}$ forbidden)

2. **The "1-1-4" Hoffmann Formula:**
   - For every **1** mole of amide, you need **1** mole of halogen ($\text{Br}_2$) and **4** moles of hydroxide base ($\text{NaOH}$ or $\text{KOH}$), losing **1** carbon atom as carbonate ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$).

3. **Reagent Selectivity Quick Reference:**
   - $\text{R-CN} \xrightarrow{\text{LiAlH}_4} \text{R-CH}_2\text{-NH}_2$ ($1^\circ$ Amine)
   - $\text{R-NC} \xrightarrow{\text{LiAlH}_4} \text{R-NH-CH}_3$ ($2^\circ$ Amine)

   - $\text{R-CONH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Br}_2 / 4\text{NaOH}} \text{R-NH}_2$ ($1^\circ$ Amine, 1 less carbon)

## 1. Physical Properties of Amines

### 1.1 Physical State, Odour, and Colour
*Priority: ⭐⭐⭐*

- **Physical State & Odour:**
  - Lower aliphatic amines (e.g., methanamine, ethanamine) are **colourless gases** with an unpleasant, **fishy ammoniacal odour**.
  - Primary amines with three or more carbon atoms are **liquids**, and higher members ($C_6$ and above) are **solids**.
  - Aromatic amines (e.g., aniline) are typically high-boiling **liquids or low-melting solids** with a characteristic unpleasant odour.
- **Colour & Storage Vulnerability:**
  - Pure arylamines are colourless; however, they become **dark/brownish on storage** due to **atmospheric oxidation** (formation of quinonoid impurities).

***

### 1.2 Intermolecular Forces and Boiling Points
*Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐*

#### A. Molecular Interpretation & Order
Amines possess polar $\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{N}$ and $\mathrm{N}-\mathrm{H}$ bonds due to the electronegativity difference between nitrogen ($\chi_{\mathrm{N}} \approx 3.0$) and hydrogen ($\chi_{\mathrm{H}} \approx 2.1$). Consequently, primary ($1^\circ$) and secondary ($2^\circ$) amines undergo **intermolecular hydrogen bonding**.

$$
\text{Extinction of H-bonding Sites: } 1^\circ \text{ Amine} \; (2 \times \mathrm{N-H}) > 2^\circ \text{ Amine} \; (1 \times \mathrm{N-H}) \gg 3^\circ \text{ Amine} \; (0 \times \mathrm{N-H})
$$

- **$1^\circ$ Amines ($\mathrm{RNH_2}$):** Two hydrogen atoms are attached to nitrogen $\implies$ forms more extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonds.
- **$2^\circ$ Amines ($\mathrm{R_2NH}$):** One hydrogen atom attached to nitrogen $\implies$ forms fewer intermolecular hydrogen bonds.
- **$3^\circ$ Amines ($\mathrm{R_3N}$):** No hydrogen atom attached directly to nitrogen $\implies$ **cannot form intermolecular hydrogen bonds** with themselves; only dipole-dipole interactions and London dispersion forces exist.

#### B. Comparative Boiling Point Order for Isomeric Amines (Same Molecular Formula, e.g., $\mathrm{C_4H_{11}N}$)

$$
\boxed{\text{Boiling Point Order: } 1^\circ \text{ amine} > 2^\circ \text{ amine} > 3^\circ \text{ amine}}
$$

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2NH_2} \; (350.8\text{ K}) > (\mathrm{CH_3CH_2})_2\mathrm{NH} \; (329.3\text{ K}) > \mathrm{CH_3CH_2N(CH_3)_2} \; (310.5\text{ K}) > (\mathrm{CH_3})_3\mathrm{N} \; (276\text{ K})
$$

#### C. Comparison: Amines vs. Alcohols vs. Alkanes of Comparable Molar Masses

$$
\boxed{\text{Boiling Point: } \mathrm{R-OH} > \mathrm{R-NH_2} > \mathrm{R-H}}
$$

**Pedagogical Rationale:**
Electronegativity of oxygen ($\chi_{\mathrm{O}} = 3.5$) is higher than that of nitrogen ($\chi_{\mathrm{N}} = 3.0$). Therefore, the $\mathrm{O-H}$ dipole is substantially more polarized than the $\mathrm{N-H}$ dipole:

$$
\Delta\chi(\mathrm{O-H}) = 3.5 - 2.1 = 1.4 \quad \text{vs.} \quad \Delta\chi(\mathrm{N-H}) = 3.0 - 2.1 = 0.9
$$

Intermolecular hydrogen bonds in alcohols are much stronger than those in amines.

***

### 1.3 Solubility in Polar Solvents (Water)
*Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐*

- Lower aliphatic amines are **completely miscible with water** because they can form intermolecular hydrogen bonds with polar water molecules ($\mathrm{R-NH_2 \cdots H-O-H}$).
- **Solubility decreases continuously as molar mass increases:**
  - The non-polar hydrophobic alkyl chain increases in size, which overwhelms the attractive solute-solvent hydrogen bonding interactions.
- **Tertiary Amines ($\mathrm{R_3N}$):** Though they cannot hydrogen bond with themselves, they possess a lone pair on nitrogen and **can accept hydrogen bonds from water molecules** ($\mathrm{R_3N: \cdots H-OH}$); hence, lower $3^\circ$ amines are moderately soluble in water.
- **Aromatic Amines (Aniline):** Almost insoluble in water due to the large, non-polar hydrophobic phenyl group ($\mathrm{-C_6H_5}$) which sterically and energetically hinders interaction with water.

***

## 2. Basic Character of Amines: Core Concepts & Quantitative Metrics

### 2.1 Equilibrium and Ionization Constants ($K_b$ and $\mathrm{p}K_b$)
*Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐*

Amines possess an unshared pair of electrons (lone pair) on the trivalent nitrogen atom, enabling them to act as **Lewis bases** and **Brønsted-Lowry bases** (proton acceptors).

#### Thermodynamic Ionization Equilibrium in Aqueous Solution:

$$
\mathrm{R-NH_2(aq)} + \mathrm{H_2O(l)} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{R-NH_3^+(aq)} + \mathrm{OH^-(aq)}
$$

Applying the Law of Chemical Equilibrium:

$$
K = \frac{[\mathrm{R-NH_3^+}][\mathrm{OH^-}]}{[\mathrm{R-NH_2}][\mathrm{H_2O}]}
$$

Since water is present in vast excess, $[\mathrm{H_2O}]$ is constant:

$$
K_b = K \cdot [\mathrm{H_2O}] = \frac{[\mathrm{R-NH_3^+}][\mathrm{OH^-}]}{[\mathrm{R-NH_2}]}
$$

The basicity constant is transformed into $\mathrm{p}K_b$:

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{p}K_b = -\log_{10} K_b}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Higher } K_b \iff \text{Lower } \mathrm{p}K_b \iff \text{Stronger Base}}
$$

***

## 3. Basicity of Aliphatic Amines: Gas Phase vs. Aqueous Phase

### 3.1 Gas Phase Basicity
*Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐*

In the gas phase, the amine and its conjugate acid are free from solvent interactions (no hydration or solvation forces). The relative basicity is dictated **solely by the inductive effect ($+I$)** of the electron-releasing alkyl substituents.

$$
\text{Electron density on nitrogen} \propto \text{Number of alkyl groups (+I effect)}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Gas Phase Basicity Order: } 3^\circ \text{ Amine } (\mathrm{R_3N}) > 2^\circ \text{ Amine } (\mathrm{R_2NH}) > 1^\circ \text{ Amine } (\mathrm{RNH_2}) > \mathrm{NH_3}}
$$

***

### 3.2 Aqueous Phase Basicity: The Three-Factor Tug-of-War
*Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐*

In an aqueous medium, basicity does **not** follow the simple inductive order. Instead, it is dictated by the thermodynamic stability of the substituted ammonium cation ($\mathrm{R_nNH_{4-n}^+}$), which is governed by three conflicting parameters:

#### Factor 1: Inductive Effect ($+I$)
Alkyl groups disperse the positive charge on the conjugate ammonium cation through electron donation, stabilizing the conjugate acid:

$$
\text{Stability order: } 3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ > \mathrm{NH_4^+}
$$

#### Factor 2: Solvation Effect (Hydration Enthalpy)
The conjugate ammonium cation is stabilized in water via **hydrogen bonding** with water molecules. More hydrogen atoms on the nitrogen cation $\implies$ greater extent of hydration $\implies$ more negative $\Delta_{\mathrm{hyd}}H$ (greater thermodynamic stability):

$$
\mathrm{RNH_3^+} \; (3 \times \mathrm{O-H\cdots N \text{ bonds}}) > \mathrm{R_2NH_2^+} \; (2 \times \mathrm{H\text{-bonds}}) > \mathrm{R_3NH^+} \; (1 \times \mathrm{H\text{-bond}})
$$

$$
\text{Solvation Stability Order: } 1^\circ > 2^\circ > 3^\circ
$$

#### Factor 3: Steric Hindrance
Bulky alkyl groups sterically crowd the nitrogen lone pair, hindering attack by a proton and impeding stabilizing water molecules from approaching the protonated cation:

$$
\text{Crowding: } 3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ
$$

***

### 3.3 The Final Observed Orders in Aqueous Solution (NCERT Canon)

Combining Inductive ($+I$), Solvation, and Steric factors yields unique experimental orders:

#### Case I: Methyl-substituted Amines ($\mathrm{R = -CH_3}$)
Because the methyl group is small, steric hindrance is minimal. Solvation energy plays a decisive role:

$$
\boxed{\text{Basic Strength: } (\mathrm{CH_3})_2\mathrm{NH} > \mathrm{CH_3NH_2} > (\mathrm{CH_3})_3\mathrm{N} > \mathrm{NH_3} \quad (2^\circ > 1^\circ > 3^\circ > \mathrm{NH_3})}
$$

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{p}K_b \text{ Order: } (\mathrm{CH_3})_3\mathrm{N}\;(4.22) > \mathrm{CH_3NH_2}\;(3.38) > (\mathrm{CH_3})_2\mathrm{NH}\;(3.27)}
$$

#### Case II: Ethyl-substituted Amines ($\mathrm{R = -CH_2CH_3}$)
The larger ethyl group exerts greater steric hindrance, depressing the stability of the $1^\circ$ and $3^\circ$ cations relative to the inductive gain of two ethyl groups:

$$
\boxed{\text{Basic Strength: } (\mathrm{C_2H_5})_2\mathrm{NH} > (\mathrm{C_2H_5})_3\mathrm{N} > \mathrm{C_2H_5NH_2} > \mathrm{NH_3} \quad (2^\circ > 3^\circ > 1^\circ > \mathrm{NH_3})}
$$

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{p}K_b \text{ Order: } \mathrm{NH_3}\;(4.75) > \mathrm{C_2H_5NH_2}\;(3.29) > (\mathrm{C_2H_5})_3\mathrm{N}\;(3.25) > (\mathrm{C_2H_5})_2\mathrm{NH}\;(3.00)}
$$

***

## 4. Basicity of Aromatic Amines (Aniline) vs. Aliphatic Amines

*Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐*

$$
\boxed{\text{Basicity: } \text{Aliphatic Amines } (\mathrm{R-NH_2}) \gg \mathrm{NH_3} > \text{Aromatic Amines } (\mathrm{Ar-NH_2})}
$$

$$
\text{Example: } \mathrm{p}K_b(\text{Cyclohexanamine}) \approx 3.3 \quad \text{vs.} \quad \mathrm{p}K_b(\text{Aniline}) = 9.38
$$

### 4.1 Primary Cause: Resonance Delocalization of the Lone Pair
In aniline ($\mathrm{C_6H_5NH_2}$), the nitrogen lone pair is conjugated with the $\pi$-electron system of the benzene ring.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{I: } & \mathrm{C_6H_5-\ddot{N}H_2} \\
\text{II: } & \text{Double bond to } =\mathrm{\overset{+}{N}H_2} \text{ with negative charge at } ortho \\
\text{III: } & \text{Double bond to } =\mathrm{\overset{+}{N}H_2} \text{ with negative charge at } para \\
\text{IV: } & \text{Double bond to } =\mathrm{\overset{+}{N}H_2} \text{ with negative charge at } ortho' \\
\text{V: } & \mathrm{C_6H_5-\ddot{N}H_2}
\end{aligned}
$$

- Aniline is stabilized by **5 resonance contributing structures**.
- Protonation yields the **anilinium cation** ($\mathrm{C_6H_5-\overset{+}{N}H_3}$), which has only **2 resonance structures**:

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5-\overset{+}{N}H_3} \longleftrightarrow \text{(Kekulé structures of benzene with fixed } -\mathrm{\overset{+}{N}H_3})
$$

$$
\Delta G^\circ_{\text{protonation}} \text{ is unfavourable because protonation results in loss of resonance energy:}
$$

$$
\text{Resonance stabilization: Aniline (5 structures)} > \text{Anilinium Ion (2 structures)}
$$

Hence, the equilibrium lies far to the left, making aniline a weak base.

### 4.2 Secondary Cause: Hybridization of the Carbon Atom
In arylamines, nitrogen is attached to an **$\mathrm{sp^2}$-hybridized carbon** ($\% s\text{-character} = 33.3\%$), which is more electronegative than the **$\mathrm{sp^3}$-hybridized carbon** ($\% s\text{-character} = 25\%$) of alkylamines. The $\mathrm{sp^2}$ carbon withdraws electron density away from nitrogen, reducing basicity.

***

## 5. Substituent Effects on Aromatic Amines

*Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐*

### 5.1 Electron-Donating Groups (EDG) / Electron-Releasing Groups (ERG)
- **Groups:** $-\mathrm{OCH_3}$, $-\mathrm{CH_3}$, $-\mathrm{NH_2}$, $-\mathrm{OH}$.
- **Mechanism:** Donate electron density via $+R / +M$ or $+I$ (hyperconjugation for alkyl groups) into the aromatic ring $\implies$ **increases** electron density on the nitrogen lone pair and stabilizes the anilinium ion.
- **Effect:** **Increases basicity** ($\mathrm{p}K_b$ decreases).

### 5.2 Electron-Withdrawing Groups (EWG)
- **Groups:** $-\mathrm{NO_2}$, $-\mathrm{CN}$, $-\mathrm{CHO}$, $-\mathrm{COOH}$, $-\mathrm{X}$ (halogens).
- **Mechanism:** Pull electron density out of the ring via $-R / -M$ and/or $-I$ $\implies$ **decreases** electron density on the nitrogen atom and destabilizes the anilinium ion.
- **Effect:** **Decreases basicity** ($\mathrm{p}K_b$ increases).

***

### 5.3 Positional Effect: The Nitroanilines Benchmark

Consider the basicity order of the nitroaniline isomers:

$$
\boxed{\text{Basic Strength: } p\text{-toluidine} > \text{aniline} > m\text{-nitroaniline} > p\text{-nitroaniline} > o\text{-nitroaniline}}
$$

#### Detailed Mechanistic Breakdown:
1. **$p$-Nitroaniline:** Both strong $-R$ and $-I$ effects operate at the para position:
   

$$
\text{Direct delocalization of nitrogen lone pair into the } -\mathrm{NO_2} \text{ group occurs:}
$$

   

$$
\mathrm{H_2\overset{+}{N}=C_6H_4=\overset{-}{O}_2N} \implies \text{severely diminished basicity.}
$$

2. **$m$-Nitroaniline:** At the meta position, **$-R$ cannot operate**; only the inductive **$-I$ effect** pulls electron density. Therefore, $m$-nitroaniline is a stronger base than $p$-nitroaniline, but weaker than aniline.
3. **$o$-Nitroaniline:** Experiencing strong $-I$ and $-R$ effects alongside steric and field interactions (**ortho-effect** in substituted anilines, which destabilizes protonation), making it the **weakest base** in this series.

## 6. Examiner Traps & Common Student Pitfalls

| # | Topic / Concept | Common Student Error | Correct NCERT Principle |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **$K_b$ vs. $\mathrm{p}K_b$** | Equating higher $\mathrm{p}K_b$ with higher basic strength. | $\mathrm{p}K_b = -\log_{10} K_b$. **Lower $\mathrm{p}K_b$ = Stronger Base.** |
| 2 | **Phase Condition Ignored** | Writing $2^\circ > 1^\circ > 3^\circ$ when the question explicitly states **gas phase**. | In gas phase, **$3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ > \mathrm{NH_3}$** (governed strictly by $+I$ effect). |
| 3 | **Methyl vs. Ethyl Order** | Confusing the sequence numbers between methyl and ethyl groups. | Methyl = **213**; Ethyl = **231**. Secondary is always top in aqueous medium. |
| 4 | **Solubility of Amines** | Assuming $3^\circ$ amines cannot dissolve in water because they have no $\mathrm{N-H}$ bond. | $3^\circ$ amines **can form H-bonds with water molecules** ($\mathrm{R_3N: \cdots H-O-H}$) and are water-soluble if alkyl groups are small. |
| 5 | **Aniline Basicity Rationalization** | Writing only: *"Aniline has resonance."* | Must state: **"Aniline has 5 resonance contributors, whereas anilinium ion has only 2; protonation leads to loss of resonance stability."** |

## 7. Speed Hacks & Memory Mnemonics

### Mnemonic 1: The Aqueous Phase Basicity Code
- For smaller alkyl group ($\mathrm{-CH_3}$): **Code 213**
  

$$
(\mathrm{CH_3})_2\mathrm{NH} > \mathrm{CH_3NH_2} > (\mathrm{CH_3})_3\mathrm{N} > \mathrm{NH_3}
$$

- For larger alkyl group ($\mathrm{-C_2H_5}$): **Code 231**
  

$$
(\mathrm{C_2H_5})_2\mathrm{NH} > (\mathrm{C_2H_5})_3\mathrm{N} > \mathrm{C_2H_5NH_2} > \mathrm{NH_3}
$$

> **Memory Rule:** The larger group ($\mathrm{-C_2H_5}$) accommodates the larger number (**231** vs. **213**). In both, the **secondary amine ($2^\circ$) leads the series**.

### Mnemonic 2: Boiling Point vs. Number of N-H Bonds

$$
\text{Boiling Point} \propto \text{Number of } \mathrm{N-H} \text{ bonds } (1^\circ > 2^\circ > 3^\circ)
$$

### Question 2 [CBSE 2023, 3 Marks]
Account for the following:
1. Primary amines have higher boiling points than tertiary amines of comparable molecular mass.
2. $\mathrm{(C_2H_5)_2NH}$ is more basic than $\mathrm{(C_2H_5)_3N}$ in aqueous solution.
3. $\mathrm{p}K_b$ of aniline is substantially higher than that of methylamine.

#### Model Solution:
1. Primary amines ($\mathrm{RNH_2}$) have two hydrogen atoms bonded to the electronegative nitrogen atom, allowing them to form extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonds. Tertiary amines ($\mathrm{R_3N}$) have no hydrogen atoms directly bonded to nitrogen, so they cannot form intermolecular hydrogen bonds with each other. Hence, more thermal energy is needed to break intermolecular forces in primary amines, giving them higher boiling points.
2. In aqueous medium, basic strength is dictated by the inductive effect, steric hindrance, and hydration of the conjugate cation:
   - Triethylamine ($\mathrm{(C_2H_5)_3N}$) has three bulky ethyl groups that sterically crowd the nitrogen atom, hindering protonation and reducing hydration of the conjugate cation.
   - In diethylamine ($\mathrm{(C_2H_5)_2NH}$), the combination of $+I$ effect and superior stabilization by hydration of the conjugate cation ($\mathrm{(C_2H_5)_2NH_2^+}$) outweighs the inductive gain of a third ethyl group. Consequently, $\mathrm{(C_2H_5)_2NH}$ is more basic.
3. $\mathrm{p}K_b = -\log_{10} K_b$; therefore, a higher $\mathrm{p}K_b$ value indicates a **weaker base**.
   - In aniline, the unshared electron pair on nitrogen is delocalized into the benzene ring through resonance, reducing its availability for protonation.
   - In methylamine ($\mathrm{CH_3NH_2}$), the methyl group exerts an electron-releasing $+I$ effect that increases electron density on nitrogen.
   - Thus, aniline is a much weaker base than methylamine, resulting in a significantly higher $\mathrm{p}K_b$ value ($\approx 9.38$ vs. $3.38$).

Delocalization of ring electrons toward $-\mathrm{\overset{+}{N}H_3}$ directs incoming electrophiles to the meta position, resulting in $47\%$ $m$-nitroaniline alongside $51\%$ $p$-nitroaniline and $2\%$ $o$-nitroaniline.

## 2. In-Depth Pedagogical Theory, Equations & Mechanisms

### 2.1 Acylation and Benzoylation (Schotten–Baumann Reaction) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### Theory & Concept
Aliphatic and aromatic primary and secondary amines react with acid chlorides, acid anhydrides, and esters by nucleophilic acyl substitution ($\text{S}_\text{N}\text{Ac}$). This reaction involves the replacement of a hydrogen atom attached to nitrogen by an acyl ($\text{R}-\text{C}=\text{O}$) group.

#### Reaction Mechanism & Equations
The reaction is conducted in the presence of a base stronger than the amine (e.g., **pyridine** or aqueous $\text{NaOH}$), which serves to neutralize the $\text{HCl}$ generated, shifting the equilibrium forward (Le Chatelier's principle).

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2 + \text{CH}_3\text{COCl} \xrightarrow{\text{Pyridine}} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{NHCOCH}_3 + \text{HCl}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Ethanamine} + \text{Ethanoyl chloride} \xrightarrow{\text{Pyridine}} \text{N-Ethylethanamide} + \text{HCl}}
$$

Reaction of aniline with acetic anhydride in the presence of pyridine yields **acetanilide**:

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + (\text{CH}_3\text{CO})_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{Pyridine}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NHCOCH}_3 + \text{CH}_3\text{COOH}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Aniline} + \text{Acetic anhydride} \xrightarrow{\text{Pyridine}} \text{Acetanilide} + \text{Ethanoic acid}}
$$

**Benzoylation of aniline (Schotten–Baumann reaction):**

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COCl} \xrightarrow{\text{aq. NaOH}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NHCOC}_6\text{H}_5 + \text{HCl}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Aniline} + \text{Benzoyl chloride} \xrightarrow{\text{aq. NaOH}} \text{N-Phenylbenzamide (Benzanilide)} + \text{HCl}}
$$

> **NCERT Key Note:** Tertiary amines ($3^\circ$) do not undergo acylation because they lack a replaceable hydrogen atom on the nitrogen atom.

***

### 2.2 Carbylamine Reaction (Isocyanide Test) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

#### Theory & Scope
* **Substrates:** Primary aliphatic and primary aromatic amines **only** ($\text{R}-\text{NH}_2$ and $\text{Ar}-\text{NH}_2$).
* **Reagents:** Chloroform ($\text{CHCl}_3$) and ethanolic potassium hydroxide ($\text{KOH}$).
* **Condition:** Heat ($\Delta$).
* **Observation:** Formation of an **isocyanide (carbylamine)** characterized by an **extremely foul / repulsive odour**.
* **Negative Test:** Secondary ($2^\circ$) and tertiary ($3^\circ$) amines do not show this test.

#### Chemical Equation

$$
\text{R}-\text{NH}_2 + \text{CHCl}_3 + 3\text{KOH (alc.)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{R}-\text{N}\equiv\text{C} + 3\text{KCl} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{CHCl}_3 + 3\text{KOH (alc.)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{N}\equiv\text{C} + 3\text{KCl} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Aniline} + \text{CHCl}_3 + 3\text{KOH} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Phenyl isocyanide (Foul smelling)} + 3\text{KCl} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}}
$$

#### Reactive Intermediate
The attacking intermediate generated *in situ* from chloroform and base is **dichlorocarbene** ($:\text{CCl}_2$):

$$
\text{CHCl}_3 + \text{OH}^- \rightleftharpoons :\text{CCl}_3^- + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow :\text{CCl}_2 + \text{Cl}^-
$$

***

### 2.3 Reaction with Nitrous Acid ($\text{HNO}_2$) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Because nitrous acid is unstable, it is always prepared *in situ* using sodium nitrite ($\text{NaNO}_2$) and mineral acid ($\text{HCl}$).

#### A. Primary Aliphatic Amines
React with cold $\text{HNO}_2$ to form unstable aliphatic diazonium salts, which decompose quantitatively into a carbocation, yielding **alcohols**, alkenes, alkyl halides, and releasing **nitrogen gas ($\text{N}_2$)** bubbles.

$$
\text{R}-\text{NH}_2 + \text{HNO}_2 \xrightarrow[\text{HCl}]{\text{NaNO}_2,\, 273-278\text{ K}} \left[\text{R}-\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^-\right] \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{R}-\text{OH} + \text{N}_2\uparrow + \text{HCl}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Quantitative evolution of }\text{N}_2\text{ is used in the estimation of amino acids and proteins.}}
$$

#### B. Primary Aromatic Amines (Diazotisation)
Aromatic primary amines react with $\text{HNO}_2$ at ice-cold temperatures ($273\text{--}278\text{ K}$ or $0\text{--}5^\circ\text{C}$) to form **benzenediazonium salts**, which are resonance-stabilized.

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{NaNO}_2 + 2\text{HCl} \xrightarrow{273-278\text{ K}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{NaCl} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Benzenediazonium chloride is stable only in cold solution }(0-5^\circ\text{C})\text{ due to resonance delocalization.}}
$$

#### C. Secondary Amines ($1^\circ$ vs $2^\circ$ distinction)
Secondary amines (aliphatic and aromatic) react with $\text{HNO}_2$ to yield **N-nitrosamines**, which separate as **insoluble yellow oily liquids**:

$$
\text{R}_2\text{NH} + \text{HONO} \longrightarrow \text{R}_2\text{N}-\text{N}=\text{O} + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

#### D. Tertiary Amines
Tertiary aliphatic amines dissolve in cold nitrous acid to form soluble nitrite salts:

$$
\text{R}_3\text{N} + \text{HNO}_2 \longrightarrow \left[\text{R}_3\text{NH}\right]^+\text{NO}_2^-
$$

***

### 2.4 Hinsberg’s Test (Benzenesulfonyl Chloride) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

The Hinsberg reagent is **benzenesulfonyl chloride** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\text{Cl}$). It is used to distinguish and separate primary ($1^\circ$), secondary ($2^\circ$), and tertiary ($3^\circ$) amines.

```
                  ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                  │          Hinsberg Reagent (PhSO2Cl)           │
                  └───────────────────────┬───────────────────────┘
                                          │
            ┌─────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┐
            ▼                             ▼                             ▼
       1° Amine                      2° Amine                      3° Amine
    (R-NH2 + PhSO2Cl)             (R2NH + PhSO2Cl)              (R3N + PhSO2Cl)
            │                             │                             │
            ▼                             ▼                             ▼
  N-Alkylbenzenesulfonamide     N,N-Dialkylbenzenesulfonamide       NO REACTION
   (Contains acidic N-H)            (No acidic N-H)             (Insoluble in alkali,
            │                             │                      dissolves in acid)
            ▼                             ▼
  SOLUBLE IN aq. KOH/NaOH     INSOLUBLE IN aq. KOH/NaOH
```

#### Step 1: Reaction with Primary ($1^\circ$) Amine

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\text{Cl} + \text{H}-\text{NH}-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3 \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\text{NHCH}_2\text{CH}_3 + \text{HCl}
$$

* Product: **$N$-Ethylbenzenesulfonamide**.
* **Alkali Solubility:** The hydrogen attached directly to nitrogen is strongly acidic due to the strong electron-withdrawing effect of the adjacent sulfonyl group ($-\text{SO}_2-$). It readily loses $\text{H}^+$ to form a water-soluble potassium salt:

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\text{NHCH}_2\text{CH}_3 + \text{KOH (aq)} \longrightarrow \left[\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\overline{\text{N}}\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3\right]\text{K}^+ + \text{H}_2\text{O} \quad \mathbf{(\text{Soluble})}
$$

#### Step 2: Reaction with Secondary ($2^\circ$) Amine

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\text{Cl} + \text{HN}(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5)_2 \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\text{N}(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5)_2 + \text{HCl}
$$

* Product: **$N,N$-Diethylbenzenesulfonamide**.
* **Alkali Solubility:** This product contains **no acidic hydrogen atom** attached to the nitrogen. Therefore, it is completely **insoluble in aqueous alkali (KOH / NaOH)**.

#### Step 3: Reaction with Tertiary ($3^\circ$) Amine
* Tertiary amines possess no hydrogen atom on nitrogen. 
* They do not react with benzenesulfonyl chloride and remain insoluble in alkali, but dissolve in dilute acids.

> **Modern Variant Note:** In modern practice, **$p$-toluenesulfonyl chloride** (tosyl chloride, $\text{TsCl}$) is often used in place of benzenesulfonyl chloride.

***

### 2.5 Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution (EAS) of Aniline ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

The amino group ($-\text{NH}_2$) in aniline is a **very powerful activating group** and strong *ortho/para*-director due to the $+R$ resonance effect of the unshared electron pair on nitrogen delocalizing into the benzene ring:

```
  :NH2           +NH2          +NH2          +NH2
   │             ║             ║             ║
 ╱───╲         ╱───╲ -       ╱───╲         ╱───╲
│     │  <->  │     │  <->  │     │  <->  │     │
 ╲───╱         ╲───╱         ╲───╱ -       ╲───╱
                                            -
 (I)            (II)          (III)         (IV)
```

Electron density is concentrated specifically at the **ortho** and **para** positions.

***

#### A. Halogenation (Bromination)

##### 1. Uncontrolled Bromination (Aqueous Medium)
When aniline is treated with bromine water at room temperature, the activation is so high that substitution occurs rapidly at all available ortho and para positions, yielding a white precipitate of **2,4,6-tribromoaniline**:

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + 3\text{Br}_2\text{ (aq)} \xrightarrow{298\text{ K}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_2\text{Br}_3\text{NH}_2\downarrow + 3\text{HBr}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Aniline} + 3\text{Br}_2\text{ (aq)} \longrightarrow \text{2,4,6-Tribromoaniline (White Precipitate)} + 3\text{HBr}}
$$

##### 2. Controlled Monobromination (Via Protection)
To prepare **$p$-bromoaniline**, the activating effect of the $-\text{NH}_2$ group must be moderated. This is achieved by **protecting** the amino group through **acetylation** with acetic anhydride in pyridine before carrying out halogenation.

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 \xrightarrow[(\text{Pyridine})]{(\text{CH}_3\text{CO})_2\text{O}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NHCOCH}_3 \xrightarrow[\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}]{\text{Br}_2} p\text{-Br}-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{NHCOCH}_3 \xrightarrow[\text{or }\text{OH}^-]{\text{H}^+} p\text{-Br}-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{NH}_2
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Overall: Aniline} \xrightarrow{\text{Ac}_2\text{O}/\text{Py}} \text{Acetanilide} \xrightarrow{\text{Br}_2/\text{AcOH}} \text{4-Bromoacetanilide} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{4-Bromoaniline (Major)}}
$$

**Mechanistic Justification for Reduced Reactivity:**
In acetanilide, the lone pair on nitrogen is delocalized towards the carbonyl oxygen atom via resonance:

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\overset{..}{\text{N}}\text{H}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{CH}_3 \longleftrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}=\overset{\overset{\text{O}^-}{\mid}}{\text{C}}-\text{CH}_3
$$

Hence, the lone pair of electrons on nitrogen is less available for donation to the benzene ring than in free aniline. Consequently, ring activation decreases, preventing poly-substitution.

***

#### B. Nitration ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Direct nitration of aniline with a nitrating mixture ($\text{conc. HNO}_3 + \text{conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4$) at $288\text{ K}$ yields an unusual mixture of ortho, meta, and para derivatives:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 \xrightarrow[\text{288 K}]{\text{conc. HNO}_3 + \text{conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4} & \quad p\text{-nitroaniline } (51\%) \\
& + m\text{-nitroaniline } (47\%) \\
& + o\text{-nitroaniline } (2\%)
\end{aligned}
$$

##### The "Meta Anomaly" Explained (CBSE Favourite):
* Aniline is basic. In strongly acidic medium, it undergoes protonation to form the **anilinium ion** ($-\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}_3$).
* The $-\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}_3$ group is strongly **deactivating** and **meta-directing**.
* Therefore, despite $-\text{NH}_2$ being *o/p*-directing, a significant amount ($47\%$) of $m$-nitroaniline is obtained due to nitration of the anilinium ion.

##### Controlled Synthesis of $p$-Nitroaniline:
Protect the $-\text{NH}_2$ group by acetylation before nitration:

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Ac}_2\text{O},\,\text{Pyridine}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NHCOCH}_3 \xrightarrow[\text{288 K}]{\text{conc. HNO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4} p\text{-Nitroacetanilide (major)} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+/\text{H}_2\text{O}} p\text{-Nitroaniline}
$$

***

#### C. Sulfonation & Zwitterion Formation ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Aniline reacts with concentrated sulfuric acid to form anilinium hydrogen sulfate, which on heating with $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ at $453\text{--}473\text{ K}$ produces **$p$-aminobenzenesulfonic acid (sulfanilic acid)** as the major product:

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}_3\text{HSO}_4^- \xrightarrow{453-473\text{ K}} p\text{-NH}_2-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4-\text{SO}_3\text{H}
$$

Sulfanilic acid exists in dynamic equilibrium with its dipolar ion or **Zwitterion**:

$$
\text{NH}_2-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4-\text{SO}_3\text{H} \rightleftharpoons \overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}_3-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4-\text{SO}_3^- \quad \mathbf{(\text{Zwitterion})}
$$

***

#### D. Friedel–Crafts Alkylation and Acylation: The Aniline Anomaly ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> **CRITICAL FACT:** **Aniline does NOT undergo Friedel–Crafts reaction (neither alkylation nor acylation).**

**Reasoning:**
1. Anhydrous aluminum chloride ($\text{AlCl}_3$), used as the Lewis acid catalyst, reacts with basic aniline ($-\text{NH}_2$ with its lone pair) to form an acid-base Lewis salt:

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{AlCl}_3 \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}_2-\overline{\text{Al}}\text{Cl}_3
$$

2. Due to the resulting formal positive charge on nitrogen, the group becomes **powerfully electron-withdrawing (strongly deactivating)**.
3. This deactivates the benzene ring toward attack by an incoming electrophile ($\text{R}^+$ or $\text{RCO}^+$).

***

## 3. High-Yield Examples & 5-Year Solved Board PYQs (2020–2025)

### Example 1: Distinction Problem [CBSE 2024, 3M]
**Question:** Give one simple chemical test to distinguish between each of the following pairs of compounds:
(i) $N,N$-Dimethylaniline and Aniline
(ii) Methylamine and Dimethylamine
(iii) Ethylamine and Aniline

**Solution:**
* **(i) $N,N$-Dimethylaniline and Aniline:**
  * *Test:* **Carbylamine Test**.
  * *Procedure:* Heat both separately with $\text{CHCl}_3$ and alcoholic $\text{KOH}$.
  * *Observation:* Aniline ($1^\circ$ aromatic) gives a foul/offensive smell of phenyl isocyanide. $N,N$-Dimethylaniline ($3^\circ$) does not react.
* **(ii) Methylamine and Dimethylamine:**
  * *Test:* **Hinsberg's Test**.
  * *Procedure:* Treat both with benzenesulfonyl chloride ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\text{Cl}$) followed by excess aqueous $\text{KOH}$.
  * *Observation:* Methylamine ($1^\circ$) forms $N$-methylbenzenesulfonamide, which **dissolves** in aqueous $\text{KOH}$. Dimethylamine ($2^\circ$) forms $N,N$-dimethylbenzenesulfonamide, which is **insoluble** in aqueous $\text{KOH}$.
* **(iii) Ethylamine and Aniline:**
  * *Test:* **Azo-dye Test**.
  * *Procedure:* Dissolve the amine in dilute $\text{HCl}$, cool to $0\text{--}5^\circ\text{C}$ ($273\text{--}278\text{ K}$), add ice-cold $\text{NaNO}_2$ solution, and couple with an alkaline solution of $\beta$-naphthol (2-naphthol).
  * *Observation:* Aniline produces an **orange/red azo dye**. Ethylamine evolves $\text{N}_2$ gas with brisk effervescence and does not form a dye.

***

### Example 2: Mechanism & Reasoning [CBSE 2023, 2M]
**Question:** Account for the following:
(a) Although $-\text{NH}_2$ group is *o, p*-directing in aromatic electrophilic substitution reactions, aniline on direct nitration gives a substantial amount ($47\%$) of $m$-nitroaniline.
(b) Aniline does not undergo Friedel–Crafts alkylation reaction.

**Solution:**
* **(a)** Nitration is conducted using a strong acid mixture ($\text{conc. HNO}_3 + \text{conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4$). Under these strongly acidic conditions, the basic $-\text{NH}_2$ group is protonated to form the **anilinium ion** ($-\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}_3$). The $-\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}_3$ group has a strong $-I$ effect and deactivates the ring, directing incoming electrophiles ($\text{NO}_2^+$) to the **meta position**.
* **(b)** Aniline is a Lewis base containing a lone pair on nitrogen, while the Friedel–Crafts catalyst ($\text{anhydrous AlCl}_3$) is an electron-deficient Lewis acid. They react to form a salt complex: $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}_2-\overline{\text{Al}}\text{Cl}_3$. The positive charge on nitrogen deactivates the benzene ring, preventing electrophilic attack.

***

### Example 3: Synthesis / Conversions [CBSE 2022 Term-2, 2M]
**Question:** How will you convert:
(i) Aniline into Fluorobenzene?
(ii) Benzenamine into 4-Bromoaniline?

**Solution:**
**(i) Aniline to Fluorobenzene (Balz-Schiemann Reaction):**

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 \xrightarrow[\text{HCl},\, 273-278\text{ K}]{\text{NaNO}_2} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- \xrightarrow{\text{HBF}_4} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{BF}_4^- \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{F} + \text{BF}_3 + \text{N}_2
$$

**(ii) Benzenamine to 4-Bromoaniline:**

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 \xrightarrow[\text{Pyridine}]{(\text{CH}_3\text{CO})_2\text{O}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NHCOCH}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{Br}_2/\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}} p\text{-Br}-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{NHCOCH}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+/\text{H}_2\text{O}} p\text{-Br}-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{NH}_2
$$

### Example 4: Chemical Identification Roadmaps [CBSE 2020, 3M]
**Question:** An organic compound $[\text{A}]$ ($\text{C}_3\text{H}_9\text{N}$) on treatment with benzenesulfonyl chloride gives a solid $[\text{B}]$ which is soluble in $\text{KOH}$. Compound $[\text{A}]$ on treatment with $\text{HNO}_2$ gives an alcohol $[\text{C}]$ with the evolution of $\text{N}_2$ gas. Identify $[\text{A}]$, $[\text{B}]$, and $[\text{C}]$, and write all associated equations.

**Solution:**
1. Since compound $[\text{A}]$ ($\text{C}_3\text{H}_9\text{N}$) reacts with benzenesulfonyl chloride to form a precipitate soluble in $\text{KOH}$, it must be a **primary ($1^\circ$) aliphatic amine**.
2. With $\text{HNO}_2$, $[\text{A}]$ evolves $\text{N}_2$ gas to yield alcohol $[\text{C}]$.
3. Therefore:
   * $[\text{A}]$ = Propan-1-amine, $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2$ (or Propan-2-amine)
   * $[\text{B}]$ = $N$-Propylbenzenesulfonamide, $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\text{NHCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3$
   * $[\text{C}]$ = Propan-1-ol, $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$

**Reactions:**

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2 + \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\text{Cl} \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\text{NHCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3 + \text{HCl}
$$

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2 + \text{HNO}_2 \xrightarrow[\text{HCl}]{\text{NaNO}_2} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{OH} + \text{N}_2\uparrow + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

## 4. Examiner Traps & Common Student Mistakes

| S.No. | High-Risk Mistake | Correct Concept & NCERT Prescription |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Assuming Carbylamine test is given by aliphatic $1^\circ$ only. | **Both aliphatic and aromatic $1^\circ$ amines** give the Carbylamine test. Aniline yields phenyl isocyanide ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NC}$). |
| 2 | Saying $3^\circ$ amines form an insoluble precipitate in Hinsberg's test. | $3^\circ$ amines **do not react at all** with $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\text{Cl}$. The starting amine remains unreacted (insoluble in base, but soluble in mineral acid). |
| 3 | Writing direct bromination of aniline to yield $p$-bromoaniline. | Direct bromination in water gives **2,4,6-tribromoaniline** quantitatively. Mono-bromination requires **acetylation protection** first. |
| 4 | Stating $m$-nitroaniline forms because $-\text{NH}_2$ is meta-directing. | $-\text{NH}_2$ is strongly *ortho/para*-directing. The meta product arises because aniline is protonated to the **anilinium ion** ($-\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}_3$) in strong acid. |
| 5 | Writing that aniline undergoes Friedel-Crafts reaction with $\text{AlCl}_3$. | Aniline forms an acid-base adduct with $\text{AlCl}_3$, deactivating the ring; the reaction **fails entirely**. |
| 6 | Omitting temperature in diazotisation. | The temperature must be specified as **$273\text{--}278\text{ K}$ ($0\text{--}5^\circ\text{C}$)**. At higher temperatures, diazonium salts decompose into phenols with evolution of $\text{N}_2$. |

## 5. Speed Hacks & Memory Mnemonics

### A. The "H-Count Rule" for Hinsberg's Test
Count hydrogens on the nitrogen atom of the amine:
* **$1^\circ$ Amine:** Has **$2$ H's** $\rightarrow$ $1$ used to react, $1$ remains on nitrogen $\rightarrow$ **Acidic H present** $\rightarrow$ **Dissolves in alkali**.
* **$2^\circ$ Amine:** Has **$1$ H** $\rightarrow$ $1$ used to react, **$0$ H left on nitrogen** $\rightarrow$ **No acidic H** $\rightarrow$ **Insoluble in alkali**.
* **$3^\circ$ Amine:** Has **$0$ H** $\rightarrow$ **Cannot react at all**.

### B. Mnemonic: "C-I-A" for Isocyanide Test
* **C**arbylamine reaction
* **I**socyanide produced (extremely foul odor)
* **A**ll primary amines only ($1^\circ$ Aliphatic & $1^\circ$ Aromatic)

### C. Mnemonic: "P-A-B-H" for Monobromination
* **P**rotect with **A**cetic anhydride
* **B**rominate ($\text{Br}_2 / \text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$)
* **H**ydrolyze ($\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$) to remove the protecting group.

# Diazonium Salts: Preparation, Chemical Transformations, Coupling Reactions and Synthetic Utility

# MASTER CLASS TEACHING NOTE: DIAZONIUM SALTS
**Target Audience:** Class XII CBSE Board & Competitive Aspirants (JEE/NEET)  
**Unit:** Amines (Organic Compounds Containing Nitrogen)  
**Topic Focus:** Diazonium Salts — Structure, Diazotisation, Chemical Transformations, Azo Coupling Reactions & Synthetic Utility

***

## 1. Pedagogical Theory, Electronic Structure & Stability

### 1.1 Definition & General Formula (Priority: ⭐⭐⭐)
Diazonium salts represent a class of organic compounds having the general structural formulation:

$$
\mathbf{R - N_2^+ X^-}
$$

Where:
* $R$ represents an aryl group ($\text{Ar}-$ or $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-$) or an aliphatic group ($\text{Alk}-$).
* $\text{N}_2^+$ is the diazonium cation ($-\text{N}^+ \equiv \text{N}$).
* $\text{X}^-$ is an inorganic or organic counter-anion such as $\text{Cl}^-$, $\text{Br}^-$, $\text{HSO}_4^-$, or $\text{BF}_4^-$.

***

### 1.2 Comparative Stability: Arenediazonium vs. Alkanediazonium Salts (Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

#### Aliphatic Diazonium Salts ($\text{R}-\text{N}_2^+\text{X}^-$)
* **Thermodynamic & Kinetic Instability:** Primary aliphatic amines on reaction with nitrous acid ($\text{HNO}_2$) form alkanediazonium salts:

$$
\text{R}-\text{NH}_2 + \text{HNO}_2 + \text{HCl} \xrightarrow{273-278\text{ K}} \left[ \text{R}-\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- \right] \xrightarrow{\text{Spontaneous}} \text{R}^+ + \text{N}_2 \uparrow + \text{Cl}^-
$$

* The aliphatic carbocation ($\text{R}^+$) forms immediately due to the extremely high thermodynamic driving force of liberating molecular nitrogen gas ($\Delta G^\circ \ll 0$, very large positive $\Delta S^\circ$, bond dissociation energy of $\text{N}\equiv\text{N}$ is $946\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$).
* The carbocation undergoes solvent capture, elimination, and rearrangement, giving a stoichiometric mixture of alcohols, alkyl halides, and alkenes. Hence, aliphatic diazonium salts cannot be isolated at normal temperatures.

#### Arenediazonium Salts ($\text{Ar}-\text{N}_2^+\text{X}^-$)
* Arenediazonium salts (e.g., Benzenediazonium chloride, $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^-$) are stable in aqueous solution at low temperatures ($273\text{--}278\text{ K}$ or $0\text{--}5^\circ\text{C}$).
* **Origin of Stability:** Delocalisation of the positive charge across the conjugated $\pi$-electron cloud of the aromatic benzene nucleus via resonance.

#### Resonance Structures of Benzenediazonium Cation:

```
    :N≡N:              :N=N:              :N=N:              :N=N:
      |                  ||                 ||                 ||
    // \               // \+              // \               // \
   |    |    <--->    |    |    <--->    |    |+   <--->    +|    |
    \\ /               \\ /               \\ /               \\ /
      +
   (Structure I)      (Structure II)     (Structure III)    (Structure IV)
```

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{I}   &\longleftrightarrow \text{II} \longleftrightarrow \text{III} \longleftrightarrow \text{IV} \\
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\overset{+}{\text{N}}\equiv\text{N} &\longleftrightarrow \text{o-dispersed} \longleftrightarrow \text{p-dispersed} \longleftrightarrow \text{o'-dispersed}
\end{aligned}
$$

The positive charge on the terminal nitrogen atom is partially transferred into the *ortho* and *para* positions of the aromatic ring, creating partial double bond character between the ring carbon and the adjacent nitrogen atom ($\text{C}-\text{N}$ partial double bond). This prevents cleavage of the $\text{C}-\text{N}$ bond at temperatures $\le 278\text{ K}$.

***

### 1.3 Physical Characteristics (Priority: ⭐⭐⭐)
* **Benzenediazonium chloride ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^-$):** A colourless crystalline solid, readily soluble in water. Stable in cold aqueous solution ($273\text{--}278\text{ K}$), but decomposes rapidly and violently when dry; reacts with water when warmed to evolve $\text{N}_2$ gas and form phenol.
* **Benzenediazonium fluoroborate ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{BF}_4^-$):** Insoluble in water and remarkably stable at room temperature due to the non-nucleophilic nature and large lattice enthalpy associated with the bulky tetrahedral $\text{BF}_4^-$ counter-ion. Can be isolated and handled in the dry state.

***

## 2. Preparation: Diazotisation Reaction

### 2.1 The Diazotisation Process (Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
The transformation of a primary aromatic amine into its corresponding diazonium salt by treatment with nitrous acid in the presence of a strong mineral acid at controlled sub-ambient temperatures ($0\text{--}5^\circ\text{C}$) is termed **Diazotisation**.

$$
\mathbf{\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{NaNO}_2 + 2\text{HCl} \xrightarrow{273 - 278\text{ K}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{NaCl} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}}
$$

#### Critical Reaction Variables:
* **Temperature:** Must be strictly maintained within $273\text{--}278\text{ K}$ ($0\text{--}5^\circ\text{C}$). If temperature exceeds $278\text{ K}$ ($> 5^\circ\text{C}$), the diazonium cation undergoes nucleophilic solvolytic attack by water:

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\Delta > 278\text{ K}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{N}_2\uparrow + \text{HCl}
$$

* **Acid Stoichiometry:** An excess of mineral acid (at least 3 moles of $\text{HCl}$ per mole of $\text{ArNH}_2$ practically; minimum 2 equivalents theoretically) is required:
  1. Mole 1: Reacts with $\text{NaNO}_2$ to generate $\text{HNO}_2$ *in situ*.
  2. Mole 2: Converts the weakly basic aromatic amine into its soluble ammonium salt ($\text{ArNH}_3^+\text{Cl}^-$), preventing unreacted free amine from coupling with freshly formed diazonium salt to yield diazoamino compounds ($\text{Ar}-\text{N}=\text{N}-\text{NH}-\text{Ar}$).
  3. Excess: Maintains an acidic pH ($\text{pH} \approx 1\text{--}2$) necessary to suppress decomposition pathways.

***

### 2.2 Detailed Reaction Mechanism (Step-by-Step)

#### Step 1: Generation of the Active Electrophile (Nitrosonium Ion, $\text{NO}^+$)

$$
\text{NaNO}_2 + \text{HCl} \rightleftharpoons \text{HNO}_2 + \text{NaCl}
$$

$$
\text{H}-\ddot{\text{O}}-\ddot{\text{N}}=\text{O} + \text{H}^+ \rightleftharpoons \text{H}-\underset{\text{H}}{\overset{+}{\text{O}}}-\ddot{\text{N}}=\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{H}_2\text{O} + \mathbf{:\!\overset{+}{\text{N}}=\ddot{\text{O}}: \longleftrightarrow :\text{N}\equiv\overset{+}{\text{O}}:}
$$

#### Step 2: Nucleophilic Attack by Free Aniline on Nitrosonium Ion

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\ddot{\text{N}}\text{H}_2 + \overset{+}{\text{N}}=\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\underset{\text{H}}{\overset{\text{H}}{\overset{+}{\text{N}}}}-\text{N}=\text{O}
$$

#### Step 3: Deprotonation to Form N-Nitrosamine

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\underset{\text{H}}{\overset{\text{H}}{\overset{+}{\text{N}}}}-\text{N}=\text{O} \xrightarrow{-\text{H}^+} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\ddot{\text{N}}\text{H}-\ddot{\text{N}}=\text{O} \quad (\text{N-nitrosophenylamine})
$$

#### Step 4: Tautomerisation (Prototropic Shift) to Diazohydroxide

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\ddot{\text{N}}\text{H}-\ddot{\text{N}}=\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\ddot{\text{N}}=\ddot{\text{N}}-\ddot{\text{O}}-\text{H} \quad (\text{Diazohydroxide})
$$

#### Step 5: Acid-Catalysed Dehydration Yielding Diazonium Cation

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\ddot{\text{N}}=\ddot{\text{N}}-\ddot{\text{O}}-\text{H} + \text{H}^+ \rightleftharpoons \text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\ddot{\text{N}}=\ddot{\text{N}}-\overset{+}{\text{O}}\text{H}_2
$$

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\ddot{\text{N}}=\ddot{\text{N}}-\overset{+}{\text{O}}\text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{-\text{H}_2\text{O}} \mathbf{\left[ \text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\overset{+}{\text{N}}\equiv\text{N} \longleftrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\ddot{\text{N}}=\overset{+}{\text{N}} \right]\text{Cl}^-}
$$

## 3. Chemical Transformations

Reactions of diazonium salts are divided into two fundamental mechanistic categories:
1. **Reactions involving displacement/replacement of nitrogen ($-\text{N}_2^+$ group)**
2. **Reactions involving retention of the diazo group ($-\text{N}=\text{N}-$ linkage)**

***

### Category A: Reactions Involving Replacement of Nitrogen

```
                       ┌──> Ar-Cl / Ar-Br  [Sandmeyer / Gattermann]
                       ├──> Ar-I           [KI, Warm]
                       ├──> Ar-F           [Balz-Schiemann: HBF4, Heat]
                       ├──> Ar-CN          [CuCN / KCN]
   Ar-N2+ Cl-  ────────┼──> Ar-H           [H3PO2 + H2O or CH3CH2OH]
                       ├──> Ar-OH          [H2O, Warm to 283-373 K]
                       └──> Ar-NO2         [HBF4, then NaNO2 / Cu, Heat]
```

***

### 3.1 Replacement by Halogen and Pseudohalogen

#### (a) Sandmeyer Reaction (Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
Treatment of freshly prepared arenediazonium salt solution with cuprous salts dissolved in the corresponding halogen acid leads to the extrusion of nitrogen gas and formation of aryl halides or aryl cyanides.

* **Preparation of Chlorobenzene:**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- \xrightarrow{\mathbf{\text{Cu}_2\text{Cl}_2 / \text{HCl}}} \mathbf{\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl}} + \text{N}_2\uparrow
$$

* **Preparation of Bromobenzene:**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- \xrightarrow{\mathbf{\text{Cu}_2\text{Br}_2 / \text{HBr}}} \mathbf{\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Br}} + \text{N}_2\uparrow
$$

* **Preparation of Benzonitrile:**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- \xrightarrow{\mathbf{\text{CuCN} / \text{KCN}}} \mathbf{\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CN}} + \text{N}_2\uparrow
$$

* **Mechanistic Pathway:** Proceeds via a single-electron transfer (SET) mechanism mediated by $\text{Cu(I)}$ generating an aryl free radical:

$$
\text{Ar}-\text{N}_2^+ + \text{Cu}^+ \longrightarrow \text{Ar}^\bullet + \text{N}_2\uparrow + \text{Cu}^{2+}
$$

$$
\text{Ar}^\bullet + \text{Cu}^{2+}\text{Cl}_2 \longrightarrow \text{Ar}-\text{Cl} + \text{Cu}^+\text{Cl}
$$

***

#### (b) Gattermann Reaction (Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐)
Modification of the Sandmeyer reaction wherein copper powder is employed in the presence of corresponding halogen acid instead of cuprous salts.

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- \xrightarrow{\mathbf{\text{Cu} / \text{HCl}}} \mathbf{\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl}} + \text{N}_2\uparrow + \text{CuCl}
$$

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- \xrightarrow{\mathbf{\text{Cu} / \text{HBr}}} \mathbf{\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Br}} + \text{N}_2\uparrow + \text{CuCl}
$$

> **Key NCERT Contrast:** The Sandmeyer reaction provides significantly higher synthetic yields of haloarenes compared to the Gattermann reaction.

***

#### (c) Replacement by Iodine (Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
Iodobenzene cannot be prepared by direct electrophilic iodination because the reaction is reversible, generating oxidisable $\text{HI}$. Neither Sandmeyer nor Gattermann methods are required; simply warming with aqueous potassium iodide is sufficient.

$$
\mathbf{\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{KI} \xrightarrow{\text{Warm / }\Delta} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{I} + \text{KCl} + \text{N}_2\uparrow}
$$

***

#### (d) Replacement by Fluorine: Balz-Schiemann Reaction (Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
Fluorobenzene cannot be prepared by direct electrophilic fluorination due to the extremely exothermic, explosive nature of elemental fluorine ($\text{F}_2$). The Balz-Schiemann reaction is the premier industrial and laboratory synthetic route.

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{HBF}_4 \xrightarrow{273-278\text{ K}} \mathbf{\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{BF}_4^- \downarrow} + \text{HCl}
$$

The precipitated benzenediazonium fluoroborate is isolated, dried, and pyrolysed:

$$
\mathbf{\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{BF}_4^- \xrightarrow{\text{Dry Heat / }\Delta} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{F} + \text{BF}_3\uparrow + \text{N}_2\uparrow}
$$

***

### 3.2 Replacement by Hydrogen (Deamination) (Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
Arenediazonium salts undergo reductive deamination by mild reducing agents, replacing the $-\text{N}_2^+$ group with $-\text{H}$.

#### Reagent 1: Hypophosphorous Acid (Phosphinic Acid, $\text{H}_3\text{PO}_2$)

$$
\mathbf{\text{Ar}-\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{H}_3\text{PO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{Cu}^+} \text{Ar}-\text{H} + \text{N}_2\uparrow + \text{H}_3\text{PO}_3 + \text{HCl}}
$$

* **Oxidation State Change:** Phosphorus is oxidised from $+1$ in $\text{H}_3\text{PO}_2$ (hypophosphorous acid) to $+3$ in $\text{H}_3\text{PO}_3$ (phosphorous acid).

#### Reagent 2: Ethanol ($\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$)

$$
\mathbf{\text{Ar}-\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH} \longrightarrow \text{Ar}-\text{H} + \text{N}_2\uparrow + \text{CH}_3\text{CHO} + \text{HCl}}
$$

* **Oxidation State Change:** Ethanol acts as a hydride donor and is oxidised to ethanal (acetaldehyde).

***

### 3.3 Replacement by Hydroxyl Group ($-\text{OH}$) (Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐)
When an aqueous solution of diazonium salt is allowed to warm up to temperatures above $283\text{ K}$ ($10^\circ\text{C}$), or boiled with dilute sulfuric acid:

$$
\mathbf{\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\Delta > 283\text{ K}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{N}_2\uparrow + \text{HCl}}
$$

> **Examiner Note:** Dilute $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ is preferred over $\text{HCl}$ during diazotisation when the objective is to synthesize phenol. If $\text{HCl}$ is used, competing nucleophilic attack by chloride counter-anions generates chlorobenzene impurity ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl}$).

***

### 3.4 Replacement by Nitro Group ($-\text{NO}_2$) (Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐)
Direct nitration of aniline produces destructive oxidation and meta-derivatives. Pure nitrobenzene or substituted nitro compounds are synthesized cleanly via fluoroborate intermediates:

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{HBF}_4 \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{BF}_4^- + \text{HCl}
$$

$$
\mathbf{\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{BF}_4^- + \text{NaNO}_2 \xrightarrow{\mathbf{\text{Cu}, \Delta}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NO}_2 + \text{N}_2\uparrow + \text{NaBF}_4}
$$

***

## 4. Category B: Azo Coupling Reactions (Retention of $-\text{N}=\text{N}-$)

### 4.1 Nature of the Reaction (Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
Arenediazonium cations act as **weak electrophiles** because the positive charge is delocalized over the two nitrogen atoms and the aromatic ring. Consequently, they react only with highly activated aromatic substrates containing powerful electron-donating groups ($+\text{M}$ groups):
* Phenols and Naphthols ($-\text{OH}$)
* Tertiary and Primary Aromatic Amines ($-\text{NR}_2$, $-\text{NH}_2$)

This electrophilic aromatic substitution ($\text{S}_\text{E}\text{Ar}$) yields extensively conjugated, vividly coloured **Azo Dyes** having the chromophore $-\text{N}=\text{N}-$.

***

### 4.2 Coupling with Phenol (Formation of Orange Dye)

#### Reaction:

$$
\mathbf{\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} \xrightarrow{\mathbf{\text{pH } 9 - 10, \; 273 - 278\text{ K}}} \text{p-H}\ddot{\text{O}}-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4-\text{N}=\text{N}-\text{C}_6\text{H}_5 + \text{Cl}^- + \text{H}_2\text{O}}
$$

* **IUPAC / Common Name:** 4-(Phenyldiazenyl)phenol / *p*-Hydroxyazobenzene
* **Colour:** Bright **Orange Dye**
* **Regiochemistry:** Attack occurs almost exclusively at the **para-position** relative to the $-\text{OH}$ group due to steric hindrance at the *ortho*-positions. If the *para*-position is blocked, coupling proceeds at an *ortho*-position.
* **Rigorous pH Control ($\text{pH} = 9\text{--}10$):**
  * Under mildly basic conditions, phenol ($\text{p}K_a \approx 10$) is converted into the **phenoxide ion** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{O}^-$).
  * The phenoxide ion has a stronger $+M$ effect than the neutral phenol molecule, activating the ring sufficiently to react with the weak diazonium electrophile.
  * **Trap:** If $\text{pH} > 12$ (excessively alkaline), the diazonium cation converts into unreactive diazohydroxide ($\text{Ar}-\text{N}=\text{N}-\text{OH}$) and unreactive diazotate ion ($\text{Ar}-\text{N}=\text{N}-\text{O}^-$), halting coupling:

$$
\text{Ar}-\text{N}_2^+ + 2\text{OH}^- \rightleftharpoons \text{Ar}-\text{N}=\text{N}-\text{O}^- + \text{H}_2\text{O} \quad (\text{Electrophilically Inactive})
$$

***

### 4.3 Coupling with Aniline (Formation of Yellow Dye)

#### Reaction:

$$
\mathbf{\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 \xrightarrow{\mathbf{\text{pH } 4 - 5, \; 273 - 278\text{ K}}} \text{p-H}_2\ddot{\text{N}}-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4-\text{N}=\text{N}-\text{C}_6\text{H}_5 + \text{HCl}}
$$

* **IUPAC / Common Name:** 4-(Phenyldiazenyl)aniline / *p*-Aminoazobenzene
* **Colour:** Brilliant **Yellow Dye**
* **Rigorous pH Control ($\text{pH} = 4\text{--}5$):**
  * Under mildly acidic conditions, the aniline nitrogen is non-protonated in sufficient concentration to maintain activation via its lone pair.
  * **Trap:** If the solution is strongly acidic ($\text{pH} < 3$), aniline is completely protonated into the anilinium ion ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_3^+$). The anilinium ion has a $-I$ effect and no unshared electron pair, deactivating the aromatic ring toward electrophilic attack.

***

### 4.4 Summary Table: Azo Coupling Conditions

| Substrate | Coupling Partner | pH Range | Medium | Product Formed | Product Colour |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^-$ | Phenol | **$9 - 10$** | Mildly Alkaline | *p*-Hydroxyazobenzene | **Orange** |
| $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^-$ | Aniline | **$4 - 5$** | Mildly Acidic | *p*-Aminoazobenzene | **Yellow** |

## 5. Synthetic Utility of Arenediazonium Salts in Organic Synthesis

Arenediazonium salts are central synthetic intermediates because the $-\text{N}_2^+$ group is one of the best leaving groups known. The elimination of $\text{N}_2$ drives these substitutions thermodynamically.

```
                                  ┌──> o-Bromotoluene / p-Bromotoluene
                                  ├──> 1,3,5-Tribromobenzene
   Synthetic Pathways via         ├──> 3-Bromotoluene (meta-directing synthesis)
   Diazonium Intermediates ───────┼──> Fluorobenzene & Iodobenzene
                                  ├──> Benzonitrile -> Benzoic Acid
                                  └──> Biphenyl (Gomberg-Bachmann Reaction)
```

### Key Synthetic Routings Impossible via Direct Electrophilic Substitution:

#### Synthesis of 1,3,5-Tribromobenzene from Aniline (Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
Direct bromination of benzene cannot yield symmetrical 1,3,5-tribromobenzene because $-\text{Br}$ is *ortho/para*-directing and deactivating.

```
Aniline (C6H5NH2)
    │
    │  Br2(aq) / Excess Bromine Water
    ▼
2,4,6-Tribromoaniline
    │
    │  NaNO2 + HCl, 273-278 K (Diazotisation)
    ▼
2,4,6-Tribromobenzenediazonium chloride
    │
    │  H3PO2 + H2O, Cu+ (Reductive Deamination)
    ▼
1,3,5-Tribromobenzene
```

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + 3\text{Br}_2\text{(aq)} &\longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_2(\text{Br})_3\text{NH}_2 \quad (2,4,6\text{-tribromoaniline}) \\
\text{C}_6\text{H}_2(\text{Br})_3\text{NH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{NaNO}_2 + \text{HCl}, \, 273-278\text{ K}} &\text{C}_6\text{H}_2(\text{Br})_3\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- \\
\text{C}_6\text{H}_2(\text{Br})_3\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{PO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}} &\mathbf{1,3,5\text{-C}_6\text{H}_3\text{Br}_3} + \text{N}_2\uparrow + \text{H}_3\text{PO}_3 + \text{HCl}
\end{aligned}
$$

***

#### Synthesis of *m*-Bromotoluene from Toluene (Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
Direct bromination of toluene produces *o*- and *p*-bromotoluene. Synthesizing the *meta*-isomer requires a directing/directable amino group that is subsequently removed.

```
Toluene
    │  HNO3 (conc.) + H2SO4 (conc.), 330 K
    ▼
p-Nitrotoluene  (separated from ortho isomer)
    │  Sn / HCl or Fe / HCl (Reduction)
    ▼
p-Toluidine (4-methylaniline)
    │  (CH3CO)2O / Pyridine (Acetylation - Protection)
    ▼
4-Methylacetanilide
    │  Br2 / CH3COOH (Bromination ortho to -NHAc)
    ▼
2-Bromo-4-methylacetanilide
    │  H3O+ / Heat (Deprotection)
    ▼
2-Bromo-4-methylaniline (3-bromo-4-aminotoluene)
    │  NaNO2 + HCl, 273-278 K
    ▼
2-Bromo-4-methylbenzenediazonium chloride
    │  H3PO2 + H2O or CH3CH2OH
    ▼
m-Bromotoluene (3-Bromotoluene)
```

## 6. Solved 5-Year Board PYQs (2020–2025)

### Problem 1 [CBSE Board 2024, 3 Marks]
**(a)** How will you convert Aniline to Chlorobenzene? Name the intermediate formed.  
**(b)** Why is the dipole moment of Chlorobenzene lower than that of Cyclohexyl chloride?

#### Model Solution:
**(a)**  
* **Step 1: Diazotisation:** Aniline is treated with $\text{NaNO}_2$ and excess concentrated $\text{HCl}$ at $273\text{--}278\text{ K}$ ($0\text{--}5^\circ\text{C}$):

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{NaNO}_2 + 2\text{HCl} \xrightarrow{273-278\text{ K}} \mathbf{\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^-} + \text{NaCl} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

*Intermediate Name:* **Benzenediazonium chloride** `[1 Mark]`

* **Step 2: Sandmeyer Reaction:** The cold diazonium salt solution is treated with cuprous chloride dissolved in $\text{HCl}$:

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- \xrightarrow{\text{Cu}_2\text{Cl}_2 / \text{HCl}} \mathbf{\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl}} + \text{N}_2\uparrow \quad `[1 \text{ Mark}]`
$$

**(b)**  
1. In chlorobenzene, the $\text{C}-\text{Cl}$ bond carbon is $\mathbf{sp^2}$-hybridised with higher $s$-character ($33.3\%$), making it more electronegative and less electron-releasing toward chlorine than the $\mathbf{sp^3}$-hybridised carbon ($25\%$ $s$-character) in cyclohexyl chloride. This shortens and strengthens the $\text{C}-\text{Cl}$ bond in chlorobenzene.
2. Delocalisation of the chlorine lone pairs into the aromatic $\pi$-system imparts partial double bond character to the $\text{C}-\text{Cl}$ bond, shifting electron density back toward the ring and opposing the inductive dipole. Hence, the net dipole moment ($\mu = q \times d$) of chlorobenzene ($1.69\text{ D}$) is lower than that of cyclohexyl chloride ($1.86\text{ D}$). `[1 Mark]`

***

### Problem 2 [CBSE Board 2023, 2 Marks]
Give reasons for the following:
**(i)** Diazonium salts of aromatic amines are more stable than those of aliphatic amines.  
**(ii)** Azo coupling of benzenediazonium chloride with phenol is carried out in mildly alkaline medium ($\text{pH } 9\text{--}10$).

#### Model Solution:
**(i)** In arenediazonium ions, the positive charge on the terminal nitrogen atom is stabilized by resonance delocalisation across the aromatic $\pi$-electron ring system, imparting partial double bond character to the $\text{C}-\text{N}$ bond. In aliphatic diazonium ions, no resonance stabilization exists; the $-\text{N}_2^+$ group departs spontaneously at all accessible temperatures to yield an alkyl carbocation and $\text{N}_2$ gas. `[1 Mark]`

**(ii)** Benzenediazonium cation is a weak electrophile and cannot directly attack neutral phenol at an acceptable rate. In a mildly alkaline medium ($\text{pH } 9\text{--}10$), phenol is deprotonated into the **phenoxide ion** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{O}^-$), which has a much stronger $+M$ activating effect on the ring, enabling electrophilic attack. `[1 Mark]`

Compound 'C' on treatment with $\text{NaNO}_2$ and $\text{HCl}$ at $273\text{--}278\text{ K}$ forms 'D'. 'D' on warming with $\text{H}_3\text{PO}_2$ in water produces 'E'. Identify compounds 'A' through 'E' and write all chemical reactions involved.

     $\implies \mathbf{C = \text{Aniline } (\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2)}$.

     $\implies \mathbf{B = \text{Benzamide } (\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CONH}_2)}$.

     $\implies \mathbf{A = \text{Benzoic acid } (\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOH})}$.
   * Compound 'C' (Aniline) undergoes diazotisation with $\text{NaNO}_2 + \text{HCl}$ at $273\text{--}278\text{ K}$ to yield 'D':  
     $\implies \mathbf{D = \text{Benzenediazonium chloride } (\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^-)}$.
   * Compound 'D' on reduction with $\text{H}_3\text{PO}_2/\text{H}_2\text{O}$ undergoes deamination to yield 'E':  
     $\implies \mathbf{E = \text{Benzene } (\text{C}_6\text{H}_6)}$.

2. **Reactions:**
   

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOH} \xrightarrow{\text{NH}_3, \, \Delta} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CONH}_2 \quad (\text{A} \rightarrow \text{B})
$$

   

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CONH}_2 + \text{Br}_2 + 4\text{KOH} \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{K}_2\text{CO}_3 + 2\text{KBr} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \quad (\text{B} \rightarrow \text{C})
$$

   

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{NaNO}_2 + 2\text{HCl} \xrightarrow{273-278\text{ K}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{NaCl} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \quad (\text{C} \rightarrow \text{D})

## 7. Examiner Traps, Structural Caveats & Common Mistakes

```
Common Mistakes Matrix
├── Temperature: Writing room temperature instead of 273-278 K -> Yields Phenol!
├── Reagent Error: Using CuCl2 / CuBr2 instead of Cu2Cl2 / Cu2Br2 (Cu(I), not Cu(II))
├── pH Confusion: Mixing up pH 9-10 (Phenol) and pH 4-5 (Aniline)
├── Direct Methods: Attempting direct F2 (explosive) or I2 (reversible) on benzene
└── Aliphatic Trap: Assuming R-N2+ can be isolated -> Decomposes to R+ + N2
```

### 1. Temperature Omission Penalty
* **Error:** Writing $\text{NaNO}_2 + \text{HCl}$ without specifying temperature, or writing "room temperature / warm".
* **Consequence:** $\text{Ar}-\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^-$ decomposes above $278\text{ K}$ ($5^\circ\text{C}$). Writing $\Delta$ or omitting temperature earns zero marks because the product is phenol ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH}$), not the diazonium salt.
* **Correction:** Always write $\mathbf{273\text{--}278\text{ K}}$ or $\mathbf{0\text{--}5^\circ\text{C}}$ on the reaction arrow.

### 2. Oxidation State of Copper in Sandmeyer vs. Gattermann
* **Error:** Writing $\text{CuCl}_2$ or $\text{CuBr}_2$ for the Sandmeyer reaction.
* **Consequence:** The reaction requires **Copper(I)** ($\text{Cu}^+$), not Copper(II). Writing $\text{CuCl}_2$ is penalized as incorrect chemistry.
* **Correction:** Use $\mathbf{\text{Cu}_2\text{Cl}_2 / \text{HCl}}$ or $\mathbf{\text{CuCl} / \text{HCl}}$.

### 3. pH Cross-Contamination in Coupling Reactions
* **Error:** Coupling phenol in acidic medium or coupling aniline in basic medium.
* **Consequence:** 
  * At acidic $\text{pH}$, phenol is not converted to the reactive phenoxide nucleophile $\implies$ no reaction.
  * At basic $\text{pH}$, aniline remains uncharged, but the diazonium cation converts into unreactive diazotate [$\text{Ar}-\text{N}=\text{N}-\text{O}^-$] $\implies$ no reaction.
* **Correction:** 
  * Phenol Coupling: **$\text{pH } 9\text{--}10$** (mildly basic).
  * Aniline Coupling: **$\text{pH } 4\text{--}5$** (mildly acidic).

### 4. Direct Halogenation Fallacy
* **Error:** Attempting to prepare iodobenzene via $\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{I}_2$ without an oxidant, or fluorobenzene via $\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{F}_2$.
* **Correction:** Always use diazonium salts:
  * $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{I}$ via $\text{ArN}_2^+ + \text{KI (warm)}$.
  * $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{F}$ via the Balz-Schiemann reaction ($\text{HBF}_4$ followed by heat).

$$

* **(b) Failure of Gabriel Phthalimide with Aniline:**  
  * Potassium phthalimide requires nucleophilic aromatic substitution ($S_N2$) attack of phthalimide anion on aryl halide:
    

$$
\text{Ar}-\text{X} + \text{Phthalimide anion} \centernot\longrightarrow \text{No reaction}
$$

  * Aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution under standard conditions because of partial double bond character of the $\text{C}-\text{X}$ bond (due to resonance) and steric repulsion from the aromatic pi-cloud. $[1\text{ Mark}]$

* **(i) Nitration of Aniline and Meta Isomer Formation:**  
  * Nitration is carried out in an acidic medium ($\text{conc. HNO}_3 + \text{conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4$).  
  * In strongly acidic medium, aniline is protonated to form the **anilinium ion**:
    

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{H}^+ \rightleftharpoons \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}_3
$$

  * The $-\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}_3$ group is strongly deactivating and **meta-directing**. Hence, $47\%$ of $m$-nitroaniline is formed alongside $51\%$ $p$-nitroaniline and $2\%$ $o$-nitroaniline. $[1\text{ Mark}]$

* **(ii) Boiling Point Comparison:**  
  * Primary amines ($\text{R}-\text{NH}_2$) have two active hydrogen atoms bonded to electronegative nitrogen, leading to extensive **intermolecular hydrogen bonding**.  
  * Tertiary amines ($\text{R}_3\text{N}$) lack hydrogen atoms bonded to nitrogen and cannot form intermolecular hydrogen bonds with each other (possessing only weak dipole-dipole interactions). Hence, primary amines possess significantly higher boiling points. $[1\text{ Mark}]$

* **(iii) Bromination with Bromine Water:**  
  * In aqueous solution, bromine water promotes ionization of aniline, giving polar conditions where the $-\text{NH}_2$ group acts as a potent activating group due to strong resonance donation ($+R$) of its lone pair into the ring.  
  * This activates positions 2, 4, and 6 so strongly that simultaneous electrophilic substitution occurs at all available ortho and para positions, yielding a white precipitate of **2,4,6-tribromoaniline**:
    

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + 3\text{Br}_2\text{(aq)} \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_2(\text{Br})_3\text{NH}_2\downarrow + 3\text{HBr}

### **Question D1**
**Tags:** [CBSE 2024 / 2025 Model Case Study, 4 Marks] | **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

**Passage:**  
Amines are organic derivatives of ammonia wherein one, two, or three hydrogen atoms are replaced by alkyl or aryl groups. The basic nature of amines is attributed to the presence of an unshared pair of electrons on nitrogen, which can be donated to an electron-deficient electrophile or proton ($\text{H}^+$). The basicity of amines in aqueous media is governed by a subtle combination of the inductive effect ($+I$), steric hindrance of alkyl groups, and solvation energy of the substituted ammonium cation via hydrogen bonding. In the aromatic series, the unshared electron pair on nitrogen is delocalized over the $\pi$-system of the aromatic ring, making aromatic amines considerably less basic than aliphatic counterparts.

Electrophilic aromatic substitution of aniline requires special protective chemistry: the amino group activates the ring strongly, often leading to poly-substitution, while strong mineral acids protonate the nitrogen to generate a meta-directing deactivated anilinium center.

**Sub-questions:**
1. Arrange the following amines in the increasing order of their $pK_b$ values in aqueous medium:  
   

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2, \quad (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{NH}, \quad \text{CH}_3\text{NH}_2, \quad \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NHCH}_3
$$

2. How is the $-\text{NH}_2$ group protected during the monobromination of aniline to obtain $p$-bromoaniline as the major product? Write the reactions involved.
3. **Attempt either sub-part (A) or (B):**  
   (A) Explain why tertiary amines do not undergo acylation reaction with acetyl chloride.  
   **OR**  
   (B) Convert aniline into fluorobenzene via diazonium intermediate. Name the specific reaction involved.

***

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**

#### **1. Order of $pK_b$ Values:**
* **Examiner Concept:** A higher $pK_b$ means lower basicity. Therefore, increasing order of $pK_b$ corresponds to **decreasing basicity**:
  

$$
\text{Basicity: } (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{NH} > \text{CH}_3\text{NH}_2 > \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NHCH}_3 > \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2
$$

  

$$
\text{Increasing order of } pK_b: (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{NH} < \text{CH}_3\text{NH}_2 < \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NHCH}_3 < \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2
$$

* **Marking:** Correct order with reasoning. $[1\text{ Mark}]$

#### **2. Protection of $-\text{NH}_2$ Group (Acetylation):**
* **Procedure:** Aniline is treated with acetic anhydride in pyridine to convert $-\text{NH}_2$ to an acetamido group ($-\text{NHCOCH}_3$):
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + (\text{CH}_3\text{CO})_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{Pyridine}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NHCOCH}_3 + \text{CH}_3\text{COOH}
$$

* The lone pair of nitrogen is delocalized with the carbonyl oxygen atom via resonance:
  

$$
\overset{..}{\text{N}}\text{H}-\text{C}(=\text{O})\text{CH}_3 \longleftrightarrow \overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}=\text{C}(-\text{O}^-)\text{CH}_3
$$

  This decreases the activating influence on the benzene ring. Monobromination with $\text{Br}_2 / \text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$ yields 4-bromoacetanilide, which on acid/base hydrolysis regenerates $p$-bromoaniline:
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NHCOCH}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{Br}_2 / \text{CH}_3\text{COOH}} p\text{-Br}-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{NHCOCH}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+ / \text{H}_2\text{O}} p\text{-Br}-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{NH}_2
$$

* **Marking:** Reaction of acetylation and deprotection steps. $[1\text{ Mark}]$

#### **3. (Option A):**
* Acylation involves nucleophilic substitution with elimination of a molecule of $\text{HCl}$.
* Tertiary amines ($\text{R}_3\text{N}$) lack any replaceable hydrogen atom directly bonded to the nitrogen atom. As a result, while an initial acylammonium complex may form reversibly, it cannot eliminate $\text{HCl}$ to yield a stable amide. $[1\text{ Mark}]$

**OR**

#### **3. (Option B):**
* **Step 1 (Diazotization):**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{NaNO}_2 + 2\text{HCl} \xrightarrow{273\text{--}278\text{ K}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{NaCl} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

* **Step 2 (Schiemann Reaction):**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{HBF}_4 \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{BF}_4^- \downarrow \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{F} + \text{BF}_3 + \text{N}_2\uparrow
$$

* **Reaction Name:** **Balz-Schiemann Reaction**. $[1\text{ Mark}]$

(b) Give reasons:  
  (i) Alkylamines are stronger bases than ammonia.  
  (ii) Aromatic primary amines cannot be prepared by Gabriel phthalimide synthesis.  
  (iii) Aniline does not react with methyl chloride in the presence of anhydrous $\text{AlCl}_3$.  

***

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**

#### **Part (a): Isomers of $\text{C}_3\text{H}_9\text{N}$ [2 Marks]
Four structural isomers exist:

1. **Propan-1-amine ($1^\circ$ Amine):**
   

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\text{NH}_2 \quad [1/2\text{ Mark}]
$$

2. **Propan-2-amine ($1^\circ$ Amine):**
   

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}(\text{NH}_2)-\text{CH}_3 \quad [1/2\text{ Mark}]
$$

3. **$N$-Methylethanamine ($2^\circ$ Amine):**
   

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{NH}-\text{CH}_3 \quad [1/2\text{ Mark}]
$$

4. **$N,N$-Dimethylmethanamine ($3^\circ$ Amine):**
   

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{N}(\text{CH}_3)-\text{CH}_3 \quad [1/2\text{ Mark}]
$$

#### **Part (b): Chemical Reasoning [3 Marks]
* **(i) Alkylamines vs Ammonia:**  
  Alkyl groups ($\text{R}-$) possess an electron-releasing inductive effect ($+I$). This increases electron density on the nitrogen atom and stabilizes the resulting conjugate alkylammonium cation ($\text{R}-\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}_3$) relative to the unsubstituted ammonium cation ($\text{NH}_4^+$), facilitating proton acceptance. $[1\text{ Mark}]$

* **(ii) Inapplicability of Gabriel Phthalimide to Aromatic Amines:**  
  The synthesis relies on nucleophilic attack of the phthalimide anion onto an organic halide:
  

$$
\text{Phthalimide}^- + \text{Ar}-\text{X} \centernot\longrightarrow \text{No substitution}
$$

  Aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution under standard conditions because the lone pair of halogen electrons undergoes resonance delocalization with the benzene ring, imparting partial double-bond character to the $\text{C}-\text{X}$ bond and rendering cleavage extremely difficult. $[1\text{ Mark}]$

* **(iii) Non-occurrence of Friedel-Crafts Reaction on Aniline:**  
  Anhydrous $\text{AlCl}_3$ used as a catalyst is an electron-deficient Lewis acid. Aniline has a lone pair of electrons on its nitrogen atom (Lewis base). Both rapidly react to form an adduct salt:
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\overset{\cdot\cdot}{\text{N}}\text{H}_2 + \text{AlCl}_3 \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}_2-\overline{\text{Al}}\text{Cl}_3
$$

  The resulting formal positive charge on nitrogen exerts an intense $-I$ effect, heavily deactivating the aromatic ring toward further electrophilic attack. $[1\text{ Mark}]$

### A. Basicity Trends in Aqueous Phase: The "213 vs 231" Hack
In aqueous medium, basic strength is dictated by the interplay of **inductive effect (+I)**, **steric hindrance**, and **solvation/hydration energy**. Never derive this in the exam hall; memorize the index:

* **Ethyl group (bulkier):** **$2^\circ > 3^\circ > 1^\circ > \text{NH}_3$** $\rightarrow$ Code: **`231`**
  

$$
\mathrm{(C_2H_5)_2NH > (C_2H_5)_3N > C_2H_5NH_2 > NH_3}
$$

* **Methyl group (smaller):** **$2^\circ > 1^\circ > 3^\circ > \text{NH}_3$** $\rightarrow$ Code: **`213`**
  

$$
\mathrm{(CH_3)_2NH > CH_3NH_2 > (CH_3)_3N > NH_3}
$$

* **Gas Phase / Non-polar solvent:** Pure inductive effect governs:
  

$$
3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ > \mathrm{NH_3}
$$

* **$\mathrm{p}K_b$ vs Basic Strength Rule:**
  

$$
\text{Stronger base} \iff \text{Higher } K_b \iff \text{Lower } \mathrm{p}K_b
$$

  *Shortcut check:* If the question asks for order of **$\mathrm{p}K_b$**, simply determine basic strength and **invert the inequality signs**!

## 2. Examiner Traps & Common Board Evaluation Mistakes

As CBSE examiners, we adhere strictly to the **CBSE Marking Scheme**. Marks are deducted ruthlessly for the following omissions:

### A. Top Deduction Traps in Class 12 Amines
1. **The Temperature Trap in Diazotization:**
   

$$
\mathrm{Ar-NH_2 + NaNO_2 + 2HCl \xrightarrow{273\text{--}278\text{ K} \; (0\text{--}5^\circ\text{C})} Ar-N_2^+Cl^- + NaCl + 2H_2O}
$$

   * **Deduction (-½ to -1 Mark):** Omitting "$273\text{--}278\text{ K}$" or "$0\text{--}5^\circ\text{C}$". Above $5^\circ\text{C}$, benzenediazonium chloride hydrolyzes rapidly to **phenol** ($\mathrm{C_6H_5OH}$) with vigorous evolution of $\mathrm{N_2}\uparrow$.
2. **Hofmann Bromamide Stoichiometry & Reagents:**
   * **Trap:** Writing $\mathrm{Br_2 + KOH}$ on arrow without specifying base, or writing an alkyl amine instead of an amide as starting material.
   * Total base consumption is **$4\text{ moles}$**:
     

$$
\mathrm{R-CONH_2 + Br_2 + 4NaOH \rightarrow R-NH_2 + Na_2CO_3 + 2NaBr + 2H_2O}
$$

   * The resulting amine contains **one carbon less** than the parent amide.
3. **Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis Limitations:**
   * **Trap:** Proposing Gabriel synthesis for aniline ($\mathrm{C_6H_5NH_2}$).
   * **Marking Criteria:** Must explicitly state: *"Aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution ($\mathrm{S_N2}$) with potassium phthalimide anion due to partial double bond character ($\mathrm{C-X}$ resonance) and steric repulsion."*
4. **Friedel-Crafts Reaction Anomaly in Aniline:**
   * **Question:** *"Why does aniline not undergo Friedel-Crafts alkylation/acylation?"*
   * **Examiner Expectation:** Stating only *"it is deactivated"* gets **0 marks**. You must write:
     

$$
\text{Aniline acts as a Lewis base and forms a salt/complex with anhydrous } \mathrm{AlCl_3} \text{ (Lewis acid catalyst): } \mathrm{C_6H_5NH_2 \cdot AlCl_3^-}
$$

     This imparts a strong negative resonance/deactivating effect ($-\mathrm{I}$) on the $-\mathrm{NH_3^+}$ group, preventing electrophilic substitution.
5. **Nitration of Aniline (The $m$-nitro product trap):**
   * Direct nitration with conc. $\mathrm{HNO_3} + \text{conc. } \mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ yields:
     * **$para$ (51%)**
     * **$meta$ (47%)** $\leftarrow$ *Huge examiner favorite!*
     * **$ortho$ (2%)**
   * **Trap:** Forgetting to explain *why* $47\%$ meta-isomer forms: *"In strongly acidic medium, aniline is protonated to form **anilinium ion** ($-\mathrm{NH_3^+}$), which is strongly meta-directing."*

***

### B. General Marking Scheme Protocol
* **For Reasoning Questions:** Always present the structural cause first (e.g., *intermolecular H-bonding*, *electronegativity of N vs O*, *steric hindrance*), followed by the physical consequence.
* **For Distinction Questions:** 
  1. Name of Reagent.

## 3. Golden Points to Remember (NCERT In-Text Lines & High-Yield Truths)

1. **Boiling Point Hierarchy:**
   

$$
\text{Primary Amine} > \text{Secondary Amine} > \text{Tertiary Amine}
$$

   * *Reason:* $1^\circ$ amines have $2$ acidic hydrogens on nitrogen (extensive intermolecular H-bonding); $2^\circ$ has $1$; $3^\circ$ amines have **no hydrogen on nitrogen** (cannot undergo intermolecular H-bonding; only dipole-dipole attractions).
   * Comparison with alcohols: $\mathrm{R-OH} > \mathrm{R-NH_2}$ because oxygen is more electronegative than nitrogen ($\chi_\mathrm{O} = 3.5 > \chi_\mathrm{N} = 3.0$), making $\mathrm{O-H}\cdots\mathrm{O}$ bonds significantly stronger than $\mathrm{N-H}\cdots\mathrm{N}$.
2. **Solubility in Water:**
   * Lower aliphatic amines are soluble in water via intermolecular H-bonds with water molecules. Solubility **decreases** with increasing molecular mass due to expansion of the hydrophobic hydrocarbon part.
3. **Electrophilic Substitution of Aniline: Bromination:**
   * Aniline with bromine water ($\mathrm{Br_2/H_2O}$) yields immediate **white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromoaniline**.
   * To prepare **monobromo (4-bromoaniline)**, the activating power of $-\mathrm{NH_2}$ must be toned down via **acetylation** using acetic anhydride ($\mathrm{(CH_3CO)_2O}$) in pyridine to form acetanilide ($-\mathrm{NHCOCH_3}$), where the lone pair on nitrogen is delocalized onto the carbonyl oxygen via resonance:
     

$$
\mathrm{- \ddot{N}H-C(=O)CH_3 \longleftrightarrow -\overset{+}{N}H=C(-O^-)CH_3}
$$

4. **Coupling Reaction Conditions:**
   * Coupling with **Phenol:** Medium must be **mildly alkaline** ($\mathrm{pH} \approx 9\text{--}10$) $\rightarrow$ produces **Orange dye** ($p$-hydroxyazobenzene).
   * Coupling with **Aniline:** Medium must be **mildly acidic** ($\mathrm{pH} \approx 4\text{--}5$) $\rightarrow$ produces **Yellow dye** ($p$-aminoazobenzene).
5. **Diazonium Salts Stability:**
   * Arenediazonium salts are stable for a short period at $0\text{--}5^\circ\mathrm{C}$ due to resonance dispersion of positive charge across the aromatic $\pi$-system.
   * Alkanediazonium salts ($\mathrm{R-N_2^+Cl^-}$) are **extremely unstable** even at $0^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and decompose quantitatively to liberate $\mathrm{N_2}\uparrow$ gas and carbocations (leading to alcohols, alkenes, halides).
