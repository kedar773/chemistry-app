## 1. Pedagogical Overview & Concept Roadmap

Amines are derivatives of ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$) formed by the progressive replacement of hydrogen atoms by alkyl or aryl groups. In board examinations (CBSE/State Boards) and competitive frameworks (JEE/NEET), the methods of preparation represent a major share of reasoning questions, multi-step conversions, identification of unknowns ($A \to B \to C$), and name reactions.

### Star Priority Matrix

| Preparation Method | Key Substrates & Reagents | Product Regiochemistry / Purity | Board Frequency & Weightage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

## 2. In-Depth Chemical Transformations & Reaction Mechanisms

***

### Method A: Reduction Processes

#### 1. Reduction of Nitro Compounds ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Nitroalkanes and nitroarenes are reduced to their corresponding primary amines using catalytic hydrogenation or active metal-acid reduction.

##### Catalytic Hydrogenation

$$
\text{Ar-NO}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \xrightarrow[\text{or Pt, Ni in Ethanol}]{\text{Pd / C, } 298\text{ K}} \text{Ar-NH}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

##### Chemical Reduction (Metal + Acid)

$$
\text{Ar-NO}_2 + 6[\text{H}] \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{Fe} + \text{conc. HCl}} \text{Ar-NH}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

$$
\text{Ar-NO}_2 + 6[\text{H}] \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{Sn} + \text{conc. HCl}} \text{Ar-NH}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

> **Crucial NCERT Key Point (Board Examiner Favorite):**
> **Why is reduction with $\text{Fe} + \text{HCl}$ preferred over $\text{Sn} + \text{HCl}$?**
> 

$$
\text{FeCl}_2 \text{ formed during the reaction undergoes hydrolysis to regenerate } \text{HCl}:
$$

> 

$$
> \text{FeCl}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{Fe(OH)}_2 \downarrow + 2\text{HCl}
>
$$

> Hence, only a **small initial aliquot of hydrochloric acid** is required to initiate and sustain the catalytic cycle, making the process cost-efficient and environmentally favorable.

***

#### 2. Reduction of Nitriles (Cyanides) & Isonitriles (Isocyanides) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

##### A. Reduction of Nitriles (Mendius Reaction)
Nitriles yield primary amines containing **one more carbon atom** than the parent haloalkane from which the nitrile was derived.

* **Reagents:** 
  1. Catalytic: $\text{H}_2 / \text{Ni}$ or $\text{H}_2 / \text{Pd-C}$
  2. Chemical: Sodium amalgam and ethanol ($\text{Na(Hg)} / \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$)
  3. Hydride Donor: $\text{LiAlH}_4$ in anhydrous ether, followed by acidic workup ($\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$)

$$
\text{R-C}\equiv\text{N} + 4[\text{H}] \xrightarrow{\text{LiAlH}_4 \text{ / ether or } \text{H}_2/\text{Ni}} \text{R-CH}_2\text{-NH}_2
$$

* **Ascent of Series Principle:**
  Haloalkane $\xrightarrow{\text{alc. KCN}} \text{R-CN} \xrightarrow{\text{reduction}} \text{R-CH}_2\text{-NH}_2$ adds exactly one $-\text{CH}_2-$ unit to the parent carbon skeleton.

##### B. Reduction of Isonitriles
Reduction of isonitriles (carbylamines) gives **secondary amines** containing a methyl substituent on the nitrogen:

$$
\text{R-N}\equiv\overset{\ominus}{\text{C}} + 4[\text{H}] \xrightarrow{\text{LiAlH}_4 \text{ or } \text{H}_2/\text{Ni}} \text{R-NH-CH}_3
$$

***

#### 3. Reduction of Amides ⭐⭐⭐
Amides undergo reductive deoxygenation on treatment with Lithium Aluminium Hydride ($\text{LiAlH}_4$), retaining the exact original carbon count.

$$
\text{R-CONH}_2 \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) } \text{H}_2\text{O}]{\text{(i) } \text{LiAlH}_4 \text{ / dry ether}} \text{R-CH}_2\text{-NH}_2 + [\text{Al and Li salts}]
$$

* **Contrast Example:**
  - $\text{CH}_3\text{CONH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{LiAlH}_4} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2$ (Ethanamide $\to$ Ethanamine: **Carbon count preserved = 2**)
  - $\text{CH}_3\text{CONH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Br}_2 / \text{NaOH}} \text{CH}_3\text{NH}_2$ (Ethanamide $\to$ Methanamine: **Carbon count degraded = 1**)

### Method B: Ammonolysis of Alkyl Halides (Hoffmann's Ammonolysis) ⭐⭐⭐

Ammonolysis is a nucleophilic substitution reaction ($\text{S}_\text{N}2$) where the carbon–halogen ($\text{C}-\text{X}$) bond is cleaved by an ammonia molecule acting as an uncharged nucleophile.

#### Stepwise Substitution Mechanism

1. **Formation of primary substituted ammonium salt:**

$$
\text{H}_3\text{N}: + \text{R}-\text{X} \longrightarrow \left[ \text{R}-\overset{\oplus}{\text{NH}}_3 \right]\text{X}^\ominus
$$

2. **Liberation of primary amine:**

$$
\left[ \text{R}-\overset{\oplus}{\text{NH}}_3 \right]\text{X}^\ominus + \text{NH}_3 \rightleftharpoons \text{R}-\text{NH}_2 + \text{NH}_4^\oplus\text{X}^\ominus
$$

3. **Subsequent alkylations:** Because the resulting primary amine ($\text{R-NH}_2$) has a lone pair and the alkyl group ($\text{R}-$) has an electron-donating inductive effect ($+I$), $\text{R-NH}_2$ is **more nucleophilic** than ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$). Consequently, it attacks remaining $\text{R-X}$:

$$
\text{R-NH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{R-X}} \text{R}_2\text{NH} \cdot \text{HX} \xrightarrow{\text{NH}_3} \underset{\text{Secondary amine}}{\text{R}_2\text{NH}}
$$

$$
\text{R}_2\text{NH} \xrightarrow{\text{R-X}} \text{R}_3\text{N} \cdot \text{HX} \xrightarrow{\text{NH}_3} \underset{\text{Tertiary amine}}{\text{R}_3\text{N}}
$$

$$
\text{R}_3\text{N} + \text{R-X} \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Quaternary ammonium salt}}{\left[ \text{R}_4\overset{\oplus}{\text{N}} \right]\text{X}^\ominus}
$$

#### Reactivity Trends & Reaction Conditions
- **Order of reactivity of halides:**
  

$$
\text{R-I} > \text{R-Br} > \text{R-Cl} \quad (\text{governed by C-X bond dissociation enthalpy})
$$

- **Reaction Conditions:** Ethanolic solution of ammonia heated in a sealed glass tube at $373\text{ K}$.
- **Controlling Product Distribution:**
  - **Excess $\text{NH}_3$:** Shifts equilibrium toward **primary amine** ($\text{R-NH}_2$) as the major product.
  - **Excess $\text{R-X}$:** Yields predominantly **quaternary ammonium salt** ($\text{R}_4\text{N}^+\text{X}^-$).
- **Limitation:** Aryl halides ($\text{Ar-X}$) and vinyl halides do **not** undergo ammonolysis under normal conditions due to partial double-bond character from resonance and $sp^2$-hybridization of the halogen-bearing carbon.

### Method C: Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

This method is dedicated to the preparation of **pure aliphatic primary amines**. It completely eliminates the formation of secondary or tertiary amines and quaternary ammonium salts.

#### Stepwise Reaction Pathway

```
      O                      O                          O
      ||                     ||                         ||
    C                      C                          C
   / \                    / \                        / \
Ar    N-H  + KOH(alc) -> Ar   N:⁻ K⁺  + R-X (S_N2) -> Ar   N-R + KX
   \ /                    \ /                        \ /
    C                      C                          C
    ||                     ||                         ||
    O                      O                          O
Phthalimide         Potassium phthalimide       N-Alkylphthalimide
                                                      |
                                                      | + 2 NaOH (aq), heat
                                                      v
                                            O
                                            ||
                                          C-O⁻ Na⁺
                                         /
                                       Ar           +  R-NH₂
                                         \             (Pure 1° Amine)
                                          C-O⁻ Na⁺
                                            ||
                                            O
                                       Sodium phthalate
```

#### Detailed Equations

1. **Deprotonation of Phthalimide:**
   The hydrogen attached to nitrogen in phthalimide is acidic ($\text{p}K_a \approx 8.3$) because the conjugate base anion is resonance-stabilized by two strongly electron-withdrawing carbonyl ($-\text{C}=\text{O}$) groups.

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_4(\text{CO})_2\text{NH} + \text{KOH (alc.)} \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_4(\text{CO})_2\text{N}^\ominus \text{K}^\oplus + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

2. **Nucleophilic Substitution ($\text{S}_\text{N}2$ Attack):**
   The nucleophilic phthalimide anion attacks a primary alkyl halide:

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_4(\text{CO})_2\text{N}^\ominus \text{K}^\oplus + \text{R-X} \xrightarrow{\text{S}_\text{N}2} \text{C}_6\text{H}_4(\text{CO})_2\text{N-R} + \text{KX}
$$

3. **Alkaline Hydrolysis (or Hydrazinolysis):**
   The $N$-alkylphthalimide is cleaved under basic aqueous conditions to yield the carboxylate salt and liberate pure primary amine:

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_4(\text{CO})_2\text{N-R} + 2\text{NaOH (aq)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{C}_6\text{H}_4(\text{COONa})_2 + \text{R-NH}_2
$$

#### Major Conceptual Exceptions & Board Traps
1. **Aromatic primary amines (e.g., Aniline) CANNOT be synthesized by this method:**
   - **Reason:** Aryl halides ($\text{Ar-X}$) do not undergo nucleophilic substitution ($\text{S}_\text{N}2$) with the phthalimide anion due to:
     1. Partial double bond character of the $\text{C}-\text{X}$ bond via resonance.
     2. Steric hindrance preventing backside attack.
     3. Instability of the hypothetical phenyl cation.
2. **Tertiary alkyl halides ($3^\circ$ R-X) CANNOT be used:**
   - The phthalimide anion acts as a base rather than a nucleophile, causing **dehydrohalogenation (elimination)** to yield an **alkene** instead of substitution.

### Method D: Hoffmann Bromamide Degradation Reaction ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

This degradation reaction converts a primary acid amide into an aliphatic or aromatic primary amine containing **one carbon atom fewer** than the starting amide.

#### Overall Balanced Stoichiometric Equation

$$
\text{R-CONH}_2 + \text{Br}_2 + 4\text{NaOH} \longrightarrow \text{R-NH}_2 + \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 + 2\text{NaBr} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

$$
\text{Alternative with Potassium Hydroxide:}
$$

$$
\text{R-CONH}_2 + \text{Br}_2 + 4\text{KOH} \longrightarrow \text{R-NH}_2 + \text{K}_2\text{CO}_3 + 2\text{KBr} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

#### Detailed Step-by-Step Reaction Mechanism

##### Step 1: Base-catalyzed Deprotonation & Bromination
The base abstracts an acidic amide proton to generate an amidate ion, which then attacks molecular bromine:

$$
\text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{NH}_2 + \text{OH}^\ominus \rightleftharpoons \left[\text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\overset{\ominus}{\text{N}}\text{H}\right] + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

$$
\left[\text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\overset{\ominus}{\text{N}}\text{H}\right] + \text{Br}-\text{Br} \longrightarrow \underset{\text{N-bromoamide}}{\text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{NH-Br}} + \text{Br}^\ominus
$$

##### Step 2: Second Deprotonation to form Bromoamidate Anion

$$
\text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\text{NH-Br} + \text{OH}^\ominus \longrightarrow \left[\text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\overset{\ominus}{\text{N}}-\text{Br}\right] + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

##### Step 3: Concerted Intramolecular Rearrangement (Rate Determining Step - RDS)
The alkyl/aryl group $\text{R}$ migrates from the carbonyl carbon to the electron-deficient nitrogen atom with simultaneous loss of the bromide leaving group, generating an **alkyl isocyanate**:

$$
\left[\text{R}-\overset{\overset{\text{O}}{\parallel}}{\text{C}}-\overset{\ominus}{\text{N}}-\text{Br}\right] \xrightarrow[\text{Rearrangement}]{\text{Slow (r.d.s.)}} \underset{\text{Alkyl isocyanate}}{\text{O}=\text{C}=\text{N}-\text{R}} + \text{Br}^\ominus
$$

> **Stereochemical Note:** If the migrating alkyl group is chiral, it migrates with **complete retention of configuration** because the migration is strictly concerted (intramolecular 1,2-shift).

##### Step 4: Alkaline Hydrolysis of Isocyanate
The isocyanate reacts with hydroxide ion to form an unstable carbamate intermediate, which decarboxylates to yield the primary amine:

$$
\text{O}=\text{C}=\text{N}-\text{R} + \text{OH}^\ominus \longrightarrow \left[ \text{R}-\text{NH}-\text{COO} \right]^\ominus
$$

$$
\left[ \text{R}-\text{NH}-\text{COO} \right]^\ominus + \text{OH}^\ominus \longrightarrow \text{R}-\text{NH}_2 + \text{CO}_3^{2\ominus}
$$

Overall, with $\text{Na}^+$ counterions:

$$
\text{CO}_3^{2\ominus} + 2\text{Na}^\oplus \longrightarrow \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3
$$

## 3. High-Yield Board Exam PYQs (2020 – 2025)

***

### Question 1 [CBSE Board 2024, 3 Marks]
**(a)** Write the chemical equation for the preparation of ethanamine from:
1. Acetonitrile
2. Acetamide

**(b)** Why cannot aniline be prepared by Gabriel Phthalimide synthesis?

#### Solution
**(a)**
1. **From Acetonitrile ($\text{CH}_3\text{CN}$):**
   Reduction of ethanenitrile using catalytic hydrogenation or $\text{LiAlH}_4$:
   

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{C}\equiv\text{N} + 4[\text{H}] \xrightarrow{\text{LiAlH}_4 \text{ / dry ether}} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2
$$

2. **From Acetamide ($\text{CH}_3\text{CONH}_2$):**
   Reduction of ethanamide using $\text{LiAlH}_4$ (Preserves carbon count: $2\text{C} \to 2\text{C}$):
   

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CONH}_2 \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) } \text{H}_2\text{O}]{\text{(i) } \text{LiAlH}_4 \text{ / ether}} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2
$$

   *(Note: Hoffmann degradation on acetamide would yield methanamine, not ethanamine).*

**(b)**
In Gabriel phthalimide synthesis, the reaction involves an $\text{S}_\text{N}2$ nucleophilic displacement of a halide ion by the potassium phthalimide anion. Haloarenes (e.g., chlorobenzene) do **not** undergo nucleophilic substitution with potassium phthalimide under mild conditions because the carbon-halogen bond possesses partial double bond character due to resonance with the benzene ring, and the ring prevents nucleophilic backside attack.

### Question 2 [CBSE Board 2023, 2 Marks]
An aromatic compound 'A' on treatment with aqueous ammonia and heating forms compound 'B', which on heating with $\text{Br}_2$ and $\text{KOH}$ forms compound 'C' of molecular formula $\text{C}_6\text{H}_7\text{N}$. 
1. Identify compounds 'A', 'B', and 'C'.
2. Write the chemical equation for the conversion of 'B' to 'C'.

#### Solution
1. **Identification:**
   - Compound 'C' has formula $\text{C}_6\text{H}_7\text{N}$, which corresponds to **Aniline** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2$).
   - Compound 'C' is obtained by treating 'B' with $\text{Br}_2/\text{KOH}$ (Hoffmann Bromamide Degradation). Hence, 'B' must be a primary amide with one more carbon than aniline, which is **Benzamide** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CONH}_2$).
   - Compound 'B' is formed by heating compound 'A' with aqueous ammonia. Thus, 'A' is **Benzoic acid** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOH}$).

   

$$
\text{A} = \text{Benzoic acid } (\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOH})
$$

   

$$
\text{B} = \text{Benzamide } (\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CONH}_2)
$$

   

$$
\text{C} = \text{Aniline / Benzenamine } (\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2)
$$

2. **Reaction Equation ($\text{B} \to \text{C}$):**
   

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CONH}_2 + \text{Br}_2 + 4\text{KOH} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{K}_2\text{CO}_3 + 2\text{KBr} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

### Question 3 [CBSE Board 2022, Term-II, 3 Marks]
Account for the following:
1. Ammonolysis of alkyl halides is not a suitable method for the preparation of pure primary amines.
2. During the preparation of primary amines by reduction of nitro compounds, $\text{Fe} + \text{HCl}$ is preferred over $\text{Sn} + \text{HCl}$.
3. Gabriel phthalimide synthesis is preferred for synthesizing primary aliphatic amines.

#### Solution
1. **Reason:** The primary amine formed initially contains a lone pair of electrons on nitrogen and an electron-donating alkyl group, making it a stronger nucleophile than ammonia. Consequently, it undergoes further alkylation with unreacted alkyl halide, yielding a complex mixture of $1^\circ, 2^\circ, 3^\circ$ amines and quaternary ammonium salts that are difficult to separate by fractional distillation.
2. **Reason:** The reaction of iron with hydrochloric acid produces $\text{FeCl}_2$, which readily undergoes aqueous hydrolysis to regenerate $\text{HCl}$ ($\text{FeCl}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{Fe(OH)}_2 + 2\text{HCl}$). Thus, only a small catalytic amount of $\text{HCl}$ is required.
3. **Reason:** The phthalimide anion undergoes selective monosubstitution via an $\text{S}_\text{N}2$ mechanism to produce exclusively $N$-alkylphthalimide. Because there are no remaining acidic protons on the nitrogen atom of the intermediate, polyalkylation is physically impossible, yielding uncontaminated $1^\circ$ aliphatic amines.

### Question 4 [CBSE Board 2020, 2 Marks]
How will you convert the following?
1. Ethanoic acid to Methanamine
2. Nitrobenzene to Aniline

#### Solution
1. **Ethanoic acid $\to$ Methanamine (Descent of Series: $2\text{C} \to 1\text{C}$):**
   

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} \xrightarrow{\text{SOCl}_2 \text{ or } \text{PCl}_5} \text{CH}_3\text{COCl} \xrightarrow{\text{excess NH}_3} \text{CH}_3\text{CONH}_2
$$

   

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CONH}_2 + \text{Br}_2 + 4\text{NaOH} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{CH}_3\text{NH}_2 + \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 + 2\text{NaBr} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

   *(Alternatively: $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} \xrightarrow{\text{NH}_3, \Delta} \text{CH}_3\text{CONH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Br}_2/\text{NaOH}} \text{CH}_3\text{NH}_2$)*

2. **Nitrobenzene $\to$ Aniline:**
   

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NO}_2 + 6[\text{H}] \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{Fe / conc. HCl}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 \cdot \text{HCl} \xrightarrow{\text{NaOH}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

## 4. Examiner Traps & Common Student Pitfalls

### Trap 1: Stoichiometric Negligence in Hoffmann Bromamide Reaction
* **Student Error:** Writing $2\text{NaOH}$ or omitting water/carbonates:
  

$$
\text{RCONH}_2 + \text{Br}_2 + 2\text{NaOH} \to \text{RNH}_2 + 2\text{NaBr} \quad \text{(INCORRECT)}
$$

* **NCERT Standard Requirement:** Must explicitly use a **$1:1:4$ molar ratio** for Amide : $\text{Br}_2$ : Base:
  

$$
\text{Amide} + \text{Br}_2 + 4\text{NaOH} \longrightarrow \text{Amine} + \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 + 2\text{NaBr} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

### Trap 2: Reagent Confusion: $\text{LiAlH}_4$ vs $\text{Br}_2/\text{NaOH}$ on Amides
* **Student Error:** Using Hoffmann bromamide to prepare an amine with the *same* number of carbons.
* **Correction:**
  - Need same number of carbons ($n \to n$)? Use $\text{LiAlH}_4$ in dry ether.
  - Need one less carbon ($n \to n-1$)? Use Hoffmann degradation ($\text{Br}_2 / \text{NaOH}$).

### Trap 3: Substrate Suitability for Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis
* **Student Error:** Choosing chlorobenzene or bromobenzene to synthesize aniline or $p$-toluidine using Gabriel Phthalimide.
* **Examiner Rule:** Zero marks awarded. Gabriel Phthalimide synthesis is strictly limited to **aliphatic primary amines with $1^\circ$ or unhindered $2^\circ$ halides**. It fails with aromatic halides and gives elimination products (alkenes) with $3^\circ$ alkyl halides.

### Trap 4: Workup of Aniline Produced via Acidic Reduction
* **Subtle Point:** When nitrobenzene is reduced by $\text{Fe/HCl}$ or $\text{Sn/HCl}$, aniline is formed initially as its hydrochloride salt:
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_3^\oplus \text{Cl}^\ominus
$$

  It requires treatment with a base ($\text{NaOH}$) to liberate free aniline base ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2$).

## 5. Speed Hacks & Golden Memory Mnemonics

### Carbon Count Decision Tree

```
Need to prepare an amine? Count the target carbons relative to the precursor:

  Target carbons = Precursor carbons + 1  (Ascent / Step-Up)
  └── Convert Precursor to Halide -> R-X + KCN(alc) -> R-CN -> Reduce (LiAlH4 or H2/Ni)

  Target carbons = Precursor carbons      (Chain Preservation)
  ├── Aliphatic: R-X + Potassium Phthalimide -> Hydrolysis  [Gabriel Synthesis]
  ├── Reduction of Amides: R-CONH2 + LiAlH4 -> R-CH2-NH2
  └── Reduction of Nitro: R-NO2 + Fe/HCl -> R-NH2

  Target carbons = Precursor carbons - 1  (Descent / Step-Down)
  └── Carboxylic Acid -> Amide (R-CONH2) -> Br2 + 4NaOH [Hoffmann Bromamide Degradation]
```

### High-Yield Mnemonics

1. **"G-A-P" Rule for Gabriel Synthesis:**
   - **G**abriel **A**lways **P**refers:
     - **A**liphatic $1^\circ$
     - **P**ure (no polyalkylation)
     - **P**recludes Aromatics ($\text{Ar-X}$ forbidden)

2. **The "1-1-4" Hoffmann Formula:**
   - For every **1** mole of amide, you need **1** mole of halogen ($\text{Br}_2$) and **4** moles of hydroxide base ($\text{NaOH}$ or $\text{KOH}$), losing **1** carbon atom as carbonate ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$).

3. **Reagent Selectivity Quick Reference:**
   - $\text{R-CN} \xrightarrow{\text{LiAlH}_4} \text{R-CH}_2\text{-NH}_2$ ($1^\circ$ Amine)
   - $\text{R-NC} \xrightarrow{\text{LiAlH}_4} \text{R-NH-CH}_3$ ($2^\circ$ Amine)
   - $\text{R-CONH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{LiAlH}_4} \text{R-CH}_2\text{-NH}_2$ ($1^\circ$ Amine, same carbon count)
   - $\text{R-CONH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Br}_2 / 4\text{NaOH}} \text{R-NH}_2$ ($1^\circ$ Amine, 1 less carbon)
