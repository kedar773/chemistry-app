### **Question A1**
**Tags:** [CBSE 2024 (Delhi), 1 Mark] | **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

**Question:**  
Which of the following amines will react with Hinsberg's reagent ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\text{Cl}$) to form a compound that is soluble in an alkali ($\text{KOH}$)?  
(a) $(CH_3)_3N$  
(b) $(CH_3)_2NH$  
(c) $CH_3CH_2NH_2$  
(d) $(C_2H_5)_3N$

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
* **Correct Option:** **(c)** $[1\text{ Mark}]$
* **CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Breakdown:**
  * **Identification of Amine Class:** Hinsberg’s reagent reacts with primary ($1^\circ$) amines to yield an $N$-alkylbenzenesulfonamide possessing an acidic hydrogen attached to nitrogen. $[1/2\text{ Mark}]$
  * **Alkali Solubility Rationale:**
    

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\text{Cl} + \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2 \xrightarrow{-\text{HCl}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\text{NHCH}_2\text{CH}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{KOH}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\text{N}^-\text{K}^+(\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3) + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

    Due to the strong electron-withdrawing effect of the sulfonyl ($-\text{SO}_2-$) group, the hydrogen atom attached to nitrogen is acidic and dissolves in $\text{KOH}$. Secondary amines form insoluble precipitates (no acidic $\text{H}$), and tertiary amines do not react. $[1/2\text{ Mark}]$

### **Question A2**
**Tags:** [CBSE 2024 (All India), 1 Mark] | **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

**Question:**  
The correct order of basic strength of the following amines in an **aqueous solution** is:  
(a) $(CH_3)_3N > (CH_3)_2NH > CH_3NH_2 > NH_3$  
(b) $(CH_3)_2NH > CH_3NH_2 > (CH_3)_3N > NH_3$  
(c) $CH_3NH_2 > (CH_3)_2NH > (CH_3)_3N > NH_3$  
(d) $(CH_3)_3N > CH_3NH_2 > (CH_3)_2NH > NH_3$

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
* **Correct Option:** **(b)** $[1\text{ Mark}]$
* **CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Breakdown:**
  * **Order Formulation:** In aqueous solution, basicity depends on the interplay of inductive effect ($+I$), steric hindrance, and hydration enthalpy of substituted ammonium cations:
    

$$
(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{NH} > \text{CH}_3\text{NH}_2 > (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{N} > \text{NH}_3 \quad (2^\circ > 1^\circ > 3^\circ > \text{NH}_3)
$$

    $[1/2\text{ Mark}]$
  * **Examiner Note:** For ethyl groups, the order changes to $2^\circ > 3^\circ > 1^\circ > \text{NH}_3$. Mention of stability balance awards full credit. $[1/2\text{ Mark}]$

### **Question A5**
**Tags:** [CBSE 2025 Sample Paper / Exam, 1 Mark] | **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

**Question (Assertion-Reason):**  
Given below are two statements labeled as Assertion (A) and Reason (R):  
* **Assertion (A):** Aniline does not undergo Friedel-Crafts alkylation and acylation reactions.  
* **Reason (R):** The amino group ($-\text{NH}_2$) in aniline forms an adduct salt with anhydrous aluminium chloride ($\text{AlCl}_3$) used as a catalyst.  

Select the correct option:  
(a) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).  
(b) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).  
(c) (A) is true, but (R) is false.  
(d) (A) is false, but (R) is true.

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
* **Correct Option:** **(a)** $[1\text{ Mark}]$
* **CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Breakdown:**
  * **Chemical Justification:** Aniline acts as a Lewis base and donates its lone pair to anhydrous $\text{AlCl}_3$ (Lewis acid) forming a salt:
    

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{AlCl}_3 \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}_2\overline{\text{Al}}\text{Cl}_3
$$

    This imparts a strong $-I$ / positive charge on nitrogen, strongly deactivating the ring toward electrophilic attack. Thus, both statements are true and (R) correctly explains (A). $[1\text{ Mark}]$

### **Question A6**
**Tags:** [CBSE 2022 (Term-2), 1 Mark] | **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Question (Assertion-Reason):**  
* **Assertion (A):** Diazonium salts of aromatic amines are more stable than those of aliphatic amines.  
* **Reason (R):** The diazonium ion in benzenediazonium chloride is stabilized by resonance with the benzene ring.

Select the correct option:  
(a) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).  
(b) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).  
(c) (A) is true, but (R) is false.  
(d) (A) is false, but (R) is true.

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
* **Correct Option:** **(a)** $[1\text{ Mark}]$
* **CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Breakdown:**
  * Aliphatic diazonium salts ($\text{R}-\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^-$) are highly unstable and decompose spontaneously into carbocations and nitrogen gas ($\text{N}_2$). Aromatic diazonium salts are stable at low temperatures ($273\text{--}278\text{ K}$) due to dispersal of positive charge over the aromatic ring via resonance structures. $[1\text{ Mark}]$

### **Question B1**
**Tags:** [CBSE 2024 (Delhi), 2 Marks] | **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

**Question:**  
Accomplish the following conversions in not more than two steps:  
(i) Benzoic acid to Aniline  
(ii) Ethanamine to Methanamine  

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
* **(i) Benzoic acid $\longrightarrow$ Aniline:**  
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOH} \xrightarrow{\text{NH}_3, \Delta} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CONH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Br}_2 / \text{aq. or ethanolic NaOH}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2
$$

  * Step 1: Formation of Benzamide ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CONH}_2$) via $\text{NH}_3 / \Delta$ or $\text{SOCl}_2$ followed by $\text{NH}_3$. $[1/2\text{ Mark}]$
  * Step 2: Hoffmann bromamide degradation using $\text{Br}_2 / \text{NaOH}$. $[1/2\text{ Mark}]$

* **(ii) Ethanamine $\longrightarrow$ Methanamine:**  
  

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{HNO}_2 \ (\text{NaNO}_2 + \text{HCl})} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH} \xrightarrow{\text{KMnO}_4 / \text{H}^+} \text{CH}_3\text{COOH} \xrightarrow[\text{(ii) }\text{Br}_2+\text{KOH}]{\text{(i) }\text{NH}_3, \Delta} \text{CH}_3\text{NH}_2
$$

  *(Alternative 2-step direct pathway:)*  
  

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{HNO}_2} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH} \xrightarrow[\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4]{\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7} \text{CH}_3\text{COOH} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{NH}_3} \text{CH}_3\text{CONH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Br}_2 / \text{KOH}} \text{CH}_3\text{NH}_2
$$

  * Diazotization to ethanol, followed by oxidation to ethanoic acid. $[1/2\text{ Mark}]$
  * Amidation followed by Hoffmann bromamide degradation. $[1/2\text{ Mark}]$

### **Question B2**
**Tags:** [CBSE 2023 (All India), 2 Marks] | **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

**Question:**  
Give chemical tests to distinguish between the following pairs of compounds:  
(i) Aniline and Ethylamine  
(ii) Ethylamine and Diethylamine  

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
* **(i) Aniline ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2$) and Ethylamine ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2$):**  
  * **Test:** Azo-dye test. $[1/2\text{ Mark}]$  
  * **Observation & Reaction:** Dissolve compound in dilute $\text{HCl}$, cool to $0\text{--}5^\circ\text{C}$ ($273\text{--}278\text{ K}$), add ice-cold $\text{NaNO}_2$ solution, and couple with alkaline $\beta$-naphthol.  
    * **Aniline** yields an insoluble brilliant **orange/red dye** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{N}=\text{N}-\text{C}_{10}\text{H}_6\text{OH}$).  
    * **Ethylamine** undergoes brisk effervescence of nitrogen gas ($\text{N}_2\uparrow$) with alcohol formation without forming any dye. $[1/2\text{ Mark}]$

* **(ii) Ethylamine ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2$) and Diethylamine ($(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5)_2\text{NH}$):**  
  * **Test:** Carbylamine Test (or Hinsberg Test). $[1/2\text{ Mark}]$  
  * **Observation & Reaction:** Heat with $\text{CHCl}_3$ and alcoholic $\text{KOH}$.  
    

$$
\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{CHCl}_3 + 3\text{KOH} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{NC} + 3\text{KCl} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

    * **Ethylamine ($1^\circ$)** produces an intensely foul-smelling ethyl isocyanide ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{NC}$).  
    * **Diethylamine ($2^\circ$)** does not show this reaction (no offensive odor). $[1/2\text{ Mark}]$

### **Question B3**
**Tags:** [CBSE 2022 (Term-2), 2 Marks] | **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Question:**  
Explain the following observations:  
(a) The $pK_b$ of aniline is much higher than that of methylamine.  
(b) Gabriel phthalimide synthesis is preferred for synthesizing primary aliphatic amines, but cannot be used for preparing aniline.  

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
* **(a) High $pK_b$ of Aniline:**  
  * Higher $pK_b$ denotes a weaker base ($K_b = 10^{-pK_b}$). $[1/2\text{ Mark}]$  
  * In aniline, the unshared pair of electrons on the nitrogen atom is delocalized over the benzene ring via resonance ($+R$ effect), decreasing electron density on nitrogen:
    

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\overset{\cdot\cdot}{\text{N}}\text{H}_2 \longleftrightarrow \text{Resonance structures (ortho/para charge dispersal)}
$$

    In contrast, in $\text{CH}_3\text{NH}_2$, the methyl group exerts an electron-releasing inductive effect ($+I$), increasing electron availability on nitrogen. Hence, aniline is much less basic and has a higher $pK_b$. $[1/2\text{ Mark}]$

* **(b) Failure of Gabriel Phthalimide with Aniline:**  
  * Potassium phthalimide requires nucleophilic aromatic substitution ($S_N2$) attack of phthalimide anion on aryl halide:
    

$$
\text{Ar}-\text{X} + \text{Phthalimide anion} \centernot\longrightarrow \text{No reaction}
$$

  * Aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution under standard conditions because of partial double bond character of the $\text{C}-\text{X}$ bond (due to resonance) and steric repulsion from the aromatic pi-cloud. $[1\text{ Mark}]$

### **Question C2**
**Tags:** [CBSE 2023 (Delhi), 3 Marks] | **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

**Question:**  
Account for the following:  
(i) Direct nitration of aniline yields a significant amount of $m$-nitroaniline along with ortho and para isomers.  
(ii) Primary aliphatic amines have higher boiling points than tertiary aliphatic amines of comparable molecular mass.  
(iii) Although $-\text{NH}_2$ group is ortho- and para-directing in aromatic electrophilic substitution reactions, aniline on bromination with bromine water gives 2,4,6-tribromoaniline precipitously.

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**
* **(i) Nitration of Aniline and Meta Isomer Formation:**  
  * Nitration is carried out in an acidic medium ($\text{conc. HNO}_3 + \text{conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4$).  
  * In strongly acidic medium, aniline is protonated to form the **anilinium ion**:
    

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{H}^+ \rightleftharpoons \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}_3
$$

  * The $-\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}_3$ group is strongly deactivating and **meta-directing**. Hence, $47\%$ of $m$-nitroaniline is formed alongside $51\%$ $p$-nitroaniline and $2\%$ $o$-nitroaniline. $[1\text{ Mark}]$

* **(ii) Boiling Point Comparison:**  
  * Primary amines ($\text{R}-\text{NH}_2$) have two active hydrogen atoms bonded to electronegative nitrogen, leading to extensive **intermolecular hydrogen bonding**.  
  * Tertiary amines ($\text{R}_3\text{N}$) lack hydrogen atoms bonded to nitrogen and cannot form intermolecular hydrogen bonds with each other (possessing only weak dipole-dipole interactions). Hence, primary amines possess significantly higher boiling points. $[1\text{ Mark}]$

* **(iii) Bromination with Bromine Water:**  
  * In aqueous solution, bromine water promotes ionization of aniline, giving polar conditions where the $-\text{NH}_2$ group acts as a potent activating group due to strong resonance donation ($+R$) of its lone pair into the ring.  
  * This activates positions 2, 4, and 6 so strongly that simultaneous electrophilic substitution occurs at all available ortho and para positions, yielding a white precipitate of **2,4,6-tribromoaniline**:
    

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + 3\text{Br}_2\text{(aq)} \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_2(\text{Br})_3\text{NH}_2\downarrow + 3\text{HBr}
$$

    $[1\text{ Mark}]$

### **Question D1**
**Tags:** [CBSE 2024 / 2025 Model Case Study, 4 Marks] | **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

**Passage:**  
Amines are organic derivatives of ammonia wherein one, two, or three hydrogen atoms are replaced by alkyl or aryl groups. The basic nature of amines is attributed to the presence of an unshared pair of electrons on nitrogen, which can be donated to an electron-deficient electrophile or proton ($\text{H}^+$). The basicity of amines in aqueous media is governed by a subtle combination of the inductive effect ($+I$), steric hindrance of alkyl groups, and solvation energy of the substituted ammonium cation via hydrogen bonding. In the aromatic series, the unshared electron pair on nitrogen is delocalized over the $\pi$-system of the aromatic ring, making aromatic amines considerably less basic than aliphatic counterparts.

Electrophilic aromatic substitution of aniline requires special protective chemistry: the amino group activates the ring strongly, often leading to poly-substitution, while strong mineral acids protonate the nitrogen to generate a meta-directing deactivated anilinium center.

**Sub-questions:**
1. Arrange the following amines in the increasing order of their $pK_b$ values in aqueous medium:  
   

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2, \quad (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{NH}, \quad \text{CH}_3\text{NH}_2, \quad \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NHCH}_3
$$

2. How is the $-\text{NH}_2$ group protected during the monobromination of aniline to obtain $p$-bromoaniline as the major product? Write the reactions involved.
3. **Attempt either sub-part (A) or (B):**  
   (A) Explain why tertiary amines do not undergo acylation reaction with acetyl chloride.  
   **OR**  
   (B) Convert aniline into fluorobenzene via diazonium intermediate. Name the specific reaction involved.

***

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**

#### **1. Order of $pK_b$ Values:**
* **Examiner Concept:** A higher $pK_b$ means lower basicity. Therefore, increasing order of $pK_b$ corresponds to **decreasing basicity**:
  

$$
\text{Basicity: } (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{NH} > \text{CH}_3\text{NH}_2 > \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NHCH}_3 > \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2
$$

  

$$
\text{Increasing order of } pK_b: (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{NH} < \text{CH}_3\text{NH}_2 < \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NHCH}_3 < \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2
$$

* **Marking:** Correct order with reasoning. $[1\text{ Mark}]$

#### **2. Protection of $-\text{NH}_2$ Group (Acetylation):**
* **Procedure:** Aniline is treated with acetic anhydride in pyridine to convert $-\text{NH}_2$ to an acetamido group ($-\text{NHCOCH}_3$):
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + (\text{CH}_3\text{CO})_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{Pyridine}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NHCOCH}_3 + \text{CH}_3\text{COOH}
$$

* The lone pair of nitrogen is delocalized with the carbonyl oxygen atom via resonance:
  

$$
\overset{..}{\text{N}}\text{H}-\text{C}(=\text{O})\text{CH}_3 \longleftrightarrow \overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}=\text{C}(-\text{O}^-)\text{CH}_3
$$

  This decreases the activating influence on the benzene ring. Monobromination with $\text{Br}_2 / \text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$ yields 4-bromoacetanilide, which on acid/base hydrolysis regenerates $p$-bromoaniline:
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NHCOCH}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{Br}_2 / \text{CH}_3\text{COOH}} p\text{-Br}-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{NHCOCH}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+ / \text{H}_2\text{O}} p\text{-Br}-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{NH}_2
$$

* **Marking:** Reaction of acetylation and deprotection steps. $[1\text{ Mark}]$

#### **3. (Option A):**
* Acylation involves nucleophilic substitution with elimination of a molecule of $\text{HCl}$.
* Tertiary amines ($\text{R}_3\text{N}$) lack any replaceable hydrogen atom directly bonded to the nitrogen atom. As a result, while an initial acylammonium complex may form reversibly, it cannot eliminate $\text{HCl}$ to yield a stable amide. $[1\text{ Mark}]$

**OR**

#### **3. (Option B):**
* **Step 1 (Diazotization):**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{NaNO}_2 + 2\text{HCl} \xrightarrow{273\text{--}278\text{ K}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{NaCl} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

* **Step 2 (Schiemann Reaction):**
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{HBF}_4 \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{BF}_4^- \downarrow \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{F} + \text{BF}_3 + \text{N}_2\uparrow
$$

* **Reaction Name:** **Balz-Schiemann Reaction**. $[1\text{ Mark}]$

### **Question E1**
**Tags:** [CBSE 2024 / 2023 (Delhi/AI), 5 Marks] | **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

**Question:**  
(a) Write IUPAC names and structures for all the isomeric amines having the molecular formula $\text{C}_3\text{H}_9\text{N}$. Classify them into $1^\circ$, $2^\circ$, and $3^\circ$ amines.  
(b) Give reasons:  
  (i) Alkylamines are stronger bases than ammonia.  
  (ii) Aromatic primary amines cannot be prepared by Gabriel phthalimide synthesis.  
  (iii) Aniline does not react with methyl chloride in the presence of anhydrous $\text{AlCl}_3$.  

***

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**

#### **Part (a): Isomers of $\text{C}_3\text{H}_9\text{N}$ [2 Marks]
Four structural isomers exist:

1. **Propan-1-amine ($1^\circ$ Amine):**
   

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\text{NH}_2 \quad [1/2\text{ Mark}]
$$

2. **Propan-2-amine ($1^\circ$ Amine):**
   

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}(\text{NH}_2)-\text{CH}_3 \quad [1/2\text{ Mark}]
$$

3. **$N$-Methylethanamine ($2^\circ$ Amine):**
   

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{NH}-\text{CH}_3 \quad [1/2\text{ Mark}]
$$

4. **$N,N$-Dimethylmethanamine ($3^\circ$ Amine):**
   

$$
\text{CH}_3-\text{N}(\text{CH}_3)-\text{CH}_3 \quad [1/2\text{ Mark}]
$$

#### **Part (b): Chemical Reasoning [3 Marks]
* **(i) Alkylamines vs Ammonia:**  
  Alkyl groups ($\text{R}-$) possess an electron-releasing inductive effect ($+I$). This increases electron density on the nitrogen atom and stabilizes the resulting conjugate alkylammonium cation ($\text{R}-\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}_3$) relative to the unsubstituted ammonium cation ($\text{NH}_4^+$), facilitating proton acceptance. $[1\text{ Mark}]$

* **(ii) Inapplicability of Gabriel Phthalimide to Aromatic Amines:**  
  The synthesis relies on nucleophilic attack of the phthalimide anion onto an organic halide:
  

$$
\text{Phthalimide}^- + \text{Ar}-\text{X} \centernot\longrightarrow \text{No substitution}
$$

  Aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution under standard conditions because the lone pair of halogen electrons undergoes resonance delocalization with the benzene ring, imparting partial double-bond character to the $\text{C}-\text{X}$ bond and rendering cleavage extremely difficult. $[1\text{ Mark}]$

* **(iii) Non-occurrence of Friedel-Crafts Reaction on Aniline:**  
  Anhydrous $\text{AlCl}_3$ used as a catalyst is an electron-deficient Lewis acid. Aniline has a lone pair of electrons on its nitrogen atom (Lewis base). Both rapidly react to form an adduct salt:
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\overset{\cdot\cdot}{\text{N}}\text{H}_2 + \text{AlCl}_3 \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}_2-\overline{\text{Al}}\text{Cl}_3
$$

  The resulting formal positive charge on nitrogen exerts an intense $-I$ effect, heavily deactivating the aromatic ring toward further electrophilic attack. $[1\text{ Mark}]$

### **Question E2**
**Tags:** [CBSE 2023 / 2022 (Term-2), 5 Marks] | **Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ [Must-Know]

**Question:**  
(a) An organic compound [A] with molecular formula $\text{C}_7\text{H}_7\text{NO}$ on treatment with $\text{Br}_2$ and $\text{KOH}$ gives compound [B], which on reaction with $\text{NaNO}_2 + \text{HCl}$ at $0\text{--}5^\circ\text{C}$ gives [C]. Compound [C] on treatment with cuprous cyanide ($\text{CuCN} / \text{KCN}$) gives compound [D]. Compound [D] upon catalytic reduction with $\text{H}_2 / \text{Ni}$ yields compound [E].  
  (i) Identify compounds [A], [B], [C], [D], and [E].  
  (ii) Write all chemical reactions involved.  
(b) How will you distinguish between a primary, secondary, and tertiary amine using Hinsberg's reagent? Write the chemical equations involved.

***

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**

#### **Part (a): Roadmap Deduction [3 Marks]**
* **Identification:**
  * Compound **[A]**: **Benzamide** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CONH}_2$) $[1/2\text{ Mark}]$
  * Compound **[B]**: **Aniline** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2$) $[1/2\text{ Mark}]$
  * Compound **[C]**: **Benzenediazonium chloride** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^-$) $[1/2\text{ Mark}]$
  * Compound **[D]**: **Benzonitrile** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CN}$) $[1/2\text{ Mark}]$
  * Compound **[E]**: **Benzylamine** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2$) $[1/2\text{ Mark}]$

* **Reactions:**
  1. Hoffmann degradation of [A] to [B]:
     

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CONH}_2 + \text{Br}_2 + 4\text{KOH} \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{K}_2\text{CO}_3 + 2\text{KBr} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

  2. Diazotization of [B] to [C]:
     

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{NaNO}_2 + 2\text{HCl} \xrightarrow{273\text{--}278\text{ K}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{NaCl} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

  3. Sandmeyer reaction of [C] to [D]:
     

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- \xrightarrow{\text{CuCN} / \text{KCN}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CN} + \text{N}_2\uparrow
$$

  4. Complete Reduction of [D] to [E]:
     

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CN} + 2\text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Ni}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2
$$

  * All four equations written with accurate reagents. $[1/2\text{ Mark}]$

#### **Part (b): Hinsberg's Test for Amine Distinction [2 Marks]**
Reagent: Benzenesulfonyl chloride ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\text{Cl}$).

1. **Primary ($1^\circ$) Amine:**  
   Forms an $N$-alkylbenzenesulfonamide that has an acidic hydrogen attached to nitrogen; it reacts with bases to form a water-soluble salt:
   

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\text{Cl} + \text{R}-\text{NH}_2 \xrightarrow{-\text{HCl}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\text{NHR} \xrightarrow{\text{KOH}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\text{N}^-\text{K}^+\text{R} \ (\text{Soluble in alkali})
$$

   $[1/2\text{ Mark}]$

2. **Secondary ($2^\circ$) Amine:**  
   Forms an $N,N$-dialkylbenzenesulfonamide. Since there is no acidic hydrogen bonded to nitrogen, it **does not dissolve in alkali** and remains an insoluble precipitate:
   

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\text{Cl} + \text{R}_2\text{NH} \xrightarrow{-\text{HCl}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\text{NR}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{KOH}} \text{Insoluble (No reaction)}
$$

   $[1/2\text{ Mark}]$

3. **Tertiary ($3^\circ$) Amine:**  
   Possesses no hydrogen atom on nitrogen. It **does not react** with benzenesulfonyl chloride and remains unreacted / insoluble in alkali. $[1\text{ Mark}]$

### **Question E3**

**Question:**  
(a) How are the following conversions carried out? (Any three)  
  (i) Nitrobenzene to Benzoic acid  
  (ii) Benzene to $m$-Bromophenol  
  (iii) Aniline to 2,4,6-Tribromofluorobenzene  
  (iv) Methyl cyanide to Ethanamine  
(b) What happens when:  
  (i) Benzenediazonium chloride is treated with hypophosphorous acid ($\text{H}_3\text{PO}_2$) in the presence of $\text{Cu}^+$ ions?  
  (ii) Aniline is treated with concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ and heated at $453\text{--}473\text{ K}$?

***

**Model Solution & Marking Scheme:**

#### **Part (a): Organic Conversions (3 Marks - 1 Mark each for any three)**

* **(i) Nitrobenzene $\longrightarrow$ Benzoic acid:**  
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NO}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Sn} / \text{HCl}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 \xrightarrow[\text{273--278 K}]{\text{NaNO}_2 + \text{HCl}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- \xrightarrow{\text{CuCN} / \text{KCN}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CN} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+, \Delta} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOH}
$$

  * Accurate succession of reduction, diazotization, cyanation, and complete acid hydrolysis. $[1\text{ Mark}]$

* **(ii) Benzene $\longrightarrow$ $m$-Bromophenol:**  
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 \xrightarrow{\text{conc. HNO}_3 + \text{conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NO}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Br}_2 / \text{FeBr}_3} m\text{-Br}-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{NO}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Fe} / \text{HCl}} m\text{-Br}-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{NH}_2

$$
m\text{-Br}-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{NH}_2 \xrightarrow[\text{273--278 K}]{\text{NaNO}_2 + \text{HCl}} m\text{-Br}-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{O}, \text{warm}} m\text{-Br}-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{OH}
$$

  * Correct routing through meta-bromination before nitro group reduction. $[1\text{ Mark}]$

* **(iii) Aniline $\longrightarrow$ 2,4,6-Tribromofluorobenzene:**  
  

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 \xrightarrow{3\text{Br}_2\text{(aq)}} 2,4,6\text{-Br}_3\text{C}_6\text{H}_2\text{NH}_2 \xrightarrow[\text{273--278 K}]{\text{NaNO}_2 + \text{HCl}} 2,4,6\text{-Br}_3\text{C}_6\text{H}_2\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- \xrightarrow{\text{HBF}_4, \Delta} 2,4,6\text{-Br}_3\text{C}_6\text{H}_2\text{F}

## Senior Examiner Tips & Recurring Marking Patterns for Class 12 Chemistry:
1. **Basicity in Aqueous Phase:** Always memorize the order for methyl ($2^\circ > 1^\circ > 3^\circ > \text{NH}_3$) vs ethyl ($2^\circ > 3^\circ > 1^\circ > \text{NH}_3$).
2. **Friedel-Crafts on Aniline:** Always highlight the Lewis base-Lewis acid adduct formation ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\overset{+}{\text{N}}\text{H}_2\overline{\text{Al}}\text{Cl}_3$).
3. **Nitration of Aniline:** Mentioning the formation of the **anilinium ion** is mandatory to get credit for the $47\%$ meta-isomer formation.
4. **Hinsberg Test:** Always emphasize whether the sulfonamide contains an **acidic hydrogen** on the nitrogen atom to explain its solubility in aqueous alkali.
