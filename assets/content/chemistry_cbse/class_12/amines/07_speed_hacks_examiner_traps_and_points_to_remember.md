## 1. Speed Hacks & Fast Solving Shortcuts

### A. Basicity Trends in Aqueous Phase: The "213 vs 231" Hack
In aqueous medium, basic strength is dictated by the interplay of **inductive effect (+I)**, **steric hindrance**, and **solvation/hydration energy**. Never derive this in the exam hall; memorize the index:

* **Ethyl group (bulkier):** **$2^\circ > 3^\circ > 1^\circ > \text{NH}_3$** $\rightarrow$ Code: **`231`**
  

$$
\mathrm{(C_2H_5)_2NH > (C_2H_5)_3N > C_2H_5NH_2 > NH_3}
$$

* **Methyl group (smaller):** **$2^\circ > 1^\circ > 3^\circ > \text{NH}_3$** $\rightarrow$ Code: **`213`**
  

$$
\mathrm{(CH_3)_2NH > CH_3NH_2 > (CH_3)_3N > NH_3}
$$

* **Gas Phase / Non-polar solvent:** Pure inductive effect governs:
  

$$
3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ > \mathrm{NH_3}
$$

* **$\mathrm{p}K_b$ vs Basic Strength Rule:**
  

$$
\text{Stronger base} \iff \text{Higher } K_b \iff \text{Lower } \mathrm{p}K_b
$$

  *Shortcut check:* If the question asks for order of **$\mathrm{p}K_b$**, simply determine basic strength and **invert the inequality signs**!

### C. Fast Conversions & Chain Length Logic
* **Stepping UP (Add 1 Carbon atom):**
  

$$
\mathrm{R-X} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{KCN/alc.}} \mathrm{R-CN} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{LiAlH_4 \; or \; H_2/Ni}} \mathrm{R-CH_2-NH_2}
$$

* **Stepping DOWN (Remove 1 Carbon atom):**
  

$$
\mathrm{R-CH_2-OH} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{KMnO_4/H^+}} \mathrm{R-COOH} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{SOCl_2}} \mathrm{R-COCl} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{NH_3}} \mathrm{R-CONH_2} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Br_2/NaOH}} \mathrm{R-NH_2}
$$

  *(Hofmann Bromamide eliminates exactly one carbonyl carbon as $\mathrm{Na_2CO_3}$).*

## 2. Examiner Traps & Common Board Evaluation Mistakes

As CBSE examiners, we adhere strictly to the **CBSE Marking Scheme**. Marks are deducted ruthlessly for the following omissions:

### A. Top Deduction Traps in Class 12 Amines
1. **The Temperature Trap in Diazotization:**
   

$$
\mathrm{Ar-NH_2 + NaNO_2 + 2HCl \xrightarrow{273\text{--}278\text{ K} \; (0\text{--}5^\circ\text{C})} Ar-N_2^+Cl^- + NaCl + 2H_2O}
$$

   * **Deduction (-½ to -1 Mark):** Omitting "$273\text{--}278\text{ K}$" or "$0\text{--}5^\circ\text{C}$". Above $5^\circ\text{C}$, benzenediazonium chloride hydrolyzes rapidly to **phenol** ($\mathrm{C_6H_5OH}$) with vigorous evolution of $\mathrm{N_2}\uparrow$.
2. **Hofmann Bromamide Stoichiometry & Reagents:**
   * **Trap:** Writing $\mathrm{Br_2 + KOH}$ on arrow without specifying base, or writing an alkyl amine instead of an amide as starting material.
   * Total base consumption is **$4\text{ moles}$**:
     

$$
\mathrm{R-CONH_2 + Br_2 + 4NaOH \rightarrow R-NH_2 + Na_2CO_3 + 2NaBr + 2H_2O}
$$

   * The resulting amine contains **one carbon less** than the parent amide.
3. **Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis Limitations:**
   * **Trap:** Proposing Gabriel synthesis for aniline ($\mathrm{C_6H_5NH_2}$).
   * **Marking Criteria:** Must explicitly state: *"Aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution ($\mathrm{S_N2}$) with potassium phthalimide anion due to partial double bond character ($\mathrm{C-X}$ resonance) and steric repulsion."*
4. **Friedel-Crafts Reaction Anomaly in Aniline:**
   * **Question:** *"Why does aniline not undergo Friedel-Crafts alkylation/acylation?"*
   * **Examiner Expectation:** Stating only *"it is deactivated"* gets **0 marks**. You must write:
     

$$
\text{Aniline acts as a Lewis base and forms a salt/complex with anhydrous } \mathrm{AlCl_3} \text{ (Lewis acid catalyst): } \mathrm{C_6H_5NH_2 \cdot AlCl_3^-}
$$

     This imparts a strong negative resonance/deactivating effect ($-\mathrm{I}$) on the $-\mathrm{NH_3^+}$ group, preventing electrophilic substitution.
5. **Nitration of Aniline (The $m$-nitro product trap):**
   * Direct nitration with conc. $\mathrm{HNO_3} + \text{conc. } \mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ yields:
     * **$para$ (51%)**
     * **$meta$ (47%)** $\leftarrow$ *Huge examiner favorite!*
     * **$ortho$ (2%)**
   * **Trap:** Forgetting to explain *why* $47\%$ meta-isomer forms: *"In strongly acidic medium, aniline is protonated to form **anilinium ion** ($-\mathrm{NH_3^+}$), which is strongly meta-directing."*

***

### B. General Marking Scheme Protocol
* **For Reasoning Questions:** Always present the structural cause first (e.g., *intermolecular H-bonding*, *electronegativity of N vs O*, *steric hindrance*), followed by the physical consequence.
* **For Distinction Questions:** 
  1. Name of Reagent.
  2. Observation with Compound A (e.g., color change, gas evolved, precipitate formed).
  3. Observation with Compound B (e.g., "No visible change" / "No reaction").
  4. Chemical equation (required for full credit in 2-mark distinction questions).

## 3. Golden Points to Remember (NCERT In-Text Lines & High-Yield Truths)

1. **Boiling Point Hierarchy:**
   

$$
\text{Primary Amine} > \text{Secondary Amine} > \text{Tertiary Amine}
$$

   * *Reason:* $1^\circ$ amines have $2$ acidic hydrogens on nitrogen (extensive intermolecular H-bonding); $2^\circ$ has $1$; $3^\circ$ amines have **no hydrogen on nitrogen** (cannot undergo intermolecular H-bonding; only dipole-dipole attractions).
   * Comparison with alcohols: $\mathrm{R-OH} > \mathrm{R-NH_2}$ because oxygen is more electronegative than nitrogen ($\chi_\mathrm{O} = 3.5 > \chi_\mathrm{N} = 3.0$), making $\mathrm{O-H}\cdots\mathrm{O}$ bonds significantly stronger than $\mathrm{N-H}\cdots\mathrm{N}$.
2. **Solubility in Water:**
   * Lower aliphatic amines are soluble in water via intermolecular H-bonds with water molecules. Solubility **decreases** with increasing molecular mass due to expansion of the hydrophobic hydrocarbon part.
3. **Electrophilic Substitution of Aniline: Bromination:**
   * Aniline with bromine water ($\mathrm{Br_2/H_2O}$) yields immediate **white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromoaniline**.
   * To prepare **monobromo (4-bromoaniline)**, the activating power of $-\mathrm{NH_2}$ must be toned down via **acetylation** using acetic anhydride ($\mathrm{(CH_3CO)_2O}$) in pyridine to form acetanilide ($-\mathrm{NHCOCH_3}$), where the lone pair on nitrogen is delocalized onto the carbonyl oxygen via resonance:
     

$$
\mathrm{- \ddot{N}H-C(=O)CH_3 \longleftrightarrow -\overset{+}{N}H=C(-O^-)CH_3}
$$

4. **Coupling Reaction Conditions:**
   * Coupling with **Phenol:** Medium must be **mildly alkaline** ($\mathrm{pH} \approx 9\text{--}10$) $\rightarrow$ produces **Orange dye** ($p$-hydroxyazobenzene).
   * Coupling with **Aniline:** Medium must be **mildly acidic** ($\mathrm{pH} \approx 4\text{--}5$) $\rightarrow$ produces **Yellow dye** ($p$-aminoazobenzene).
5. **Diazonium Salts Stability:**
   * Arenediazonium salts are stable for a short period at $0\text{--}5^\circ\mathrm{C}$ due to resonance dispersion of positive charge across the aromatic $\pi$-system.
   * Alkanediazonium salts ($\mathrm{R-N_2^+Cl^-}$) are **extremely unstable** even at $0^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and decompose quantitatively to liberate $\mathrm{N_2}\uparrow$ gas and carbocations (leading to alcohols, alkenes, halides).

## 4. Comprehensive Formula Bank & Key Named Reactions

### A. Named Reactions Formula Checklist

| Named Reaction | Reactants & Catalysts | Products | Critical Board Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Carbylamine Reaction** | $\mathrm{R-NH_2 + CHCl_3 + 3KOH(alc.) \xrightarrow{\Delta}}$ | $\mathrm{R-NC + 3KCl + 3H_2O}$ | Diagnostic test for $1^\circ$ amines only. Reagent must be **alcoholic** $\mathrm{KOH}$. |
| **Hofmann Bromamide** | $\mathrm{R-CONH_2 + Br_2 + 4NaOH}$ | $\mathrm{R-NH_2 + Na_2CO_3 + 2NaBr + 2H_2O}$ | Yields pure $1^\circ$ amine with **$n-1$** carbons. |
| **Gabriel Phthalimide** | Phthalimide $\xrightarrow{\mathrm{KOH}} \mathrm{K\text{-salt}} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{R-X}} N\text{-alkyl} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{NaOH(aq)}}$ | $\mathrm{R-NH_2} + \text{Sodium phthalate}$ | Cannot prepare aromatic $1^\circ$ amines or $3^\circ$ alkylamines. |
| **Sandmeyer Reaction** | $\mathrm{Ar-N_2^+Cl^- \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Cu_2Cl_2/HCl \; or \; Cu_2Br_2/HBr}}}$ | $\mathrm{Ar-Cl \; or \; Ar-Br} + \mathrm{N_2}\uparrow$ | Better yield than Gattermann reaction. |
| **Gattermann Reaction** | $\mathrm{Ar-N_2^+Cl^- \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Cu/HCl \; or \; Cu/HBr}}}$ | $\mathrm{Ar-Cl \; or \; Ar-Br} + \mathrm{CuCl} + \mathrm{N_2}\uparrow$ | Uses copper **powder** ($\mathrm{Cu}$). |
| **Balz-Schiemann** | $\mathrm{Ar-N_2^+Cl^- \xrightarrow{\mathrm{HBF_4}} Ar-N_2^+BF_4^- \xrightarrow{\Delta}}$ | $\mathrm{Ar-F} + \mathrm{BF_3} + \mathrm{N_2}\uparrow$ | The only standard route for fluorobenzene. |

### B. Governing Thermodynamic & Equilibrium Formulas (Physical Chemistry Cross-Over)

$$
\mathrm{p}K_b = -\log_{10} K_b
$$

$$
\mathrm{p}K_a + \mathrm{p}K_b = 14 \quad (\text{at } 298\text{ K})
$$

$$
K_b = \frac{[\mathrm{RNH_3^+}][\mathrm{OH^-}]}{[\mathrm{RNH_2}]}
$$

$$
\Delta G^\circ = -2.303 RT \log_{10} K
$$

* **Gas Constant ($R$):** Use $8.314\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$ for energy ($\Delta G^\circ$), and $0.0821\text{ L atm K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$ or $0.08314\text{ L bar K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$ for osmotic pressure ($\Pi = CRT$).
* Always ensure conversion of temperature to **Kelvin** ($T = \theta^\circ\mathrm{C} + 273.15$).
